Phone sketches

Hold The Line's opening, rendered again with one thing changed at a time. Nothing here is a shipped file. Play these on the phone's own speaker, then once more in headphones.

Fourth round, 29 September: "Now" at the top is the shipped build and the drums bare. Second round, 28 September. The gentler tilt was decided from the first round and the three human tracks now ship with it, so every file here carries that master unless its row says "old master". New: set 1b (two hybrids and the other bass voices), set 1c (the backing guitar), set 2b (the shadows' second cut), a half-gap level file in set 3, and set 4 (The Far Bank on the human master).

How to A/B. Every file in a set is the same bars from the same seed, so switching between rows keeps your place: tap another row mid-riff and it carries on from the same beat with only the voice or the mix changed. Turn "keep my place" off at the foot to start each file from its top instead.

Now: as it ships

Every pick from the rounds below is in the forge, and all six tracks are rebuilt on it. These three are the current state: the first is what the track sounds like now, the other two are the last two notes open, the drums bare so nothing covers them. Tap a step to jump to it.

Four bars of the riff's drums, three times: as it ships, then pitched down a tenth, then a fifth. You said it sounded high alone, a drum too small; this is the step you asked for, twice.

1. The driven bass

The riff, the turn and the riff on power chords (21 bars, the bass from bar 5). The shipped chain is a plucked string into tanh, then a hard clip, then a flat low-pass. Each row below changes that chain one step further.

1 As shipped
tanh, a hard clip, a growl band 200 to 500 Hz, a flat low-pass at 2.2 kHz. The reference.
2 Through a bass cabinet
The same chain, but the flat low-pass becomes a cabinet: resonant peaks at 100, 250, 700 and 1600 Hz, closed steeply above 4 kHz. The cheapest change.
3 Two stages, no hard clip
Pre-emphasis, a hot asymmetric tanh stage (even harmonics, the tube's warmth), a tone control, a gentler second stage, the cabinet. The clip is gone.
4 Clean low, driven high
The string below 250 Hz kept clean for pitch and body; only the part above goes through the stages and the cabinet. The bass recording's usual trick, and the sub is never clipped.
5 Drive follows the note
The split, with the drive riding the string's own envelope (hot at the front, cleaning up as it decays), and brightness, cabinet peaks and a little fret noise a hair different every note.
All five alone
Each voice on the riff by itself over a quiet kick and hat, four bars each with a bar's rest between, in the order above.

Listen for: the buzz on a held note (the clip), whether the low E still has a fundamental on the speaker, and whether ten minutes of it would tire you. Fatigue lives at 2 to 5 kHz.

2. The mix on a speaker

The riff, the turn and the first lift (25 bars): kick, toms in the fill that closes the lift, the soft sub taking the riff, the driven bass. Every file is levelled back to the skirmish's loudness, so what a change frees is heard as mids, not as volume.

1 As shipped
The reference, at the skirmish's level.
2 Shadows under the low end
A knock at 200 Hz and a band of generated harmonics (150 to 600 Hz) under the kick, the toms and the sub. The speaker plays the shadow; the ear infers the fundamental.
3 The sub cut
Nothing below 45 Hz, 6 dB down below 80. What the speaker could never play stops spending the limiter's and the level's budget, so the mids come up.
4 Gentler tilt, a presence lift
The master's high shelf halved (1.5 dB from 3) and a narrow lift at 1 to 2 kHz instead. Presence without the sting.
5 All three
Shadows, the sub cut and the tilt together. The shipped bass voice throughout, so this set and the last are separable.

Listen for: whether the kick and the tom fill exist at all on the speaker, whether the sub's riff in the lift can be followed, and whether the result then sounds thin in headphones.

2b. The shadows, second cut

First-round notes: the shadowed kick went "pew-pew" in headphones (its knock was a pitch sweep), the sub a little digital, the sub cut thin and tinny, the crashes clipped and hissy everywhere. So: the kick's shadow is a static 200 Hz burst plus the harmonics, no sweep; the sub's shadow is gentler and lower; a softer crash; no sub cut. Both rows are on the human master, so compare them with row 4 of set 2.

6 Shadows, second cut
Kick shadow without the sweep, sub shadow gentler, softer crash, the toms as in row 2. No sub cut.
7 The softer crash alone
Only the crash changed against row 4, to hear that one thing.

Listen for: in headphones, whether the kick still reads as the kick; on the speaker, whether row 6 keeps what row 5 did for the lift without the sub cut's thinness.

2c. The third cut, and the drums alone

Second-round notes: the second cut sounds really good on the speaker; the toms still pew a little there (too high or too bright); in headphones the kick is a floor tom, a hit followed by mush, a club bass rather than a metal kick. So the kick is rebuilt as a metal one (a quicker, shorter fall in pitch, more beater, a short static knock at 190 Hz in place of the sweep and the harmonics band, less room), and the tom is pitched lower and duller (a smaller fall, a lower shell, the stick low-passed, its shadow at 120 to 500 Hz). A drums-only file plays the three kits in turn, so the kick and the fill can be judged bare.

8 Third cut
The metal kick, the lower tom, the gentler sub shadow, the softer crash. Compare with row 6.
The drums alone, three kits
Four bars of the riff's drums with the lift's tom fill in the last bar: the shipped kit, the second cut, the third cut, a second's gap between.

Listen for: in headphones, a kick with a front and a short body, no mush; on the speaker, whether the fill's toms stop pewing.

3. The levelling gap

The loops are levelled by whole-file RMS, and three quarters of The Far Bank's power sits below 80 Hz, where a phone is silent. So on the phone it plays far quieter than its number says. Each file is twenty seconds of Hold The Line from the bass's entrance, a second's gap, then twenty seconds of The Far Bank from its groove.

1 At the shipped levels
-20.7 / -22.5 dB
Hold The Line at the skirmish's level, The Far Bank at the boss theme's.
2 Levelled by what a speaker plays
The same two, The Far Bank brought up until its loudness through a phone-speaker model (high-passed at 220 Hz, a presence bump, rolled off at 9 kHz) matches Hold The Line's.
3 Half the gap
First round: the full gap left The Far Bank a little loud on the speaker and far too loud in headphones. The same two, The Far Bank up by half.
4 A quarter of the gap
Second round: half still came across as loud in both. The same two, The Far Bank up by a quarter.

Listen for: where between rows 1 and 2 the speaker and the headphones both sit right. If no single point does, the answer is the speaker/headphones setting: shipped levels for headphones, an offset per track on the speaker profile.
