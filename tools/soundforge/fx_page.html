Effects, second pass

A fresh page. Every effect the game plays, sketched again beside what ships, in the three registers the screen already has: the console's green, the iron of the player's machines, the violet of the other side, and the red where it is about to hurt. Nothing here is a shipped file.

2 October: the third cut, to your direction of last night. More oomph. The weapons are over-the-top Doom energy, drawn out, since each die spent is deliberate and nothing fires in a row; every blow gets its weight in the band a speaker can play (its own mids, saturated, added back) and a boom behind it. Shields and buffs the same. The console is retro 90s game flair: super stereotypical computer sounds, deliberate, weighty, ironically edgy. Every row wearing a 3rd cut tag changed; the shield breaking joins as new. The first notes of 1 October (the breech, the rack's gap, the punch, the deflecting block, the shield hit, the two-second death) are all still in, bigger.

Levels. Not understated, so the third cut is written over the shipped levels: the console 4 dB up, blows 2, shields and buffs 2, kills 1. Say which should come back.

Play on the phone's own speaker first, then once more in headphones. Notes by section and row, as ever: "console 3 new", "rift 2 keep the old one".

How to A/B. A dashed button is what ships now; a solid one is the sketch. In the four rounds at the top the two files are the same bars with the same moments, so tapping the other button mid-round keeps your place (turn that off at the foot). The single sounds start from their top each tap. Every file is written at the level its sound plays at in the game, so the mix between them is the real one.

1. The console

CARE's reconstruction, the green lines, and since the third cut a 90s computer: deliberate, with intent, not understated. One grammar for every press: a clack (a mechanical key with its casing, a thing pressed on purpose), a voice (a square and a saw with an octave under them for weight, thick rather than chirpy, on the music's E: G3, B3, E4, nothing higher), data (a two-tone burst with the keys typing in it, the machine working) and steps (discrete tones in a row: the confirm, the grant, the cancel). The die is a mechanism, not a button. The 24 September "future-digital, not retro" is retired for the interface; the 25 September note (too high, too chirpy) still rules the pitches.

2. The iron

The player's machines, nineties 3D in five makers' metals. A blow sounds by the weapon's shape, since the mount rule made the shape the thing on screen. A hit, a block and a kill sound in the metal of the machine struck. A footfall by silhouette. The third cut: over-the-top Doom energy, drawn out. A phone speaker plays nothing under about 200 Hz, so a blow's weight here is its own mids saturated and added back, a boom tail behind every blast, and length. Nothing glides in pitch (the Pac-Man ruling stands for weapons).

By shape

By metal: struck, holding, dying

Third cut. The hit is heavier: more body and drive in the thud, a short boom behind it, the weight in the speaker's band. The block deflects rather than knocking hollow (your 1 October note), with more front and a longer skid now: a scrape whose band climbs as it glances off, the plate's knock bright and short, the little metal allowed at a higher pitch for the ring of it. The kill is near three seconds and the big deal it should be: the blast with its boom, hydraulics letting go, the frame groaning on E1 as it gives, a second lower blast, the magazine going up as a third, the maker's plate falling in pieces for two seconds, sparks twice, the hull hitting the ground as the biggest thud of the set with a slab of plate under it and a boom of its own, steam, the last pieces.

3. The rift

The other side, drawn flat and painterly against the player's 3D. Each family gets its own blow, heavy blow and arrival. The Risen are dry bone and grave-light. The Infernal Horde is fire, loud and telegraphed. The Chorus is geometry: the choir on a cluster, gated and crushed as if the feed cannot hold it, and it talks to the console's own ticks. Now, every foe is the same slash, slam and spawn.

What a pick means

A console pick lands as its recipe in the forge. A shape pick means the blow's sound is chosen by the component's shape rather than its damage type, with plasma for the Arsenal's energy, and a punch for the melee pieces you name as blunt. A metal pick means a hit, block and kill carry the struck machine's maker. The shield hit means the replay tells three things apart that it now plays as two: a standing shield taking the blow (the shield hit), armour turning it on bare hull (the block), and the hull taking it (the hit). A family pick means a foe's blow and arrival carry its faction, and the reveal gets a sound of its own. A footfall pick means the map plays the mover's silhouette. The shield breaking lands as its recipe, no hook needed. A level pick (keep the offset, or come back) lands in the forge's levels table. Each hook is small; none is written yet.
