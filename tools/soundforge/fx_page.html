1. The console

CARE's reconstruction, the green lines, and since the fourth cut a 90s computer game's cockpit: StarCraft 1's Terran panel, MechWarrior 2's targeting computer, Descent's menus. Retro-future, deliberate, weighty. One grammar for every press: a click (a dry plastic button, no spring, no casing), a tone (a clean sine with a triangle edge, an octave over and under it, a short glide into the note through a resonant filter that opens and closes with it, a chorus on it), the computer talking (a fast run of clean tones on a few pitches around E, the filter opening over the run), air (a filtered sweep for things leaving or arriving) and a thump under anything confirmed. The die is a mechanism, not a button. Glides are back in the console only, short and into a note; the 25 September note still rules the pitches (G3 to E4 for the voice, the run up to E5).

2. The iron

The player's machines, nineties 3D in five makers' metals. A blow sounds by the weapon's shape, since the mount rule made the shape the thing on screen. A hit, a block and a kill sound in the metal of the machine struck. A footfall by silhouette. The third cut: over-the-top Doom energy, drawn out. A phone speaker plays nothing under about 200 Hz, so a blow's weight here is its own mids saturated and added back, a boom tail behind every blast, and length. Nothing glides in pitch (the Pac-Man ruling stands for weapons).

5. The voice

Your references: MechWarrior 2's Bitching Betty, StarCraft's Adjutant ("nuclear launch detected"), Doom's VEGA for a male-presenting one. That era's speech was synthesised or processed to sound so, which is what a formant voice gives: a glottal pulse through three resonators set per sound and glided between them, noise for the hisses, closures and bursts for the stops, over a comm channel that opens on a click and closes on a tick of static. Crude by design, spelt by hand a word at a time: the question is whether CARE's console should speak at all, and in which register, before the words are made good. Two voices: a Betty at 215 Hz, crisp and quick; a VEGA at 102 Hz, lower and slower. Nothing plays now.

What a pick means

A console pick lands as its recipe in the forge. A shape pick means the blow's sound is chosen by the component's shape rather than its damage type, with plasma for the Arsenal's energy, and the blunt impact for a component whose sound word says blunt (the plow and the knuckles carry it; the rest derive their sound from shape and face). A metal pick means a hit, block and kill carry the struck machine's maker. The shield hit means the replay tells three things apart that it now plays as two: a standing shield taking the blow (the shield hit), armour turning it on bare hull (the block), and the hull taking it (the hit). A family pick means a foe's blow and arrival carry its faction, and the reveal gets a sound of its own. A footfall pick means the map plays the mover's silhouette. The shield breaking lands as its recipe, no hook needed. A level pick (keep the offset, or come back) lands in the forge's levels table. A voice pick means a proper pass on the words (a bigger piece of work than any sound here) and a line per moment in the game. Each hook is small; none is written yet.
