Effects pass

A fresh page. Every effect the game plays, sketched again beside what ships, in the three registers the screen already has: the console's green, the iron of the player's machines, the violet of the other side, and the red where it is about to hurt. Nothing here is a shipped file.

Where it stands. The weapons, shields, kills, the rift and the red are the third cut of 2 October, parked as acceptable. The console is the palette in its eleventh cut (section 1), every glass levelled by measurement, and section 6 is a baseline mix against the music, built from the profiles you liked and reshaped cut by cut to your notes; the earlier cuts are gone from this page and live in the repo's history. The voice (section 5) is decided: Jenny, an openly licensed synthesiser, through the mid chain slowed a third with her plosives tamed.

Levels. The iron, the rift and the red are written over the shipped levels (blows 2 dB up, shields and buffs 2, kills 1); say which should come back. The console is levelled by what the phone model plays of each file, at a stated level per row.

Play on the phone's own speaker first, then once more in headphones. Notes by section and row, as ever: "console 3 new", "rift 2 keep the old one".

How to A/B. A dashed button is what ships now; a solid one is the sketch. In the four rounds at the top the two files are the same bars with the same moments, so tapping the other button mid-round keeps your place (turn that off at the foot). The single sounds start from their top each tap. Every file is written at the level its sound plays at in the game, so the mix between them is the real one.

1. The console

The palette, eleventh cut (7 October): a levelling pass on every glass with the tenth cut's rule. Each recipe now hands its under-layer and what rides on it to one function that measures both through the phone model and sets the under-layer from the rider by a lead, so none is set by a recipe's gain any more. The general rule's start is ten decibels under the rider (your passes of 7 October: every undertone comes down, the decibel-and-a-half steps were not enough, down more, try ten); the rows you called fine keep a decibel and a half over, the ones you wanted down a little sit three and a half under, and each row's note says which. The codex's undertones are pitched down toward the menu's. The under-layers: a press, dismiss, confirm, the lock and unlock, the map's acquire, the reroll, the round opening, a route set, the hold's touch under the shoo, and the codex. Every under-layer, glass or plate: the menu's plate under its boops and refuse's scrape under P28 are balanced the same way. The one exception is a die spent, whose pulse sits below what the phone plays, so measuring it through the phone model would mean nothing; the glass is the sound there. The end of the turn is the plate alone, with nothing riding on it. And the baseline mix (section 6): the round opening, the menu and refuse come down from level with a hit to seven decibels over the music; the reroll and a route set to six. The rules so far: the die is a virtual object and lives in the glass register alone, both registers where it meets the UI; a press, a menu item and a selection are one act and one sound; the map is where something is acquired; the map, confirm, the round opening and a route set each have their own figure; the chunk is kept for handing control over at the end of the turn; wherever a boop rides on a glass bow the boop comes first; the common sounds are soft; a glass under a boop is measured level with it.

2. The iron

The player's machines, nineties 3D in five makers' metals. A blow sounds by the weapon's shape, since the mount rule made the shape the thing on screen. A hit, a block and a kill sound in the metal of the machine struck. A footfall by silhouette. The third cut: over-the-top Doom energy, drawn out. A phone speaker plays nothing under about 200 Hz, so a blow's weight here is its own mids saturated and added back, a boom tail behind every blast, and length. Nothing glides in pitch (the Pac-Man ruling stands for weapons).

5. The voice: Jenny, decided

7 October, decided. CARE's console speaks in Jenny's voice: an openly licensed synthesiser (the Jenny dataset, rendered offline by Piper), through the mid chain (one comb fed back hard, a sub-octave, a light grain) with the model slowed a third and her plosives tamed (the band above 1.5 kHz ducked wherever its short envelope leaps, before a gentle saturator). Nobody expects an Irish adjutant; she hits her consonants hard, and her cadence of rushed and slow words plays well for repeated listening. 6. Levels: a baseline

7 October. Every family measured through the phone model (a speaker plays nothing under about 220 Hz), as the loudest fifty milliseconds, against the music's mean the same way. The music varies ten decibels between tracks on a phone: the rift tracks lose their power below the speaker, so the same effects sit seven decibels hotter under The Crossing than under Hold The Line, and the map's tracks sit another six under. Hold The Line is the reference (Ryan, 7 October): the fight is where the mix is thickest, and the other tracks have not been levelled against it yet.

The sliders. Both are linear and go to the buses through linear_to_db: music at 0.8 (the default) is -1.9 dB, effects at 1.0 is 0. The baseline is music 0.63 (-4 dB) and effects 1.0, which is how every sequence on this page is mixed. Under it Hold The Line's mean on the phone is -24 dB, and the ladder below is each family's loudest moment against that mean. Reading it: the console's common sounds sit a few decibels over the music and its statements seven; the voice ten; shields and the rift's blows eight to ten; hits and blows ten to fifteen; a kill and the contact at the top.

What a pick means

A console pick lands as its recipe in the forge. A shape pick means the blow's sound is chosen by the component's shape rather than its damage type, with plasma for the Arsenal's energy, and the blunt impact for a component whose sound says blunt (the plow and the knuckles carry it; the rest derive their sound from shape and face). A sound list means layers: the first word is the blow, each word after it rides on the landing, and the forge builds the layer as a recipe timed to the blow. The list is game effects only, never gameplay: it is not read from keywords or tags. A metal pick means a hit, block and kill carry the struck machine's maker. The shield hit means the replay tells three things apart that it now plays as two: a standing shield taking the blow (the shield hit), armour turning it on bare hull (the block), and the hull taking it (the hit). A family pick means a foe's blow and arrival carry its faction, and the reveal gets a sound of its own. A footfall pick means the map plays the mover's silhouette. The shield breaking lands as its recipe, no hook needed. A level pick (keep the offset, or come back) lands in the forge's levels table. A voice pick means a proper pass on the words (a bigger piece of work than any sound here) and a line per moment in the game. Each hook is small; none is written yet.
