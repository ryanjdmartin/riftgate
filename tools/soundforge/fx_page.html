Effects pass

A fresh page. Every effect the game plays, sketched again beside what ships, in the three registers the screen already has: the console's green, the iron of the player's machines, the violet of the other side, and the red where it is about to hurt. Nothing here is a shipped file.

Where it stands. The weapons, shields, kills, the rift and the red are the third cut of 2 October, parked as acceptable. The console is the palette in its ninth cut (section 1), built from the profiles you liked and reshaped cut by cut to your notes; the earlier cuts are gone from this page and live in the repo's history. The voice (section 5) is an openly licensed synthesiser through the machine chain, Amy the baseline and Jenny the one being played with.

Levels. The iron, the rift and the red are written over the shipped levels (blows 2 dB up, shields and buffs 2, kills 1); say which should come back. The console is levelled by what the phone model plays of each file, at a stated level per row.

Play on the phone's own speaker first, then once more in headphones. Notes by section and row, as ever: "console 3 new", "rift 2 keep the old one".

How to A/B. A dashed button is what ships now; a solid one is the sketch. In the four rounds at the top the two files are the same bars with the same moments, so tapping the other button mid-round keeps your place (turn that off at the foot). The single sounds start from their top each tap. Every file is written at the level its sound plays at in the game, so the mix between them is the real one.

1. The console

The palette, ninth cut (6 October, late). The map's acquire is one soft boop on G over more glass. The reroll is four clicks. A route set has more glass under it. The shoo is held, then tails shorter. The menu opening has the menu closing's plate shape (swelling at the start, the chunk, tailing off) under its own tone, E4 twice. The rules so far: the die is a virtual object and lives in the glass register alone, both registers where it meets the UI; a press, a menu item and a selection are one act and one sound; the map is where something is acquired; the map, confirm, the round opening and a route set each have their own figure; the chunk is kept for handing control over at the end of the turn; wherever a boop rides on a glass bow the boop comes first; the common sounds are soft; a glass under a boop is loud enough to hear or it is not there.

2. The iron

The player's machines, nineties 3D in five makers' metals. A blow sounds by the weapon's shape, since the mount rule made the shape the thing on screen. A hit, a block and a kill sound in the metal of the machine struck. A footfall by silhouette. The third cut: over-the-top Doom energy, drawn out. A phone speaker plays nothing under about 200 Hz, so a blow's weight here is its own mids saturated and added back, a boom tail behind every blast, and length. Nothing glides in pitch (the Pac-Man ruling stands for weapons).

By shape

1Barrel3rd cut
A slug cannon, a railgun: one heavy report with a boom behind it, the shell cracking the air, the hit as a blast of its own, the gun's recoil hydraulics, then the breech cycling well after (the case out, the breech closing). Now, every kinetic line weapon is a burst of four.
2Twin barrel3rd cut

3Rack3rd cut
A missile rack: four launches, each an ignition thud and a whoosh (a band opening, not a pitch), the salvo in flight for most of a second, then four impacts as blasts of their own and the air rolling after them. Now, indirect fire is one cannon shell.
4Blade3rd cut
A chainsaw claw: the motor revving up in loudness with its filter opening (no glide), the teeth a flutter on it, three bites each a rip through plate with the plate knocked and sparks off it, the motor dying away. Over a second.
5Blunt impact (new)new
One blow for the Breaching Plow and the Gravity Knuckles alike: a multi-ton machine's mass arriving, a car into a wall. The mass coming (a low rumble rising, the hydraulics driving), the impact as the biggest thud of the blows with the crumple on it (a dense run of plate knocks over a sixth of a second), a broadband crack, glass going, the boom behind it, debris, a rattle settling, the hydraulics venting, and a little of the wet under it for what it hit. The content says so: a sound word on the component, or a list where the first word is the blow and the rest ride on the landing. The plow says blunt; the knuckles say blunt, plasma, and the third button is what the second word adds: the hot splash and its crackle on the impact.

By metal: struck, holding, dying

Third cut. The hit is heavier: more body and drive in the thud, a short boom behind it, the weight in the speaker's band. The block deflects rather than knocking hollow (your 1 October note), with more front and a longer skid now: a scrape whose band climbs as it glances off, the plate's knock bright and short, the little metal allowed at a higher pitch for the ring of it. The kill is near three seconds and the big deal it should be: the blast with its boom, hydraulics letting go, the frame groaning on E1 as it gives, a second lower blast, the magazine going up as a third, the maker's plate falling in pieces for two seconds, sparks twice, the hull hitting the ground as the biggest thud of the set with a slab of plate under it and a boom of its own, steam, the last pieces.

3. The rift

The other side, drawn flat and painterly against the player's 3D. Each family gets its own blow, heavy blow and arrival. The Risen are dry bone and grave-light. The Infernal Horde is fire, loud and telegraphed. The Chorus is geometry: the choir on a cluster, gated and crushed as if the feed cannot hold it, and it talks to the console's own ticks. Now, every foe is the same slash, slam and spawn.

4. The red

Where it is about to hurt. Tuned to E so they sit in any of the six tracks; the dive counts in on the console's key; defeat ends on the feed losing signal.

1Contact
The horn a power chord on E2 and B2, the klaxon the console's own tone on B4 twice, the thud bigger. Now the horn is E alone and the klaxon sits on A.
2The dive
No drive spinning down (that was a glide, the one left in the set). The feed's floor rises as a filter opening, the console arms with a key under each count-in beep on B3, the beeps closing in, the last long on E4, until the cut.
3The gate struck
The same explosion, the structure groaning on B1 with E1 under it, three knocks of a big plate. Now the groan sits between notes.
4Victory
The power chord on E, and CARE logging it: the console works, a key, and the all-clear as two flat tones, B3 up to E4.
5Defeat
The hum on E flickering out, the last thing to fall, then the reconstruction losing the signal: a crushed burst of static and two console ticks into silence.

Kept as they are: the cut, the vent, overheat, withdraw, a foe stepping into the line. The shield breaking is in the iron now (row 17).

Listen for: whether the dive still builds without its spin-down, whether the two-tone all-clear is irony or parody, and whether the signal loss lands as the end of the feed.

What a pick means

A console pick lands as its recipe in the forge. A shape pick means the blow's sound is chosen by the component's shape rather than its damage type, with plasma for the Arsenal's energy, and the blunt impact for a component whose sound says blunt (the plow and the knuckles carry it; the rest derive their sound from shape and face). A sound list means layers: the first word is the blow, each word after it rides on the landing, and the forge builds the layer as a recipe timed to the blow. The list is game effects only, never gameplay: it is not read from keywords or tags. A metal pick means a hit, block and kill carry the struck machine's maker. The shield hit means the replay tells three things apart that it now plays as two: a standing shield taking the blow (the shield hit), armour turning it on bare hull (the block), and the hull taking it (the hit). A family pick means a foe's blow and arrival carry its faction, and the reveal gets a sound of its own. A footfall pick means the map plays the mover's silhouette. The shield breaking lands as its recipe, no hook needed. A level pick (keep the offset, or come back) lands in the forge's levels table. A voice pick means a proper pass on the words (a bigger piece of work than any sound here) and a line per moment in the game. Each hook is small; none is written yet.
