# Keyword line-up

A proposal for Ryan (11 October), not a decision: every keyword, rider, status and unique
rule on both sides, lined up by the niche it fills, to see what is needed, what merges and
what can go. Nothing here is built. Carriers are today's content; "locked" is a fixed
component, "stock" one a chassis carries in a hardpoint, "unstocked" a blueprint no chassis
stocks.

Ryan, 11 October: "we have redundant keywords. We also have words that differ between ally
and enemy that fill the same niche but operate slightly differently. Let's line everything
up and see what's needed and what can go", and "we could probably make some heuristics on
what keywords are owned by what maker".

## 1. The player's keywords

| Keyword | What it does | Carriers | Proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spread | Also hits the target's neighbours in its rank, at full | Chainsaw Claw (Minerva, locked), Breaching Plow (Minerva, locked), Proximity Klaxon (Minerva, stock) | Keep as the full sweep, or fold into Splash (section 6) |
| Penetrate | Also hits the other rank of the target's column | Enfilade Beam (W&S, stock) | Keep: the column shape and back-rank access; needs carriers |
| Burn | Each foe hit gains a Burning stack per 10 of the blow | Incendiary Shells (W&S), Solar Flare Array (Aphelion, locked), Photosphere Lance (Aphelion, locked), Blazethrower, Meteor Artillery (Arsenal), Gravity Knuckles (Arsenal, locked) | Keep: a core verb |

| Lure | Each foe reached turns on the spender for the round | Proximity Klaxon (Minerva) | Keep: redirection is a core verb; needs carriers |
| Disrupt | Each foe reached takes its next move, with a new target | Differentiation Probe (Aphelion) | Keep; needs carriers |
| Rapid | The entry switches off, the die rerolls free, the machine acts again | Wake Diffuser (Kestrel), Flakshot Repeater (W&S, locked), Pulse Vacillator (Aphelion, unstocked) | Keep: a core verb (the Laminar Deflector's twist adds a carrier) |

| Overshield | A shield that may pass the maximum; the excess fades at the round's end | Backwash Ionizer (Kestrel, locked) | Fold into the ionizer as a unique rule |
| Spillover | Damage past a front-rank kill carries into the foe behind | None: the Wild Boar's Juggernaut Drive grants it to every weapon | Merge with Breach (section 4) |

Fourteen today. With the three folds and the merge: Spread, Penetrate, Burn, Suppress,
Shock, Lure, Disrupt, Rapid, Volatile, Cleanse and Breach, eleven; ten if Splash replaces
Spread, eleven again if Splash joins it.

**The rule this suggests:** a keyword earns its place on three carriers, or on two makers.
Anything on one or two pieces is a unique rule printed on the entry, the way Vents all and
the missing-hull bonus already are, so the codex teaches it on the piece rather than as a
word to learn.

## 2. Unique rules (component properties)

Printed on the entry, never a keyword: rolled or flat, strikes, whom equipment reaches (self,
ally, both), **Vents all** (the Afterburner Cannon), **missing hull** (the Sparrowhawk's
finisher), **the hopper** (the Badger's pair, built 11 October). The folds above join them:
the Arc Fault Cannon's chain, the Soulcutter Talons' devour, the Backwash Ionizer's overshield.

Open, from the cracked question: whether a crack strips a unique rule along with the
keywords. Measured 11 October, a crack changes almost nothing in a fight (most cracked
entries carry no keyword), and one state, an injury destroying outright, moves no row by
more than a point; the numbers are in the bible's section 12. It is a campaign question:
what an injury costs for good.

## 3. Statuses as they stand

- **On a machine, from its own kit:** Overcharged (+5 a stack), Braced (armour +5 a stack),
  Primed (blows leave Burning), Retaliate (strikers are Shocked), Crucible (the Nemesis's
  melee +5 a stack), Hopper (the Badger's loads).
- **On a machine, from a foe:** Overheating (heat a stack at the round's end), Scrambled (the
  next reroll lands anywhere), Disabled (an entry blank through the next turn), Corrupted
  (spending an entry costs hull, shield first).
- **On a foe, from the team:** Burning, Suppressed, Shocked, Paradox.
- **On a foe, from its own moves:** Folded (Harden: armour until its next action), Kindled
  (Rally: hits harder), Getting Back Up (Reraise).
- **Its own kind:** Numbed, the one resistance.

## 5. Who owns which keyword (a heuristic)

A maker's own pieces carry its keywords; another maker's keyword appears on a locked piece,
or with a twist of its own. It streamlines what each maker means on the die, the way the
naming vocabularies do for names. A guideline, never checked by the validator.

| Maker | Owns | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Minerva Heavy Works | Spread, Lure, Braced | Heavy industry holding a line: sweep, draw, brace |
| Kestrel Dynamics | Rapid, Overcharged (and the chain and overshield rules) | Speed: tempo, burst, escort |
| Wainwright & Sutler | Suppress, Penetrate, strikes | Indirect fire, volume, pinning |
| Aphelion | Disrupt, Shock, Cleanse, Primed (and the Paradox) | The status house |
| The Arsenal | Volatile, Burn, Retaliate (and the devour rule) | Pain, fire, retribution |

Today's exceptions, and what each could do:

- Rapid on the Flakshot Repeater (W&S): locked, so it stands.
- Rapid on the Pulse Vacillator (Aphelion, unstocked): give it an Aphelion verb.
- Suppress on the Umbra Inducer (Aphelion, stock): Suppress shared by W&S and Aphelion, or
  the inducer takes Disrupt.
- Shock on the Tesla Gauntlets (Minerva, unstocked): the gauntlets move to Aphelion, or
  Minerva shares Shock (the Armadillo's live hull is Minerva's too).
- Burn on the Incendiary Shells (W&S, stock) and on Aphelion's locked Solar Flare Array and
  Photosphere Lance: the locked two stand; the shells are the question. Burn shared by the
  Arsenal and W&S, or the shells take a W&S verb.

## 6. Splash, and what Indirect means

Ryan, 11 October: inspect the indirect splash penalty and whether it is needed; whether
splash is really a keyword taking the place of Spread or working like it; with a four-wide
rank, area damage is powerful; and every indirect weapon splashing implies they all explode,
where a beam tagged Indirect would open wider synergies.

Today **danger close** is a rule of reach: Indirect fire on the front rank costs the firer
shield, half the damage the target took, never hull (the Artillery Rangefinder spares it).
It prices Indirect against Direct, and it makes every Indirect weapon an explosive.

The proposal:

1. **Indirect is reach only.** It reaches any foe, and its price is its number: at equal heat
   Direct hits harder (decided). Danger close goes. A beam, a phased emitter or a lobbed
   shell can all be Indirect, and only a tag says how it travels.
2. **Splash is a keyword**, the explosive's: "the foes beside the target take half the blow"
   (rounded up to a five). Missiles, shells and the Gravity Knuckles carry it, on any reach.
3. **Spread** either stays as the full sweep on a few melee pieces (the Chainsaw Claw, the
   Breaching Plow), or folds into Splash so there is one area keyword. With four columns a
   full sweep reaches three of four front foes; half keeps area damage a bonus, not the
   kill.
4. If a price for firing explosives into one's own front line is still wanted after
   measuring, it belongs to Splash, never to reach.

**What danger close does today** (11 October, 50 seeds a cell, every row with the Artillery
Rangefinder fitted to every machine, which is the game without danger close, against the same
run without it): no row's win rate moves by more than three points, and most not at all.
Indirect's share of attack spends rises from 23% to 25%, and less of it goes to the back rank
(25% to 19% over every pack; 63% to 51% against packs with a back rank). So the splash
steers Indirect toward the back a little and prices nothing: Indirect is already priced by
its numbers, about 12 hull a spend for 8 heat against Direct's 18 for 9. It can go without
moving the balance.
