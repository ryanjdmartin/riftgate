# Keyword line-up

A proposal for Ryan, begun 11 October: every keyword, rider, status and unique rule on both
sides, lined up by the niche it fills, to see what is needed, what merges and what can go.
Where Ryan has answered, the line says so; everything else is still a proposal. Carriers are
today's content; "locked" is a fixed component, "stock" one a chassis carries in a
hardpoint, "unstocked" a blueprint no chassis stocks.

Ryan, 11 October: "we have redundant keywords. We also have words that differ between ally
and enemy that fill the same niche but operate slightly differently. Let's line everything
up and see what's needed and what can go", and "we could probably make some heuristics on
what keywords are owned by what maker".

## 1. The player's keywords

| Keyword | What it does | Carriers | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spread | Also hits the target's neighbours in its rank, at full | Chainsaw Claw (Minerva, locked), Breaching Plow (Minerva, locked), Proximity Klaxon (Minerva, stock) | The row keyword; proposed at half, one word with Splash (section 7) |
| Penetrate | Also hits the other rank of the target's column | Enfilade Beam (W&S, stock) | The column keyword (section 7); needs carriers |
| Burn | Each foe hit gains a Burning stack per 10 of the blow | Incendiary Shells (W&S), Solar Flare Array (Aphelion, locked), Photosphere Lance (Aphelion, locked), Blazethrower, Meteor Artillery, Gravity Knuckles (the Arsenal, the knuckles locked) | Keep: a core verb |
| Suppress | Each foe hit skips its action (Numbed may resist) | Suppression Battery, Megataser Harpoon (W&S, the harpoon locked), Umbra Inducer (Aphelion) | Keep: a core verb |
| Shock | Each foe reached and left standing takes the team's next blow double | Polarity Projector (Aphelion), Tesla Gauntlets (Minerva, unstocked) | Keep: a core verb |
| Lure | Each foe reached turns on the spender for the round | Proximity Klaxon (Minerva) | Keep: redirection is a core verb; needs carriers |
| Disrupt | Each foe reached takes its next move, with a new target | Differentiation Probe (Aphelion) | Keep; needs carriers |
| Rapid | The entry switches off, the die rerolls free, the machine acts again | Wake Diffuser (Kestrel), Flakshot Repeater (W&S, locked), Pulse Vacillator (Aphelion, unstocked) | Keep: a core verb (the Laminar Deflector's twist adds a carrier, section 5) |
| Volatile | Spending it costs its owner 10 hull, past shield and armour | Oblation Ward (the Arsenal) | Keep: the Arsenal's price (the Gravity Knuckles and the Entropy Condenser's twist add carriers). Corrupted stays its own (section 4) |
| Cleanse | The machine it lands on loses a foe's debuffs | Corona Shield (unstocked), Phased Sublimator (Aphelion) | Keep |
| Chain | A kill repeats the attack on the next target, rolling and paying heat again | Arc Fault Cannon (Kestrel) | Keeps its keyword: the kill's area shape, the Sparrowhawk's swarm answer (section 7); wants a second carrier |
| Overshield | A shield that may pass the maximum; the excess fades at the round's end | Backwash Ionizer (Kestrel, locked) | Kept for now as a rare keyword (Ryan, 11 October) |
| Spillover | Damage past a front-rank kill carries into the foe behind | None: the Wild Boar's Juggernaut Drive grants it to every weapon | One word with the foes' Breach (Ryan, 11 October); the word is weighed in section 8 |

Thirteen today, Devour having become a rule printed on the Soulcutter Talons (Ryan, 11
October; built). With Ryan's answers and the area proposal the count stays thirteen: Spread,
Penetrate, Burn, Suppress, Shock, Lure, Disrupt, Rapid, Volatile, Cleanse, Chain, Overshield
and Breach, the last shared with the foes. The gain is clarity (one row keyword, one
overflow word for both sides, the merges across the sides), not number: the count comes
down toward ten only if Penetrate, Lure or Disrupt never gain carriers.

**The guide this suggests:** a keyword earns its place on three carriers, or on two makers.
Anything on one or two pieces is better as a unique rule printed on the entry, the way Vents
all and the missing-hull bonus already are, so the codex teaches it on the piece rather than
as a word to learn. Ryan allows a rare keyword where more carriers are meant to follow
(Overshield, 11 October), so this is a guide, never a check.

## 2. Unique rules (component properties)

Printed on the entry, never a keyword, and marked on the die by the star chip: rolled or
flat, strikes, whom equipment reaches (self, ally, both), **Vents all** (the Afterburner
Cannon), **missing hull** (the Pinion Driver), **the hopper** (the Badger's pair), **devour**
(the Soulcutter Talons). All built.

Injuries (Ryan, 11 October): the threshold is a share of maximum hull on purpose, so the
tank and the glass cannon both have a place and full greedy aggression risks something
against its reward. Measured the same day: the threshold sorts the machines as meant (no
heavy machine is ever injured, the light ones 1.4 to 1.9 times a fight), but a crack costs
little inside a fight (0.4 keywords on average; most cracked entries carry none), so the
stake lives in the campaign, where a destroyed hardpoint component is gone. Whether a crack
should also take a unique rule, or its number, is the bible's question in section 12.

## 3. Statuses as they stand

- **On a machine, from its own kit:** Overcharged (+5 a stack), Braced (armour +5 a stack),
  Primed (blows leave Burning), Retaliate (strikers are Shocked), Crucible (the Nemesis's
  melee +5 a stack), Hopper (the Badger's loads).
- **On a machine, from a foe:** Overheating (heat a stack at the round's end), Scrambled (the
  next reroll lands anywhere), Disabled (an entry blank through the next turn), Corrupted
  (spending an entry costs hull, shield first, for the skirmish; cleansable).
- **On a foe, from the team:** Burning, Suppressed, Shocked, Paradox.
- **On a foe, from its own moves:** Folded (Harden: armour until its next action), Kindled
  (Rally: hits harder), Getting Back Up (Reraise).
- **Its own kind:** Numbed, the one resistance.

## 5. Who owns which keyword (a heuristic)

A maker's own pieces carry its keywords; another maker's keyword appears on a locked piece,
or with a twist of its own. It streamlines what each maker means on the die, the way the
naming vocabularies do for names. A guideline, never checked by the validator.

| Maker | Owns | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Minerva Heavy Works | Spread, Lure, Braced | Heavy industry holding a line: sweep, draw, brace |
| Kestrel Dynamics | Rapid, Overcharged, Overshield (and the chain rule) | Speed: tempo, burst, escort |
| Wainwright & Sutler | Suppress, Penetrate, strikes | Indirect fire, volume, pinning |
| Aphelion | Disrupt, Shock, Cleanse, Primed (and the Paradox) | The status house |
| The Arsenal | Volatile, Burn, Retaliate (and the devour rule) | Pain, fire, retribution |

Today's exceptions, and what each could do:

- Rapid on the Flakshot Repeater (W&S): locked, so it stands.
- Rapid on the Pulse Vacillator (Aphelion, unstocked): give it an Aphelion verb.
- Suppress on the Umbra Inducer (Aphelion, stock): Suppress shared by W&S and Aphelion, or
  the inducer takes Disrupt.
- Shock on the Tesla Gauntlets (Minerva, unstocked): the gauntlets move to Aphelion, or
  Minerva shares Shock (the Armadillo's live hull is Minerva's too).
- Burn on the Incendiary Shells (W&S, stock) and on Aphelion's locked Solar Flare Array and
  Photosphere Lance: the locked two stand; the shells are the question. Burn shared by the
  Arsenal and W&S, or the shells take a W&S verb.

**The twists** (proposed 11 October; built with the basic kit in the stock pass, if the
runner agrees). The basic-kit rule says every piece that is not a basic carries something of
its own, and two plain pieces carry nothing: the Laminar Deflector (a Kestrel shield, 30) and
the Entropy Condenser (an Arsenal vent, 30). Each takes its maker's keyword:

- **Laminar Deflector:** a shield of 20 with Rapid. Kestrel's tempo: shield, reroll, act
  again; it pairs with the Wake Diffuser.
- **Entropy Condenser:** a vent of 40 with Volatile. Arsenal equipment is the biggest number,
  paid in hull, like the Oblation Ward; on the Nemesis every vent is a Crucible stack.

## 6. Splash, and what Indirect means

Ryan, 11 October: inspect the indirect splash penalty and whether it is needed; whether
splash is really a keyword taking the place of Spread or working like it; with a four-wide
rank, area damage is powerful; and every indirect weapon splashing implies they all explode,
where a beam tagged Indirect would open wider synergies.

Today **danger close** is a rule of reach: Indirect fire on the front rank costs the firer
shield, half the damage the target took, never hull (the Artillery Rangefinder spares it).
It prices Indirect against Direct, and it makes every Indirect weapon an explosive.

**Decided (Ryan, 11 October, "agreed on splash"):** Indirect is reach only. Danger close goes,
so a beam, a phased emitter or a lobbed shell can all be Indirect, and only a tag says how it
travels; an explosive says so with an area keyword, on any reach. If a price for firing
explosives into one's own front line is still wanted after measuring, it belongs to the
keyword, never to reach. Not built: it waits on the area keywords (section 7), and the
Artillery Rangefinder, whose rule spares danger close, needs a new one.

**What danger close does today** (11 October, 50 seeds a cell, every row with the Artillery
Rangefinder fitted to every machine, which is the game without danger close, against the same
run without it): no row's win rate moves by more than three points, and most not at all.
Indirect's share of attack spends rises from 23% to 25%, and less of it goes to the back rank
(25% to 19% over every pack; 63% to 51% against packs with a back rank). So the splash
steers Indirect toward the back a little and prices nothing: Indirect is already priced by
its numbers, about 12 hull a spend for 8 heat against Direct's 18 for 9.

## 7. Area, by shape

Ryan, 11 October: "Evaluate splash versus spread and whether there's a place for two AoE
keywords (and whatever the line pierce is)", and "Chain needs to be part of the AoE
discussion".

Every area effect today, by the shape it reaches and when:

| Shape | Always | Only on a kill |
|---|---|---|
| The row (the target's neighbours) | Spread, at full; Splash, at half (proposed) | |
| The column (the foe behind or in front) | Penetrate, at full | Spillover: what is left past the kill |
| Anywhere | | Chain: the whole attack again, rolled and heated again |

Measured (11 October, 40 seeds a cell, random and greedy averaged over twenty packs):

- **Spread at half:** the Chainsaw Claw and the Breaching Plow hitting their neighbours for
  half instead of full costs the Badger 3 points (45/69 to 42/66, random/greedy) and the
  Wild Boar 2 to 3 (62/85 to 60/82); the team rows do not move.
- **Penetrate** off the Enfilade Beam: nothing changes in any pack but the four formations,
  because only a formation has a back rank today (a pack's extra foes wait as
  reinforcements, never behind). Against the formations the Clydesdale loses either way; the
  Wainwright & Sutler team drops from 78 to 70 (greedy) against the Meadow formation.
- **Chain** off the Arc Fault Cannon: the Sparrowhawk falls from 46/68 to 38/55, every swarm
  it beat collapsing (the Goblins and the Revenants 20/88 to 0/0, the Bombs 52/100 to 0/8,
  the Needlers 72/100 to 18/100); the Goshawk loses 3 points greedy, the Kestrel team 3.
  Chain is how a single-target glass cannon answers a swarm: each kill buys the next shot.
- **Spillover** is the Wild Boar's whole passive, and with every passive removed (the stat
  prices' run, 30 seeds a cell, greedy) the Boar loses about one point, the least of any
  passive that works in a lone fight: like Penetrate, it needs a foe behind.

**The proposal: area keywords split by shape, never by strength.**

1. **One row keyword, at half.** Spread and Splash reach the same foes and differ only in a
   number, and two words for one shape teach a number, not a rule: the keyword form of the
   plain pieces that differ only by number. Halving costs the claw's and the plow's machines
   about three points, and with four columns a full sweep reaches three of four front foes,
   so half keeps area a bonus rather than the kill. The word is Ryan's: **Spread** fits a
   chainsaw, a plow, a siren and a shell alike; **Splash** says explosive, which a chainsaw
   is not. If two row keywords are wanted after all, they differ by footprint, never by
   number: a sweep (the neighbours) against a blast (the neighbours and the foe behind).
2. **One column keyword: Penetrate**, the line pierce. It is the only way a Direct weapon
   reaches past a standing front foe without a kill, which matters more once Indirect is
   reach only and owns the back rank cleanly. Ryan calls it the pierce; **Pierce** is the
   shorter word. But the column is worth nothing until fights have a back rank: today only
   the formations do, so the column shape (Penetrate and the Boar's spill alike) waits on
   the foes' fresh start (formations with a front and a back spawned by the rift).
3. **Chain keeps its keyword.** It is the one area shape that scales with a blow's size
   rather than spreading it, and it carries a decision (aim at the foe you can kill, to
   keep going); the runner shows it doing a job nothing else does, the Sparrowhawk's swarm
   answer. It is Kestrel's, and it wants a second carrier. The Boar's spill is the other
   kill shape: a rule of its passive, written with the overflow word (section 8), and weak
   until there is a back rank to spill into.

So two area keywords by shape, one for the row and one for the column, each with one
number, and Chain beside them as the kill's.

## 8. The overflow word

Ryan, 11 October: unify Breach and Spillover under one term, and "to evaluate breach as a
word I think we need to look at our vocab and see if there's a more clear word that isn't
similar to something we already have. It's not terrible though."

What the word must cover: a needle volley, a sandworm's swallow, a bomb's burst, a slag fist,
a dragon's dive and a fire spell going through a shield (the foes' seven carriers today), and
the Boar's weapons going through a front-rank kill.

What the vocabulary already holds near it:

- **Penetrate** (the column keyword): also "goes through", always and at full. The one real
  neighbour: a player could read "breach" and "penetrate" as one idea. Their text keeps them
  apart (Breach is only what is left past a barrier; Penetrate is the whole blow, always),
  and renaming Penetrate to Pierce does not change that.
- **The Breaching Plow**, the Boar's own locked weapon. The same root on the same machine
  helps: the Boar breaches.
- **The Over- words**: Overcharged, Overheating, the overheat, Overshield. Overflow, Overrun,
  Overwhelm and Overkill would crowd them.
- **Broken**: Ryan's word for a destroyed component; Break-through would sit beside it.
- **Spillover** itself: literal, but soft for a foe's move ("spills over the shield" reads
  as an accident, not a threat).

Candidates weighed:

| Word | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Breach** | Fits every carrier on both sides (a breach in a shield, a breach in the line); short; already taught | Sits nearest Penetrate |
| Trample | The card-game word for exactly this: damage past a kill carries on | A beast's word: needles, a burst and a fire spell do not trample |
| Sunder | Old, strong, nothing near it | Gamers read it as armour reduction |
| Rupture | Nothing near it; a shield or a line torn open | Names the barrier breaking, not the blow carrying on |
| Through | Plain English; the foes' text already says "through the shield" | Weak on a chip |

**The lean:** keep Breach. It reads right for every carrier on both sides, it is taught
already, and its one neighbour, Penetrate, is kept apart by its text.
