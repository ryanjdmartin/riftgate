Riftgate
Mech Sheet

Player units are grounded military mecha in the Mechwarrior lineage with a Terran-armour attitude: heavy, angular, serviced-not-cobbled, bolted plates and exposed hydraulics, a small crew cockpit where a face would be. Each chassis exists at two scales, a 48 × 64 combat sprite that carries the fantasy, and a 16 × 16 chibi map sprite that only has to be told apart at hex size. Everything below is still a code-drawn style sketch, rendered at load from hand-typed character grids, not final art; what is being tested is silhouette, mass, livery, hardpoint legibility and whether the front reads, all of which survive a real pixel artist.

Skirmish scale

Combat sprites (round-two brief)

Round twoOlive is gone: every mech is now a cold steel ramp with one saturated livery on the armour plates and amber or white markings, and every combat sprite is redrawn three-quarter front so the player sees cockpit, chest plate and gun muzzle. The build is heavier throughout, with knee actuators, wide feet, bevelled plates, multi-pane canopies and short fat cannons in place of the round-one boxes.

48 wide by 64 tall (the Raptor is 64 by 48). The enemy side is above the mechs on screen, but the sprite faces the viewer, turned slightly toward the upper-left, so a cannon pointing at the enemy is drawn as a foreshortened muzzle face: wide bore, brake slots, thick housing behind it. One pixel of #1a1d23 outline on the silhouette, at most sixteen colours per unit, interior lines only where a plate changes value or a rivet line runs along an edge. Each is shown at 3× and, beside it, at 1× next to the 120 px die outline it will share a card with.

Bulwark

HALVARD HEAVY WORKS · TRACKED · 48 × 64 · DEEP RED

A tank with fists. Low wide tracked hull with a drive sprocket on each track face, a bevelled superstructure with a three-pane cockpit slit, and the slug cannon on the right shoulder drawn muzzle-first: hot bore, brake slots, recoil housing and a belted ammo feed running down to the hull. Two Shredder Claw arms hang forward on hydraulic upper arms, three prongs each. Red plates on the glacis, pauldrons and forearms; amber chevron and unit block; a searchlight on the left shoulder and a sensor mast behind it.

Goliath-shaped. Four jointed legs with wide feet: the front pair steps outward on thick upper legs with a visible ram, a bevelled knee actuator with an amber tell-tale, and a piston in the lower leg; the rear pair stands darker and inboard. Raptor

KESTREL DYNAMICS · AIRCRAFT · 64 × 48 · COBALT

A stubby, brutal interceptor seen three-quarter front from slightly above, hovering. Thick cobalt fuselage with a steel spine, short thick swept wings with a hardpoint under each and the amber marking on the near wingtip, a three-pane canopy with a lighter highlight pane, dark intake cheeks, and a steel nose cone with a sensor slot. Battlefield scale

Map sprites

16 × 16 chibi, one pixel ink outline, a shared steel ramp plus one livery on the plates so the maker reads even at hex size: Halvard red, Orrin & Pike bone, Kestrel cobalt. Big silhouette, no attempt at scale, facing east or north-east; these stay simple. The Bulwark is the heavy and gets a 24 × 28 grid that overflows its hex upward. Map sprites drop all customisation detail: a Sentinel with a different lance is still the same map sprite, and the skirmish is where the loadout shows.

Tiles

Terrain

Pointy-top hexes, 52 px wide at 1× logical (circumradius 30). Each tile is an 18 × 20 pixel grid on the same 3 px unit as the map sprites, clipped to the hex, so terrain pixels and unit pixels are the same size on screen. Shown here at 3×. Unchanged from round one; the mechs no longer share a hue with the green tiles.

Same unit

Same unit, two scales

The Bulwark as the map sees it and as the skirmish sees it. The map version keeps the silhouette cues (superstructure, claws, tracks) and the red, and drops everything else; the combat version is where the cannon gets a bore and the arms get hydraulics.

Decide

What to judge

  • Heavy hardware. Do the combat sprites read as heavy military hardware, weight in the hull and legs and a crew somewhere inside, or do they still read as characters?
  • Livery at 1×. Cover the 3× versions: does red, bone or cobalt tell you who built the unit at card scale, and on the map patch?
  • The front reads. Can you find the cockpit panes, the chest plate and the gun muzzle on each one without the caption? Does the muzzle-first cannon read as a cannon rather than a wheel?
  • Cheapness fixed? Did dropping olive for steel plus one saturated livery fix the cheap read at pixel scale, or is the problem somewhere else (outline weight, contrast, dither)?
  • Hardpoint legibility. Can you point at the claws, the mortar, the lance, the autoguns, the railgun, the howitzer and the shell rack? If a hardpoint needs the caption, it needs a redraw.
  • Terrain at hex size. At 1× on the patch, can you tell road from rough, ruins from rough, water from mountain, without the tile legend?
Every grid on this page is width-checked at load: each row of each character grid is compared to the grid's declared width and a console warning names any mismatch. Canvases render at device pixel ratio with image smoothing off. Hex maths: pointy-top, width = √3 × R, row step = 1.5 R, odd rows shifted right by half a hex.
