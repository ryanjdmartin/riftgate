Working title: Riftgate  ·  sprite sheet, enemies

Riftgate
Enemy Sheet

The enemies are old Final Fantasy monsters in spirit: large, ornate, over-the-top high fantasy, drawn with more detail, more colour and more flourish than anything the player fields, because the contrast is the point of the game, one genre invading another. Where the mechs are gritty gunmetal with one hazard stripe and sixteen colours, a foe gets up to twenty colours, bone filigree, ragged cloth, glowing sigils, horns, teeth and too many eyes, and the elites are a different order of thing at 64 × 96. Everything on this page is a code-drawn sketch from a hand-typed character grid, not final art; what is being tested is silhouette, palette separation and whether the ornament survives 48 px.

Faction 1

The Risen

Undead · cold and rotten

Cold greens over bone, with a rot purple in the shadows and one red eye. The horde is cheap and numerous; what hides behind it is the threat. Combat sprites face south-west, toward the mechs at the bottom of the skirmish screen.

Faction palette

Faction 2

The Furnace Host

Demonic · hot and furnace-lit

Dark red iron cracked open on orange and ember yellow, bound in brass. Everything is lit from inside. The front rank is a wall that hits back; the back rank is artillery with opinions.

Faction palette

Faction 3

The Chorus

Eldritch · violet and wrong

Three violets, a teal, and a pale eye glow on void black. Nothing here is shaded like a body; planes fold where they should not, and the pale glow is the only warm thing.

Faction palette

Battlefield

Map tokens

One 16 × 16 chibi per foe type, one pixel ink outline, four to six colours, facing south or south-west. On the map they are squad tokens, so they carry a count chip in the hex corner; the Bone Colossus is a three-hex boss and gets a 40 × 40 sprite that overflows the centre hex of its triangle.

Genre clash

Contrast strip

The whole art direction in one image: a mech-sized block, a brute, a necromancer. The player's side is a plain machine in one grey; the other side is a costume.

Review

What to judge

  • Faction at 1×. Cover the labels. Does each foe read as Risen, Host or Chorus from colour and silhouette alone at the 1× copy?
  • Ornament at 48 px. Ribs, filigree, chains, sigils and orbiting fragments are cheap at 3×; do they survive at 1×, or collapse into noise that a real artist would have to cut?
  • Elites as another order. Beside a rank-and-file foe, do the three elites read as a different kind of thing, not just a bigger one?
  • Palette separation. Put the three faction swatch rows together: cold green, furnace red, void violet. Any shared hue between factions is a bug.
  • The clash. In the contrast strip, does the mech look under-drawn, or does it look disciplined? It should look disciplined.
Every grid on this page was width-checked at load: each row of each sprite was compared to its declared width and any mismatch is reported in the console. Canvases render at device pixel ratio with image smoothing off; outlines are #14161c, never pure black.
