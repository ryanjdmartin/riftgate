# The rider and keyword pass, 14 November (a first round; nothing decided)

Ryan, 13 November, ruling on the reboot: "we'll need to audit every rider and keyword after
this, no matter what", and of the foes' Burn on a machine, "Ignore enemy Burn for now, we
might be forced to change it regardless". 14 November, after his play kept the reboot and his
logic for the stock basics was read (the refit brief's sections 22 and 23): "On that note,
let's do the rider and keyword pass now."

What it reads against:

- *The reboot* (the brief's section 22): nothing regenerates or cools by itself; a machine
  that has spent nothing may give its turn to a reboot, bare that turn, cold and whole the
  next; heat past the maximum stays and burns hull every turn; a shield at nothing is Broken
  and gains none until the reboot.
- *His yardstick for the basics* (section 23): the vanilla gun one under the standard foe, a
  3 against a plain 4; a rider paid for in damage first and heat second, "until it turns into
  Equipment"; the gun that deals one more paid for in heat, "a first build step".
- *His scope*: the basics and tier one. "Tier two foes are facing a more coherent build from
  the player, so these +/- 1 questions aren't relevant there."
- *His expectation, no strict rule*: what grants regen, cooling, flat shield or flat heat
  reduction is a kit's, a locked piece's or gated behind a synergy ("they should be something
  special"); a shield spend may not be standard fare ("it's so expensive").

How to read it. Every row is a reading and a lean, his to mark. A price is a first price for
the bench of section 9, never a number for content. Pieces whose machine has a session coming
are named and left to it. Section 11 lists what is his to decide.

## 1. What the pass finds

1. **No rider's rule breaks under the reboot. Prices move, in four places.** Heat is a real
   price now, since none of it leaves: the foes' Burn and Scorch, Cryo, the two modules that
   cool and every vent are worth more than they were built for. A blow prevented is worth
   more, since the shield it would have taken does not mend until the reboot: Sap, Suppress,
   Lure, a Barrier, a Ward and Armoured all gain. What grants shield lands on a live shield
   alone, which leaves two modules nearly dead. And a blank or disabled die has spent
   nothing, so it is the turn to reboot: the rule softens every Disable and Steal.
2. **One under the standard foe makes every stray point the point more.** A vanilla of 3
   leaves a plain 4 standing at 1, so whatever adds a point finishes it: a chip of 1 from
   Spread or Splash, a stack of Burning, a stack of Empowered, a Ricochet, a module's point
   on a marked foe. So the chips and Burn measure highest of all the riders, a point to two
   and more in hull kept, and the first build step has a dozen doors. That is the yardstick's
   best property, since a 3 and a chip make a kill and where to aim becomes the question. It
   is also its pricing trap: under it a chip of 1 is no small rider.
3. **A rider's worth is the foe it answers.** The measure splits the riders in two. The point
   more, a split gun, Burn and the area chips answer a horde of plain 4s. Flak, Shock, Breach
   and Expose read as nothing against a horde and as a point or more against a massive foe
   behind plate, where all four match a flat 4 in rounds. So one price a rider is a mean
   over the roster, the table by the kind of formation is the truer read, and it is the
   domain identity his bench asks for at no cost.
4. **A point of heat is worth half a point of damage at most**, a quarter once the gauge
   already outlasts the fight, and nothing in a duel. So heat prices the half steps his
   ladder needs between rungs, which is his point about the second lever. It does not price
   the point that crosses the kill line: the roster does (section 23).
5. **The Barrier is half of every fight against a spike, and heat does not price it.** With
   the Barrier basic at a vanilla shot's heat the bot spends it nearly as often. With none on
   the dice an elite costs 2.2 times the hull, and a boss 1.8 times with a wreck in nine
   fights of ten.
6. **Tier one's finds are not on the ladder.** Of fourteen slottable tier-one guns eleven
   deal 4 or more, nine of them in one blow, most at today's vanilla heat or a point over it.
   The point more is what a find is today, and it has no bite. The bench of section 9 starts
   there.

## 2. The measure

`docs/measurements.md`, 14 November, has the runs. The method: the six tier ones of the
anchor as trios, tier one's ten formations at their rating, the greedy bot, the reboot on,
the trial's basics (a vanilla of 3 for 3 heat). Every plain basic gun on every die is
replaced by one test gun at 3 heat, the Ankle Mortar keeping its Indirect, and the fights'
rounds and hull are read against the same fights with a flat gun of 1 to 5 in its place.
"Reads as a flat 3.5" means the fights ran as they would with a plain gun between 3 and 4.
300 fights a row: a direction.

What it cannot say, each a reason the bench and his play come after it:

- It is the greedy bot's play. A rider that needs a second, planned spend reads low: Shock,
  Flak, Expose, a Lure that covers a reboot.
- Every basic gun carries the rider at once, so a squad of nothing but Lures, Disrupts or
  Suppresses is read, which is no squad. Those three rows are no fair read.
- Formations alone. A duel, an elite and a boss price a rider differently: the big-foe
  riders are the first to gain there.
- A rounds column moves in tenths. A difference under 0.2 rounds or half a point of hull is
  the run's grain.

## 9. The tier-one bench

His proposal: "Worth noting that at tier 1, where builds are more limited, a 2x2 gun is
worse than straight 4. If we wanted a 4 damage option that performs the same against goblins
but worse against a different domain where the standard is like 3hp +1 armour. Just a point
about variety at tier 1, including both a hot 4 and a less hot 2x2 costs us nothing as far
as content, gives us more data to work with, and helps to create domain identities without a
ton of work. The same logic can be applied to all of the tier 1 components, rather than just
creating arbitrary shapes, we can make placeholder weapons and equipment for a variety of
combinations of riders, damage, heat, and hit counts, and then see what sticks and is
interesting and what is redundant enough to cut. Agreed?"

Agreed, and section 12 of the refit brief had asked for the same table ("the tier-one
slottable pieces brought to the basics' band ... as one table with their heat"). Section 2 is
its first cut: thirty-one rows. Four things keep it small and readable.

- *One trade a piece.* Each bench piece is the vanilla with one thing traded, named: a
  point more for heat, a point more split in two, a point less for a rider, the rider kept
  for a point of heat, Indirect for a point of heat. A piece with two trades waits for its
  two parents to have been read. That keeps the grid to dozens, and says what each result is
  a result of.
- *A kill signature.* `tools/basics_model.py`, its eighth table, gives each gun its spends to
  kill five marks: a plain 4, a 3 behind a point of plate (his second standard), a thin 3, a
  thick 6 and a 4 behind plate. Two guns with the same row and the same heat are one gun,
  and the later one is cut before it is built. A hot 4 and 2 twice differ on exactly one
  mark, the plated 3, which is his point.
- *Read by the kind of formation, by the bot's pick share and by his play.* Section 2's
  second table is the pattern: a rider that reads the same in every column is a number, one
  that reads apart is a domain's piece. The next cut puts a bench piece in one slot of a die
  beside the vanilla, so the bot's pick share reads it as the balance row already does.
- *Marks as well as guns.* His second standard, 3 behind a point of plate, is in no
  formation today: plate stands on six of tier one's sixty-nine formation slots, four of
  them a massive. So the column that tells a hot 4 from 2 twice is thin. A placeholder
  formation is as cheap as a placeholder gun: one standard foe eight times, for each of the
  five marks. The bench reads every gun against those before the roster, and the domains'
  standards can be picked from what they show.

## 10. The defence side: an approach

His question: "How are we going to approach enemy kits and damage in relation to the
various shield and hull values we have? Since our shield and hull values are, bluntly,
pretty completely random, so there's no reason to keep those locked either."

The same way as the guns, mirrored. On the offence side the foe was fixed first, a plain 4,
and the gun measured against it. On the defence side the machine is fixed first and the
foes' blows measured against it. One thing does not mirror, and it shapes the rest: a gun's
damage past a kill is wasted, so a gun is read in thresholds, shots to kill. A blow's damage
past a shield reaches the hull, so a shield is a pool, read in totals. The thresholds on the
defence side are the Barrier, which eats one blow whatever its size, and armour, which takes
a point off every blow.

1. *Three clocks, three units.* The round's: a Barrier is one blow, Armoured a point a blow.
   The cycle's, from one reboot to the next: the shield and the heat. The battle's: the hull,
   by his budget of 9 November (a formation's fight a twentieth of the squad's hull at its
   rating, a flanker's a thirtieth, a hunter's a tenth, an elite's a quarter, the boss what
   is left).
2. *A shield is counted in standard blows*, as a foe is counted in shots. The standard blow
   is the defence side's first number, as the standard foe's 4 was the offence's: 62% of
   tier one's formation slots strike for 3 today and a quarter for 4. At 3, tier one's
   shields of 6 to 12 are two to four blows.
3. *A fight is counted in cycles*: the foes' damage over the squad's shields. The reads of
   the trial give it today's values: a formation lands 26 damage on a trio whose shields sum
   to 18 to 29; an elite lands 53 and a boss 107 on a four whose shields sum to 26 to 35. So
   a formation is one cycle, an elite nearly two and a boss three to four, and the kinds of
   fight are already a ladder in that unit. Each cycle past the first is bought with a
   reboot, turned by a Barrier, or paid in hull.
4. *Two clocks, one reset.* A machine's heat runs out in its maximum over its guns' heat,
   and its shield in its size over the blows it draws. The reboot resets both. If one always
   runs out first the other gauge is decoration: a duel today does not read heat at all,
   since cooler guns leave it as long and as dear. A formation has them about level: its 4.7
   rounds are a gauge of four vanilla shots and a little over, and its damage is the squad's
   shields once over. So a standard machine's two clocks should run out within a round or
   two of each other at its fights' rating, and a role is which one it leans on.
5. *A hull has two jobs.* It is the battle's pool, and a quarter of it is the blow that
   destroys a component: a 5 on a hull of 20, a 7 on 28, a 10 on 40. So a hull's number says
   which foes can break a die, and the rank and file's 3 and 4 break none today.
6. *The standard machine.* A handful of shapes in those units, by role, in place of the
   numbers as they fell: a shield of two, three or four blows; a hull by the budget and by
   the blow that should break its die; plate for the machine that means to be hit.
7. *A foe's kit is which clock it presses, or which it goes round.* The swarm presses the
   shield of the whole squad, and armour is its answer. The spike, a blow the size of a
   shield, presses one machine's, and the Barrier is its answer. Scorch and Burn press the
   heat. A breach goes round the shield to the hull. A blow at every machine catches the
   reboot. The heat aim and the exposed aim find the machine a reboot is due on. A disable,
   a corrupted entry and a drained reroll attack the die. A domain is two or three of these,
   and its standard foe.
8. *The foes' ladder mirrors the guns'.* A plain blow is the vanilla; a rider is paid for
   in the blow's number first; the spike is the foes' point more, and its price is its
   telegraph and its count, one a pack.
9. *A model first, then dials.* `tools/shield_model.py` already runs the reboot against the
   foes' shapes; it takes the units above and prices each kit in hull past the swarm's.
   Then role shapes and kits as a table in chat, dials for the shields, the hull and the
   blows, the bot at the anchor, and his play.

What the measures already say for it: a formation's damage falls 57% on shields, 30% on hull
and 9% on a Barrier; an elite's and a boss's fall nearly half on a Barrier. So the swarm is
priced by shields and hull, and the spike today by the Barrier basic alone.

## 11. For Ryan

Each with Claude's lean. Nothing here is built or changed in content.
