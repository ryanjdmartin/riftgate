# Wainwright & Sutler: new chassis

Ryan's six candidate silhouettes for Wainwright & Sutler (two true spider tanks, a chicken
walker, a medium gunship, a large multi-rotor, a fan-boat hovercraft), worked through eleven
rounds of his notes from 11 to 13 October 2026 and left here for the batch evaluation: a fresh
session weighs every maker's proposals together before anything is built (Ryan, 12 and 13
October). Nothing here is in content. The machines go by silhouette, with working names in the
headings; designations, final names and lore are Ryan's. Numbers are placeholders at the
content's scale.

**How to read it.** A line marked "Ryan" with its date is his call and stands unless the
evaluation argues it down in the open; everything else is Claude's proposal, there to be
weighed. Each machine's section gives its fantasy, passive, how it plays, its engine work, its
numbers, locked kit and stock (today's pieces as stand-ins) and its model. The questions for
the evaluation are gathered after the table; the retired ideas at the end are there so they are
not proposed again.

## For the evaluation

- **What each waits on.** The gunship needs the stock pass's tags on components (Ballistic,
  Beam, Missile and Arc among the working set) and a hangar that shows what each fitted weapon
  adds. The fan-boat's shells read best once the area keywords and the stock pass land. The
  multi-rotor's shown text waits on what shown text calls the die, a session of its own (Ryan,
  13 October). The others need only their own engine work.
- **The engine work, in brief** (each section has it in full). The boiler bends two floors
  (negative cooling, stats floored at zero) and gives its vents a rider; the multi-rotor lets a
  machine spend another's die; the revolver's die steps instead of rolling; the funnel-web's
  Suppress becomes a delayed debuff; the gunship counts its fitted weapons' tags into its
  stats; the fan-boat rewrites its other weapons' shots.
- **The questions, by machine.**
  - The boiler: is cooling -10 the right pressure (the gain must stay under a vent's number);
    the blast at the full heat vented, or half; when, if ever, an ally's vent should blast.
  - The multi-rotor: is lending its whole die the right size for a tier three; does the gesture
    (its die dropped on an ally's) read on a phone.
  - The revolver: is the Rapid chain funny rather than strong, as Ryan expects (if not, Rapid
    once a round on it); should every fight open on the first chamber.
  - The funnel-web: the next foe to act, or both neighbours (Ryan's alternative, the lever if
    it is weak); the web bomb dealing nothing; the fangs' 20% of current hull.
  - The gunship: armour 3 a weapon (five tie the Armadillo, every build measured under it) or 4
    (strictly the most, the extreme die past the Armadillo, where a falling armour ladder would
    earn its caveat); is a middle at the Clydesdale's level right for a machine whose passive is
    its only power; what five Beams are worth over a battle, since shield returns between fights
    (the campaign runner's to read); whether two-tag pieces stay rare enough to chase once the
    stock pass tags the components.
  - The fan-boat: the deck gun's number (15) against Rapid shells, its strongest build; a
    shell's own heat not counting; whether the magazine makes a better tier two.
- **The tier spread.** Wainwright & Sutler fields the Prairie Dog and the Dingo at tier one and
  the Clydesdale at two; these six add a tier one (the fan-boat), four tier twos and a tier
  three (the multi-rotor).

## The tests, the tags and the stock

The tests these answer: a chassis's identity (passive, numbers, locked components) opens build
space; a map action that buys a fight buff is no condition; a chassis exists to bend a rule,
and whether the bend is healthy is the debate; Wainwright & Sutler is simple and
straightforward. A maker's profile is an identity, never an exclusion (Ryan, 13 October): a
piece fits wherever its fantasy fits, so the funnel-web paralyses because spiders do, and
statuses are Aphelion's strength without being its alone. A passive that is pure upside with
no interest fails (the third round's Ballistic splash); so does a payoff not worth its setup
(the third round's missile-marked reach). A damage rider on a support action the player takes
anyway costs no tempo, so it needs a limit (Ryan, 13 October). Rapid is a high-budget keyword:
its pieces stay small (Ryan, 13 October, probably). Out of bounds: a kill making a spend
Rapid, every weapon Indirect, a decoy every hunter picks, debuffs as the engine of the gunship
and the fan-boat (a gunship is about its weapons).

The gunship counts its weapons by tag, each tag raising numbers of its own, so it waits on the
stock pass that puts tags on components, and never dictates the roster of components (Ryan,
13 October). No chassis passive reads a tag today (the Nemesis's melee bonus was the model until
its rage replaced it), and the bible's bar applies: a chassis passive reads a tag only if it
opens build space. The fan-boat's deck gun keeps a shell's tags,
so a tag reader still sees a Flame shell as Flame.

The stock lists name today's pieces as stand-ins. Under the stock rule (Ryan, 12 October) a
tier one stocks basics beside its locked piece and every chassis stocks only below its own
tier, each component carrying its tier, so the stock pass rewrites them; in it the Coolant Dump
retires (decided 12 October, not built). Two pieces these stocks lean on are built as they
will stay (12 October): the Laminar Deflector, a shield of 20 with Rapid, and the Entropy
Condenser, a vent of 40 with Volatile (10 hull a spend).

## Eight-legged spider: the boiler

- **Passive.** "Its vents blast a random foe for the heat they take off it." With **cooling
  -10**: it gains 10 heat at the start of every round, the boiler building pressure on its own
  (Ryan, 12 October). The heat is its own (Ryan, 13 October): a vent it spends on an ally cools
  the ally and blasts nothing, since an ally needing a vent is a decision of its own, and
  venting a teammate's heat into a foe is thematically odd.
- **The random target** (Ryan, 13 October). A crazy steampunk machine venting everywhere. The
  player times the chance: vent when only decent targets stand, as an opener while every
  target is alike, or at a lone boss, where random is no chance at all. It is the limit the
  passive needs, since a damage rider on a support action the player takes anyway costs no
  tempo: the boiler vents every turn, so the blast fires every turn. The bible's tests agree:
  chances live on riders, and a chance the player shifts by their choices (here, when) is a
  decision.
- **An ally venting the boiler** (Ryan, 13 October): it cools the boiler and blasts nothing,
  so the pressure stays the boiler's own problem and no spare vent on the team turns into a
  free attack. Letting an ally's vent blast (and the Puffin's shield, which sheds 10 heat) is
  the first buff if the boiler proves weak.
- **What it opens.** Heat is ammunition and every vent is a gun. Routes to heat: the boiler
  itself, hot weapons of any maker, and the foes' heat debuffs (built 13 October): Burning,
  which adds its heat at the end of the player's turn and so waits for the next turn's vents,
  and Scorched, which runs every weapon it fires hotter. Against the Peak it is paid for
  shedding heat it must shed anyway. Payoff: any vent of any maker; the Entropy Condenser is
  its biggest shot, 40 for 10 hull.
- **Balance.** The gain must stay under a vent's number. At -10 a vent of 20 or 30 still wants
  a hot weapon behind it; at -20 or more a die of nothing but vents fires a free blow every
  round. The overheat lands at the end of the player's turn, so the boiler vents or burns
  every turn: the pressure is the play. Levers past the random target: the gain, or the blast
  dealing half the heat.
- **Engine.** The validator refuses negative cooling and the pipeline floors every stat at
  zero; both bend for it (the cooling step already adds heat when cooling is negative). A vent
  it spends on itself gains a rider: the heat it removed, as one blow on a random live foe,
  drawn from the skirmish's seeded stream. The forecast shows the blast's number and marks
  every foe it may land on.
- **Numbers and locked kit.** Heavy, walker, move 20; hull 150, shield 30, armour 10, heat 100,
  cooling -10, field recharge 15. Locked: the Steam Cannon, Direct, flat 30, heat 30. Four
  hardpoints (d5). Stock: two vents and two guns (stand-ins: the Coolant Jacket, a basic vent,
  the Suppression Battery, whose heat of 20 feeds the vents, and the Maxigun).
- **Model.** A riveted cab with eight lamps for eyes, eight legs arched high over a body slung
  low (the Wild Wild West homage), the abdomen a boiler with its stack, the Steam Cannon
  between the fangs, vent grilles along the abdomen glowing brighter as the heat climbs, and
  a blast of steam from a grille at whichever foe the vent finds.

## Large multi-rotor: the sutler's deck

- **Passive.** "Any machine in the fight may spend its die as its own." "As its own" carries
  the rest: that machine's passive and buffs apply to the spend, and it pays the heat and any
  bite. The codex says so in a line; the shown text stays one sentence. Working name for it:
  the Sutler's Manifest. The word "die" in shown text is a question for another session (Ryan,
  13 October: the dice look like no dice, the problem that kept "face" out of shown text), so
  the wording waits on it.
- **What it opens.** Its loadout is built for the team: flat guns for the Sparrowhawk's double
  damage, hull-biting kit for the Nemesis's rage, stack appliers for the Stingray, shields
  for the Puffin, status pieces for the Baboon. A sutler supplied an army in the field; the
  maker's own name, in the air. It supplies; it never launches, which leaves Aphelion's
  proposed Carrier its verb.
- **The gesture.** Its die dropped on an ally's die hands it over for the round, and that
  machine spends it as usual; dropped anywhere else it is spent by itself. The bot chooses
  the spender by its forecast.
- **Engine.** A spend by another machine: the die handed over for the round and spent as the
  spender's own (its passives and buffs on the blow, its heat and any bite paid), the hand-over
  undone at the round's end; the forecast and the bot read the spender's numbers; the hangar and
  the codex say whose die it is while it is lent.
- **Numbers.** Assault, flyer, move 20; hull 170, shield 50, armour 5, heat 80, cooling 5,
  field recharge 15. Nothing locked: its kit is the team's. Six hardpoints (d6). Stock:
  Howitzer, Enfilade Beam, Maxigun, Incendiary Shells, Laminar Deflector, Coolant Jacket.

### The look: the helicarrier deck

Ryan, 13 October, from three looks built in lowforge and rendered through the game's own 3D
view (`docs/looks/ws_multirotor.png`; the models are look tests in
`tools/lowforge/looks/ws_multirotor.py`, never content): the helicarrier is this chassis's
look. The V-12 is cool too, without the wagon cover, and is kept for another machine; the
tandem is set aside.

- **Homage.** The helicarrier played straight, in Wainwright & Sutler iron: a flat deck held up
  by four rotors in rings at its corners.
- **From above.** A long flat iron deck with a blunt bow, a centre line in hazard dashes and a
  hazard landing square aft; at each corner a rotor in an iron ring on an arm from the hull,
  blades drawn as blades. It reads as a multi-rotor at once, and it is the biggest footprint
  of the three.
- **The hull.** A keel slung under the deck, slab sides of riveted iron (`steel.panel`) with a
  warm brown band (`livery.livery2`), amber lamps at the bow (`amber.light`).
- **The island.** On the starboard edge: a riveted block with the bridge glazed round
  (`glass.glass`), a roof, a mast with an amber lamp and a stack.
- **The mounts.** Six round the deck's edge and at the bow, bolted on and squared off, all
  reading from above.
- **The crane.** At the stern, a mast and a jib swung aft, a hook on a line hanging below the
  hull: the tell for the passive.
- **Size and float.** Assault: it overflows its hex, drawn south over north. Nothing touches
  the ground; only the hook hangs low.
- **Joints and acts.** The four rotors spinning (`b.spin`), faster under way; the crane's yaw
  and luff, the hook swinging; the mounts' yaw within the turret rule. Resupply: the crane
  swings out, the hook drops and rises. A battery drill: the mounts traverse in turn. A gust:
  a corner dips, its rotor bites, the deck rights itself and settles.
- **Under way.** The helicopter style, unpinned at last: it holds its facing and tilts into
  its travel, heavy and slow to start, barely a bank.
- **Death.** Wainwright & Sutler's winding down: the rotors spooling to a stop.

## Chicken walker: the revolver

- **Passive.** "Whenever its die rolls, it moves to the next entry instead, in sequence."
  (Ryan's wording, 12 October.) A reroll is a roll, so it moves one on; a lock holds it.
- **What it opens.** The loadout's order is a fire plan: a stun lined up for the boss's
  telegraphed round, a shield before the big blow, a duplicate to repeat a beat (two Howitzers
  in six chambers fire every third round), a blank as a known rest. Rapid becomes a sequence:
  its reroll is a roll, so it fires the next chamber at once, and a Rapid piece set before the
  big gun is a known double. Routes: Rapid pieces of any maker (the Flakshot Repeater, the
  Pulse Vacillator, the Wake Diffuser, the Laminar Deflector).
- **The first chamber.** Each fight opens on its first entry, so the plan is the same every
  fight and the hangar shows it. (The cylinder could keep its place between fights instead; a
  hidden state for the player to carry, so not that.)
- **Showing the order.** On the die, a small chip at its edge shows the next entry, so the next
  turn is planned without holding anything; held, the die lays its entries out in order as a
  ring, the current one lit and an arrow to the next; the hangar numbers its hardpoints as
  chambers; the model's revolver drum clicks round a chamber at every roll; the codex prints
  the order. A foe's Scramble jams it: the next roll lands anywhere, and the sequence runs on
  from there.
- **The cost.** No luck either way: a destroyed component is a known dead chamber every turn.
- **Engine.** A die whose roll is a step: the next entry in the die's order (its fixed entry,
  then the hardpoints), a reroll a step, a lock a hold, Scramble a true roll; each fight opens
  on the first entry; the die's next-entry chip, and the ring on the hold.
- **The Rapid chain.** Five Rapid pieces and a gun fire all six chambers every round, against
  three and a half on a rolled die. Ryan, 13 October: a player who slots that and neither
  overheats nor gets blown up has earned the broken game; and Rapid is a high-budget keyword,
  so its pieces' effects are small and the chain should be funny and rather weak. If it is
  not, Rapid once a round on it: one double, never a chain.
- **Numbers and locked kit.** Medium, walker, move 40; hull 90, shield 40, armour 5, heat 60,
  cooling 5, field recharge 20. Locked: the Hogleg, Direct, two strikes of 10, heat 10, the
  hammer fanned. Five hardpoints: a six-chamber die. Stock: Twin Autoguns, Howitzer, Ankle
  Mortar, Incendiary Shells, Laminar Deflector.
- **Model.** The AT-ST (a squared riveted cab on two long digitigrade legs, no arms, guns under
  the chin) with the revolver drum on the cab's flank.

## Four-legged spider: the funnel-web

- **The name.** Ryan leans to the funnel-web spider (13 October); clear in the reserved names. The
  plan below is his call ("a good plan").
- **Passive** (Ryan, 13 October). "A foe it would Suppress takes a web bomb instead: at the end
  of that foe's turn it bursts, Suppressing the foe and the next foe beside it." The web bomb
  is Ryan's working name.
- **How it plays.** The webbed foe still acts this round: the spider's stun is no interrupt.
  Then the burst holds it for the next round and catches the foe beside it that acts next
  (foes act left to right), which loses its action now, before it strikes. So the trick is to
  web the foe standing just left of the threat: the burst stops the threat this round, and the
  webbed foe the next. One stun takes two actions, one now and one next round. With no foe to
  its right, the burst catches the one to its left, which loses its next action.
- **Which neighbour.** One adjacent needs a rule for which: random would be noise (the burst
  comes in the enemy's turn, so the player cannot time it), a pick by the player costs a
  gesture, and the next to act uses the order the player already watches every round. Both
  neighbours, Ryan's alternative, is the lever if it proves weak. The plainer timing, the
  bomb bursting at the end of the enemy's turn and both foes losing the next round's action,
  reads easier and loses the trick.
- **The web bomb deals nothing** (Claude's lean): the web holds and the fangs kill, one job
  each.
- **The fangs** (locked, Ryan): "Direct, flat 10, and 20% of a Suppressed target's current hull
  more." Current hull, never maximum, so it opens on a boss and never finishes one; once a
  bite. They read any Suppressed foe, so the team's stuns feed them as well as its own webs.
- **What it opens.** Its webs come from its hardpoints: a Suppress piece of any maker lays them
  (the stock's Suppression Battery reaching the back rank, the Umbra Inducer). Payoff: the
  fangs, and every blow on a foe that cannot answer. Every other stun on the team feeds the
  fangs: the Baboon's meter, the Dingo's harpoon, the Arsenal pitch's Misharu if it is built.
  A web bomb is a debuff, so it feeds the Baboon's Paradox as it lands, and the burst's two
  Suppresses feed it again.
- **Engine.** A Suppress this machine lands becomes a web-bomb debuff on the foe (a chip of its
  own) that bursts at the end of that foe's turn, Suppressing it and the next foe to act beside
  it; the fangs' bonus reads Suppressed and the foe's current hull, through `sim/effects.gd`.
- **Edge cases.** Numbed is checked at the burst, each foe for itself; a webbed foe killed
  before its turn takes its web with it; a webbed foe already Suppressed by the team skips its
  action and its web still bursts at its turn's end.
- **Numbers and locked kit.** Medium, walker, move 30; hull 110, shield 40, armour 5, heat 60,
  cooling 5, field recharge 20. Locked: the Fangs, Direct, flat 10, the bonus above, heat 5.
  Four hardpoints (d5). Stock: Suppression Battery, Slugthrower, Howitzer, Laminar Deflector.
- **Model.** A low glossy-dark casemate slung between four long legs whose knees rise above
  the deck, the fangs hanging under its prow and a web-sac mortar on its back; an act rears it
  up, fangs bared, as a funnel-web does.

## Medium gunship: the weapon to surpass all weapons

- **The fantasy** (Ryan, 13 October). The Hind-D and Metal Gear's running joke, "a weapon to
  surpass Metal Gear": the overbuilt gunship that becomes whatever its guns make it. It lives
  in the Clydesdale's utility space, and like the Clydesdale it locks nothing. The lore is
  Ryan's to write.
- **Passive** (Ryan's shape, 13 October). "Its Ballistic weapons raise its armour and cooling,
  its Beams its shield and recharge, its Missiles its move and hull; its Arc weapons raise the
  others' highest rolls." The first machine whose numbers the player builds. Five of one tag
  make it the best at that number (Ryan: what makes it interesting, not shades of generalist):
  Ballistic a brick, Beam a shield wall, Missile the fastest machine; Arc makes a striker of
  the rolled guns beside it. Its other tags (Flame, Plasma, Blade, Melee) raise nothing. A
  weapon wearing two of its four tags raises both, and those pieces are its chase.
- **Four tags** (Ryan, 13 October: the most Wainwright & Sutler ones). Metal Gear REX's own kit:
  Vulcan cannons, missiles, a laser, a railgun. Six of the twenty-eight pairs of tags raise two
  of its rows, so a two-tag piece is rare enough to chase; by their own fantasy a railgun or a
  taser harpoon wears Ballistic and Arc, a rocket-assisted shell Ballistic and Missile, a
  laser-guided missile Beam and Missile.
- **Its only power** (Ryan, 13 October): it locks nothing, so the passive carries what other
  machines' passives and locked pieces carry. Measured against the whole roster below, not one
  machine (the Baboon and the Stingray are support machines, weak alone by design).
- **The hangar it is** (Ryan, 13 October), over growing in the fight. His test for no clear
  winner: nothing a tag grants feeds that tag.
- **Numbers, never mechanics** (Ryan, 13 October): stats, or what reads like them on the die;
  no rerolls, no new rules on any row.
- **The fives and tens are magnitudes** (Ryan, 13 October): the rule meant the scale, not the
  step, so the rows can be sized finely. Move alone keeps steps of 5, a hex costing 5 or 10.
- **Offence as a raised roll** (Ryan, 13 October): a higher top or bottom roll, never a
  straight damage buff, from a tag whose weapons seldom roll. In the stock every rolled weapon
  is kinetic and every energy weapon flat, so Arc, flat by its pieces, raises the highest roll
  of the others (the Muzzle Velocity Booster's effect, and a railgun's muzzle velocity). The
  row says "the others", so an Arc weapon that rolls takes nothing from it.
- **Cooling, a soft offensive stat** (Ryan, 13 October). It sits on the cold tag, Ballistic,
  since Missile, Beam and Arc pieces run hot (missiles make heat), so it feeds the hot guns
  beside the Ballistic ones and never its own. Measured: worth nothing on a die that never
  overheats (the Beam, the mixed and the Ballistic kits at 60 heat and 5 cooling), up to about
  100 hull on a die of the hottest pieces (the Overclocked Railgun, the Photosphere Lance, the
  Meteor Artillery), and little more once it pays a round's heat. Not a problem: a rider that
  pays only for hot pieces.
- **One machine never dictates the roster** (Ryan, 13 October). Missiles make heat, an armour
  cannon stays possible, and so on; the gunship takes the components as they come.
- **Measured** (13 October). `tools/balance.gd`, every machine alone against the twenty packs,
  greedy, 100 seeds a cell, the mean win %. The stand-in is the Goshawk's frame stripped of its
  passive and locked piece and set to this machine's numbers and a five-weapon kit by `--set`,
  each bonus a `--bump` or the Booster module; a lone machine is a diagnostic, and teams and
  whole runs decide at the evaluation. At 100 seeds a row mean over twenty packs carries a band
  of about 3 points (the bible's balance row), so these are directions: a gap under about 5
  points is noise, and the bot values these stats through its forecast. The roster, whole: the Armadillo 92, the Wild Boar 83,
  the Nemesis 72, the Badger 70, the Sparrowhawk 68, the Clydesdale 48, the Goshawk 36, the
  Ammit 34, the Prairie Dog 30, the Stingray 26, the Baboon 25, the Dingo 11, the Puffin 11.
  The stand-in without its passive: 23 to 28, whatever its kit. Prices on it, in hull at its
  kit's own: armour 5 about 78, 10 about 106, 15 about 222, 20 about 349 (on five Ballistic
  guns, steeper as more blows bounce whole); shield about 1 a point; a top roll 5 higher 20 to
  29 on a rolled kit, 10 about 60, 15 about 100; 5 more on every blow 58 to 68.
- **The tables tried** (a weapon's amounts: Ballistic armour and cooling, Beam shield, Missile
  hull, Arc the others' top roll; move and recharge are map numbers a fight cannot read):

  | Build | None | Low 2, 2, 8, 8, 2 | Mid 3, 2, 12, 12, 3 | Bold 4, 3, 16, 16, 3 | Mid, riders up 3, 4, 12, 12, 5 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Five Ballistic | 28 | 46 | 66 | 84 | 66 |
  | Five Beam | 23 | 27 | 29 | 32 | 29 |
  | Five Missile | 27 | 34 | 39 | 44 | 39 |
  | Two Ballistic, a Missile, a Beam, an Arc | 27 | 42 | 45 | 54 | 50 |
  | Two Ballistic beside three hot energy guns | 28 | 41 | 43 | 47 | 48 |
  | The first mix, its two Ballistic guns Missile too | 27 | 43 | 53 | 65 | 61 |

  | Five Ballistic, Missile and Beam pieces | 28 | 61 | 86 | 95 | 86 |

  The riders-up column was run on the three mixes; the rest equal mid by construction (cooling
  buys nothing on a die that does not overheat, measured, and an Arc's roll reaches no Arc
  weapon). A falling ladder on armour (5, 4, 3, 2, 1 a Ballistic weapon, five still 15) was run
  on the mixes too: 57, 52 and 66.
- **Reading.**
  - The middle, single-tag weapons in a mix, wins 48 to 50 with the riders up: the Clydesdale's
    48, above the medium machines (the Goshawk 36, the Ammit 34), as a machine whose passive is
    its only power should.
  - Five of one tag is the best at its number: Ballistic in the heavy band (66 at armour 15),
    Missile 39 with the fastest move, Beam 29 alone with the biggest shield. The shield wall's
    worth is the battle (shield returns between fights and hull never heals), which one fight
    cannot see and the campaign runner can.
  - The warp is armour, not the doubles. Every steep build is five Ballistic tags, a second tag
    adding its own row on top (hull 60 about 17 points, shield 60 about 8); five Missile and
    Beam pieces, with no armour, win 52.
  - The likely late die, two two-tag pieces in a mix, wins 61, about 11 over its plain mix.
    Five pieces sharing two tags win 52 to 83, five sharing three 86, all under the Armadillo's
    92. Bold's armour 20 puts five Ballistic at 84 and the three-tag die at 95, past it.
  - So diminishing returns are not needed at these amounts. If the middle should sit closer to
    the top, the riders only a mix can use lift it with no caveat (+5 here), and the falling
    ladder on armour lifts the mixes 9 to 12 points with five Ballistic unchanged, at the cost of
    a caveat; it is kept in reserve.
- **The rows** (recommended: mid with the riders up; placeholders):
  - Ballistic: +3 armour and +4 cooling (five: armour 15, the Armadillo's; cooling 25).
  - Beam: +12 shield and +3 field recharge (five: shield 80, the most; recharge 30).
  - Missile: +5 move and +12 hull (five: move 55, the fastest; hull 160, the Badger's).
  - Arc: the others' highest roll 5 higher (the Booster's own number).
  - Flame, Plasma, Blade and Melee: nothing.
- **Engine.** Tags on components (the stock pass); a passive that counts the fitted weapons' tags
  and grants static stat modifiers through `sim/effects.gd` (armour, cooling, shield, field
  recharge, move, hull), and Arc's higher top roll as an on-attack ceiling scoped to the weapons

## Reproducing the gunship's measurements

The runner as it stands, no code added. The stand-in is the Goshawk's row (a medium flyer with
five hardpoints) stripped and reset; one cell set, 100 seeds, greedy, about a minute a run on
one core. Point `--out` and `--json` away from `docs/balance.md`.

```sh
STAND="--rows goshawk --nopassive --set chassis/goshawk/fixed=[] --set chassis/goshawk/hull=100
  --set chassis/goshawk/shield=20 --set chassis/goshawk/armour=0 --set chassis/goshawk/heat_max=60
  --set chassis/goshawk/cooling=5"
perl -e 'alarm 900; exec @ARGV' godot --headless --path . -s tools/balance.gd -- --seeds 100 \
  --policies greedy $STAND --set 'chassis/goshawk/loadout=["maxigun","twin_autoguns","slugthrower","rivet_gun","flakshot_repeater"]' \
  --bump armour:15 --bump cooling:20 --out /tmp/gun.md --json /tmp/gun.json
```

- The kits: five Ballistic (Maxigun, Twin Autoguns, Slugthrower, Rivet Gun, Flakshot Repeater);
  five Beam (Laser Carbine, Enfilade Beam, Zenith Emitter, Umbra Inducer, Photosphere Lance);
  five Missile (Howitzer, Ankle Mortar, Shotfire Rack, Suppression Battery, Incendiary Shells);
  the hottest (Overclocked Railgun, Photosphere Lance, Meteor Artillery, Arc Fault Cannon,
  Enfilade Beam); the first mix (Maxigun, Howitzer, Enfilade Beam, Arc Fault Cannon,
  Slugthrower); two Ballistic beside three hot energy guns (Maxigun, Slugthrower, Photosphere
  Lance, Enfilade Beam, Arc Fault Cannon). The tags stand in by each piece's fantasy until the
  stock pass assigns them.
- A full die's bonus is the row times the count of its tag, given as `--bump stat:N` (armour,
  cooling, shield, hull); a two-tag piece adds both rows. Arc's row is the Booster fitted with
  its value set: `--module muzzle_velocity_booster --set
  modules/muzzle_velocity_booster/text/effects/0/value=N` (it reaches every rolled weapon, and no
  Arc weapon rolls in these kits). Move and field recharge are map numbers a fight cannot read.
- Prices convert a win-rate change to hull by the kit's own runs at +20 and +40 hull. The
  roster's numbers are each machine's row as it stands; a machine's passive and locked pieces
  together are its row against `--nopassive --set chassis/<id>/fixed=[]`.
