# The graded tide

Ryan's design of 23 November for the battle's enemy side, built on a dial on the session's
leans since 24 November ("I think we build your leans and I'll see"). None of it is the game's
until he says. Section 3 is what is built and on the trials' page; section 4 the design as the
session reads it; section 6 where the session pushed back and what was built of each; section
7 the Meadow's packs, in content for the trial. The formation pass (the to-do's design
sessions) runs from here.

## 1. What Ryan said (23 and 24 November)

After his play of the field's dials:

- "Right now, there is NO REASON to send a machine to the flanks to fight the 1s and 2s ...
  Any time I tried splitting my forces, I lost the battle. Any time I stayed in the middle, I
  won. The game as it was, was fundamentally flawed and not meeting our goals."
- "I don't actually think we were that far off, we just needed a paradigm shift: All fodder
  safer to fight, reducing the 'fight short for efficiency, fight full for safety' decision
  in favour of 'where do I put my forces.' More fodder overall, with choices of pack sizes and
  pack types - bring back threat 2 packs as fodder and not just hunters ... Maybe add a flat
  fracture bonus to a pack on top of the per-foe weight, so that splitting into 4 fights
  across the map and grouping for bigger packs are both viable ways of farming fracture.
  Reduce foe damage to the wall so that quantity matters. Spread fodder across the map,
  either through more Tears, or through multiple spawns at the rift headed in different
  directions (or both). Tears need to spread wide, the crawl forward isn't as important as
  distributing enemy forces."
- "I like our reboot mechanic, but it is a death sentence for a flanker fighting alone, which
  is why I only ever grouped. A well-tuned and well-played flanker should be able to finish a
  fight on the sides without rebooting, and we leave reboots to the longer, more intense,
  hunter, elite, or boss fights."
- Of a first trial that dealt every formation as packs for one machine and for two: "you're
  missing the point. A pack of slow treants coming down the center, scaled for 3 machines, is
  still a good pack and offers a decision point. I don't want the packs split, I want them
  SCALED. I didn't say to get rid of threat-3, I said to add threat-2 back in. Massive foes
  don't go away, they go where they make sense. Maybe a single treant is too strong to flank
  at C2, but not at C3."
- The shape: "Packs vary in strength, threat 1-3, 4 for elites. Hunters probably bumped to 3
  but still want to put a pin in those for now. Spawns reduce in strength the further to the
  sides of the map they target. So the far edges are 1s with a sprinkling of 2s, then 2s in
  between, and 3s coming right down the center. Move speed for pushers probably should reflect
  that, with 1s fastest and 3s slowest (a hypothetical hunter threat 3 would be fast,
  obviously)."
- No authored waves: "Variability is good, chaos is good, and allowed when each particular
  fodder pack is less threatening to both the player and the wall."
- The Convergence: "A set form of each formation, for each C. Bosses too ... It's fundamental
  for balance"; and never "just straight quantity. Going from, say, 3 goblins to 2 goblins and
  a mandragora should also be a bump, ideally."
- The elites: "I don't want to be able to lead around Elites. I want it to be a forced fight,
  without feeling like a forced fight"; their purpose, "get everyone back to the center to
  take this on together."
- The packs as content: "I would mark a table."
- Of the session's leans (24 November): "I think the only way we're going to know some of
  those answers is to try it. I think we build your leans and I'll see."
- The massive foes: "Our use of massive foes doesn't really align with my original intent. I
  always just wanted it to be another kind of unit, offering variety, not necessarily more
  threat. Multiple massive foes per domain. Putting massive in the bucket of 'slow and strong'
  in order to raise the difficulty of a pack is really just a soft Elite at that point. Why
  can't 'Treant Grove' be like, two or three treants? Or two treants and mandragoras? Or a
  huge Cyclops to accompany some Goblin parties, maybe they ride it to go faster? Or a pack of
  huge, fast, werebears? My point is, big and swarm offer different challenges, which adds
  variety."
- A squad's growth: "Our tiers of components are still very rough and in progress ... it means
  statements like 'the growth doesn't exist in a tier' are a tautology. I will greatly enjoy
  filling out the component tiers, once our targets are set."
- The mouths: "I wouldn't clutter the map with a ton of tears. I think enabling multiple
  spawns at the rift and at tears is plenty - due to our hex layout, it's the same absolute
  distance to any point on the wall from any point on the rift edge, not including terrain. The
  purpose of tears hasn't changed - they declutter attack lanes since packs aren't all trying
  to land in the same spot, and new ones spawn further up, to increase the possible volume,
  yes, but mostly to increase proximity. If we enable multiple simultaneous spawns from all
  spawn points, it gives us a very useful tuning dial for convergences. More so then the beat,
  I think."
- The trials' page: "clean up the trials tab. Remove anything no longer relevant. There's too
  many variables in there, we need to tone it down to just what is actually needed to try in
  person versus just chatting it out."

## 3. On trial: the graded tide and five dials

A debug build's SETTINGS, TRIALS, each read as the next battle starts. SET lays the first four
at once (GRADED) or the page back (AS BUILT).

| The dial | The set | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| TIDE | GRADED | The graded tide, below, in the tide's place |
| SENDS | 2 | The packs every mouth sends at a peak, the rift and each open tear alike; the Rupture's size counts them. His dial for the Convergence |
| STRIKE | 5 | What a pip of threat rating strikes the wall for a turn, ten the game's |
| PACK BONUS | +½ | What a pack pays the Rupture meter as it falls, over its rating; the meter is sized to match, the tally untouched |
| FIELD DEPTH | as he has it | The field's rows, 12, 14 or 16 |

PHASED, the domain statuses proposal's dial, shares the page. The runners keep three what-ifs
with no dial: the opening's distance, the Rupture's beats and the leaders' step.

**The graded tide as built**, each part the session's lean and none of it the game's:

- The wall is five bands. A band's rating is the pack that makes for it: one at each end, two
  between, three in the middle.
- At a peak every mouth sends SENDS packs, each to a band of its own drawn by the battle's
  seed: the rift from all five, a tear from its own band and the ones beside it. The pack is
  the next of its band's rating the domain has. One in five of an end's packs is for two.
- A pack walks at its composition's pace where it names one, else its slowest member's. The
  Meadow's packs for one walk 3; its twos and threes walk as their foes do, 2, and 1 with a
  treant among them.
- A pack bound for a far stretch crosses the field high and comes down onto its own stretch,
  so none runs along the wall or stops short on it.
- The opening is the game's own count in the graded packs, a three before the middle and a
  one at each end, each a move farther out than the game stands them.
- The tears open as built, one a peak, a row nearer the wall each time and never within four
  rows of it. None stands open at the start.
- A hunting pack comes out of the rift every other peak from the second. The hunters, the
  elites and the boss are otherwise as built.
- A domain with no packs written for it plays the graded tide on its patrols and formations
  as built, by their ratings. Only the Meadow has them written.

Cut from the game since 23 November, each with the test it failed: the tide sending before
the packs walk (his own, "a bandaid solution"); every formation dealt as small packs drawn by
rule (it took the threes and the massive foes off the field and left spreading the one
answer); tears open from the first turn ("I wouldn't clutter the map with a ton of tears");
the fodder's lower worth on the meter (at half the middle still won); the tide's density and
the rift's own count (the sends do the work of both); a flat hex on every pack's move (the
pace is a pack's own).

## 4. The design, as the session reads it

1. A pack is rated one to three, an elite four. The rating is the machines it is for, and it
   is honest: that many machines end it comfortably, and fewer pay for it.
2. Packs are scaled, never split: each is sized to its rating at the tier's first battle and
   has a set form for each Convergence after. What steps is what the pack holds as much as
   how many.
3. Strength falls toward the wall's ends: threes down the middle, twos between, ones at the
   ends with a two now and then. The massive foes stand where they make sense, the middle
   first.
4. Pace runs the other way: ones fastest, threes slowest. A hunter is its own case.
5. More packs than today, each striking the wall for less; a pack pays a flat bonus on the
   meter as it falls.
6. The hunters are pinned, probably rated three; the elite gathers the squad.
7. A massive foe is another kind of unit, big where the others swarm, and no measure of
   threat: a domain has several, at any pace, alone, together or carrying others.
8. Few tears. Volume is the packs every mouth sends at once, and that count is the
   Convergence's dial; the tears' work is to part the lanes and bring the mouths nearer.
9. A squad's growth inside a tier is a target to set, and the components are filled to it.

## 6. Where the session agrees, and where it pushes back

**Agrees.**

- The threes stay, and they are what makes the squad's shape a choice. Four machines stand
  as three and one, two and two, or two, one and one: a three and a two cannot both be met at
  their rating. In the model a squad that camps loses the wall and so does one that never
  gathers; with the middle split, holding a lane each cost little, so the trial of the
  morning had only swapped one answer for another.
- Scaled, never split. The size that makes a pack safe is the same at every rating, eight hit
  points a machine, and today's ones, twos and threes are each half again over it. The horde
  two heads lighter is a clean fight for three and a survivable one for two; as built it is a
  reboot each and a coin's toss short.
- The massive foes in the middle. They move 1 in content already, so the slow lane is theirs
  with no new rule, and a treant alone is a good pack for two.
- Strength falling toward the ends and pace rising. It gives a lane a kind of machine: the
  fast light ones at the ends, the heavy ones in the middle.
- The wall struck for less as the count rises: at this count even the model's free squad
  loses 150 to 270 at ten a pip.

**Pushed back, and what was built** (his answer to all five: "we build your leans and I'll
see"):

1. **The packs for two at the wall's ends.** In every row of the model a free squad's
   greatest loss is to the twos, and the ends' twos are the worst of it (176 with none, 370
   with half). Built: one in five of an end's packs is for two, and the Meadow's twos hold 12
   to 16 hit points and walk 2.
2. **Slow the threes before speeding the ones.** Paces 3, 2 and 1 lose 151 in the model, 4, 3
   and 2 lose 274. Built: ones 3, twos and threes 2, a pack with a treant in it 1; the
   opening a move farther out.
3. **Pace is the pack's, not the rating's.** Built: a composition may name its pace
   (`docs/rules.md`, Content conventions), and his fast massive packs are that.
4. **Growth inside a tier.** The session read that a kit of today's finds never flanks a lone
   treant and called it a limit; he called that a tautology, the tiers of components being
   unwritten, and he is right: the read measures placeholders. What stands is the order of
   work he gave, the targets first. A rating's size at each Convergence is a target for him to
   set, and the components are filled to meet it.
5. **Whether a three should fall without a reboot.** Built: the horde at six, seven and eight
   by the Convergence, so his play at each says where it sits.

**Built from his words.** The rift sends to every band, since its edge is as near any stretch
of wall as any other but the far ends, and a pack for one walks faster to reach them; a tear
sends to the bands round it. A pack whose rating falls as the squad grows is two entries in
content, each with its gate (the lone whisker cat, for two at the tier's first two battles and
for one after); the meter and the wall strike follow the rating it is dealt at.
