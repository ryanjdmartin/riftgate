# The batch evaluation: every maker's proposed chassis, read against the roster

The depth-and-breadth session and the batch evaluation, held as one session on 14 October
2026 (Ryan, 13 October: a fresh session evaluates every proposed design together before any
is built, and measures the roster against itself for mechanic depth and breadth). Its brief
is `docs/mechanic_budgets.md`; its inputs are the bible's first row, section 5's stun row,
`docs/arsenal_proposal.md`, `docs/ws_chassis_proposal.md` and `docs/aphelion_chassis_proposal.md`
(read as a list of ideas in a vacuum, Ryan's word). Minerva's and Kestrel's proposals are
struck (Ryan, 13 and 14 October): each starts fresh from the latest rules, in a session of
its own, with nothing of the struck designs kept in the records.
Every run it made is in `docs/measurements.md` under 14 October; the rows it settled are in
the bible (v0.155) and the questions it could not are in section 12.

**How to read it.** Everything here is Claude's recommendation for Ryan to move. A line marked
"Ryan" with its date is his call and stands unless argued down in the open, which this page
does where it has a reason. A verdict is one of four: **go** (build it, in the order below),
**go later** (build it once the thing it waits on lands), **wait** (not in this batch, for the
reason given, with the condition that brings it back) and **bench** (an idea set aside, kept
so it is not pitched again by accident). Every number is a direction at 100 seeds unless it
says 200; the bands are in `docs/measurements.md`. The greedy bot values only what it knows
(bible section 11): the Arsenal's four, five of Wainwright & Sutler's six and the Aphelion
jellyfish's rider spread are not in the engine, so the runner was not asked about them, and
a reading of a stand-in says so.

**The reading.**

- **Hard control's count is fine; its price is the problem.** Three pieces and a passive in 54
  sit under the brief's one source per eight. What Ryan saw on 13 October (prevalent, needs
  dialling back) is pick share: a stun on a small die is the die (58% and 64% on a d7 and a
  d4), while the same keyword on the Clydesdale's d9 at 20 heat is a quarter of its spends. So
  the budget is two rules, not one: the count stays where it is, and a stun is priced like the
  Suppression Battery (heat, a rolled number, a reach), which is the stock pass's heat
  re-pricing (bible section 5) and not a new rule. Four of the batch's ideas are hard control
  by another door, two of them amplifiers of the class (the funnel-web's two for one, the
  jellyfish's three for one): the evaluation lets one new stun source through and names the
  lever on the amplifiers (section 5).
- **Soft control and positioning are the thinnest classes**, two pieces the bot barely spends.
  Minos is the one proposal that changes the enemy's problem rather than removing its turn
  (the brief's third rule), and the Arsenal bench holds a second (Haunted). Both are foe-rank
  verbs, so both wait on the foes' fresh start putting back ranks in more fights.
- **Sustain is the fullest class** at sixteen of 54, and it is where the roster's "plain
  entries differing only by number" live: the stock pass's basic kit answers that, not this
  batch. The batch adds sustain only as locked tools (a pavise that baits, a ward that counts
  fires, a pall that moves a wound), which pass "a locked shield that does something".
- **"Changes what pieces do" is empty today** and the batch fills it six times over. It is the
  class that opens build space by definition (the brief's second rule), so it is where the go
  list comes from; the limit is how many rules the player can hold, one machine at a time.
- **The heat spectrum has one hot machine** (the Sparrowhawk) and no build that wants to run
  hot. The boiler is the one proposal that is hot by identity, which the heat row asks for
  on purpose.

**The four rules, as applied here** (the brief's, with what this session changed): hard
control stays at its count and is priced per piece (above); a new chassis earns its place by
reading a state nobody reads, producing one nobody produces, or changing what a kind of piece
does for everyone, and another source of an existing state needs a reason beyond its maker's
flavour; prefer verbs that change the enemy's problem over verbs that remove its turn; every
passive needs two routes in and two payoffs from other makers' pieces. Every go below is
checked against the four; the one that fails a rule says which.

## 4. The heat spectrum, one chassis three ways

The brief asks for one chassis fielded three ways across the heat row's spectrum. The
Clydesdale (d9, heat 60, cooling 5, no passive that touches heat) carried three kits of
today's pieces by `--set`, greedy, 50 seeds a cell (`docs/measurements.md`, 14 October): a
cold kit tuned to its cooling (the rolled kinetics and a shield, no vent), a hot kit with
vents (the Railgun, the Lance, the Meteor Artillery, the Suppression Battery, the Enfilade
Beam, the Zenith Emitter, two vents and the Condenser) and a mixed kit (half and half, one
vent). The heat report the to-do asks for does not exist yet, so the signals are the
runner's tallies: overheat hull, the vents' pick share, damage and heat a fight.

| Kit | Packs, bosses, formations won (greedy) | Hull lost | Damage a fight | Heat a fight (a round) | Overheat hull a fight | Vents' share of spends |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | 72, 0, 0 | 49%, 100%, 100% | 80 | 45 (6.8) | 4.9 | none carried |
| Mixed | 72, 20, 0 | 47%, 96%, 100% | 81 | 60 (9.4) | 5.3 | 1% |
| Hot, with vents | 79, 60, 0 | 44%, 82%, 100% | 99 | 114 (19.6) | 19.3 | 9% |

**The reading.** Demand passes budget as the heat row's test wants: the hot kit crosses the
maximum by its third round of firing (about 20 heat a round against 60 and cooling 5) and
burns 19 hull a fight for it. But at today's prices that is a good trade and the bot takes
it: the hot kit deals a quarter more damage a fight, opens the bosses (60 against 0 and 20)
and loses no more hull than the cold kit even with the burn counted, and it vents under a
tenth of the time rather than spend a turn on cooling. The cold kit pays in reliability and
collects nothing back, which is the half of the row ("a cold build pays elsewhere and
collects in the long ones") the content does not yet honour: with no round limit and the
fights two to seven rounds long, there is no long fight for endurance to collect in. So heat
bites, and it bites too cheaply: the levers are the overheat's rate (hull one for one is the
price the bot shrugs), the vents' numbers against a weapon's heat, and the fights' length,
which is the fresh start's. The heat report's forced-choice and slack signals are what the
stock pass tunes against; this read says the direction, and the boiler (hot by identity,
venting every turn by design) is the machine that will feel whichever price lands.

## 6. The questions folded in

**The Honey Badger's identity** (bible section 7, open; discuss first, Ryan). Measured, it is
the strongest solo tier one after the Armadillo (section 2): it takes any small pack outside
the Peak for under a tenth of its hull and loses every boss, a detachment that cannot finish
a battle alone. Of the three forms in its row, the in-fight stand (once a battle, a blow that
would wreck it leaves it at 1 hull) is the binary the Arsenal bench retired as the guardian's
stay, and the threshold already spares a heavy machine's components. The campaign form
(never sits out after a wreck, never loses a component: today's "never destroyed" grown up)
is the one that reads where the four-machines row needs it: a detachment is a decision the
map prices with time, and the Badger becomes the one machine whose wreck costs the run
nothing but the fight, the roster's expendable detachment. Claude's lean: the campaign form,
with the hopper staying its kit. The salvage form is an economy lever and waits on the
economy pass either way.

**The Armadillo's Faraday Hull** (bible section 7, open). Two things answer a foe's blow on
bare hull today, the Faraday Hull and the Retribution Thurible, both with Shocked; the
Nemesis's rage reads the same blow for another payoff. With the Onryō waiting, nothing in
this batch crowds it, so nothing must go now. Its measured worth without the discharge is
modest (64 and 85 against 85 and 92 with it, a 50-seed row mean; `docs/measurements.md`, 11
October), and Minerva's team wins everything either way. The exit stays as Ryan set it: when
an Arsenal retaliation lands (Grudge, or whatever returns in its place), the Faraday Hull is
the first to go, and the Armadillo keeps the Lockdown Shutters' shock on a blocked blow as
its taunt. Until then it stands.

**The cars' missing solo niche** (bible section 7, the cars row). Section 2 reads it: the
Prairie Dog takes the back-rank pairs (the Mandragoras, the Cockatrices) and the Ghosts for
under a fifth of its hull and nothing else; the Dingo takes the two back-rank pairs and
nothing else; neither is a detachment by the brief's bar, and the machines that are (the
Badger, the Armadillo, the Boar, the Sparrowhawk, the Nemesis) are the slow ones bar the
Sparrowhawk. The cars' map rules stay their identity (a scout never hunted, a pack animal
that fights twice), and their worth is the second fight a turn and the fight they are never
caught in, which a team row reads and a solo row cannot. The stock pass gives them basics;
the detachment-class fast machine the four-machines row asks for is a new machine's job,
and the batch has one candidate: the fan-boat (tier one, light, move 50, a deck gun that
fires the same 15 whatever shows, so its damage is a number the player can plan a detachment
on). The Dingo's harpoon at 64% of its spends is the
stun pick-share case in miniature; its price is the stock pass's.

**Cooldowns in a skirmish** (bible section 8, open; the one question the brief asks the
session to weigh). The revolver makes the case for them legible: a known dead chamber is a
cooldown the player can see coming, and nobody will call it hidden. The cost is where it
lands: a blank entry on a d4 is a quarter of the die for a round, on the Clydesdale's d9 a
ninth, so a cooldown prices a big hit by die size, dearest on the small reliable dice, which
is a decision (a small die is consistent and pays for its big hits in reliability; a big die
carries more and shrugs a cooldown) rather than a tax. Claude's lean, unchanged from the
row: a printed rule on a few rare pieces, never on a basic and never as a general axis, the
entry shown blank for a round or two after it fires, with the chip carrying the count. Heat
stays the one cost on the whole die; a cooldown is a second cost on the chase pieces alone.
