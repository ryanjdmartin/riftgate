# The enemy side in the Final Fantasy register

> **Superseded the same day.** Ryan, 9 October, after reading it: fresh slate. The direction
> is that the whole JRPG genre invades our world, Final Fantasy its spine but the genre blended
> so ours reads as its own; the leaders look like Final Fantasy bosses, elaborate and over the
> top; the Cactuar is in, made sinister; three factions are good for scouting but not fixed,
> since other frames may give the same read. Sections 1, 2 and 6 (the art's constraints, the
> holes in the current factions, the palette by lightness) still hold. Sections 3 to 5 (the
> recast roster and shapes) are retired as too attached to the three factions and to one series;
> the next pass starts from the organising principle Ryan picks (the chat of 9 October, in status).

A proposal, 9 October 2026, for discussion. Nothing here is decided: the content, the bible
and the engine are untouched. Ryan asked for the factions and the pack directions to be
reworked to match where the art landed. They were written on 11 to 15 September, before the
allies went native 3D (16 September) and then to the nineties register (21 September), and
before the enemy side became, literally, the old Final Fantasy world: sixteen-bit battle
sprites (15 September), chibi overworld tokens, the flat painted ground that bleeds into the
3D field (19 September), foes that move by the overworld rule and animate only to attack
(2 October). The factions never caught up. This is the rework, with the holes in it listed at
the end, for Ryan to tweak against his own notes before any of it lands.

## 1. What the art now asks of a foe

These are the constraints the factions have to satisfy. Every one of them is already decided;
this just reads them together.

- **A foe is a flat picture, three times.** A 48x64 battle sprite (64x96 massive), a 16x16
  chibi on the map, and a codex portrait. No facing, no lighting, no idle but a breath, one
  lunge to attack. So a foe is a costume, not an anatomy: one silhouette, one gesture, and
  the telegraph is the pose it holds.
- **It must be the other genre.** "Doom and Space Marine energy" is the human side's tone.
  The enemy's whole job is to be the thing a space marine never fights, drawn with more
  colour, ornament and flourish than anything the player owns, and played dead straight.
- **Faction reads before foe at 1x**, and never by hue alone. Ryan is protanomalous; the
  sheet's rule of "no shared hue between factions" is the wrong rule. Each faction needs a
  lightness band and a silhouette family of its own (the numbers are in section 6).
- **A pack is one type times a strength**, and on the map it is a chibi with a count. Under
  the round of 8 October a pack does one of two things, pushes for the gate or hunts one
  machine, and a fight is at the pack the player attacks, joined by every pack whose ring
  covers it; machines are ground to foes and stop nothing. So a faction's identity lives in
  how its packs stand to one another, which rings overlap and who hunts, as much as in the
  sprite.
- **Enemy numbers are static and telegraphed; only the player rolls.** That is exactly how
  Final Fantasy's enemies work: fixed, named, gimmick moves you learn and answer (the
  thousand needles, the bad breath, the doom count, the grudge, the self-destruct). The
  register brings a mechanical vocabulary with it, and the current roster barely draws on it.

## 2. Where the three factions fall short of that

- **The Infernal Horde is Doom's bestiary.** Red hide, black iron, brass, hellfire, a pit
  lord with a furnace for a chest. That is the genre the mechs already live in. A space
  marine fighting a demon is one genre, not two, and the sheet's own contrast strip (a brute
  beside a gunmetal block) is the weakest clash on the page. Its mechanics are fine; its skin
  cancels the dichotomy the game is built on.
- **The Chorus looks like Lovecraft.** Violet flesh, too many eyes, folded planes, a maw
  into nothing. That is the kaiju-from-a-rift genre, Pacific Rim's, which is again the mech
  genre's own. Its mechanics (it talks to your dice) are the best in the roster and are
  precisely Final Fantasy's enemy identity, the status ailment. The idea is right and the
  skin is wrong.
- **The Risen is the one faction already in register** (zombies, skeletons, ghosts, bone
  dragons, the undead that keep coming are the series' staple), but its skin is the Diablo
  necromancer, sickly green flesh in purple rags, rather than the series' pale, armoured,
  faintly theatrical dead.
- **The answer column names verbs the game does not have.** "Cleansing" (no cleanse exists;
  Scrambled was removed), "damage over time against regen" (only the Colossus regrows),
  "armour piercing" (armour is flat and Breach is the foes' keyword, not the player's). The
  three tactical signatures that are built and measured (numbers and a summoner; a telegraph
  and heat; attacks on the die) are good. Keep them; retire the table.
- **The palette folds for Ryan.** Under the protan simulation the Risen green against the
  Host red comes out at a distance of 18, the pair that cannot be told apart on a phone.
- **Every faction makes the same pack shape** (two front packs and one back pack), and the
  ghoul and the brute are the only hunters. On the map the factions barely differ.

## 3. The proposal in one line

Keep the three tactical signatures and what each faction teaches; recast each faction's
fiction, look and pack shape into one of Final Fantasy's own monster families; and let the
register supply the gimmick each foe is named for. Working names throughout are Claude's,
for Ryan to replace.

### The Risen (undead), the pageant of the dead

Kept: the name, the signature (numbers, the caller behind them, hard to keep down), the
lesson (armour against volume, reach to the caller). Recast: the look and the roster's
costumes.

- **Look.** Bone ivory and rust over a grave-blue shadow. Everything tall and thin or a
  ragged crowd: tatters, lanterns, rusted ornate plate, tower shields. Silhouette family:
  verticals and tatters. The pale band.
- **Revenant** (was Ghoul; front, aim anything, hunts the nearest with a small ring, as
  built). A crowd of armoured dead, the swarm claw as built. The sprite is a knight's silhouette gone wrong: the helm empty, the
  plate hanging off the ribs.
- **Lantern-bearer** (was Necromancer; back, aim focus). A hooded ghost whose lantern flame
  is the brightest pixel in the faction. Hex Bolt and Raise as built.
- **Bone Knight** (new, the elite tier's candidate; front, armour 10, one big blow). The
  Risen's one mitigating foe, so the faction's second lesson (big single hits against armour)
  has a body. Reads as the series' death knight: a cape, a sword as long as it is tall.
- **Bone Colossus** (boss, as built). Already in register: a walking cathedral of ribs is a
  bone dragon by another name.
- **The leader** (the final; pitches only): the **Phantom Train**, the series' most famous
  undead, a massive unit that runs the gate line and would use the three-hex boss the engine
  has parked; or a **Lich** on a throne of the caller's own kind, the summoner's summoner.

### The Menagerie (working name; replaces the Infernal Horde), the beasts

Kept: the signature (hard hits on a telegraph, and heat) and the lesson (shield the
telegraph, lure the fire onto a cold machine, suppress the breach). Recast: it is no longer
hell. It is the series' monsters proper: behemoths, bombs, drakes, chimeras, the oversized
bright animals that are furious rather than evil.

- **Look.** Mass and horizontals: wide, low, quadruped, no cloth, fur and scale in primary
  colours. The telegraph becomes animal, the rear before the charge, the swell before the
  burst, the inhale before the breath. Heat becomes a creature's fire. Silhouette family:
  mass. The mid band, in the series' own Behemoth violet.
- **Behemoth** (was Brute; front, hunts the strongest at 30). Charge (was Haymaker) and
  Roar, which is now literally a roar. The biggest silhouette among the rank and file.
- **Fire Drake** (was Hellcaster; back, aim exposed). A lizard that breathes over the front
  rank: Firebolt with Scorch and Stoke as built. The halo and cannon go; it is an animal.
- **Bomb** (new, the elite tier's candidate; front, hunts the nearest at 30). A floating
  fireball with a face. It swells, and its blow breaches and kills it: the series'
  self-destruct, which makes a foe whose death is its attack, costly for the Sparrowhawk to
  stay beside. Needs one effect in the engine (a blow that ends the foe).
- **King Behemoth** (was Pit Lord; massive boss). Charge with Breach, Roar the pack (was
  Kindle), Fire Breath (was Eruption: heat to every machine) below half.
- **The leader** (pitches): the **Elder Wyrm**, the series' apex, a dragon that does not fly
  (foes never do; it is a wall of scale that breathes); or the **Alpha**, a King Behemoth
  with the pack's every roar on it.

### The Chorus (eldritch), the court

Kept: the name, which was always the best thing about it, and the signature (it attacks the
die: corrupt, disable, drained rerolls) and the lesson (redundancy on the die, show it a
blank or a shield). Recast: not a thing from outside reality but a court from the other
world's own opera, masked singers, a conductor, floating eyes, and the slow walker with the
knife. The series' sorcerers and oddities, and its theatre.

- **Look.** Symmetry and floating parts: masks, halos, orbiting fragments, upright and still,
  nothing animal. Midnight with gold and ivory accents. Silhouette family: the upright and
  the orbiting. The dark band.
- **Mask** (was Spawn; front). A tall masked chorister with a shield of song: Hold the Note
  (was Fold: armour until its next move) when a back-rank foe stands behind it or it is
  below half.
- **Cantor** (was Whisperer; back, aim armed). Murmur with Corrupt and Hush as built. The
  sprite is a mouth-first figure with its halo of fragments.
- **The Eye** (new, a back-rank candidate; aim focus). The series' floating eye: a Stare that
  disables what a machine shows (built), or, if the gimmick is wanted, a Doom count, a blow
  that lands two rounds after it is announced (needs a delayed blow in the engine).
- **The Penitent** (new; the dedicated hunter the to-do asks for). A small hooded figure
  with a lantern and a knife. A hunter of the slowest at 20 with a small ring (the round row:
  hunters of the slow cannot be outrun, and a hunter pins what it catches until it is dead);
  the lock it wears from its spawn says who it is coming for. Its knife breaches and grows
  every round of the fight it has forced (needs a per-round scalar in the engine, the series'
  grudge), so the fight it opens is a clock: kill it first, or the knife gets worse. The
  decision is on the map, before it arrives: meet it with company, or send the machine it
  can never pick, the Prairie Dog, to do the work behind it.
- **The Conductor** (was The Maw; massive boss). Two strikes of the baton (was Bite),
  Unmake, Unravel as built.
- **The leader** (pitches): the **Prima Donna**, the opera's own star, every Mask on the field
  singing for her; or the **Harlequin**, the series' clown-court register, which is the tonal
  risk named in section 7.
- **The commander hook** (bible section 5, 15 September: a pack with a commander whose death
  scrambles its targeting) belongs to this faction by nature. Kill the Cantor and every Mask
  in the fight aims at anything from the next round (needs an aim change on a kin's death in
  the engine). The Risen's caller already is this hook (no caller, no Raise); the Menagerie
  has none on purpose, since it is dumb by design.

## 4. Pack shapes on the map

Today the three factions spawn the same shape. Under the round of 8 October a shape is how a
faction's packs stand to one another: which rings overlap, so which packs join which fight,
and who hunts. Each faction gets one, so faction reads on the map before it is scouted and
the formation Ryan asked for on 1 October has a front and a back by faction. Rings are the
placeholders the round row left open.

| Faction | Shape | Behaviours | Pace | What it asks of the player |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Risen | **The file**: Revenant packs ahead with small rings, the Lantern-bearer behind with a large one covering them | Revenants hunt the nearest, the caller pushes | 20 | Attack a Revenant pack and the caller joins; get round to the caller and the front stays out unless adjacent. Reach past the front, or fight the whole wall |
| The Menagerie | **The loose hunt**: no file, hunters scattered, the Drake pushing on its own with a large ring | Behemoth hunts the strongest, the Bomb the nearest, the Drake pushes | 30, the Drake 20 | Every fight is forced on the player's turn; the question is where the machines stand when the beasts arrive, and who is caught alone |
| The Chorus | **The procession**: Masks and Cantors alternating in a column, their rings overlapping, the Penitent walking apart | the column pushes, the Penitent hunts the slowest | 20 | Any order on the column pulls two packs; break it at a Cantor; decide what to do about the walker before it arrives |

A guest's packs come in the guest's shape, so a mixed front still reads as two things. The
rift's spawns would place by shape rather than at the rift's mouth (today a spawn list is
placed, never shaped; that is a placement rule to write, small). Ryan's worry from the
sandbox (8 October: getting round a front pack of ring one to its back rank looks easy) is
answered per faction: the Risen is meant to be got round, that is its lesson; the Chorus's
overlapping rings are not; the Menagerie has no back to get round.

## 5. Elites, commanders and leaders in one place

The pacing row wants an elite tier that is costly for the Sparrowhawk to stay on, and the
15 September direction wants commanders. The register answers both without a new system:

- **An elite is the faction's gimmick foe**, the one the series named a move after: the Bone
  Knight (armour), the Bomb (its death is its blow), the Penitent or the Eye (the walker, the
  count). Each is one foe entry and at most one small engine effect.
- **A commander** exists where it makes sense: the Lantern-bearer (already), the Cantor (the
  aim change). The Menagerie has none.
- **A leader** is a tougher boss with the faction's whole idea on it, proposed above as
  pitches; each is content once the three-hex boss or a bigger sprite frame comes back.

## 6. The palette, by simulation

Machado matrices at full severity, CIELAB distance, worst case over protan, deutan and
tritan. The faction key is the sprite's dominant mass colour; the HUD's three chromes, the
enemy red and the heat orange were checked against each.

| Set | Keys (L*) | Worst pair | Flags against the HUD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today | Risen green 46, Host red 37, Chorus violet 31 | **18** (green vs red, protan) | green vs enemy red 25, red vs enemy red 27, red vs heat 21 |
| Proposed | Risen bone 88, Menagerie violet 43, Chorus midnight 11 | **35** (violet vs midnight, tritan) | bone vs amber chrome 10 (tritan only; chips carry glyphs) |

Lightness bands do the work: pale, mid, dark. Hue is then free for the picture. A warm key
for the beasts (tawny, ox-blood, moss) was tried and refused: every warm key folds into the
enemy red and the heat orange, the two warm colours the HUD already owns.

## 7. The holes

Looked for before building, as the rules ask.

- **Comedy.** A cactus killing a pilot is a joke, and the city falling is never funny. The
  roster above keeps to the families that are menacing in the original (behemoth, bomb,
  drake, the dead, the eye, the walker with the knife, the masked court) and leaves the
  cactuar, the chocobo and the comic octopus out. The absurdity comes from scale, colour and
  ornament against gunmetal, never from a gag. The Harlequin leader is the one pitch that
  crosses the line and is listed so Ryan can refuse it.
- **Does losing hell lose the fire?** No. Bombs and drakes are fire, and heat stays the
  Menagerie's rider.
- **A court against a Council.** The Chorus as a court mirrors the Council's own register.
  That may be a feature (the other world has its politics too) or a muddle; Ryan's call.
- **Three factions still.** The series' bestiary is wider, but the campaign (one lead, one
  guest, three finals) is built on three. More families fit inside these as elites.
- **The Risen barely changes.** It was the one in register. Its cost is mostly art.
- **The Penitent's threat.** A hunter pins what it catches, so a hunter whose knife grows
  every round is the one fight the team cannot leave cheaply, and a lone machine caught by it
  is the fight the runner already says is lost three times in five. It belongs at the elite's
  rate, never in the rank and file, and its ring should stay small so company can be sent.

## 8. What the recast costs

- **Content.** `factions.json` and `foes.json`: names, fiction, codex and flavour, the looks
  that feed foeforge's prompts (`FACTION_LOOK`), the enemy sheet's palettes. The three new
  foes and the leaders are new entries. Shown names change freely; the ids are Ryan's.
- **Ids.** `undead`, `demonic`, `eldritch` and the nine foe ids are named in about a hundred
  test lines across seven files, in the sound sets (`boss_eldritch`), the effects' recipes
  (`foe_undead_hit`) and `sheet_export.py`. A rename is mechanical and worth doing once, at
  the content revision, not now.
- **Engine, each small.** A blow that ends the foe (the Bomb); a per-round scalar on a blow
  (the Penitent); an aim change on a kin's death (the Cantor); optionally a delayed blow (the
  Eye); pack placement by shape.
- **Art.** Eight foes are still sketch grids. foeforge builds its prompt from each foe's
  fantasy and codex text, so the recast text is the art brief: land the words, then
  generate. Worth asking at the same time whether rank and file go to a bigger frame than
  48x64, since the series' monsters were larger than its party and the point is that they
  out-draw the machines.
- **Audio.** Each faction's spawn and hit sounds are keyed by faction; a beast's hit and a
  demon's are different recipes.
