# The invasion: the JRPG genre in our world

The enemy side from a fresh slate, 9 October 2026. The frame below is decided (Ryan, 9
October, in chat; bible section 9): the whole JRPG genre invades our world, Final Fantasy its
spine and the genre blended so ours is its own; the domain a rift brings with it dictates what
spawns; the leaders are the classic party; there are four; every foe move wears the genre's
name and a plaque announces it. Everything under "the roster" is a pitch: working names,
numbers as placeholders, each for Ryan to replace against his own notes before it lands in
`content/`. The first proposal of the day (the three factions recast) is retired; its holes in
the old factions, its reading of the art's constraints and its palette simulation carried into
this one where they still hold.

## 1. The frame

**The other world is a JRPG world.** The rift is where its overworld pushes into ours, which
the map already shows: the bleed paints the classic overworld over our ground, and the foes
are flat sixteen-bit pictures with no facing that animate only to attack. What comes through
is that world's wildlife, by the biome that bleeds, and, once the commander matters enough,
that world's heroes. Played dead straight: CARE classifies a running cactus the way it
classifies anything, and nobody in the game finds it funny. The joke belongs to the player who
notices, as the Council's acronym does.

**Domain dictates spawns.** A domain is one biome of the classic overworld: its bleed (the
tiles the flattened ground paints), its wildlife (the bestiary with one tactical signature),
and its hero. A rift rolls its domain when it opens, as a faction was rolled (bible section 3);
one lead and at most one guest, as today. The front card says the domain and the tier; the
bleed shows it on the map before a single pack is scouted; the domain biases the generator's
map type. Scouting is the land.

**The leaders are the party.** From their side they are the heroes, levelled and on a quest,
and our city is the dungeon. They kill thousands of monsters without remorse and never talk
to us. The classic four: the Warrior, the Thief, the White Mage, the Black Mage, one to a
domain. Each is a boss fight in the party's own shape, the hero in its rank with its domain's
wildlife filling the other columns, since four columns in two ranks is already a party. Kill
enough of a domain's wildlife and its Anathema fills (section 3's bar): you are the boss of
this dungeon, and the hero comes for you.

**The finale is the party assembled, promoted.** The genre's class change: the Knight, the
Ninja, the White Wizard, the Black Wizard, in two ranks at the capital. A hero beaten in its
final is an empty slot in that formation, which is what thinning a faction now means. The
Convergence is their level: escalation is the party grinding, and tiers are depth, the
overworld's wildlife at one, the dungeon's at two, and at three the register every JRPG final
dungeon shares, which is cosmic horror. The Lovecraftian dial lives there, in depth, not in a
faction.

**Four.** Four cities round the capital, four domains, four heroes, a finale against four.
What was keyed to three (the sound sets, the guest rule, the finals' count, the enemy sheet's
palettes, the effects' recipes) is content and small code, all up for change (Ryan).

**Hero-plus-beast pairs, answered.** Some jobs are defined by what they command (a summoner's
esper, a dragoon's dragon, a beastmaster's behemoth). For those, the leader fight would be a
pair: the hero human-sized in the back as the brain, the companion massive in front as the
hitter, and killing the hero first makes the companion dumb. None of the classic four needs
it: the Warrior is the massive thing itself, and the mages and the Thief stand behind their
domain's wildlife. So pairs are a pattern for a later hero, not a rule now.

**The genre's names, and the plaque.** Every foe move wears the genre's name where the genre
has one (Protect, Shell, Cure, Fire, Fira, Flare, Meteor, Holy, Steal, Mug, Flee, Stone, Doom,
Self-Destruct) while the engine's words stay ours (harden, regrow, revive once, heat to all,
breach). When a foe uses a named move, a plaque with its name shows on screen as a classic
JRPG would, and the replay holds for it. The plaque is the other world's own window, the blue
box with the double white border, drawn in the foes' pixel register over our vector HUD, never
in the HUD's chrome; a plain blow gets none, so the slowing lands where it counts. Decided 9
October; the build note is in section 6.

## 2. The roster

Four domains. For each: the bleed, the wildlife with its signature and what the build must
answer in the engine's own vocabulary, the hero, and the leader fight. Names are working
names; the homage is the shape and the move, never a trademark's silhouette.

### The Meadow: grassland and forest, the first overworld

- **Bleed.** Bright green plains, round trees, a road that goes nowhere. The classic.
- **Signature.** Numbers that split and swarm. Dumb and many.
- **Answer.** Area damage, armour against volume, kill order.
- **Wildlife.** The **Gel** (the slime; front, hunts the nearest; splits on death into two
  smaller, a summon on death the engine lacks). The **Goblin** (front, the swarm claw as the
  ghoul's). The **Wolf** (a hunter of the slowest, in packs). The **Mandragora** (back; its
  Scream drains a reroll). The **Treant** (the elite; front, armour 10, Regen). The **Needler**
  on the Meadow's desert edge is the Waste's, below.
- **Hero: the Warrior.** Front, massive. A sword the size of the tank. Slash (one telegraphed
  blow), Cover (it takes the blow aimed at another foe in the fight, an interpose the engine
  lacks on the foe side), and below half its limit, Cleave, a blow at every machine (new, or
  three blows at anything as the Colossus does). The promoted form: the Knight, the same with
  armour 10.

### The Waste: desert and badlands

- **Bleed.** Sand, dunes, bleached bones, cacti in rows all facing the same way.
- **Signature.** Fixed damage that no shield answers, and theft of what you have.
- **Answer.** Hull and tempo over shields; redundancy on the die against theft.
- **Wildlife.** The **Needler** (the Cactuar, made sinister: a figure in a running pose that
  has never been seen to move; front, arrives in numbers; Needles is exactly ten and breaches,
  and it leaves the fight after striking, so the pack is never fully dead and comes back; needs
  a flee effect). The **Sandworm** (massive; Swallow breaches; Burrow hardens). The
  **Cockatrice** (back; Stone disables the showing entry through the next turn, as Hush does).
  The **Antlion** (a hunter that pins, the trap at the bottom of the funnel). The **Gold Bug**
  (the metal slime: a rare pack that flees on the map and pays out if caught, the fast
  machines' own hunt).
- **Hero: the Thief.** Front, fast, a hunter of the slowest. Steal (drains a reroll), Mug (a
  blow that corrupts the showing entry), and Flee below half: it leaves the fight and comes
  back next battle, the recurring boss who gets away, played straight. Its final is beaten
  only by killing it before it can flee, a tempo check. The promoted form: the Ninja, two
  moves a round.

### The Marsh: swamp, graveyard, the dungeon

- **Bleed.** Dark marsh, dead trees, tombstones, the poison swamp's purple.
- **Signature.** Hard to keep down: they regrow, get back up and are called again.
- **Answer.** Kill order and reach (the healer behind), burst that beats regrow.
- **Wildlife.** The **Revenant** (the dead in rusted plate; front, numbers, the swarm claw).
  The **Ghost** (back, aim focus; Hex Bolt and Raise, the Lantern-bearer of the morning). The
  **Bone Dragon** (massive, the Colossus as it stands: Knit, Bone Storm). The **Mimic** (loot
  on the map that is a pack when claimed: the genre's chest that bites, which the salvage row's
  Relic already invites). The **Zombie Knight** (the elite; front, armour 10, Reraise, the
  revive-once the engine has).
- **Hero: the White Mage.** Back. Cure (regrows an ally, which the engine has only for self),
  Protect (hardens every foe in the fight), Reraise (revive once on an ally), and below half
  Holy, the one big telegraphed blow. The healer who heals the dead: kill it first, the lesson
  the genre taught everyone. The promoted form: the White Wizard, with Shell (half damage,
  the old Fold).

### The Peak: volcano and mountain, the fire cave

- **Bleed.** Lava, black rock, the fire cave's orange.
- **Signature.** Hard hits on a telegraph, and heat.
- **Answer.** Shields for the telegraph, cooling, a lure onto a cold machine.
- **Wildlife.** The **Bomb** (front, hunts the nearest at 30; it swells, and Self-Destruct is a
  breach that kills it: needs one effect). The **Drake** (back; Breath with Scorch, Stoke, the
  Hellcaster's moves). The **Salamander** (front; its blows burn, and it burns what hits it, a
  retaliate the foe side lacks). The **Magma Golem** (massive, armour 10, the King Behemoth's
  Fire Breath to every machine). The **Fire Bat** swarm.
- **Hero: the Black Mage.** Back. Fire (a blow with Scorch at the hottest), Fira (heat to
  every machine), Flare (the biggest telegraphed blow, breaching), and below half Meteor,
  three blows at anything. The promoted form: the Black Wizard, Flare every other round.

### The finale

The four in two ranks: Knight and Ninja ahead, White Wizard and Black Wizard behind, every
hero beaten in its final an empty slot. Their level is the Convergence's tier. Whether something
cosmic follows them through, the thing they were questing to kill, is parked: the genre's
superboss is an optional fight past the Rupture, never the finale.

## 3. Escalation inside a domain

The recolour tier, the genre's own: a worse pack is the same foe repainted with a bigger
number (Gel, Bubble Gel, Metal Gel), which foeforge's cutter can repaint without a new drawing.
Depth three's forms go wrong in the final-dungeon way: extra eyes, folded geometry, the colours
inverted. That is where the earlier proposal's Lovecraft lives now.

## 4. Scouting and the read on the map

The domain reads before any foe does, by structure: the bleed's tiles are sand or grass or
marsh or lava, and every pack stands on a disc of its domain's ground. So the sprites need not
carry the domain in their hue, which frees the palette and ends the fold the morning's
simulation found (today's green against red at 18 for a protan viewer). If a key colour per
domain is still wanted for the sheet, the lightness-band method stands: pale, mid, dark, and
a fourth checked by simulation at the art pass.

## 5. The holes

- **A boss that gets away.** The Thief's Flee is the genre's joke played straight and could
  read as a cheat. Its price must show before the fight (the plaque, the intent) and the
  reward for catching it must be large. Ryan's call.
- **Heroes who lead nobody.** The wildlife is not led; it pours through with the land. The
  hero is the apex that arrives when the commander matters. Only the mages buff what stands
  with them, which is enough.
- **The player's own names.** The genre's names go on foe moves only. Our components keep
  our names, or the clash goes.
- **Four lightness bands** may not exist on one phone palette. The ground carries the read,
  so this is a sheet question, not a game question.
- **The class change doubles the boss content.** Eight hero entries, not four. The promoted
  forms share the base's sprite with a repaint and one move more, so the cost is a repaint and
  a line each.

## 6. What it costs, and what to build first

- **Content.** `factions.json` becomes domains (four), `foes.json` the wildlife and the
  heroes, every move renamed in the genre's words with the engine's word in its text. The
  three faction ids and nine foe ids are named in about a hundred test lines; the rename is
  the content revision's.
- **Engine, each small.** A summon on death (the Gel). A flee (the Needler, the Thief). A
  blow that ends the foe (the Bomb). A cure and a revive on an ally (the White Mage). An
  interpose on the foe side (the Warrior). A retaliate on the foe side (the Salamander). A
  blow at every machine (Cleave), or the Colossus's three at anything. A rare pack that flees
  on the map (the Gold Bug). The Mimic as loot. Placement by domain and the bleed by domain.
- **The plaque, ready to build.** When a named move (every defend, buff or debuff, and every
  boss blow) fires in the enemy replay, the other world's window slides in over the foes'
  rows: a blue gradient box, a double white border, the name in a pixel face, at the foes'
  pixel scale, and the replay waits about a second for it; a plain blow shows none. Judged
  running, so it ships with a switch that fires it constantly, and Ryan sets the hold.
- **Art.** Four bleeds (the flattened ground's tiles by domain). Every foe through foeforge
  from the new text, the heroes at the boss register, elaborate and over the top. Whether rank
  and file go to a bigger frame than 48x64 is still worth asking, since the point is that they
  out-draw the machines.
- **Audio.** Four boss sets; The Far Bank reassigned to one domain (it was The Chorus's).
  Spawn and hit recipes keyed by domain.
