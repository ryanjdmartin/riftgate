# The vocabulary pass, 14 November (two rounds; nothing decided)

Every rider, keyword, cost, buff and debuff on both sides, read against the reboot and the
yardstick for the basics. It began as the rider and keyword pass and Ryan widened it in its
second round: "I want to examine enemy riders as well, and all existing buffs and debuffs on
both sides, not just weapon riders. This is a vocabulary domain pass."

**His words, the first round.** 13 November, ruling on the reboot: "we'll need to audit every
rider and keyword after this, no matter what", and of the foes' Burn on a machine, "Ignore
enemy Burn for now, we might be forced to change it regardless". 14 November, after his play
kept the reboot and his logic for the stock basics was read (the refit brief's sections 22
and 23): "On that note, let's do the rider and keyword pass now."

**His words, the second round**, the same day, on the first round's report:

> Why measure penetrate 2, burn 2, and not 1?
>
> How convinced are you of the +1 paradigm for tier 1? Your list kind of makes it sound easy
> to get that +1. Should we still be considering +2?
>
> Do remember that the plain vanilla gun is the benchmark for "viable," not "good." There
> needs to be a reason to upgrade. I hope, at tier 1, that the reason to slot in found
> components is to start the Synergy scaling with their units, not an obviously straight
> power increase. I think tier 2 and up, it's probably safe to use the TCG heuristic that a
> vanilla is slightly under the average, not right on the average. It's something we
> discussed a while back.
>
> I want to examine enemy riders as well, and all existing buffs and debuffs on both sides,
> not just weapon riders. This is a vocabulary domain pass. I'm, as always, particularly
> concerned with Armour, which is why I asked about foe hit sizes relative to shield and
> armour sizes.
>
> A point you made earlier, is that the reboot is like the "reload" and the heat meter the
> ammunition. I think Burn directly attacking the ammunition is dangerous. Previously, there
> was two counterplay options: Proactively cool, or cleanse it. Now, all we have is cleanse,
> and the imminent threat isn't overheat damage - it's the loss of your shield, and all
> protection with it. It's effectively a DoT that hits both meters, with few answers.
> Scorched at least has the option of waiting it out. I think it might be an opportunity to
> make another symmetrical debuff on both sides. Is there a word that we could use to
> represent not just strict burning, but a general eroding over time, that we could apply to
> it on both sides?
>
> I'm not sure on the barrier basic. With cooldown 1 it reads as not degenerate to me, but
> I'm really not sure how to unwind it from the data.

What it reads against:

- *The reboot* (the brief's section 22): nothing regenerates or cools by itself; a machine
  that has spent nothing may give its turn to a reboot, bare that turn, cold and whole the
  next; heat past the maximum stays and burns hull every turn; a shield at nothing is Broken
  and gains none until the reboot.
- *His yardstick for the basics* (section 23): the vanilla gun under the standard foe, a
  rider paid for in damage first and heat second, "until it turns into Equipment"; the gun
  that deals one more paid for in heat, "a first build step". Whether the vanilla sits one
  under or two is section 3.
- *The vanilla is viable, not good*: his words above, and two rows already decided (section
  13).
- *His scope*: the basics and tier one. "Tier two foes are facing a more coherent build from
  the player, so these +/- 1 questions aren't relevant there."
- *His expectation, no strict rule*: what grants regen, cooling, flat shield or flat heat
  reduction is a kit's, a locked piece's or gated behind a synergy ("they should be something
  special"); a shield spend may not be standard fare ("it's so expensive").

How to read it. Every row is a reading and a lean, his to mark. A price is a first price for
the bench of section 13, never a number for content. Pieces whose machine has a session
coming are named and left to it. Section 15 lists what is his to decide. The runs are
`docs/measurements.md`, 14 November, two entries; every measure here is the greedy bot's, 300
fights a row unless it says otherwise, and a direction.

## 1. What the pass finds

1. **No rider's rule breaks under the reboot, and one should change.** Prices move in four
   places. Heat is a real price now, since none of it leaves: Scorch, Cryo, the two modules
   that cool and every vent are worth more than they were built for. A blow prevented is
   worth more, since the shield it would have taken does not mend until the reboot: Sap,
   Suppress, Lure, a Barrier, a Ward and Armoured all gain. What grants shield lands on a
   live shield alone, which leaves two modules nearly dead. A blank or disabled die has spent
   nothing, so it is the turn to reboot: the rule softens every Disable and Steal. The one
   rule that should change is the foes' Burn (finding 6).
2. **A rider is worth the same wherever it rides.** Burn 1 adds nine tenths of a point to a
   gun of 3, of 2 and of 1 alike; Spread 1 a point; Splash 1 a point and a half; Shock nine
   tenths; Penetrate 1 and Breach six tenths; Flak 1 half; Sap a point of hull a stack and
   nothing in rounds. So his ladder is arithmetic: a gun's number, less what it paid, plus
   its rider's worth. The first round said one under the standard foe makes every stray
   point the point more. Over the roster that was wrong: the riders read the same on a
   vanilla of 2 as of 3. It holds in a pure horde, which is section 3.
3. **A rider's worth is the foe it answers.** The point more, a split gun, Burn and the area
   chips answer a horde. Flak, Shock, Breach and Expose read as nothing against a horde and
   as a point or more against a massive foe behind plate, where all four match a flat 4 in
   rounds. So one price a rider is a mean over the roster, the table by the kind of formation
   is the truer read, and it is the domain identity his bench asks for at no cost.
4. **A point of heat is worth half a point of damage at most**, a quarter once the gauge
   already outlasts the fight, and nothing in a duel. So heat prices the half steps his
   ladder needs between rungs, which is his point about the second lever.
5. **The kill line is a cliff wherever it sits, and one under puts it a cheap point away.**
   In a horde of eight plain 4s the gun that crosses it takes 38% off the hull the horde
   costs, and a point past it buys nothing. One under leaves a tier-one build a point of
   room on its standard foe, and six of the nine dealt tier ones carry that point in their
   own kit. Two under leaves two points of room, the first worth an eighth of the hull and
   the second the cliff. Claude's lean is two under, at a vanilla of 3 against a standard
   foe of 5, loosely held: his play decides it, and a dial would make all three playable
   (section 3).
6. **The foes' Burn fails its own row's test under the reboot.** The row of 13 October made
   it a debuff because "heat applied straight is so polarising that it needs several avenues
   of counterplay, or it spirals", and counted two answers, the vent and the cleanse. The
   reboot took the vent. Measured on every rank-and-file blow, Burn 1 reads as a third of a
   point on the blow and Burn 2 as a point and a half with one fight in twelve lost: nothing,
   then a cliff. A status that erodes shield and hull on both sides, past armour, is ordinary
   damage, and it is the soft answer to a wall and to a Barrier that both sides lack
   (section 9). Claude's lean of the first round, to keep Burn as heat, is withdrawn.
7. **The foes' riders, in points on the blow.** Breach is two points on every blow. Corrupt 1
   and Crush 1 are each over a point. Disable, Drain and Scorch 1 are nearly free in hull,
   which makes them the riders that change what the player does without costing a fight.
8. **Armour is a threshold, and its meaning is the blow it meets.** The standard blow is 3, 4
   and 5 by tier, so a point of armour is a third of a blow at tier one and a fifth at tier
   three, and the Armoured basic's 2 leaves a third of a tier-one blow. On our guns a point
   of plate takes a third of a vanilla of 3 and half of a vanilla of 2. The rule of 22
   October stands; what the pass adds is sizes, and an answer to plate that is no breach
   (section 6).
9. **The Barrier is worth the blow it meets.** Nothing against a kit of many strikes, three
   quarters of the damage of a kit that is one blow a round. Over the elites it is worth a
   spike half again as hard; its cooldown is a gentle slope; one boss alone stalls on it.
   His read holds: not degenerate (section 11).
10. **Tier one's finds are not on the ladder**, and two decided rows already say what they
    should be: tier zero is tier one's power, and a find in tier-one rifts "is a new trick
    and never a bigger number". Of fourteen slottable tier-one guns eleven deal 4 or more
    (section 13).

## 2. The measure of the player's riders

The method: the six tier ones of the anchor as trios, tier one's ten formations at their
rating, the greedy bot, the reboot on, the trial's basics (a vanilla of 3 for 3 heat). Every
plain basic gun on every die is replaced by one test gun at 3 heat, the Ankle Mortar keeping
its Indirect, and the fights' rounds and hull are read against the same fights with a flat
gun of 1 to 5 in its place. "Reads as a flat 3.5" means the fights ran as they would with a
plain gun between 3 and 4. The reads are drawn from the ten fights' own rows.

Why the first round ran Burn 2 and Penetrate 2 and no 1: those were the smallest numbers
content prints, on the Incendiary Shells, the Blazethrower and the Cottonmouth's drum. That
was the wrong reason for a ladder priced in units. The second round ran the 1s, a second
number for each rider, and the same riders on a gun of 1.

What it cannot say, each a reason the bench and his play come after it:

- It is the greedy bot's play. A rider that needs a second, planned spend reads low: Shock,
  Flak, Expose, a Lure that covers a reboot.
- Every basic gun carries the rider at once, so a squad of nothing but Lures, Disrupts or
  Suppresses is read, which is no squad. Those three are no fair read.
- Formations alone. A duel, an elite and a boss price a rider differently: the big-foe
  riders are the first to gain there.
- A rounds column moves in tenths. A difference under 0.2 rounds or half a point of hull is
  the run's grain.

**What it says.**

1. *The kill line is a cliff in every world.* The gun that crosses it takes a third to
   nearly half off what a horde of the standard foe costs, and a point past it buys nothing:
   0.2 rounds over the roster. Two under does not remove the cliff. It moves it a point
   further from the vanilla.
2. *So the yardstick is room.* One under leaves a tier-one build one point of room on its
   standard foe before the plateau. Two under leaves two.
3. *The point is easy to get.* Section 2 prices Burn 1, Shock, Spread 1 and a split gun at
   about a point each, at par. Six of the nine dealt tier ones carry a point or more in
   their own kit: the Skua's Ricochet, the Cassowary's Spin-Up, the Cottonmouth's drum, the
   Prairie Dog's Flak, the Polar Bear's point against Cryo, the Storm Petrel's field. At one
   under that is the kill line crossed by the choice of machine. His test of the morning,
   "how accessible are we making that extra damage", reads: very.
4. *His three tests point the same way.* The vanilla two-shots the standard foe in all
   three worlds, so it is viable in all three. A find that starts a unit's scaling without
   being a straight power increase is a point that is worth an eighth of a horde alone and
   the cliff with the unit's own point: two under. "Something to work for as a first build
   step" is a kill line two sources away: two under.
5. *Area is its own thing.* Spread 1 on the vanilla reaches the kill line's hull in every
   column, since a chip on each neighbour is two points of damage with none wasted. No
   yardstick prices that; its own number does.
6. *The first round overstated it.* Over the roster a point is worth the same from 2 to 3
   as from 3 to 4, since the domains' standards run from 2 to 6. The cliff is each domain's
   horde, not the tier.

**What each world asks.**

- *One under, 3 against 4.* The trial he is about to play. The shortest fights. The first
  trick is the whole step, and the build has nowhere to go on a horde after it.
- *Two under, 2 against 4.* Today's printed numbers. Its two shots kill exactly. One rung
  under the vanilla, and plate takes half of every basic shot: a massive of 16 behind a
  point of plate is sixteen vanilla shots, which is much of the "shot too many" his play
  found. Section 2's dials have it at 2 heat: 5.0 rounds and 8.8%.
- *Two under, 3 against 5.* The ladder keeps two rungs down and gains two up. Plate takes a
  third. His pair survives a rung down: a hot 4 and 2 twice are one gun against plain 5s,
  4.6 rounds and 11.8%, and part against 3 behind plate. It costs a rank and file
  renumbered, with tier two's standard already at 5, and dearer fights until the blows are
  read again: 5.4 rounds and 11.0% over the roster where one under runs 4.7 and 8.1%. A
  point off every rank-and-file blow is worth four points of hull (section 8), so the
  anchor's read can pay for it.

**Claude's lean, his to take or leave**: two under, at 3 against 5. How convinced: less than
the first round sounded. The measure says the yardstick matters less over the roster than a
pure horde suggests, and what it feels like to be a point short is his play's to say. All
three are a dial away. BASIC GUNS is built. A second dial, a hit point more on tier one's
rank and file, would make the third world playable beside the other two. It is offered, not
built.

## 9. Burn, and a status that erodes on both sides

**Where the record stands.** Decided 12 and 13 October: "Foes heat machines only through
debuffs: Burning and Scorched. Heat applied straight is so polarising that it needs several
avenues of counterplay, or it spirals." Burning was given two answers, "the player can vent
the heat already there or cleanse it", and one word with two effects by body: "Metal does
not burn, it heats."

**His point**, in full above: the reboot took the vent, so one answer is left, and what Burn
threatens is no longer the overheat's damage but the shield, which the first overheat
breaks. "It's effectively a DoT that hits both meters, with few answers."

He is right, and the row's own test says so: one avenue of counterplay where it asked for
several. Claude's lean of the first round was to keep it as the foes' press on the heat
clock, the mirror of a blow on the shield. That missed what he names: a blow costs shield
and stops, where Burn's tick can take the gauge over its maximum with no spend made, and the
shield with it.

**What the measure says** (section 8's rows, and the same fights with a machine's Burning
taking shield then hull in place of heat, a what-if in the scratch probe):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burn 1, as heat | 4.9 | 10.0% | 99% | 0.06 | 1.1 | 0.3 |

As heat it is nothing until a gauge is near full and then an overheat, a broken shield and a
burn every turn: a third of a point at 1, a point and a half at 2, and the dearest row in
wrecks. As erosion it is plain damage and dearer at the same number, since a stack is a

**The form, a first statement, his to shape.** N stacks. As its side's turn comes round it
loses a point a stack, then a stack fades. On a foe: hit points, past armour, at the start

**What it does in the system.**

1. Heat is the player's own again. The foes tax it with Scorch, which a hold waits out, and

2. It is ordinary damage. A shield takes it and a reboot gives the shield back, so the

3. It passes armour on both sides: the answer to a wall that is no breach (section 6).
4. It passes a Barrier, and a blow's riders already land through one. The row of 17 October

5. One word, one effect, on either side, and the Arsenal's cleanse that moves a machine's

The cooldown is a gentle slope, two to four points of hull a round of it. The step is
having one at all. The fights do not lengthen with it: an elite's runs 5.0 to 5.4 rounds on
every row.

**By the spike it is worth**

| The squad, and the foe | An elite: hull, won, wrecks | A boss: hull, won, wrecks |
|---|---|---|
| With the Barrier basic, as built | 11.1%, 100%, 0.03 | 26.5%, 99%, 0.22 |
| With it, the foe's blows half again as hard | 24.4%, 97%, 0.40 | 60.0%, 73%, 1.63 |
| With it, the blows doubled | 34.3%, 92%, 0.81 | 76.4%, 46%, 2.68 |

Against an elite the Barrier basic is worth blows half again as hard: a squad with it pays
for those what a squad without it pays for today's. Against a boss it is worth less, a
quarter to a third, since more of a boss's damage comes in many blows.

**By the fight.** Its share of the foes' damage, and the hull with it and without:

| The fight | A Barrier's share | Hull with it | Hull without |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Thunder Ape, three strikes a move | 34% | 12.5% | 11.9% |
| The Fiend of Fire, blows at every machine | 19% | 39.3% | 39.1% |
| Floraison | 13% | 23.0% | 24.5% |
| The Shaman's warband | 14% | 13.6% | 18.5% |
| The Wild Hunt | 30% | 17.1% | 27.3% |
| Gilgamesh | 57% | 10.3% | 24.8% |
| The Old Man of the Desert | 58% | 15.0% | 41.1% |
| The Mantis Ant | 66% | 11.1% | 42.7% |
| The Goblin Nob, one blow a round | 73% | 15.6% | 53.3% |
| The Gargantuan King | 65% | 35.1%, 21 rounds, 85% won | 55.2%, 9 rounds, 95% won |

So the Barrier is no flat discount. It is worth nothing against a kit of many strikes or of
blows at every machine, and three quarters of a kit that is one blow a round. One fight
stalls on it, the Gargantuan King's, twice as long with it as without.

**The reading.** His read holds: with its cooldown it is not degenerate. It lengthens no
fight but one, its cooldown is no cliff, and in a formation it is worth a vanilla shot.
What the data shows as a hole is on the foes' side: a kit that is one blow a round is three
quarters turned by one basic. And the budget squares in an unexpected place. His budget for
an elite's fight is a quarter of a four's hull. Today's elites cost that without a Barrier
and half of it with one. So the elites are sized for a squad with none, and the Barrier is
an edge worth half an elite, which is his "sub for a Tank" of 12 November.

**Leans.**

1. Leave the Barrier basic as built, cooldown and all, and do not size the spike against
   it: a squad without one is then on budget and a squad with one is ahead.
2. Mend the one-blow kits in the foes' pass. A blow's riders land through a Barrier
   already, so a spike that carries one is never turned whole, and the eroding status of
   section 9 is the rider that carries damage past it. A second, smaller blow does the
   same.
3. The Gargantuan King's stall is its own fix, with the bosses' numbers.
4. A vanilla shot's heat on it is a fair price for a player and changes little: 12.7% for
   11.1% against an elite. It is not needed.

## 13. The vanilla as viable, and the tier-one bench

**His words**, in full above: "the plain vanilla gun is the benchmark for 'viable,' not
'good.' There needs to be a reason to upgrade. I hope, at tier 1, that the reason to slot in
found components is to start the Synergy scaling with their units, not an obviously straight
power increase. I think tier 2 and up, it's probably safe to use the TCG heuristic that a
vanilla is slightly under the average, not right on the average."

**The rows that already hold it.** 28 October, the pricing row: vanillas "sit low on the
scale ('otherwise the game flattens to nothing'), the floor of their tier, every keyword
piece and every hot piece above it by what it offers and what it pays". The tiers row: tier
zero is tier one's power, "only simpler", and "a find in tier-one rifts is a new trick and
never a bigger number".

**What it changes in this pass.**

- A tier-one find is priced at par: alone it reads as the vanilla. Section 4's last column
  is those pieces, and section 2 says the arithmetic holds from rung to rung. The reason to
  slot one is what a unit does with it, so the bench's next question is by machine: the
  same piece on each die, read by pick share and by the fight.
- From tier two the vanilla sits a little under its tier's mean, so a keyword piece is a
  little over the vanilla alone, as the pricing row has it.
- The straight point more is a basic's, paid in heat, by his logic of the morning. It is no

domain identities without a ton of work. The same logic can be applied to all of the tier 1
components, rather than just creating arbitrary shapes, we can make placeholder weapons and
equipment for a variety of combinations of riders, damage, heat, and hit counts, and then
see what sticks and is interesting and what is redundant enough to cut. Agreed?"

Agreed, and section 12 of the refit brief had asked for the same table. Sections 2 and 3 are
its first cuts. Five things keep it small and readable.

- *One trade a piece.* Each bench piece is the vanilla with one thing traded, named: a
  point more for heat, a point more split in two, a point less for a rider, the rider kept
  for a point of heat, Indirect for a point of heat. A piece with two trades waits for its
  two parents to have been read.
- *A kill signature.* `tools/basics_model.py`, its eighth table, gives each gun its spends to
  kill five marks: a plain 4, a 3 behind a point of plate (his second standard), a thin 3, a
  thick 6 and a 4 behind plate. Two guns with the same row and the same heat are one gun,
  and the later one is cut before it is built.
- *Marks as well as guns.* A placeholder formation is as cheap as a placeholder gun: one
  standard foe eight times. Section 3 runs seven of them. The bench reads every gun against
  those before the roster, and the domains' standards can be picked from what they show.
- *Par, then the unit.* A piece that reads over the vanilla alone is a bigger number by
  another road. The bench prices each to par first, then reads it on each machine.
- *Read by the kind of formation, by the bot's pick share and by his play.* A rider that
  reads the same in every column is a number; one that reads apart is a domain's piece.

Where the bench pieces live is his: as content with working names, or as rows a debug dial
deals and the runner reads, with no name, text or model owed until one is kept. Claude leans
to the second for the grid and the first for what survives it, since a name and a line are
his to write.

## 14. The defence side: an approach

His question: "How are we going to approach enemy kits and damage in relation to the
various shield and hull values we have? Since our shield and hull values are, bluntly,
pretty completely random, so there's no reason to keep those locked either."

The same way as the guns, mirrored. On the offence side the foe was fixed first and the gun
measured against it. On the defence side the machine is fixed first and the foes' blows
measured against it. One thing does not mirror, and it shapes the rest: a gun's damage past
a kill is wasted, so a gun is read in thresholds, shots to kill. A blow's damage past a
shield reaches the hull, so a shield is a pool, read in totals. The thresholds on the
defence side are the Barrier, which eats one blow whatever its size, and armour, which takes
a point off every blow (sections 6 and 11).

1. *Three clocks, three units.* The round's: a Barrier is one blow, Armoured a point a blow.
   The cycle's, from one reboot to the next: the shield and the heat. The battle's: the hull,
   by his budget of 9 November (a formation's fight a twentieth of the squad's hull at its
   rating, a flanker's a thirtieth, a hunter's a tenth, an elite's a quarter, the boss what
   is left).
2. *A shield is counted in standard blows*, as a foe is counted in shots. The standard blow
   is the defence side's first number: 3, 4 and 5 by tier today (section 6). The shields do
   not grow with it, so a shield is two to four blows at tier one and two to three at tier
   three.
3. *A fight is counted in cycles*: the foes' damage over the squad's shields. The reads of
   the trial give it today's values: a formation lands 26 damage on a trio whose shields sum
   to 18 to 29; an elite lands 53 and a boss 107 on a four whose shields sum to 26 to 35. So
   a formation is one cycle, an elite nearly two and a boss three to four, and the kinds of
   fight are already a ladder in that unit. Each cycle past the first is bought with a
   reboot, turned by a Barrier, or paid in hull.
4. *Two clocks, one reset.* A machine's heat runs out in its maximum over its guns' heat,
   and its shield in its size over the blows it draws. The reboot resets both. If one always
   runs out first the other gauge is decoration: a duel today does not read heat at all,
   since cooler guns leave it as long and as dear. A formation has them about level: its 4.7
   rounds are a gauge of four vanilla shots and a little over, and its damage is the squad's
   shields once over. So a standard machine's two clocks should run out within a round or
   two of each other at its fights' rating, and a role is which one it leans on.
5. *A point on every blow is the foes' unit*, four to seven points of a four's hull in a
   formation (section 8), and every rider has a price in it.
6. *A hull has two jobs.* It is the battle's pool, and a quarter of it is the blow that
   destroys a component: a 5 on a hull of 20, a 7 on 28, a 10 on 40. So a hull's number says
   which foes can break a die, and the rank and file's 3 and 4 break none today.
7. *The standard machine.* A handful of shapes in those units, by role, in place of the
   numbers as they fell: a shield of two, three or four blows; a hull by the budget and by
   the blow that should break its die; plate for the machine that means to be hit.
8. *A foe's kit is which clock it presses, or which it goes round.* The swarm presses the
   shield of the whole squad, and armour is its answer. The spike presses one machine's, and
   the Barrier is its answer. Scorch presses the heat. A breach goes round the shield and
   the plate. The eroding status goes round the plate and the Barrier and not the shield. A
   blow at every machine catches the reboot. The heat aim and the exposed aim find the
   machine a reboot is due on. A disable, a corrupted entry and a drained reroll attack the
   die. A domain is two or three of these, and its standard foe.
9. *A model first, then dials.* `tools/shield_model.py` already runs the reboot against the
   foes' shapes; it takes the units above and prices each kit in hull past the swarm's.
   Then role shapes and kits as a table in chat, dials for the shields, the hull and the
   blows, the bot at the anchor, and his play.
