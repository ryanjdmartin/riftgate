## 1. What the pass finds

1. **No rider's rule breaks under the reboot, and one should change.** Prices move in four
   places. Heat is a real price now, since none of it leaves: Scorch, Cryo, the two modules
   that cool and every vent are worth more than they were built for. A blow prevented is
   worth more, since the shield it would have taken does not mend until the reboot: Sap,
   Suppress, Lure, a Barrier, a Ward and Armoured all gain. What grants shield lands on a
   live shield alone, which leaves two modules nearly dead. A blank or disabled die has spent
   nothing, so it is the turn to reboot: the rule softens every Disable and Steal. The one
   rule that should change is the foes' Burn (finding 6).
2. **A rider is worth the same wherever it rides.** Burn 1 adds nine tenths of a point to a
   gun of 3, of 2 and of 1 alike; Spread 1 a point; Splash 1 a point and a half; Shock nine
   tenths; Penetrate 1 and Breach six tenths; Flak 1 half; Sap a point of hull a stack and
   nothing in rounds. So his ladder is arithmetic: a gun's number, less what it paid, plus
   its rider's worth. The first round said one under the standard foe makes every stray
   point the point more. Over the roster that was wrong: the riders read the same on a
   vanilla of 2 as of 3. It holds in a pure horde, which is section 3.
3. **A rider's worth is the foe it answers.** The point more, a split gun, Burn and the area
   chips answer a horde. Flak, Shock, Breach and Expose read as nothing against a horde and
   as a point or more against a massive foe behind plate, where all four match a flat 4 in
   rounds. So one price a rider is a mean over the roster, the table by the kind of formation
   is the truer read, and it is the domain identity his bench asks for at no cost.
4. **A point of heat is worth half a point of damage at most**, a quarter once the gauge
   already outlasts the fight, and nothing in a duel. So heat prices the half steps his
   ladder needs between rungs, which is his point about the second lever.
5. **The kill line is a cliff wherever it sits, and one under puts it a cheap point away.**
   In a horde of eight plain 4s the gun that crosses it takes 38% off the hull the horde
   costs, and a point past it buys nothing. One under leaves a tier-one build a point of
   room on its standard foe, and six of the nine dealt tier ones carry that point in their
   own kit. Two under leaves two points of room, the first worth an eighth of the hull and
   the second the cliff. Claude's lean is two under, at a vanilla of 3 against a standard
   foe of 5, loosely held: his play decides it, and a dial would make all three playable
   (section 3).
6. **The foes' Burn fails its own row's test under the reboot.** The row of 13 October made
   it a debuff because "heat applied straight is so polarising that it needs several avenues
   of counterplay, or it spirals", and counted two answers, the vent and the cleanse. The
   reboot took the vent. Measured on every rank-and-file blow, Burn 1 reads as a third of a
   point on the blow and Burn 2 as a point and a half with one fight in twelve lost: nothing,
   then a cliff. A status that erodes shield and hull on both sides, past armour, is ordinary
   damage, and it is the soft answer to a wall and to a Barrier that both sides lack
   (section 9). Claude's lean of the first round, to keep Burn as heat, is withdrawn.
7. **The foes' riders, in points on the blow.** Breach is two points on every blow. Corrupt 1
   and Crush 1 are each over a point. Disable, Drain and Scorch 1 are nearly free in hull,
   which makes them the riders that change what the player does without costing a fight.
8. **Armour is a threshold, and its meaning is the blow it meets.** The standard blow is 3, 4
   and 5 by tier, so a point of armour is a third of a blow at tier one and a fifth at tier
   three, and the Armoured basic's 2 leaves a third of a tier-one blow. On our guns a point
   of plate takes a third of a vanilla of 3 and half of a vanilla of 2. The rule of 22
   October stands; what the pass adds is sizes, and an answer to plate that is no breach
   (section 6).
9. **The Barrier is worth the blow it meets.** Nothing against a kit of many strikes, three
   quarters of the damage of a kit that is one blow a round. Over the elites it is worth a
   spike half again as hard; its cooldown is a gentle slope; one boss alone stalls on it.
   His read holds: not degenerate (section 11).
10. **Tier one's finds are not on the ladder**, and two decided rows already say what they
    should be: tier zero is tier one's power, and a find in tier-one rifts "is a new trick
    and never a bigger number". Of fourteen slottable tier-one guns eleven deal 4 or more
    (section 13).

## 2. The measure of the player's riders

The method: the six tier ones of the anchor as trios, tier one's ten formations at their
rating, the greedy bot, the reboot on, the trial's basics (a vanilla of 3 for 3 heat). Every
plain basic gun on every die is replaced by one test gun at 3 heat, the Ankle Mortar keeping
its Indirect, and the fights' rounds and hull are read against the same fights with a flat
gun of 1 to 5 in its place. "Reads as a flat 3.5" means the fights ran as they would with a
plain gun between 3 and 4. The reads are drawn from the ten fights' own rows.

Why the first round ran Burn 2 and Penetrate 2 and no 1: those were the smallest numbers
content prints, on the Incendiary Shells, the Blazethrower and the Cottonmouth's drum. That
was the wrong reason for a ladder priced in units. The second round ran the 1s, a second
number for each rider, and the same riders on a gun of 1.

What it cannot say, each a reason the bench and his play come after it:

- It is the greedy bot's play. A rider that needs a second, planned spend reads low: Shock,
  Flak, Expose, a Lure that covers a reboot.
- Every basic gun carries the rider at once, so a squad of nothing but Lures, Disrupts or
  Suppresses is read, which is no squad. Those three are no fair read.
- Formations alone. A duel, an elite and a boss price a rider differently: the big-foe
  riders are the first to gain there.
- A rounds column moves in tenths. A difference under 0.2 rounds or half a point of hull is
  the run's grain.

As heat it is nothing until a gauge is near full and then an overheat, a broken shield and a

wrecks. As erosion it is plain damage and dearer at the same number, since a stack is a
point of shield or hull that armour and a Barrier do not stop: 1 is a point on the blow and
a little over, and 2 is three points over two turns. So the form is safer in kind and its
numbers are damage numbers, smaller than today's. Content as built barely moves, since one
tier-one foe lays it: the ember pyre costs 12.4% of a four's hull for 8.9%.

**His rulings, 15 November.** "Burn - hits shield first, I think. Pretty deadly otherwise.
Bypasses armour when damaging hull." And its word: "Ablate actually works well, I think ...
Ablating or ablate is very clinical, it fits the military notation, and evokes eating though
armour to me." So the form below stands with its first hole settled, and it is Ablate,
laying Ablating, on both sides. It is built with the reboot's fold-in. His fourth round
settled the second hole: "Tick of ablate ignores barrier yes." Still his: its numbers.

**The form.** N stacks. As its side's turn comes round it
loses a point a stack, then a stack fades. On a foe: hit points, past armour, at the start
of its action, as Burning does today. On a machine: shield first, then hull, past armour, as
the player's turn ends, so the player has a turn to answer what the foes laid. A tick is no
blow: a Barrier neither stops it nor is spent on it. A Cleanse lifts it and a Ward turns it,
as today.

**What it does in the system.**

1. Heat is the player's own again. The foes tax it with Scorch, which a hold waits out, and
   never drain it.
2. It is ordinary damage. A shield takes it and a reboot gives the shield back, so the
   reboot is an answer where it swallowed one tick of heat.
3. It passes armour on both sides: the answer to a wall that is no breach (section 6).
4. It passes a Barrier, and a blow's riders already land through one. The row of 17 October
   said a common Barrier would need "more damaging riders to carry past it". This is that
   rider (section 11).
5. One word, one effect, on either side, and the Arsenal's cleanse that moves a machine's
   debuffs onto a foe (12 October) gets a debuff that means the same when it arrives.

**The holes.** Two are ruled and the third is his.

- *Shield first, or hull alone?* Ruled, 15 November: the shield first. It is the same rule
  as on a foe, whose hit points are its one meter, and it lets the reboot answer it. Hull
  alone is a breach over time that no reboot helps.
- *Past the Barrier.* Ruled, 15 November: a tick ignores a Barrier. So neither threshold
  defence answers it. Its answers are the Cleanse, the Ward, the kill and the reboot's
  refill, and tier one deals one Cleanse and one Ward.
- *The clock.* A stack fading a turn climbs under a steady feed, which is why Empowered
  halves. Ours climbs on a foe by design, a burn build's point. On a machine a pack of four
  that feeds it wants numbers of 1 or a low weight on the move.
- *Its numbers.* As damage: 1 on a blow at tier one, the foes' point more that passes plate;
  2 or more as a move of its own, as the Drake's Stoke is, three points over two turns for a
  foe's whole action.
- *Nothing lasting presses the heat clock then.* Scorch, and the player's own spending.
  Claude reads that as right: the heat is the ammunition, and the foes price a shot without
  taking rounds from the magazine.
- *What reads hull damage fires on a tick that reaches the hull*: the Skua's Ricochet halves
  on each. Their machines' sessions.
- *The words to rewrite.* Burning's codex, "Metal does not burn, it heats", and the foes'
  moves that name it.

a general eroding over time, that we could apply to it on both sides". The bench as it was

## 11. The Barrier, unwound

His words: "I'm not sure on the barrier basic. With cooldown 1 it reads as not degenerate to
me, but I'm really not sure how to unwind it from the data." It is his open call of 12
November (the to-do), and his fourth ruling on the reboot gave it its job: "a mechanic for
the player to cheat out another safe turn before rebooting ... Or, to cover it while it
reboots."

What makes it hard to unwind: the elites and the bosses were sized with it on the dice, so
a read without it looks like a broken game and a read with it looks like a solved one. Four
reads take it apart. All are squads of four at the trial's basics under the reboot.

**By what it is worth a spend.** A Barrier takes the blow it meets. In a formation that is
3.7 points a spend, a vanilla shot's worth. Against an elite or a boss it is 9.6.

**By its cooldown, and against no Barrier at all**

| The Barrier basic | An elite: hull, its share of the foes' damage, spends a fight | A boss: hull, share, spends, wrecks |
|---|---|---|
| No cooldown | 8.8%, 57%, 3.5 | 23.1%, 56%, 7.8, 0.19 |
| Sits out a round, as built | 11.1%, 48%, 2.7 | 26.5%, 46%, 5.2, 0.22 |
| Sits out two | 13.0%, 41%, 2.0 | 31.0%, 34%, 3.3, 0.28 |
| As built, at 3 heat | 12.7%, 45%, 2.5 | 31.7%, 39%, 4.6, 0.31 |
| Off the dice, the Armoured basic in its slot | 24.6%, none | 46.8%, none, 0.90, and 92% won |

The cooldown is a gentle slope, two to four points of hull a round of it. The step is
having one at all. The fights do not lengthen with it: an elite's runs 5.0 to 5.4 rounds on
every row.

**By the spike it is worth**

| The squad, and the foe | An elite: hull, won, wrecks | A boss: hull, won, wrecks |
|---|---|---|
| With the Barrier basic, as built | 11.1%, 100%, 0.03 | 26.5%, 99%, 0.22 |
| With it, the foe's blows half again as hard | 24.4%, 97%, 0.40 | 60.0%, 73%, 1.63 |
| With it, the blows doubled | 34.3%, 92%, 0.81 | 76.4%, 46%, 2.68 |
| Without it, as built | 24.6%, 100%, 0.23 | 46.8%, 92%, 0.90 |
| Without it, the blows halved | 6.0%, 100%, 0.00 | 9.7%, 100%, 0.00 |

Against an elite the Barrier basic is worth blows half again as hard: a squad with it pays
for those what a squad without it pays for today's. Against a boss it is worth less, a
quarter to a third, since more of a boss's damage comes in many blows.

**By the fight.** Its share of the foes' damage, and the hull with it and without:

| The fight | A Barrier's share | Hull with it | Hull without |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Thunder Ape, three strikes a move | 34% | 12.5% | 11.9% |
| The Fiend of Fire, blows at every machine | 19% | 39.3% | 39.1% |
| Floraison | 13% | 23.0% | 24.5% |
| The Shaman's warband | 14% | 13.6% | 18.5% |
| The Wild Hunt | 30% | 17.1% | 27.3% |
| Gilgamesh | 57% | 10.3% | 24.8% |
| The Old Man of the Desert | 58% | 15.0% | 41.1% |

So the Barrier is no flat discount. It is worth nothing against a kit of many strikes or of
blows at every machine, and three quarters of a kit that is one blow a round. One fight
stalls on it, the Gargantuan King's, twice as long with it as without.

**The reading.** His read holds: with its cooldown it is not degenerate. It lengthens no
fight but one, its cooldown is no cliff, and in a formation it is worth a vanilla shot.
What the data shows as a hole is on the foes' side: a kit that is one blow a round is three
quarters turned by one basic. And the budget squares in an unexpected place. His budget for
an elite's fight is a quarter of a four's hull. Today's elites cost that without a Barrier
and half of it with one. So the elites are sized for a squad with none, and the Barrier is
an edge worth half an elite, which is his "sub for a Tank" of 12 November.

**His lean, 15 November**: "I don't want the existence of barrier as an accessible-to-all
tool, to justify the existence of massive enemy strikes. That's my main concern, degenerate
or not. I think that's why I lean towards not having it as a basic." It answers the leans
below from the other side, and section 16 reads what would stand in its slot. A debug
build's TRIALS has had it playable since 13 November: BARRIER BASIC on NONE.

**Claude's leans of the second round**, the first of which his lean replaces:

1. Leave the Barrier basic as built, cooldown and all, and do not size the spike against
   it: a squad without one is then on budget and a squad with one is ahead.
2. Mend the one-blow kits in the foes' pass. A blow's riders land through a Barrier
   already, so a spike that carries one is never turned whole, and the eroding status of
   section 9 is the rider that carries damage past it. A second, smaller blow does the
   same.
3. The Gargantuan King's stall is its own fix, with the bosses' numbers.
4. A vanilla shot's heat on it is a fair price for a player and changes little: 12.7% for
   11.1% against an elite. It is not needed.

## 14. The defence side: an approach

His question: "How are we going to approach enemy kits and damage in relation to the
various shield and hull values we have? Since our shield and hull values are, bluntly,
pretty completely random, so there's no reason to keep those locked either."

The same way as the guns, mirrored. On the offence side the foe was fixed first and the gun
measured against it. On the defence side the machine is fixed first and the foes' blows
measured against it. One thing does not mirror, and it shapes the rest: a gun's damage past
a kill is wasted, so a gun is read in thresholds, shots to kill. A blow's damage past a
shield reaches the hull, so a shield is a pool, read in totals. The thresholds on the
defence side are the Barrier, which eats one blow whatever its size, and armour, which takes
a point off every blow (sections 6 and 11).

1. *Three clocks, three units.* The round's: a Barrier is one blow, Armoured a point a blow.
   The cycle's, from one reboot to the next: the shield and the heat. The battle's: the hull,
   by his budget of 9 November (a formation's fight a twentieth of the squad's hull at its
   rating, a flanker's a thirtieth, a hunter's a tenth, an elite's a quarter, the boss what
   is left).
2. *A shield is counted in standard blows*, as a foe is counted in shots. The standard blow
   is the defence side's first number: 3, 4 and 5 by tier today (section 6). The shields do
   not grow with it, so a shield is two to four blows at tier one and two to three at tier
   three.
3. *A fight is counted in cycles*: the foes' damage over the squad's shields. The reads of
   the trial give it today's values: a formation lands 26 damage on a trio whose shields sum
   to 18 to 29; an elite lands 53 and a boss 107 on a four whose shields sum to 26 to 35. So
   a formation is one cycle, an elite nearly two and a boss three to four, and the kinds of
   fight are already a ladder in that unit. Each cycle past the first is bought with a
   reboot, turned by a Barrier, or paid in hull.
4. *Two clocks, one reset.* A machine's heat runs out in its maximum over its guns' heat,
   and its shield in its size over the blows it draws. The reboot resets both. If one always
   runs out first the other gauge is decoration: a duel today does not read heat at all,
   since cooler guns leave it as long and as dear. A formation has them about level: its 4.7
   rounds are a gauge of four vanilla shots and a little over, and its damage is the squad's
   shields once over. So a standard machine's two clocks should run out within a round or
   two of each other at its fights' rating, and a role is which one it leans on.
5. *A point on every blow is the foes' unit*, four to seven points of a four's hull in a
   formation (section 8), and every rider has a price in it.
6. *A hull has two jobs.* It is the battle's pool, and a quarter of it is the blow that
   destroys a component: a 5 on a hull of 20, a 7 on 28, a 10 on 40. So a hull's number says
   which foes can break a die, and the rank and file's 3 and 4 break none today.
7. *The standard machine.* A handful of shapes in those units, by role, in place of the
   numbers as they fell: a shield of two, three or four blows; a hull by the budget and by
   the blow that should break its die; plate for the machine that means to be hit.
8. *A foe's kit is which clock it presses, or which it goes round.* The swarm presses the
   shield of the whole squad, and armour is its answer. The spike presses one machine's, and
   the Barrier is its answer. Scorch presses the heat. A breach goes round the shield and
   the plate today, and section 17 asks whether the foes keep it. The eroding status goes
   round the plate and the Barrier and not the shield. An Exposed turns the whole field on
   one machine with its plate off. A
   blow at every machine catches the reboot. The heat aim and the exposed aim find the
   machine a reboot is due on. A disable, a corrupted entry and a drained reroll attack the
   die. A domain is two or three of these, and its standard foe.
9. *A model first, then dials.* `tools/shield_model.py` already runs the reboot against the
   foes' shapes; it takes the units above and prices each kit in hull past the swarm's.
   Then role shapes and kits as a table in chat, dials for the shields, the hull and the
   blows, the bot at the anchor, and his play.

### Exposed, on a machine: the rule as ruled

Decided, not built. It rides with the vocabulary's build.

1. *Who lays it.* A foe of tier two or above, "only in formations that feel thematic and
   fair, so that the player has tools to answer by that point". None at tier one.
2. *Announced.* The move that lays it is a targeted intent like any. A Lure moves it to
   another machine ("You can also lure the initial Exposed rider like anything else"), and a
   Ward turns it away as it turns every rider.
3. *As it lands.* The machine's armour counts for nothing, at once and until the end of the
   next round: "all armour of all kinds", its own, any Armoured, and what a kit grants by a
   condition. Armour gained while Exposed is gained, so what triggers on the gain fires, and
   it does nothing until the mark ends. The shield is untouched, and a reduction that is no
   armour stays: "Exposed does not strip the petrel's resistant shield", its Riot Field's
   point off every blow while the shield stands.
4. *The next round.* As it opens every targeted intent turns on the Exposed machine,
   whatever each foe's aim, until the blows already drawn would wreck it. From there the
   rest pick as they would. So it is the whole field and never an overkill: "keep the logic
   where enemies don't try and overkill. That helps keep it fair. So the whole field targets
   up until predicted death, then the rest pick intent targets as normal." A foe whose move
   aims at no machine is not made to strike. His reasons for the whole field: it lives where
   the player has tools; "for a tank, it's too much of a good thing, which is the point";
   and "not all foes will attack every round".
5. *After the intents.* "Exposed sets the intents, everything after proceeds as normal." A
   Lure laid in the player's turn pulls a blow off it as off any machine, "Lure wins", and a
   foe that picks again after the opening picks by its own aim.
6. *A Cleanse* gives the armour back and leaves the intents, chosen already.
7. *One at a time.* "Enemy units should be smart enough to only try to Expose one player
   unit at a time. We can set them up for success with their formations and kits." It is a
   rule of announcing, the drain's kind and built the same way: no foe announces an Expose
   while another's is out.

Still his, one point. May a machine that stands Exposed be marked again? Two foes that lay
it in turn would hold the whole field on one machine every round. Claude's lean: no foe
announces it while a machine stands Exposed either, so the fire comes every other round at
most, and that is "one at a time" read over rounds as well as over machines.

For the build, two notes. The aim that content calls exposed, shown as "aims at the
exposed", is no mark and wants another name once the word means one. And the
player's Exposed already counts a foe's armour for nothing, its own and any Armoured, so
the two sides share the armour rule whole and differ in the second half alone: every
machine's Direct reach on a foe, every foe's targeted intent on a machine.

twice; a foe that reads the reboot, since the turn it is announced is the turn to reboot
and the turn the fire comes is the worst; and work for the Lure and the Ward.

### Where a piece that ignores armour may sit

His fifth round: "the player armour piercing pieces should be higher tier, obviously, or
gated, I think. Else they become obvious best faces for certain domains, right?"

Right, and the pass had measured it. Armour is flat, so ignoring it is worth the plate on
every strike, whatever the strike's size:

| The piece | Against plate 1: lands, and with the property | Against plate 2 |
|---|---|---|
| A vanilla of 3 | 2, and 3 | 1, and 3 |
| 2 twice | 2, and 4 | nothing, and 4 |
| 1 four times, the Talons' shape | nothing, and 4 | nothing, and 4 |
| One shot of 8, the Railgun's shape | 7, and 8 | 6, and 8 |

On the marks of section 6, eight foes of 3 behind a point of plate, the vanilla cost 13.6%
of a four's hull, the gun that deals one more 6.9%, and the vanilla with the property 6.4%.
So on a small gun it is the whole gun wherever a domain is plated, and at the point of heat
section 4 priced it at it beats the next rung of the ladder. That is no decision, and his
rule follows.

What it reaches today:

- *The Ammit's Soulcutter Talons*: its own fixed piece, at tier three, and four strikes of 1
  that are nothing without it. Already both.
- *The Wild Boar's charge*: at tier two, and only after it gains a Barrier. Already both.
- *The Overclocked Railgun*: a tier-one find any machine may slot. The one piece the rule
  moves.

Two things Claude adds.

1. *A gate does more than a tier.* Plate grows with the tiers, 2 on the promoted forms and 3
   on the Metal Gel, so the property is worth more up there, and a higher tier alone makes
   it rarer and no less the best face. A kit's own piece or a condition keeps a decision
   beside it. So the firmer reading of his rule is "a kit's or gated", with the tier as the
   lighter of the two.
2. *The open answer to plate is already size.* A flat point comes off every strike, so one
   big shot loses an eighth where two small ones lose half, and that needs no property. The
   Railgun's 8 is that answer, which is why Claude leans to take the property off it and
   leave it at tier one, where the hot single shot against the split gun is the bench's own
   question. Its place is his, in the component pass, and his sixth round adds a reason of
   its own to move it: "Railgun might need to move up a tier regardless just because there
   won't be room for an even higher damage, even hotter gun."

The rule of thumb it leaves for a new piece: never on an open gun of small or many strikes.

### Whether the foes need Breach

**His call, the fifth round: "Sure, let's cut breach."** What follows is the question and
the reasoning he agreed to, kept for the record. The cut leaves the engine and content with
the fold-in, since its premise is the reboot's, and a debug build's FOE BREACH dial plays
the foes without it now. Thirty-four foes carry it on thirty-seven moves, all but four of
whose texts name the shield, and some two dozen codex entries say "through the shield", so
the cut carries those drafts for his writing.

His question: "My question about enemy breach is if it's even needed. It could be a domain
identity, i.e. shields suck here, but only if it asks an interesting question and not an
unfair one. We've already nerfed shields pretty hard already with the other changes."

Claude's answer: it is not needed, and the lean is to cut the word on both sides.

**What it was for.** It has done three jobs. The first was to reach a hull behind a shield
that mended every round, so that a small hitter mattered. Nothing mends now: every point a
shield takes stays taken until the reboot, so every foe already does that job. The second
was to answer plate. Exposed and Ablate hold it, announced and answerable. The third is to
mark a foe to kill first, and any rider worth fearing does that.

**What each defence meets once it is gone.**

| A machine's defence | What the foes have against it | What the player has back |
|---|---|---|
| The shield | Every blow, since none of it mends. Ablate's ticks. An overheat, which Scorch pushes. The whole field on an Exposed machine | The reboot. A kill. Sap, Suppress, a Lure |
| Armour | Exposed. Ablate, past it on the hull | A Ward, a Lure, a Cleanse, the reboot before the fire |
| A Barrier | Ablate's ticks. Many small blows. A foe's Purge | A Ward or a Cleanse for the ticks. Armour for the small blows |
| A Ward | Nothing: it turns marks, a Purge among them, and never a blow | |

Every defence has a threat, every threat is announced or fades, and nothing goes round the
shield. Breach as built is the one piece that does, and round the plate with it. The day it
goes, "the shield first, always" is a rule with no exception, and the shield is a clock the
player can read.

**The shield form, read as a domain's identity.** A blow that deals double to a shield is a
blow that is bigger while a shield stands and plain once it is gone. Its worst case is
bounded, which is its fairness: a machine may always go without a shield, and then it is a
plain blow that hull and armour meet in full. Its question depends on how many carry it.

- *On every foe of a formation* it is "your shields are half here": a tax, settled once on
  the map and never in the fight.
- *On a few foes that can be killed* it asks two things. Which machine takes it: one whose
  shield is already broken takes it as a plain blow, so a broken shield has a use and the
  Lure a job. And when: the breacher is the first kill while shields stand and the last
  once they are down.
- *On one big blow* it is the binary piece again: a shield gone in a blow, and a Barrier
  its one answer, which is the strike his third round does not want justified.

So his test has a plain form. It is fair when hull and armour still answer it in full. It
is interesting when it changes who takes a blow or when the reboot comes.

**Why Claude still leans to cut it.**

1. The identity needs no word of its own. A domain heavy in Ablate is already one where
   shields erode, with the Cleanse, the Ward and the fade as answers. A domain heavy in
   Scorch already breaks shields by the overheat. "Shields suck here" is a weighting of
   words the game has.
2. The shield is what the reboot buys, and his point stands: no regen, broken at nothing,
   broken by the overheat, Ablate on it first, a field's fire on one. A rider that halves
   what a reboot buys makes the turn it costs a worse trade, on the one answer every
   machine carries. The bot, which does not weigh the trade, reboots more under it.
3. One word fewer on both sides, and an engine path with it.

**On the shelf**, the form that passes the test is narrow: double and no more; on a few
killable rank and file of one domain; never on an elite's or a boss's one blow; never on
every foe of a formation; and at tier two and up, as Exposed is. The Waste carries four of
its lines today, the Marsh one and the Peak three.

**What cutting it costs.** Content as built, the reboot on, the trial's basics, hull as a
share of a four's. The first three columns have the Barrier basic on the dice, the last
three have none.

| The foes' Breach | A formation | An elite | A boss | A formation, no Barrier basic | An elite | A boss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| As built: past shield and armour | 8.1% | 11.1% | 26.5% | 9.2% | 24.6% | 46.8% |
| Double to a shield | 6.8% | 10.8% | 25.8% | 7.5% | 23.4% | 46.0% |
| Cut: a plain blow | 5.7% | 10.6% | 25.6% | 6.4% | 22.0% | 46.5% |

Breach is three tenths of what a tier-one formation costs in hull, a tenth of an elite's
with no Barrier on the dice, and next to none of a boss's, whose blows pass a shield by
their size. Reboots hardly move: 0.64 a machine a formation as built, 0.70 cut, 0.81 at
double. So the bill is the rank and file's. Thirty-four foes lose the rider, and the six
tier-one lines that carry it want their numbers or a mark read again, which is the defence
side's pass (section 14).

## 19. The foes' moves: his question, and what the format forces

His sixth round, 15 November: "the list of foe moves, those aren't a comprehensive list of
all enemy moves, are they? If it is, it's pretty small. Enemy moves don't have to fit into
the same damage+rider+targeting explicit bucketed format as machine components. It kind of
seems like the moves as currently designed are trying to do that - it's like jamming a
square peg into a round hole. There's nothing wrong if we ended to with every enemy special
move having unique text and rules, if it serves the gameplay. That's kind of the point,
really, we're not fighting mechs vs mechs we're fighting mechs versus JRPG enemies, who
cast elaborate spells and stuff."

**The answer.** The table lists the words, not the moves. Content has 284 moves on 128
foes. But what he senses is true of every one of them: each is put together from one small
kit.

| What the 284 moves are | Moves |
|---|---|
| A plain blow | 89 |
| A blow with one tag, from a list of ten | 85 |
| A blow with two tags or more | 16 |
| One special, from a list of twenty-three | 94 |

Of the twenty-three specials five have one carrier: the Magma Lizard's Stoke the Pack, the
Thunder Ape's Golden Rage, the King Gel's Merge, the Djinn's Unmake and the Summoner's
Diamond Dust. Three tags have one: the World Eater's Crush, Gilgamesh's Steal and the
Vampire Lord's Drain. Everything else is shared. The names and the texts are spells. The
rules under them are a gun's.

**What the format forces today.**

1. A move is a blow or one special, never both and never two. A spell cannot strike and
   curse, or heal one foe and summon another. Bad Breath is a blow with five tags because
   tags are the one way to stack effects.
2. Tags and specials are closed lists the validator holds, so a move is designed by picking
   from them.
3. A rider rides a blow and nothing else.

**What a move owes the player, whatever it does.** These are decided rows, and they are why
the fights can be planned. His direction frees what a move does and leaves these.

1. It is announced before the player acts, with its target where it has one.
2. The forecast shows what will land.
3. The team's answers meet it by rules learned once. What it lays on a machine is a mark: a
   Ward turns it and a Cleanse lifts it. What it deals is a blow: the shield, the armour
   and a Barrier meet it. What is aimed, a Lure moves. A kill, a Suppress or a Disrupt
   stops the move, unless the foe's plaque says it is immune.
4. It reads its own side for its conditions, never the player's.

So the freedom is in the effect and the discipline is in the sockets. A spell that Wards do
not turn and Cleanses do not lift, case by case, would be Breach again.

**The marks stay shared.** A JRPG's elaborate spell is most often the shared ailments in a
new bundle, on new targets, at a new time. Bad Breath is the model, four marks on every
machine at once. So this pass's work stands under his direction: the statuses are the
words both sides share, and a spell is free in how it deals them.

**What opens it.** A move becomes a short list of steps where it is one effect today. A
step is a shared one, a blow, a mark on a set of machines, a recovery, a summon, a state on
itself, or a rule of its own. The intent shows the move's name, its largest number and its
marks; a tap gives its text; the forecast walks the steps. Composing shared steps costs
nearly nothing once that is built. A rule of its own costs its forecast, its chip, the
bot's read and its tests, named for planning and no argument against.

**The budget is reading, not code.** Eight intents stand on a phone at once. His own marks
of this round say it twice: Countdown "causes a lot of mental overhead", and Follow-up need
not be spelled out. So the test for a unique move is whether the player must read it to
play the turn, and whether it is worth the read. Claude's lean on where to spend it: the
foe a formation is built round, the elites, the heroes and the bosses, with the rank and
file's blows left as plain numbers so the field reads at a glance. Three goblins and a
shaman, and the shaman's spell is the fight's question.

**What it does to this pass.** The foes' half of the table stands as the marks and the
shared buffs. "A rider on a blow" stops being the unit a foe is designed in: a foe's moves
come from its fantasy first, and the runner prices them after, since it plays whatever the
engine runs. Section 14's units, the standard blow and the clock a kit presses, stay as the
measure of what a kit costs the player and are no menu to build from.

### What the table shows

**1. A, B and C hold one moment under three names.** A machine's Armoured ends as the round
opens and its Barrier and Ward as the round before it ends. Nothing happens between the two.
A foe's Armoured ends after END TURN and its Barrier and Ward at END TURN: again one moment.
The standard is his phrase for Armoured, on both sides: **until its next turn begins**, for
Armoured, Barrier and Ward. Nothing changes in play, and the three become one clock in the
engine.

**2. Halving wants one sentence: it halves as the turn it works in ends.** Empowered works
in its carrier's own turn and now halves there. Brittle works in the other side's turn: a
machine's is read by the foes, and halves as their turn ends, which fits. A foe's is read by
the player and still waits for the round's end. Moving it to END TURN changes one case: Brittle
laid on a foe in the enemy's turn, by a Mine's blast as a mined foe falls to its Ablating,
would stand whole for the player's next turn, where today it is half before any machine can
use it. That is the fault he fixed for Empowered.

**3. A machine's stacks tick at two moments.** Its Ablating bites at END TURN and its
Regenerating pays as the round opens; a foe's both tick at its action, Ablating first. One
moment a side would put Regenerating at END TURN, after Ablating. It is not free: the shield
would arrive before the blows of the round it was bought in, a round sooner than today, and
the one piece that grants it draws fire as it does (the Storm Petrel's St. Elmo's
Regenerator, a Lure). Today a shield the lured blows break takes none of its regen at all.
Left as it is, the rule to say is that harm ticks as the turn ends, with a turn to answer
it, and help as the turn opens.

**4. A tax on its next turn says three things.** On a machine, Scorched lasts "until the end
of the player's turn", Sapped "until after its next turn" and Jammed "through its next turn".
On a foe, Sapped lasts "after its next action" and Suppressed "one action". All five are
**until its next turn ends**, a foe's turn being its own action. Nothing changes in play;
Jammed's engine end would move to END TURN to match.

**5. Two smaller ones.** Numbed loses a stack as the round ends where every other stack on a
foe ticks at its action; either reads the same in the player's turn. Exposed and Phased both
say "the next round's end" and cover different lengths: Exposed the rest of the player's turn
and the whole of the next, Phased one round. Each is right for its own rule.

**The standard, if he takes all four.** Five clocks would cover every timed status: until
its next turn begins (defence), until its next turn ends (a tax on its own turn), halves as
the turn it works in ends, a stack each time its turn comes, and until spent. What is left
is the round's own rules (Phased and its wait, the pins, Essence Flare, Exposed), the fight,
and timers that count rounds. Claude's lean: take 1, 2 and 4, which cost nothing in play but
the one Mine case, and rule on 3 with the Storm Petrel's piece in hand.
