# The Arsenal's new chassis

A pitch of 11 October 2026 on Ryan's call that day (more Arsenal chassis: medieval, demonic,
eldritch, sinister), revised 12 October on his notes and against the live bible (v0.119, which
other sessions had moved that day): Misharu and the Onryō leave Overcharged for archetypes of
their own, Mastema loses its cauldron, Minos turns every foe it reaches. The frame those notes
settled is in the bible (v0.120, sections 1 and 7). Everything below is still a proposal:
names, numbers, kit and looks are Ryan's to move before any of it lands in `content/`, and the
holes in section 3 are for discussion first (CLAUDE.md).

## 1. The frame, as decided

- **The occult is the Council's.** The domains moved the invasion to the genre's high fantasy,
  so the demonic and the eldritch belong to the Council and its Arsenal alone: the one place the
  player meets the dark.
- **Twisted, never merged.** Each Arsenal machine is a battlemech with a sealed occult power
  source where a fusion reactor would be, drawn by an engineer who has had things whispering in
  his ear. Mechanical only, no flesh and no gore. It may borrow another line's frame, twisted;
  it never has a mind of its own, and CARE pilots fly it. Grimdark is the Council's and the
  Arsenal's, to be taken as ironic dark humour; Evangelion's demon-mecha is too far.
- **Loud in look, liturgical in word.** Demonic in look and effect, liturgical in what the
  player reads: the manual never says demon. Names keep the through-line, figures who punish
  from any culture, and Hell is what a player finds behind them (the 11 October row: what the
  Council names a machine and what it manifests as can differ). Shown names stay sanctified on
  the face.
- **A passive may break a rule as a one-off.** That is the point of one.
- **Two lines the bible drew the same day.** Retaliation is a debuff, never thorns: the 10 a
  Shocked striker paid went as "thorns left in". And a damage bonus against a striker would be
  a third copy of Shocked (the Thurible's and the Armadillo's), as the Nemesis's open signature
  row says. So Misharu punishes with a stun and the Onryō repays through a weapon of its own,
  neither by damage on a blow.

**One for each word, one for each domain.** Each machine leads with one of the four words and
answers one domain's question, so each is the right pick somewhere and a slower fight elsewhere
(mismatch costs time, never ability). None of the four scales on Overcharged. Tier is
complexity (bible section 10): a sharper passive and more locked pieces, never more power.

| Word | Machine | Tier, size, moves | Its idea | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medieval | **Misharu**, the judge | 2, heavy, walker | A foe that breaks a shield loses its next action | The Peak: shields for the telegraph |
| Demonic | **Mastema**, the gargoyle | 2, heavy, flyer | Fire passes from the dead to their neighbours | The Meadow: numbers that swarm |
| Eldritch | **Minos**, the corrupted frame | 3, medium, hover | Every foe it reaches changes rank | The Marsh: the healer behind |
| Sinister | **Onryō**, the crawler | 3, medium, walker | It takes its allies' wounds and repays every striker at once | The Waste: damage no shield answers |

With the Nemesis and the Ammit that makes six, three at tier two and three at tier three: a
walker that judges, the Arsenal's first large aircraft, its first hover on legs, and a crawler.

## 2. The four

Numbers are placeholders in the content's scale (player damage in fives; hull, shield and heat
in tens). Stock is drawn from today's pool and is provisional until the stock pass places the
basics. Codex and flavour are Ryan's. Every passive below needs engine work (section 4).

### Misharu, Arsenal Instrument II (medieval: justice)

- **Name.** Mīšaru, the Mesopotamian god of justice and equity, who stands beside Shamash, the
  god who handed Hammurabi his laws. Alternates: **Víðarr**, the silent Norse avenger;
  **Zhong Kui**, the demon-queller of Tang legend.
- **The look.** A gothic knight, too tall and too narrow at the waist, fluted black chrome with
  red glass in the seams; a flat-topped great helm with a T-shaped slit lit red (a sensor head;
  the cockpit rides in the chest); poulaines; a pavise as tall as its torso on the left forearm,
  ridged and carrying the Council's seal; a halberd taller than the machine. Mounts: hp_1 the
  right pauldron, hp_2 the left shoulder behind the pavise's top edge, hp_3 a reliquary on the
  back. Character: at vigil (leans on the pavise, salutes with the halberd, kneels and rises,
  turns the helm slowly).
- **Passive: Assize Relay.** *A foe whose blow breaks a machine's shield is Suppressed: the
  sinner loses its next action.* (The assizes were the travelling courts of medieval England; on
  reflection, the Bloody Assizes. The punishment is the pillory's: held fast for a turn.) Any
  machine's shield counts, Misharu's own included. The foe has acted when it breaks the shield,
  so the suppress costs its next action, shown on its chip through the player's turn. Numbed
  resists it as it resists any suppress, so a boss that keeps breaking shields shrugs more of
  them off: the slope says spread the bait.
- **Why the shield.** Of Ryan's three triggers, a shield breaking is the one the player can
  build toward: shields break most fights, every maker makes them, and a standing shield blocks
  a whole blow, so the moment it goes is already the moment that matters. A blow that would
  have wrecked an ally is rare and comes too late (and the Badger's open identity row already
  holds "once a battle, it stands at 1 hull"); a component breaking is rare and rides the
  injury roll, which is shelved for its own session.
- **Why a stun.** It is the punishing debuff the game already has, so it adds no chip and no
  rule a player must learn. A sentence in damage (the shield it broke, owed at its next action)
  was the first draft, and it is thorns by the 12 October line.
- **The softer alternative.** Penance, a new debuff: its blows deal 5 less a stack, never below
  5, a stack fading at each of its turns. Mitigation the game does not have yet, at the price of
  a new chip; the floor keeps a swarm's 5s from going to nothing, the toxicity the Paradox row
  warns of.
- **Fixed.** The **Vigil Pavise**: shield 30, self only, Overshield, the only self overshield in
  the game, so its own shield can stand high in front of a telegraphed blow. The **Interdict
  Halberd**: Direct, flat 10, heat 5, Penetrate (tags Blade, Melee). An interdict is the
  Church's sentence; interdiction is fire behind the lines.
- **Stock, three hardpoints.** Oblation Ward, Proximity Klaxon, Plasma Arquebus: a big shield
  for an ally, a lure that brings the blows onto the pavise, a gun.
- **Numbers.** Hull 120, shield 40, move 20, heat 80, cooling 5, armour 10, field 20, two
  modules. A die of five.
- **Build space.** Every shield on the team is bait: small fresh ones in front of the heavy
  hitters' telegraphs, so the hitter breaks it and stands idle next round; ally shields from
  anyone (the Oblation Ward, the Puffin's overshield, the Halo Shield, the Laminar Deflector,
  which gives the turn back); modules that stand shield up (the Spall Liner, the Boundary-layer
  Bleed). The judgement comes from the team's shields, so Misharu earns its place in a shield
  team, and the lure on its own die makes it the bait when no one else is.
- **Its cost.** Slow, little damage of its own, nothing to judge while shields hold, and the
  Numbed slope against a boss.
- **Answers the Peak.** A small fresh shield before the golem's fist: the fist lands on nothing
  and the golem stands idle next round.

### Mastema, Arsenal Instrument XI (demonic: everything burns)

- **Name.** Mastema, the chief of the spirits in the Book of Jubilees, who keeps a tenth of them
  under him because great is the wickedness of men: God's executioner with demons at his
  command. Alternate: **Alastor**, the Greek avenging spirit that demonology made Hell's chief
  executioner.
- **The look.** A creepy hovering thing: a hunched torso and a horned head with a long jaw, two
  broad bat wings of black chrome panels ribbed in red glass that tilt and vector and never
  flap, two long arms ending in claws, and nothing below the ribs but a tapering keel: no legs,
  no feet. Boosters under the wing roots, each exhaust one glow, pale at the centre and deep
  red at the rim. The homage: the Stryge, Viollet-le-Duc's chimera on Notre-Dame, off its
  perch. Mounts: hp_1 and hp_2 on the wing roots, hp_3 and hp_4 a pair on the shoulders.
- **Character: a thing on a wire.** It rides the flyer's slow swell and hangs too still between
  acts. Acts: spreads its wings and holds; flexes its claws, embers spitting from the
  forearms; wraps its wings round itself like a cloak, then opens; tilts its head down along
  its arms. Under way: heavy and wide, banking, the wings vectoring, the arms trailing.
- **Passive: Brimstone Bellows.** *While it fights, a Burning foe that dies passes its Burning
  to the foes beside it.* One engine piece: a death handing its stacks to its neighbours in the
  rank (the row, Spread's shape).
- **Fixed.** No cauldron. The seed moves into the arms: the **Aspergillum**, Indirect, flat 10,
  heat 15, Burn and Spread (tag Flame), sprayers in the forearms with no prop to hold. An
  aspergillum sprinkles holy water over the congregation, and "holy water sprinkler" was an old
  nickname for a spiked mace; this one sprinkles a rank with fire. Danger close is retiring
  (decided 11 October, not built), so the front rank will cost it nothing. The other way: no fixed part at all, five
  hardpoints, the burn left to the stock and the team.
- **Stock, four hardpoints.** Meteor Artillery, Blazethrower, Incendiary Shells, Entropy
  Condenser (now a volatile vent of 40): three burners and a vent that bleeds it.
- **Numbers.** Hull 120, shield 40, move 30 (the slowest flyer), heat 100, cooling 5, armour 0,
  field 20, two modules. A die of five. Tier two: one plain rule and one locked piece.
- **Build space.** Every burn on the team spreads, not only its own: the Stingray's deepened
  stacks, the Aurora Lattice's Primed, the Solar Flare Array, the Incendiary Shells. Kill order
  is the decision: the burning foe in the middle of a rank is worth more dead than the one at
  its end, and a Burning tick that kills spreads too, so a rank can catch in the enemy's turn.
- **Its cost.** Nothing to spread to against a lone boss; hot, unarmoured and slow for a flyer.
- **Answers the Meadow.** A crowded rank of goblins is fuel.

### Minos, Arsenal Instrument XIII (eldritch: the ranks turned)

- **Name.** Minos as Dante drew him at the mouth of the second circle: the judge who hears each
  soul and sends it to its place, which is what this machine does to every foe it touches.
  Alternates: **Gozu**, Ox-Head, the warden of Hell's gate who drags the dead before their
  judge; **Rashnu**, the Zoroastrian judge whose scales weigh the soul at the bridge.
- **The look.** A corrupted Aile Strike, or an Evangelion frame: tall, thin and long-limbed, a
  narrow waist under hunched shoulders; digitigrade legs on hoverjets, clawed feet with the jets
  in the heels; an Aile-style pack on the back with two short swept wings and twin thrusters;
  the V-fin crest grown into two horns, twin eyes in red glass. The corruption is mechanical:
  one wing notched shorter than the other, seams lit red like cracks, the crest bent. The
  Gibbet Hook coils on the right forearm as a chain whip. From above: the pack's two short
  wings and the horned crest. Mounts: hp_1 and hp_2 a pair of pods beside the thrusters, hp_3
  the left forearm (where the Strike carries its shield), hp_4 the right hip.
- **Character: a predator at rest.** Acts: unfolds the pack's wings and flares the thrusters;
  drops into a crouch on its digitigrade legs and rises; uncoils the whip and lets it hang,
  then reels it in; turns its head, the crest catching the light, and holds. Under way: it
  leans into its travel with its legs drawn back, the thrusters flaring at each start, with the
  weight every machine moves with.
- **Passive: Catherine Wheel.** *Every foe its weapons reach and leave standing changes rank,
  trading places with whoever stands there.* Not optional. The wheel is the saint's emblem and
  medieval justice's breaking wheel, and Fortune's wheel turns the first to the last. The
  rules it needs: a foe changes rank once a spend, after the spend resolves, however many
  strikes reached it; Spread turns three columns; Penetrate reaches both ranks of a column, so
  the two change together and trade places; a lone foe steps into the empty slot; a massive
  foe fills its column and never moves; intents and targets go with the foes. It breaks "the
  ranks hold" for one machine, which is the point.
- **Fixed.** The **Gibbet Hook**: Direct, flat 10, heat 5 (tag Melee), a chain whip. Struck
  with it, a front-rank foe is cast back and the foe behind it comes forward.
- **Stock, four hardpoints.** Suppression Battery, Shotfire Rack, Plasma Arquebus, Entropy
  Condenser: Indirect fire reaches the back rank and brings it forward stunned or wounded, a gun
  for what arrives, a vent.
- **Numbers.** Hull 90, shield 40, move 30, heat 80, cooling 5, armour 0, field 25, two
  modules. A die of five. Tier three: its passive asks the most of the whole team.
- **What the team works around.** Minos's turn is the round's hinge. First, it brings the
  healer, the caster or the Drake into the front rank's fire for every Direct gun on the team;
  last, it sends a wounded front-liner behind its fellows, out of their reach. Spillover finds a
  different foe behind, the front rank acts first so the order of the enemy's turn changes, and
  a back slot it empties fills from the reinforcements at the round's end. It shelters what it
  sends back as surely as it exposes what it brings forward.
- **Its cost.** Little damage of its own, nothing to turn against a boss, and a turn misplayed
  undoes the team's.
- **Answers the Marsh.** The White Mage and the Ghost come forward into the guns.

### Onryō, Arsenal Instrument IX (sinister: retribution)

- **Name.** The onryō, the Japanese spirit that comes back to avenge a wrong. Alternate:
  **Hannya**, the Noh mask of a woman turned demon by jealousy and resentment. Spelling: the
  Rajdhani and Barlow faces carry ō and Share Tech Mono does not, so content may want Onryo.
- **The look.** A long thin body slung low between four long limbs, every joint bending the
  wrong way for a person; a smooth mask with two red slits for a sensor head (the cockpit rides
  in the body) under a fall of black cable hanging forward like hair; along its spine a row of
  pipes like a church organ's, the Requiem Pipes; the Misericorde folded along the right
  forearm, katar-style. A knowing homage to the crawling ghosts of Japanese horror (The Grudge
  above all), the shape and the move. Mounts: hp_1 and hp_2 on the shoulders, hp_3 at the base of
  the spine, all pointing forward. Character: still, then sudden (twitches its mask, rears and
  drops, drags itself a step on its arms, sounds a low note through its pipes); under way, an
  offbeat crawl with a pause every few steps.
- **Passive: Martyrology Engine.** *It takes the hull damage foes deal its allies, straight to
  its own hull, and every striker carries a Grudge of what it dealt.* A martyrology is the
  Church's register of martyrs and their sufferings: it takes the wounds and keeps the record of
  who owes them. The ally's shield and armour come first, and only what would reach its hull
  moves; the moved wound lands past the Onryō's shield and armour and never injures, as a bite
  does. Grudge is a new debuff, a chip with its number on the striker; grudges on one foe add up.
- **Fixed.** The **Requiem Pipes**: Indirect, heat 10. *Strikes every foe carrying a Grudge, in
  any rank, each for its Grudge, and spends them.* Its number is the debt, so it shows a
  different number over each foe and nothing over a foe that owes nothing. The **Misericorde**:
  Direct, flat 10, heat 5, Rapid (tags Blade, Melee), the mercy dagger for the stroke that ends
  a wounded knight, its own blade when no one owes.
- **Why a weapon of its own.** Ryan asked what the taken wounds are for. Repaid as a bonus on
  the Onryō's next blow they would be the third copy of Shocked; repaid automatically they would
  be thorns. The Pipes repay exactly what was dealt, to everyone who dealt it, when the player
  chooses: a debt, called in. The passive and the Pipes are a pair, the way the Badger's claw
  fills the hopper its fragmenter fires (locked pieces may work together).
- **Stock, three hardpoints.** Meteor Artillery, Plasma Arquebus, Laminar Deflector: Indirect
  fire, a gun, a shield against the blows aimed at the Onryō itself.
- **Numbers.** Hull 150, shield 20, move 30, heat 60, cooling 5, armour 0, field 20, two
  modules. A die of five. Medium: it carries the team's wounds. Tier three: the passive changes
  how the whole team takes damage, and two locked pieces work together.
- **Build space.** Its hull is the team's: plating modules (Ablative Plating, the Ram Frame) are
  worth more on it than anywhere. Its allies change: a glass cannon can stand where it likes,
  and a lure on a tough ally draws the blows whose debts it will call in. Timing is the
  decision: fire the Pipes now at what is owed, or let the debts grow against a boss, while its
  own hull runs down.
- **Its cost.** Every wound it saves comes off its own hull, so it wrecks when the wounds outrun
  it, and it starves the allies whose passives read hull damage (the Nemesis's Crucible, the
  Grievance Reliquary). Half the wound is the lever if it proves too much.
- **Answers the Waste.** The Needlers' breaches go past every shield; it takes them, and every
  Needler that breached owes it.

## 3. Holes for discussion

1. **Minos's turn, written out.** Section 2 settles what Ryan's note leaves open: once a spend,
   after it resolves; Spread, Penetrate, a lone foe, a massive one. Check those calls before the
   engine has them.
2. **A crowd round the striker.** The Retribution Thurible and the Armadillo's live hull Shock a
   striker, the Nemesis's open signature once marked one, and now Misharu stuns a shield's
   breaker and the Onryō bills a hull's striker. The Arsenal's theme is retribution, so a family
   of them may be right; each pays out differently (a doubled blow for the team, a lost action, a
   debt called in), and that difference is what has to hold.
3. **Two Gundams.** Aphelion's next chassis is open as a Gundam homage, never too on point
   (bible section 7). Minos as its corrupted twin, the clean frame and the Council's, is a pair
   worth making on purpose; otherwise one of them moves off Gundam.
4. **Hands.** Mastema's claws and Minos's whip hand make two more chassis with hands, which is
   what the open hand question (bible section 7) says it needs to pay.
5. **The interpose, unparked.** The bible parks a draw by an ally until a support role needs
   one, and Guard was built and taken out on 9 October. The Onryō is that role, but it moves only
   the hull damage after the ally's own shield and armour: the foes' aims, the intents and the
   forecast stay as they are. The Warrior's planned Cover is the same piece on the foe side.
6. **An ally that cannot be injured.** While the Onryō stands, its allies take no hull damage
   from blows, so no injuries. Strong on purpose; the half-wound lever and its own hull are the
   answers, and the injury session may change what an injury is anyway.
7. **One new chip.** Grudge carries a number on a foe's chip row beside Burning and Paradox.
   Misharu's Suppressed adds none; Penance, if it replaces it, would add a second.
8. **Mastema's seed.** The Aspergillum keeps a guaranteed burn in its arms; no fixed part leaves
   five hardpoints and a passive that idles without burners on the team.
9. **Minos hovers.** Hover keeps the Arsenal's ways of moving apart (Mastema flies) and keeps
   its legs on hoverjets; the winged pack could make it a flyer.
10. **Hellfire.** Free now that no demon faction wears it, but shown names stay liturgical and
    the Summoner's move is called Hellfire: kept for what a machine manifests as, off its names,
    or dropped from the word list? (Bible section 12.)
11. **The Arsenal's shelf.** Six machines over six Arsenal components (the Plasma Arquebus,
    Blazethrower, Meteor Artillery, Entropy Condenser, Oblation Ward and Retribution Thurible,
    with the Gravity Knuckles to come as a blueprint) leaves most of their stock borrowed.

## 4. What it costs

- **Content.** Four chassis and six fixed components, the passives marked planned until the
  engine runs them, so the machines run without their passives as soon as they land.
- **Engine, in rising size.** A foe's blow that breaks a shield suppresses its striker (small:
  Misharu; the suppress already exists). Burning passed on at a death (small: Mastema). An ally's
  hull damage moved to the Onryō, shared with the Warrior's Cover; Grudge; the Pipes' strike on
  every debtor at its own number (medium). The turn: slot moves after a spend, its forecast and
  drawing, the save, and the greedy bot, which reads one spend's trade and must learn the
  round's order (medium).
- **Models.** Four lowforge functions with joints, four acts each and a gait, each a session or
  more of passes on Ryan's notes (the Nemesis took three). New kinds: a legless winged flyer, and
  digitigrade legs on hoverjets under a winged pack. The crawler is nearest the Ammit's rig.
  Each dies in the Arsenal's flames over a ghost, as built.
- **The runner.** Four new rows, read as diagnostics (a chassis alone is one, bible section 11),
  and an Arsenal team row becomes possible (each maker's first three in content order). The
  Onryō and Misharu read weak alone by design: the Onryō has no allies to cover, and Misharu's
  own shield is the only one to break.

## 6. The bench

Set aside, kept in case one is swapped:

- **The first Misharu**: gaining shield Overcharged it (an alternate Nemesis, Ryan, 12 October).
- **The sentence**: a shield's breaker takes the shield it broke at its next action; thorns by
  the 12 October line.

  indirect blow, Spread and Penetrate, with Shock.
