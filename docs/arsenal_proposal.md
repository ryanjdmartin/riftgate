# The Arsenal's new chassis

Proposals, none of them built. Pitched on 11 October 2026 on Ryan's call (more Arsenal
chassis: medieval, demonic, eldritch, sinister) and worked through with him on 12 October over
four rounds of notes. His call that evening: build nothing yet; a fresh session evaluates every
proposed design as a batch, together with the other makers' proposals. This file is written for
that session: each design in full, Ryan's calls on it, the rules and engine it touches, its
holes, and what the balance runner should read. Names and numbers are placeholders until he
moves them; nothing is in `content/`.

## How to read this

- **Read first**: `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/rules.md` (the skirmish part), then the bible
  (`docs/design-bible.html`, v0.126 or later) where this file cites it: section 1 (tone, the
  world), section 2 (the design tests), section 5 (control and statuses, mitigation and heat),
  section 7 (machines and the die, components, makers and names) and section 10 (tiers).
- **Decided**: the frame in section 2 below, which the bible records. Reopening it takes a
  reason (bible section 2: every rule is open to debate, argued in the open).
- **Proposed**: sections 4 to 8. Ryan's calls are marked as his; everything else is Claude's
  proposal for him to move.
- **Not to pitch again by accident**: the bench, section 11, with the reason each idea went.
- **The other makers' proposals** for the same batch: `docs/status.md` says where each lives.

## 1. The brief and Ryan's calls, in order

- **11 October, the brief.** More Arsenal chassis: medieval, demonic, eldritch, sinister. With
  the changes to the enemy factions (the domains moved the invasion to the genre's high
  fantasy), the demonic, occult and eldritch register is filled by the Council and the Arsenal
  exclusively.
- **12 October, first notes.**
  - The first Misharu (gaining shield Overcharged it) was an alternate Nemesis: the Arsenal needs
    archetypes other than Overcharge scaling. Push the divine personification of justice, with a
    trigger such as a foe's blow that would have killed an ally this round, a component breaking
    or a shield breaking, inflicting a punishing debuff on those it deems sinners.
  - Mastema is the everything-burns archetype, and liked. Ditch the cauldron: a bat-winged flyer
    with arms is plenty of visual, no feet, a creepy hovering flying thing. Fire spreading from
    the dead is more interesting than "every blow burns".
  - Minos, a step further: hitting any unit flips its rank. Not optional; powerful, and the whole
    team has to work around it. A backpack with short wings, digitigrade legs, a corrupted Aile
    Strike Gundam or Evangelion frame.
  - The Onryō, as for Misharu but leaning to retribution: perhaps still the hull-damage trigger,
    but it must open build space; perhaps it absorbs damage dealt to other allies, for something.
  - Mechanical only, no gore or flesh. They have not merged with the occult, they are twisted by
    it: a battlemech powered by a sealed occult source in place of a fusion reactor, its engineer
    hearing things whisper in his ear. CARE pilots fly them; none has a mind of its own, or CARE
    would never let it into its hangars. True Evangelion demon-mecha is too far. The loudness
    take (demonic in look, liturgical in word) is right. Passives can break the rules as a
    one-off: that is the point.
- **12 October, second notes.**
  - Grimdark is playing it straight, for this game.
  - Thorns are dangerous but can be balanced by a drawback of their own, never on a megatank.
    Retribution is the Arsenal's thing; if any retaliation mechanic has to go, his vote is the
    Armadillo's (armoured, not a porcupine; he is thinking about it).
  - Misharu with the suppress: liked, needing lots of testing. If it proves unbalanced or too
    easy to control: hull damage instead, or several procs to a suppress.
  - The Onryō needs refinement. It cannot always take its allies' hull damage; a guard may come
    back conditionally, on a locked component, never as a core mechanic. Grudge is the thematic
    build-around, whatever it turns out to be: perhaps a stacking damage over time on the foes
    that injure the Onryō or the team, so not straight thorns. How does that open build space, or
    is another stacking debuff enough?
  - Minos: hover movement, one flip per spend, everyone hit moves in its column. It is bad
    against massive foes, which is fine, and extraordinarily powerful against formations. Two
    Gundams are fine, since this is the stereotypical dark one and not the hero frame.
  - Shelve Hellfire for now; freed up, it is best used as a component prefix.
- **12 October, third notes.**
  - No popping the Grudge for burst: a different playstyle, one the game has plenty of already,
    and it cheapens the effect. If it needs reliability, it can stay after the machine dies.
  - The rule-text budget is three rules on the chassis's description; components appear in their
    own popup and are not counted with it.
  - An innate area burn on Mastema would be the default best entry and want no upgrades. Better
    more tools, unique utility: a locked component that doubles a target's burn; one that grants
    shield for each burning foe or stack on the board; one that moves burn from an ally to a foe.
- **12 October, fourth notes.** An Arsenal cleanse that moves the applicable debuffs onto a foe
  is interesting, but depends on which terms the two sides even share (section 8). Build
  nothing yet: the proposals documented in full, for a fresh session to evaluate as a batch with
  the other makers'.

## 2. The frame, as decided

Each line is a bible row (sections 1, 5 and 7).

- **The occult is the Council's.** The domains moved the invasion to high fantasy, so the
  demonic and the eldritch belong to the Council and its Arsenal alone: the one place the player
  meets the dark.
- **Twisted, never merged.** Each Arsenal machine is a battlemech with a sealed occult power
  source where a fusion reactor would be, drawn by an engineer who has had things whispering in
  his ear. Mechanical only, no flesh and no gore. It may borrow another line's frame, twisted,
  and still moves with the game's weight; it never has a mind of its own; CARE pilots fly it.
- **Grimdark is playing it straight.** The world is grim and nobody in it winks, though a player
  may take it as dark humour. Never edgy, and a city falling is never funny.
- **Loud in look, liturgical in word.** Demonic in look and effect, liturgical in what the
  player reads: the manual never says demon. Names are figures who punish, from any culture
  (justice, vengeance and retribution, Hell's wardens and avenging spirits among them; never
  war). Component names are sanctified on the face and sinister on reflection. Hellfire, the
  King James Bible's own word, is pinned as a future component prefix.
- **A passive may break a rule, as a one-off.** The rule bends for one machine and holds for
  every other.
- **Retribution is the Arsenal's strongest suit, a lean; thorns need a drawback of their own**
  and never ride a megatank. If a retaliation mechanic must go, the Armadillo's Faraday Hull goes first (open).
- **An interpose comes back only on a locked piece**, never as a core mechanic.
- **The rule-text budget is the description's**: about three rule texts on a chassis's
  description, its passive and its modules. A component's rule shows in its own popup and is not
  counted, so a locked piece may carry a unique rule without spending the machine's three.
- **A chassis's tier is complexity** (a sharper passive, more locked pieces), never power.
- **A chassis's identity must open build space**; a passive that only feeds its own fixed
  components adds nothing. **Locked components may work together.**

## 4. Misharu, Arsenal Instrument II (medieval: justice)

**The idea.** The judge. The enemy's own act convicts it: a foe that breaks a shield has sinned
and loses its next action, so the team baits the heavy hitters with small fresh shields.

**Passive: Assize Relay.** Shown, a draft: *A foe whose blow breaks a machine's shield is
Suppressed: it loses its next action.* (The assizes were the travelling courts of medieval
England; on reflection, the Bloody Assizes.)

- What counts: a foe's blow that takes any machine's standing shield to zero, blocked whole or
  breached, Misharu's own included, while Misharu fights.
- What it does: the striker is Suppressed. It has already acted, so the suppress costs its next
  action (the rule for a suppress landing on a foe that has acted), and its chip shows it
  through the player's next turn.
- Numbed resists it like any suppress, and a landed one adds two Numbed (three on a massive foe),
  so sentencing the same foe again and again stops landing: the brake on locking a boss.
- A foe with several strikes: the strike that breaks the shield convicts it, and its other
  strikes this action still land.
- It counts toward the Baboon's Paradox as a debuff gained.
- Not a trigger: the splash an Indirect spend costs its own firer, a Corrupted entry's bite, a
  move that adds heat. None is a foe's blow.
- **Ryan's call:** liked, to be tested hard. The fallbacks if it proves unbalanced or too easy
  to control: hull damage in its place (the sentence, the shield it broke owed as damage; a thorn
  is allowed with a drawback, and Misharu is no megatank), or several breaks to a suppress (a
  meter in the Paradox's shape).

**Locked pieces.**

| Piece | Kind | Reach | Number | Heat | Keywords | Targets | Tags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vigil Pavise | shield | | 30 | | Overshield | self | |
| Interdict Halberd | attack | Direct | flat 10 | 5 | Penetrate | | Blade, Melee |

The Pavise is the game's only self overshield (the Puffin's Backwash Ionizer reaches allies
only), so Misharu can stand its own bait. The Halberd reaches through a column, front and back.

**Stock, three hardpoints** (provisional until the stock pass places the basics): Oblation
Ward, Proximity Klaxon, Plasma Arquebus.

**The look.** A gothic knight, too tall and too narrow at the waist, fluted black chrome with
red glass in the seams; a flat-topped great helm with a T-shaped slit lit red (a sensor head;
the cockpit rides in the chest); poulaines; a pavise as tall as its torso on the left forearm,
ridged and carrying the Council's seal; a halberd taller than the machine. Mounts: hp_1 the right
pauldron, hp_2 the left shoulder behind the pavise's top edge, hp_3 a reliquary on the back.
Character: at vigil (leans on the pavise, salutes with the halberd, kneels and rises, turns the
helm slowly). A heavy walker's stride.

**Build space.** Every shield on the team is bait. Foes draw their targets as the round opens
and commit to them, so a small fresh shield on the machine a heavy hitter has picked is a
sentence waiting: it blocks the blow whole (a standing shield always does) and convicts the
striker. Ally shields from anyone (the Puffin, the Halo Shield, the Votive Ward), modules that
stand shield up (the Spall Liner, the Boundary-layer Bleed), and the lure on its own die when no
other machine is the bait.

**Holes and risks.**
- Control density: a team that shields whoever each heavy hitter picked could suppress every
  big blow every other round. Numbed's ramp is the only brake; the boss cells say whether it
  holds.
- Size does not matter: a 10 shield in front of a 40 blow blocks it and convicts. A shield
  topped up past the blow is a plain block, so the bait is small on purpose. That is a new
  decision about shields (how much, on whom), and a strong one.
- Suppress is common (the Suppression Battery, the Umbra Inducer, the Megataser Harpoon, the
  Baboon's Paradox); Misharu's is the only one the enemy sets off itself. Suppress is a keyword
  Aphelion leans toward, never its own (Ryan, 13 October: a profile is an identity, never an
  exclusion), so Misharu's needs no twist to justify it; the evaluation reads it on its merits.
- The player must see why a foe is suppressed: a mark on the convicted foe and a log line.

**Engine and tests.** Small. In `_enemy_turn` (`sim/skirmish.gd`), after each `_damage_mech`,
the blow's event already carries `broke_shield`; with Misharu fighting, `_suppress(foe)` (the
striker is in `_acted`, so it costs the next action) and `_foe_gained_status(foe,
"suppressed")`. A passive trigger for it in `sim/effects.gd`, and the vocabulary's word. Tests:
a blocked break and a breached break both convict; a shield left standing does not; Misharu's
own shield counts; no Misharu, no sentence; a multi-strike foe; Numbed resisting; the Paradox
counting it.

**What the runner should read.** The boss cells for a lock (the Treant, the Sandworm, the Bone
Dragon, the Magma Golem), the Peak's cells, team rows beside shielders, and how often shield
entries are spent on Misharu's team. The greedy bot (`sim/policy.gd`, `shield_score`) knows
nothing of the sentence: it must value the striker's lost action when it weighs a breakable
shield, or the runner under-reads Misharu.

## 5. Mastema, Arsenal Instrument XI (demonic: everything burns)

**The idea.** Fire that passes from the dead to the living, and two tools that work the fire
rather than light it: one deepens it on one foe, one draws on how many burn.

**Passive: Brimstone Bellows.** Shown, a draft: *While it fights, a Burning foe that dies passes
its Burning to the foes beside it.*

- Any death of a Burning foe counts, however it comes: a team blow, its own Burning tick,
  Spillover, a Chain link, a Bomb's burst, a foe that will get back up.
- The stacks it carried as it died pass whole to each foe beside it, not split between them.
  Beside is Spread's shape: the columns either side, in its rank. A massive foe's neighbours are
  the front-rank foes in the columns either side, as Spread finds them.
- A foe killed by the same blow receives nothing.
- In the enemy's turn the fire travels: a foe that dies of its tick at its action passes its
  stacks on at once, and a neighbour later in the order ticks with them, so a rank can catch and
  burn down within one enemy turn.
- Each neighbour that gains Burning counts toward the Paradox.
- **Ryan's calls:** the everything-burns archetype, liked; spreading from the dead over "every
  blow burns".

**Locked pieces: tools, not a gun.** Ryan's third notes: an innate area burn would be the die's
best entry and want no upgrades; give it more tools of unique utility. Neither piece deals
damage, and each is a blank until the team has burn down, so the burners live in the
hardpoints, where the upgrades go.

| Piece | Kind | Reach | Number | Heat | Its rule | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chrism Ewer | attack | Indirect | none | 10 | *Doubles the target's Burning.* | a foe |
| Votive Ward | shield | | 10 for each Burning foe | | *Shield 10 for each Burning foe.* | self or an ally |

- **The Chrism Ewer.** The target gains as many Burning stacks as it carries. Everything else in
  the spend lands first, so a Primed stack is doubled too. It cashes nothing in: the damage
  still lands a stack at a time, at the foe's own actions. It deals no damage, so it spends no
  shock and splashes no shield. The die shows the stacks it will leave, over the foe it is
  dragged across. (Chrism is the oil of anointing; on reflection, oil poured on a burning man.)
- **The Votive Ward.** Its number is read when spent: 10 for each foe on the field carrying
  Burning, either rank, a massive foe once, up to the target's maximum (no overshield). The die
  shows the number now. (A votive is a candle lit as an offering; here the foes are the
  candles.)
- **Per foe, not per stack.** Per stack, the Ewer would fill the Ward every time and the two
  pieces would say one thing. Per foe they pull apart: the Ewer wants one foe burning deep, the
  Ward wants many foes burning a little, and the Bellows turns deep into wide at a death.
- **The arithmetic.** Burn lays a stack per 10 of the blow, rounded up (Meteor Artillery's 15
  lays two, the Blazethrower's 10 one). A stack deals 5 at the foe's action and then one fades,
  so stacks still to burn are worth 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, 105, 140 and 180 for one to eight. The Ewer
  on four (50 to come) leaves eight (180 to come), and the foe's death passes eight to each
  neighbour.
- **Ryan's third tool**, moving burn from an ally to a foe, is section 8: the cleanse that moves
  debuffs, which he took further in his fourth notes.

**Stock, four hardpoints:** Meteor Artillery (Indirect, flat 15, heat 15, Burn), Blazethrower
(Direct, flat 10, heat 10, Burn), Incendiary Shells (Indirect, 5 to 15, Burn), Entropy
Condenser (a vent).

**The look.** A creepy hovering thing: a hunched torso and a horned head with a long jaw, broad
bat wings of black chrome panels ribbed in red glass that tilt and vector and never flap, two
long arms ending in claws, and nothing below the ribs but a tapering keel. Boosters under the
wing roots. The Ewer is a long spout under its right wrist; the Ward is a rack of votive lights
in its open chest, one lit for each Burning foe, a count read without hue. The homage: the
Stryge, Viollet-le-Duc's chimera on Notre-Dame, off its perch. Mounts: hp_1 and hp_2 on the wing
roots, hp_3 and hp_4 on the shoulders. Character: a thing on a wire (spreads its wings and holds,
flexes its claws as embers spit from its forearms, wraps its wings round itself like a cloak,
tilts its head down along its arms). It flies, the slowest flyer.

**Build space: deep or wide.** The Ewer wants one foe carrying a lot (the Stingray's deepened
stacks, the Aurora Lattice's Primed, the Photosphere Lance); the Ward wants many foes carrying a
little (a burner spent on each, an area burner found as a blueprint, the Bellows at every
death), since the area burn kept off its locked pieces belongs in a hardpoint. Kill order is the
bridge: the burning foe in the middle of a rank is worth more dead than the one at its end, and
more again once the Ewer has doubled it. Every burn on the team counts, not only its own.

**Holes and risks.**
- A slow start: two of its six entries are blanks until burn is down, so its first round is
  the burners' and the rerolls'. A team with no burn wastes a third of its die; that is the cost.
- No cap on the doubling, and a doubled stack passed whole to two neighbours compounds. The
  runner says whether it wants one (ten stacks more at most, say).
- The Ward against a swarm: eight burning foes are 80, but a shield stops at its maximum (40 to
  60 on most machines) and a standing shield blocks any blow whole, so the count buys blows
  turned rather than a bigger number.
- A Gel's halves join the reinforcements as new foes and carry no Burning.
- The screen must show the fire travelling along the rank in the enemy's turn, and how many
  foes burn at a glance (the Ward reads it).

**Engine and tests.** Small, three pieces. The Bellows: a hook at a foe's death (the tick kill
in `_enemy_turn`, the kills in `_resolve_attack`, Spillover), reading the foe's Burning before
the dead are cleared and stacking it on the neighbours as Spread finds them. The Ewer: a
component rule that doubles a status, run last in the spend. The Ward: a shield whose number is
a question through `sim/effects.gd` (10 for each Burning foe), so the die and the forecast show
it. Tests: a pass from a team kill, a tick kill and a Spillover kill; both sides and the edges; a
massive foe; no Mastema, no pass; the Ewer on none, on some, with Primed; the Ward on none, one
and many, and at the maximum. The bot: `BURN_WEIGHT` is a flat guess on a burning blow; the Ewer
needs the payout of the stacks it adds over the foe's expected life, or the runner reads it as
a wasted spend.

**What the runner should read.** The Meadow's swarms and formation, team rows with burners, and
the Ewer's and the Ward's spend shares against the burners'. Mastema alone is a diagnostic.

## 6. Minos, Arsenal Instrument XIII (eldritch: the ranks turned)

**The idea.** The judge of the second circle sends each soul to its place: every foe Minos
strikes changes rank. Powerful, not optional, and the whole team plays around it.

**Passive: Catherine Wheel.** Shown, a draft: *Every foe its weapons reach and leave standing
changes rank in its column, trading places with whoever stands there.*

- When: once a spend, after the spend resolves (every strike, every Chain link, Spillover, and
  any Lure or Disrupt on the entry), however many strikes reached a foe.
- Who: every foe the spend reached and left standing, Spillover's foe behind included. Each
  trades places with the other foe in its column, or steps into the slot if it stands alone
  (the dead are cleared first, so a kill's hole takes it).
- Spread turns three columns. Penetrate reaches both foes of one column, which trade places
  once, not twice.
- A massive foe fills its column and never moves, so Minos does nothing to one.
- Intents and targets go with the foes: each keeps the move and the target it drew.
- Weapons only: Minos's shields and vents move nothing.
- The ranks hold for everyone else. Nothing walks a moved foe back, and reinforcements fill empty
  slots by their own rank as now, so a foe moved out of its rank stays there.
- Foes strike from either rank, so the turn changes not who can hit the team but whom the team
  can reach (Direct reaches the front, and the back only through a hole), the enemy's order
  (the front acts first), where an Indirect spend splashes (fired at the front rank), where
  Spillover carries, and anything that reads a foe's rank.
- It breaks "the ranks hold" for one machine, which is the point of a passive.
- **Ryan's calls:** hitting any unit flips its rank, not optional; hover; one flip per spend;
  everyone hit moves in the column; nothing against a massive foe, which is fine;
  extraordinarily strong against formations.

**Locked piece.**

| Piece | Kind | Reach | Number | Heat | Keywords | Tags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gibbet Hook | attack | Direct | flat 10 | 5 | | Melee |

A chain whip coiled on its right forearm. Its own number is small: the turn is the point.

**Stock, four hardpoints:** Suppression Battery, Shotfire Rack, Plasma Arquebus, Entropy
Condenser.

**The look.** The stereotypical dark Gundam beside Aphelion's hero frame (Ryan: two Gundams are
fine): a corrupted Aile Strike or an Evangelion frame, tall, thin and long-limbed; digitigrade
legs on hoverjets; an Aile-style pack with two short swept wings and twin thrusters; the V-fin
crest grown into horns, twin eyes in red glass, one wing notched shorter, seams lit red like
cracks. Mounts: hp_1 and hp_2 pods beside the thrusters, hp_3 the left forearm, hp_4 the right
hip. Character: a predator at rest (flares the pack, crouches and rises on its digitigrade legs,
uncoils the whip, turns its horned head and holds). It hovers, balancing like a platform on
water.

**What the team works around.** Minos's turn is the round's hinge. Spent first, it brings the
healer, the caster or the Drake into the front rank, in reach of every Direct gun on the team.
Spent late, it sends a wounded front-liner behind its fellows, out of the team's Direct reach,
and brings the one behind forward. A Penetrate or a Spillover through a kill brings the foe
behind into the empty front at once, and the enemy's order changes with every turn.

**Holes and risks.**
- Only formations have a back rank today, so outside them Minos mostly shuffles the enemy's
  order (a lone foe stepping back leaves the column's back reachable through the hole anyway).
  Its worth waits on the foes' fresh start, whose formations the rift will spawn.
- The forecast: the die must show who will move while it is dragged, and the screen must draw
  the turn.
- The bot must weigh the state after the turn, or the runner cannot read Minos at all.

**Engine and tests.** Medium. A turn of the ranks after `_resolve_attack`, collected across the
Chain's recursive calls (its list of foes touched is local today); the swap of `front` and
`back` in a column and each foe's `rank`; the forecast and the drawing on the skirmish screen;
the greedy bot learning the round's order; the save already keeps positions. Tests: two foes
trade; a lone foe steps into the empty slot; a massive foe holds; Spread turns three columns;
Penetrate trades once; a Chain turns every link's survivors once; the dead never move; intents
are kept; the next enemy turn runs in the new order; Direct's reach follows.

**What the runner should read.** The four formation cells, and team rows. Minos alone is a
diagnostic.

## 7. The Onryō, Arsenal Instrument IX (sinister: retribution)

**The idea.** The machine that suffers, and the curse that outlives it. Every foe that wounds
the team carries a Grudge, a slow and permanent damage over time; an onryō is the ghost of
someone who died wronged, so its vengeance runs on after the machine falls.

**Passive: Martyrology Engine.** Shown, a draft: *While it fights, each blow a foe lands on a
machine's hull gives that foe a stack of Grudge: 5 damage a stack at its action, past armour.
Grudge never fades, even after the Onryō falls.* (A martyrology is the Church's register of
martyrs and their sufferings: it keeps the team's, and curses whoever caused them.)

- What counts: a foe's blow that puts hull damage on any machine, on bare hull or through a
  breach's overflow, while the Onryō fights. A blow a shield blocks or armour turns earns
  nothing.
- A stack a wounding blow, never a stack per 10: Grudge never fades, so Burning's rate would make
  one golem's fist four stacks for the rest of the fight.
- Grudge ticks at the start of the foe's action, before it acts, past armour, like Burning, and
  ticks even when the foe is suppressed. It never decays and nothing lifts it (a foe's cure might,
  once foes cure).
- The Onryō's fall stops new Grudge and lifts none.
- Not a trigger: bites (Corrupted), Volatile, an overheat, splash. None is a foe's blow.
- It is a debuff the team's machine put on, so it counts toward the Paradox and, once listed
  with the others, for the Susceptibility Tensor.
- **Ryan's calls:** Grudge is the thematic build-around; a stacking damage over time on the
  foes that injure it or the team, so not straight thorns; never popped for burst; for
  reliability it stays after the machine dies.

**Why another stacking debuff is enough.** Its source differs from Burning's. Burning comes
from the team's weapons: the player spends dice to lay it, and it burns out. Grudge comes from
the foes' own blows: the player lays it by being wounded and surviving, and it lingers. The
build is around taking hits, which nothing else rewards with damage, and the team's own
mitigation trades against it.

**Why it is not thorns.** Nothing lands at the blow: the striker pays at its own actions, slowly,
and only for wounds. A shield that blocks the blow or armour that turns it stops the curse too,
so a team built to block earns little and a megatank's team almost none.

**Locked piece: the Pall**, the guard Ryan allowed on a locked piece. A buff, an ally only:
*Until the round's end, the hull damage foes deal this ally is dealt to the Onryō instead.*

- The ally's shield and armour still come first; the hull loss after them moves.
- The moved wound lands straight on the Onryō's hull, past its shield and armour (it is the
  ally's wound, not a blow on the Onryō), and injures neither machine, as a bite does. So the
  Pall also shelters a fragile ally's components.
- Its striker still earns a Grudge.
- If the Onryō falls mid-round, the rest of the round's wounds land on the ally as usual.
- Aims, intents and arrows stay as they were shown; only the hull loss moves.
- The Warrior's planned Cover is the same piece on the foes' side. (A pall is the cloth over a
  coffin.)

**Stock, four hardpoints:** Meteor Artillery, Plasma Arquebus, Laminar Deflector, Proximity
Klaxon.

**The look.** Wrong in how it moves: a long thin body slung low between four long limbs, arms
and legs alike, every joint bending the wrong way for a person; a smooth mask with two red slits
for a sensor head (the cockpit rides in the body) under a fall of black cable hanging forward
like hair; the Pall a mantle of black lamellar plates on its back, spread toward the ally it
covers. From above, an X of limbs with the cables spread over the front. A knowing homage to the
crawling ghosts of Japanese horror (The Grudge), the shape and the move. Mounts: hp_1 and hp_2
on the shoulders, hp_3 at the base of the spine, hp_4 under the left forelimb. Character: still,
then sudden (freezes, then twitches its mask to one side and back; rears onto its hind limbs,
holds and drops; drags itself a step forward on its arms and back; spreads the Pall and folds it
away). Under way: an offbeat crawl, limbs out of step, a pause every few steps.

**Build space, in four places.**
1. *What it reads.* Every wound the team takes, so the team's make-up is the build: a team
   built to bleed (light armour, glass cannons, the Nemesis, which banks rage on hull damage
   anyway) curses more than a team built to block.
2. *Where the wounds land.* The Pall moves a fragile ally's wounds onto the Onryō; a lure (the
   Proximity Klaxon) draws a pack onto whichever machine can afford them.
3. *What reads it.* Grudge is a debuff the team put on, so the Susceptibility Tensor and the
   Baboon's Paradox read it.
4. *Keeping it standing.* It earns only while it fights, so plating and the Spall Liner (each
   wound stands up 10 shield) buy more curses, and its four hardpoints choose between guns and
   its own guard.

**Its cost.** No armour, a target the foes' exposed aim prefers, and the Pall puts its allies'
wounds on its own hull, which never heals in a battle: it is the one that suffers.

**Answers the Waste.** The Needlers breach every shield, and every breach earns its Needler a
Grudge that never fades.

**Holes and risks.**
- Two damage-over-time chips on a foe (Burning and Grudge) need their own glyphs and structure,
  never hue alone.
- A Grudge that never fades piles up on a boss that wounds often: six wounds is 30 a round for
  the rest of the fight. The runner reads it against the bosses' hull.
- Three things now answer a foe's blow on bare hull: Retaliate (the Retribution Thurible), the
  Armadillo's Faraday Hull (both Shock the striker) and Grudge. The same trigger with different
  payoffs (the team's next blow doubled, against a slow curse), which is the Arsenal's
  retribution and the open question of the Armadillo's.
- Grudge on a foe and Corrupted moved onto a foe would be the same shape (section 8).
- Mastema's Bellows stays off Grudge unless Ryan wants the Arsenal's two curses to spread
  together.
- The greedy bot prevents wounds; it must value a wound's Grudge, and the Pall's move, or the
  runner reads the Onryō as a worse tank.

**Engine and tests.** Medium. Grudge is nearly free: a status that ticks 5 a stack with no decay
(`Effects.tick` already skips decay for `DECAY_NONE`), laid on the striker where `_damage_mech`
sees hull damage and knows the source (`_hull_damaged` takes the machine only today), then
`_foe_gained_status`. Outliving the Onryō costs nothing: the stacks sit on the foe. The Pall:
a machine buff, and the redirect in `_damage_mech`, skipping the injury. The chip and its glyph.
Tests: a stack a wounding blow; none on a blocked blow or one armour turns; a breach's overflow
counts; the tick past armour, while suppressed, never fading, after the Onryō's fall; no Onryō,
no Grudge; the Pall moves the hull loss, injures neither, ends if the Onryō falls, and still
curses the striker.

**What the runner should read.** The Waste's cells, team rows beside fragile machines, and the
boss cells (long fights).

## 8. The cleanse that moves debuffs

**Ryan's notes.** His third: a tool that moves burn from an ally to a foe. His fourth: an Arsenal
cleanse that moves the applicable debuffs onto a foe is interesting, but depends on which terms
are even shared between ally and enemy. The working name is the **Scapegoat Brand** (Leviticus
16: the goat that carries the people's sins into the wilderness; sanctified on the face,
Azazel on reflection). The first pitch's sin-eater, which took its allies' heat onto itself, is
its cousin on the bench.

**What the two sides shared on 12 October: nothing; since 13 October, Burning** (the heat
debuffs took the names Burn and Scorch that day; the table stands as written on the 12th).

| A machine's debuff | On a machine | Who lays it today | What it could mean on a foe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overheating, the heat tick (Burning on a machine if the heat debuffs take the names Burn and Scorch) | 5 heat a stack at the round's end, then a stack fades | The Peak: scorch on the Bomb's Flare Up, the Salamander's Bite, the Drake's Breath, the Black Mage's Fire | Burning: 5 damage a stack at its action, then a stack fades. The same shape, heat for hull: shared once it is named Burn |
| Scorched (decided, unnamed: the softer heat debuff) | every weapon it fires runs hotter, per stack | The Peak's heat moves once they become debuffs (Stoke, Eruption, Fira, Hellfire) | Nothing: a foe has no heat |
| Scrambled | the die's next reroll lands anywhere | No foe today (the engine's scramble effect is unused) | Nothing: a foe never rolls |
| Disabled (one entry) | the entry is blank through the machine's next turn | The Waste: the Cockatrice's Stone | Nearest: Suppressed. A translation, and a strong one |
| Corrupted (one entry, stacking) | each spend of the entry bites its owner 10 a stack, for the skirmish | The Waste: the Thief's Mug | Nearest: a bite of 10 a stack at each of its actions, for the skirmish. No foe word today; it is the Grudge's shape |

The other way, Burning is the only foe debuff a machine would carry, once the heat tick takes
its name; Suppressed, Shocked and Paradox never touch a machine, and Numbed is a resistance that
nothing moves. So the Meadow and the Marsh lay no debuff on a machine at all, the Peak lays heat
and the Waste lays Disabled and Corrupted: a mover is blank in two domains of four whatever it
moves.

**Four shapes.**
- **A. Shared words only.** It moves what means the same on a foe and leaves the rest. Simplest
  to read, and thin: Burning alone once the heat debuffs are named, so the Peak alone.
- **B. Shared words and translations.** Disabled becomes Suppressed, Corrupted a bite at each
  action. Live in the Peak and the Waste; each translation is a rule to learn, and a stun from a
  cleanse is strong (Numbed still resists it).
- **C. One conversion.** Every debuff stack it takes becomes a Burning stack on the foe: one rule
  in the Arsenal's fire, live wherever the team carries debuffs, at the cost of "moves" meaning
  "turns into".
- **D. One meaning on any body.** The buffs-and-debuffs pass (bible section 5, open: polarity
  belongs to the effect, not the carrier) writes each debuff to mean one thing on whatever
  carries it: Burning is heat on a machine and hull on a foe; Corrupted bites whatever its
  carrier does next. Then the mover moves every debuff with no exceptions and no table. The
  principled shape, waiting on that pass and on the heat debuffs' names.

**How it is spent.** On a foe, as a weapon (a debuff on a foe stays a weapon), taking from every
machine at once: one target and one drag, and the scapegoat carries the whole people's sins. The
alternative, spent on one ally with the foe chosen by a rule (the one that laid it, say), needs a
second target the gesture does not have, and the engine records a debuff's source by foe type,
not by the foe.

**Where it lives.** A rare Arsenal component with its unique rule (the chase), fitted anywhere and
best against the Peak and the Waste: returning the enemy's curses is retribution, the maker's,
more than Mastema's alone. Locked on Mastema only if the evaluation accepts a die of seven with
an entry blank in two domains of four.

**The lean.** D as the target, A as the first build once the heat debuffs are named; B or C if
the vocabulary pass keeps the two sides apart. If D gives Corrupted a meaning on a foe, the
Onryō's Grudge and a moved Corrupted would be one shape twice (a bite at each action for the
fight): either Grudge is Corrupted laid on a foe, one word fewer, or the two differ on purpose
(Grudge 5 and permanent, Corrupted 10 and per entry). Ryan named Grudge as the Onryō's
build-around word, so that call is his.

**Engine.** Small once the words are settled: read each machine's debuff stacks, remove them, and
stack their foe meaning on the target; `CLEANSABLE` already tags what a cleanse may take, and
a Rapid switch-off (not a debuff) never moves.

## 9. The four as a set

- **Coverage.** One machine for each of the brief's words and for each domain's question; three
  of the Arsenal's six at each tier.
- **Different events, different payoffs.** Misharu reads a foe's blow breaking a shield; the
  Onryō reads a foe's blow reaching hull; Mastema reads a burning foe's death; Minos reads its own
  reach. Only a breach can set off Misharu and the Onryō with one blow (the break convicts, the
  overflow curses): the Waste's Needlers answered twice.
- **Pairs.** Mastema's Votive Ward feeds Misharu: every Ward spent is a fresh shield that may
  break. Minos can line a burning foe up beside fresh ones before it dies, or bring a burning
  back-ranker into the front's crowd, for Mastema's Bellows. Mastema and the Onryō lay two damage
  over time that never mix: Burning fades and spreads, Grudge stays and does not spread.
- **An Arsenal team.** Misharu baits, Mastema burns, Minos arranges and the Onryō punishes: a
  team row worth running once the four exist.
- **Copies to watch.** Suppress is common, and Misharu's is the only one the enemy sets off.
  Three answers to a blow on bare hull (Retaliate, the Faraday Hull, Grudge). Burn deepened in
  three places (the Stingray's Exothermic Suite, the Aurora Lattice's Primed, the Chrism Ewer).
  Grudge against Corrupted on a foe.
- **The screen.** New things to show, each with structure and never hue alone: the Grudge chip
  and glyph, the mark on a convicted foe, the fire travelling along a rank, Minos's turn
  forecast while the die is dragged, and the count of burning foes the Ward reads.
