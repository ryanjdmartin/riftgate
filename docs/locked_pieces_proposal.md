# The locked pieces' footing: a proposal

Ryan's direction of 10 November for the question his play of Phase 2 raised ("Locked pieces need
a new approach, as even the tier 1 chasses are warping", 9 November; the bible's section 12): a
locked piece earns its lock by gluing its chassis's identity together or by being something
nobody else has, its number even with the kit beside it; a machine holds a level, bought in the
hangar once the Convergence reaches it, that lifts the whole machine, its locked pieces and its
stats, with a branch; and tier zero is tier one's power, only simpler. His words carry his name
and the day; everything else is Claude's for him to move. Nothing here is in `content/`: the direction is the bible's locked row and tiers row since his answers of that day; the content,
the rules as built and the code follow in section 9's order.

"Level" is a working word for the bought step, since "tier" stays the chassis's complexity; the
name is his.

## 1. Ryan's calls, 10 November, in his words

- **The package**: "The signature is part of what makes the chassis, the chassis, along with the
  passive. The whole package is what makes the chassis unique and something you want on your
  team." "The cassowary's spin up could be used by anyone, but it would be broken - on the
  cassowary, it serves a very specific purpose. The Badger's two locked components are basically
  it's whole identity."
- **The sort, the first step**: "We can probably identify what pieces help glue the identity
  together, and what are just 'it needs one of these to work stock,' pretty easily. Maybe that's
  the first step." Of the second kind: "The Lance could be a normal weapon, available to all."
  Asked whether those become ordinary tiered weapons anyone can fit: "Ordinary, yes."
- **The sort's edge**: of the Flechette Pod, the Breaching Plow and the Megataser Harpoon, "Yes
  for flechette for sure. The other two correspond to big fixed geometry pieces on those models,
  especially the plow. So maybe the answer is rework if they seem too generic."
- **Tier zero may carry a status**: "Tier zero can have statuses and such, I never actually
  prohibited that. The bar was 'uncomplicated' not 'pure vanilla.'"
- **The Repeater's chase** was no fault of locking: "everything else on the prairie dog sucked,
  and my team had no aoe. So it was the best of a lot of bad options. An issue already
  identified."
- **Synergy pieces age too**, of the Fragmenter and the Afterburner Cannon: "Outfitting the rest
  of the slots lets you use them more often, but that's a self-closing hole. If a tier 3 weapon
  deals the same damage as the fragmenter with no setup, then the right play is to blast that
  one, bank the plate, and never fire the fragmenter." So every locked piece wants a tier under
  it, whatever its shape.
- **The upgrade is bought, for permanence**: "The one thing I like about paying for the upgrade
  in the hangar is that right now, we have no PERMANENCE to any of the player's units. Which was
  the intent, to allow experimentation, but a little piece of 'I like this one, I want to invest
  in it' is always a nice thing to include, I think."
- **Power tiers**: "the idea of machine tiers was to aim at complexity only, while component
  tiers was to perhaps add complexity and flexibility, but also power, to give a reason to
  upgrade. It's possible that is misguided, and that's part of our balance woes. If higher tier
  machines are also STRONGER (i.e, more durable, better outfitted from the start, better stats),
  then allowing an upgrade to lower tier units means we have strict power tiers to pit machines
  and enemies against. Obviously, that's the simple way out, but it's worth stating."
- **The whole machine, with a branch**: "I would think the whole machine including it's locked
  pieces and stats, yes. Keeps it simple, both for the player AND for our own struggling ramp
  balance. Only reason not to is to give branching paths that you can upgrade independently, but
  even so that could be part of the total upgrade package. I actually like the idea of a
  branching upgrade. Since higher tiers won't need as many upgrades, a little bit of
  customization could give that little push to want to invest in a lower tier chassis."
- **A locked piece's number sits even with the kit beside it**, never above it: asked which,
  "Level."
- **A higher chassis arrives at its level**: "I would say higher chassis come at their level,
  since you get them later in the campaign. Last thing you want is to get a chassis you really
  wanted in the 11th battle and not have the resources to field it properly."
- **A wreck keeps its level**: "Wrecks should keep their upgrades, for sure. It's baked into the
  chassis."

His second round, the same day, on the proposal's four questions and its sort:

- **Tier zero is tier one's power, simpler** (of the ladder topping out below tier three's
  blueprints): "Good point. And maybe that points to another issue. Maybe tier zero should be
  the same power as tier 1, just simpler. That actually solves a couple issues - we no longer
  need basics (i.e. starting blueprints) with advanced mechanics, but we can still put those into
  stock loadouts." So a status piece is tier one, stocked on its machine, never a starting
  blueprint (his answer to the tier-zero question: "Read 1").
- **The Convergence is the gate, not the price**: "Yes it shouldn't be that expensive. Really the
  better gate is the convergence, you can't upgrade to tier 2 until you reach tier 2 rifts and
  start getting access to tier 2 chassis."
- **A branch is a tweak, never stats**: "I would make the option a tweak to a locked component
  and/or passive number or mechanic, yes. Probably not stats, since that's more the job for
  Modules."
- **The four keep their locks for now**: "I don't entirely want to give up on the Stingray, Skua,
  Baboon and Prairie Dog not having locked components. Not yet, anyway. Sometimes having access
  to something nobody else has is a good enough locked piece, so we should look at tweaking their
  pieces before cutting. Cottonmouth is weird so its hard to make a locked piece, but I wouldn't
  count it out. The Clydesdale's point is it's a sandbox, barring that's just not good enough."
  This stands over "Ordinary, yes" above: the cut is the fallback, a tweak comes first.

His later rounds, the same day:

- **The two bands meet at the basics**: "We need to be careful we don't step on the refit works
  toes with the removing tier zero stuff. I'm not sure the 'player damage is low' holds anymore.
  I'd be more inclined to lower things to the tier zero level." (His first read of the refit's
  trial that morning found two lights "nigh unkillable".)
- **Against keyword soup**, three shapes for a branch, his: "1. Tier 1-2 is just a straight
  upgrade, and 2-3 is where the branch happens. 2. There's only two directions, with two upgrades
  on either side. Tier 1-2 you pick between the two closest to the center. Tier 2-3 you pick
  between the further out one from the same branch (further specializing), or you pick the tier 1
  upgrade you skipped. 3. Same as 2, except the tier 2-3 upgrades don't depend on the previous
  choice. The main issue with 3 over 2 is that all four need to be somewhat different and equally
  attractive, and that can be difficult to achieve."
- **The Pod**: "What if flechette pod wasn't the fixed one, and we gave it another? I guess it
  just depends if we want Sweep to be a generally accessible targeting type or not."
- **The Dingo**: "I agree about the harpoon, but the dingo needs an identity other than 'acts
  twice.' So either the harpoon gets really cool, or it gets cut and we shuffle the model around
  and go all in on the translocator."
- **The floor, not the step** (of the lowering): "The first step in power was always going to be
  tier two, wasn't it? I'm just lowering the floor."
- **A branch tweaks the locked piece, not the passive**: "I was for the most part leaning towards
  tweaking the locked pieces more than the passives when it comes to upgrades. Many passives have
  enough going on already, and I don't want to add complexity for the sake of complexity, we have
  enough of that. A passive numbers increase can be part of the straight upgrade if it warrants
  it. But like the dingos passive, what would you really add there as a meaningful choice that
  isn't just giving it a whole new passive on top?" Claude's lean of one direction on the piece
  and one on the passive is withdrawn on it.
- **The shape of a branch, settled**: "I think keep the mixed build so that we aren't making 6
  distinct upgrades per tier 1 machine." So his second shape: two directions two steps deep, the
  second pick the deeper step or the first one skipped, four forms a tier-one machine.
- **The machines with no locked piece branch too**: "I think the Clydesdale and Cottonmouth
  upgrades aren't that complicated. Clydesdale can add rerolls or module slots or something of
  the like, and cottonmouth can just modify its passive since the passive itself is a weapon, so
  it fits."
- **The guard is an aim, never a rule**: "Don't hard-lock the upgrades to one keyword or so on, I
  think the principle is sound but I don't like arbitrary restrictions."

## 3. The sort

A locked piece earns its lock one of two ways (his words): it is **glue**, the passive and the
piece needing each other, or the piece broken or meaningless in anyone else's hands; or it is
**an exclusive**, "access to something nobody else has". Either way its number sits even with the
kit beside it. Claude's first cut, moved by his words where they stand above:

| | Pieces | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Glue** | the Honey Badger's Chainsaw Claw and Bullion Fragmenter (his word); the Cassowary's Spin-Up (his word); the Kite's Pinion Driver; the Nemesis's Knuckle of Fury; the Ammit's Soulcutter Talons; the Buzzard's Afterburner Cannon; the Funnel-web's Web Bomb; the Storm Petrel's St. Elmo's Regenerator; the Dingo's Combat Translocator; the Lammergeier's Lorentz Contractor and Syrinx Drone | stay locked, and ride their machine's level (section 5) |

| **Their own sessions'** | the Polar Bear's, the Puffin's, the Whip Scorpion's, the Swallowtail's, the Musk Ox's, the Needletail's and the Giant Petrel's | sorted when each session designs them, on this footing |

## 5. The tiers and the levels

Built from his words; each line a consequence unless it is marked a question:

1. **Tier zero is tier one's power, only simpler** (his word), **and the two meet at the
   basics' numbers** (his lean: "I'd be more inclined to lower things to the tier zero level").
   The basics, the starting blueprints, are the plain pieces; a tier-one piece is the same power
   with a mechanic on it; a tier-one machine's stock may hold either. What follows:
   - Tier one's numbers come down (a plain tier-one gun is 4 today beside a basic's 2 or 3), so
     the basics, every stock die and every read on file stand as they are, and the refit pass's
     numbers are not moved from under it.
   - The Lance's 4 with Burn comes down with them: even with the kit beside it is the basics'
     number less what its keyword is worth.
   - The plain tier-one guns (the Slugthrower, the Plasma Arquebus, the Shotfire Rack) are then
     basics by another name: each takes a mechanic, becomes a basic or retires, the component
     pass's call.
   - In tier-one rifts a find is a new trick and never a bigger number; the first step in power
     is tier two's, at the Convergence's gate, as it was always going to be (his word: the
     lowering moves the floor, never the step's place).
2. **A machine holds a level, one to three.** A chassis arrives at the level of its tier (his
   word), so a tier one is bought up twice, a tier two once and a tier three never. The chassis's
   tier keeps its meaning, complexity, and gains one: where it starts.
3. **An upgrade is bought in the hangar, cheap, and gated by the Convergence** (his word): level
   two opens as tier-two rifts and chassis do, level three with tier three. It lifts the whole
   machine in one purchase: its stat line (hull and shield; under the refit pass its regen and
   cooling, a number a level) and the number on every locked piece it carries. It belongs to the
   chassis and a wreck keeps it (his word; a wreck sits out a turn and returns).
4. **Each upgrade carries a branch**, a pick of one from two, a tweak to a locked piece: never a
   stat (his word: stats are the modules' job) and not the passive, whose number may rise with
   the plain lift where it warrants one (his word). A tier
   one picks twice, a tier two once, which is what makes a lower chassis worth investing in (his
   reason). Twenty-nine choices and fifty-eight forms on today's roster, each proposed in chat.
5. **A locked piece at level N is a tier-N piece in power**, even with the kit beside it (his
   word, and his "Good point" on the ladder's top): level one tier one's band, which is tier
   zero's too, level three tier three's, so no signature is ever the entry to skip.
6. **The strict band**: a front's tier is tuned against machines of its level with kit to match,
   which is how the runner already reads tier two, and what the refit's ramp reads against.
   Blueprints stay the growth of the hardpoints; the level is the growth of what is the
   chassis's own.
7. **The engine**: a level is a Modifier with the chassis as its scope, read through
   `sim/effects.gd` like every number, never a write to a stat or a component; a machine's level
   and its branches are the run's to save.

## 6. Settled, and what is still his

Both questions of the second round are settled on Claude's leans (Ryan, 10 November: "Your leans
make sense"), and the bible's locked row and tiers row carry the direction:

1. **What a machine stocks**: up to its own tier at tiers one and two; a tier three stocks to
   tier two, its tier-three kit still found, so its locked pieces are a band above its stock as
   it arrives, in a team whose blueprints are there already.
2. **One ladder of three**: the rift's tier, the chassis's, the level and the component's; a
   basic is a plain tier-one piece whose blueprint is owned from a run's start, and "tier zero"
   is no longer a tier.

For the tweak round, his word the same day: "Remember during the tweak that the special identity
can also be after it's upgrades, not just at its base form." So a piece may be plain at level one
and become the thing nobody else has in a branch.

Still his: the six tweaks (section 4), the sort's glue row as Claude cut it, the name for a
machine's level, and three things his later rounds opened:

1. **The shape of a branch: settled, his second** (section 1). A tier one ends in one of three
   builds (one direction taken twice, the other taken twice, or one step of each) from four
   forms, only three of which must stand alone, since the deeper step is only ever offered to a
   machine that took the first; the deep end is one idea taken further, the place an exclusive
   can arrive late. Both directions live on the locked pieces: a machine with two takes a
   direction on each (the Badger's Claw or its Fragmenter), a machine with one carries both on
   it, the Clydesdale branches on rerolls or module slots and the Cottonmouth on its passive (his
   words). A tweak that changes what is there before it adds a word, and a piece that still reads
   in one line after two picks, are the aim and no rule (his word). A tier two, which arrives at
   level two with no first pick, picks one of its two directions' single step (Claude's lean,
   unmarked): three ends for a tier one, two for a tier two, none for a tier three.
2. **The Pod's lock.** Area is in the pool at tier one in three shapes already (Splash on the
   Howitzer, Spread on the Airburst Shell, Penetrate on the Enfilade Beam), and a tier-one
   machine may now stock them, so no team needs Sweep to have area: his "my team had no aoe" was
   the stock rule's. Claude's lean: the Pod stays the Skua's, an exclusive by his own test and
   glue besides (the Breech has a clause written for Sweep); a slottable Sweep of another flavour
   is the component pass's to add and takes nothing from it. **Answered 11 November**, in the
   Skua's rewrite (the bible's Kestrel row): "Flechette gun - unlocked but still slotted", its
   lock the Hypersonic Swoop and the Breech gone, so the Pod is an ordinary tier-one weapon and
   the tables' rows for it below read the piece as it was. Sweep's own future is his question
   of the same day (the bible's section 12).
3. **The Dingo.** Claude's lean: all in on the Translocator, the game's one Swap, with the
   Harpoon's Shock kept as what the swap leaves on the foes it moves if he wants the pin to
   survive; a session of its own, since "what a build on it would look like" is the question he
   put to Minos's movement and it is the Dingo's too. Claude's answer for the car: a swap changes
   who stands beside whom and behind whom, which is what Spread, Splash, Penetrate, the Web's
   burst, a Mine's blast and the Wild Boar's spill each read. The cost to come: its model's
   harpoon and a hardpoint or a second piece in its place.

## 7. The bench

What was offered and set aside, one line each with what took it off, so he can overrule the
reason and not only the idea:

- Locked pieces as utility only: his word, "way too difficult and way too limiting"; it also
  ends the signature guns, which are the package.
- Locked pieces that scale by synergy only: his word, the same; and their ceiling does not grow
  with the kit (the Fragmenter), so they want a tier as much as a plain piece.
- A locked piece's tier read from the kit on its machine's hardpoints: no permanence, since
  components move freely between machines.
- A locked piece's tier read from the Convergence: free, but tied to nothing the player did, and
  no investment. The Convergence is the gate instead, and the purchase the investment.
- An upgrade bought piece by piece: the whole machine is simpler, for the player and the ramp.
- An upgrade that lifts the locked pieces alone, durability flat: the same word; and the content
  already makes a higher tier tougher.
- A dear upgrade, a choice of which machine to lift: a machine left a band behind is dead
  weight; the branch is the choice.
- A branch in the stat line: the modules' job.
- Every chassis arriving at level one: "the 11th battle".
- A locked piece as the die's best entry once set up: his "Level"; the chase he felt.
- Cutting the four tier-one pieces to ordinary weapons at once: "Not yet"; the fallback.
- Status pieces at tier zero, owned from a run's start: no advanced mechanic among the starting
  blueprints; they are tier one, in a machine's stock.
- A level-three locked piece in tier two's band: a tier-three blueprint would out-hit it with no
  setup, the Fragmenter's fault at the top.

## 8. What it touches, on his go

- **The bible**: the locked row of section 7 (never a blueprint and no tier: a locked piece is
  glue or an exclusive, and rides its machine's level); the tiers row of section 10 (a chassis's
  tier is complexity and where it starts, its level is power, bought, the chassis's own; tier
  zero tier one's power; the stock rule); the basic kit row; section 12's item retired into them.
- **The rules**: the chassis and hangar lines, the stock rule's wording, the level.
- **Content**: the basics' numbers; the plain tier-one guns; a ladder of three numbers for each
  locked piece and each stat line; the six tweaks; the branches.
- **The engine**: the level as a Modifier and in the saves; the hangar's upgrade verb and the
  Convergence's gate on it, which the economy pass has not built; the validator's stock rule;
  the runners fielding a machine at a level.
- **The other passes**: the refit pass's regen and cooling numbers become a number a level, its
  stock re-deal deals tier one into tier-one dice, and its foes are read on the basics' new
  numbers; the economy pass prices the upgrade; every rework in design sorts its own pieces on
  this footing.
