# Brief: the Cryo party defence, Minerva's mammoth-type (new chassis)

**Ryan's direction (5 November)**: I think this design is overthinking it. The cryo is the payoff.
The build challenge should be getting there efficiently. So no free cryo on the passive. Stacks of
cryo lower damage by 1 per stack. A locked piece detonates the cryo everywhere, dealing a small
amount of damage per stack and suppressing foes with enough stacks. Merge the other ideas into this
one, it's Minervan defense but also a form of setup -payoff that Minerva is desperately missing.

The session designs Ryan's Cryo synergy party defence of 4 November: a Minerva machine under whose
watch a foe carrying Cryo hits every machine lighter. "The Cryo party defence" is a working label;
the name is Ryan's (Mammoth and Mastodon are both on the reserved list: Heavy Gear's strider and
Halo's Mammoth; BattleTech's, Heavy Gear's and 40K's Mastodon).

## Claude's first round (4 and 5 November)

**What stands.** Cryo never fades on its own, and any weapon hit cashes it for cooling. Ryan's
"minor rider later" is this idea: a foe carrying Cryo hits every machine a point lighter while
this machine fights. It lives on one machine's passive, as the Paradox lives on the Baboon's. The
Prairie Dog's passive already has the shape for itself alone: a foe carrying Flak hits it 1
lighter.

**A grant to the whole team, of another kind.** Ryan's taste rules out team tank stats in bulk
("giving the team tank stats is just as toxic as taking all the blows"). This one differs in kind:
a debuff the team must lay and keep on the foe, and any weapon hit cashes it away.

**The holes.**

1. **The decision it asks for.** A weakened foe stays weak only while nobody shoots it, so "hold
   fire on the frosted foe, or cash its cooling" is a real choice. Against rank and file, killing
   beats weakening, so the passive pays on the big foes and on foes out of Direct reach.
2. **Scale.** A point off a blow of 2 or 3 is a third to a half of it. With armour 1 or 2 it is
   immunity (the lure tank's brief: armour at or over the tier's blows). Read it on the runner
   against tier one's compositions.
3. **Cryo on its strikers.** This is the retaliation row's shape, a debuff on the striker, which is
   fine; it scales with the blows the machine takes, which a debuff may do, and the thorns line
   governs damage. The echidna lays its debuff on its strikers too, so the two want different
   triggers.
4. **The trigger's home.** Claude's first lean was a blow on any ally's shield, the party-wide
   trigger Ryan asked for, while the echidna's quills come from a blow on its own hull. That shield
   trigger sits beside Misharu's, though: a foe whose blow breaks a shield is weakened by Penance.
   That would be two weakens on the shield layer, so the trigger wants another home (the being
   struck orbit, the briefs README).
5. **Cryo's orbit.** With the Snowy Owl on Cryo (Ryan, 5 November), four machines orbit it: the
   Armadillo lays it, this one keeps it, the Owl cashes it to stay hidden, the Arsenal's frost
   figure blows it up. This machine is the one that wants the frost left in place.
