# The Polar Bear: its seal's trigger

Minerva's form-changing tank (the Armadillo until 9 November), built and fielded, its kit Ryan's
try of 9 November (the bible's Polar Bear row: armour 1 open and 4 sealed, one Cryo Flinger
without Rapid on a die of five, Spread on it while sealed, its weapons 1 harder on a foe carrying
Cryo; his play the judge). The refit pass sends its seal back (`docs/briefs/refit_pass.md`): the
kit is a day old and stands, but the model takes away the two spends that work it.

## Why the model breaks it

- Lockdown seals on "a shield it spends on itself" and opens on "a vent or a weapon". With no
  shield face and no vent face on any die, nothing seals it, and only a weapon opens what
  cannot close. Its stock carries a Canopy and a Bleed Valve, which retire.
- Sealed, "its own cooling stopped, 3 hotter at the end of each turn" priced the seal in heat a
  vent paid off; with no vent the price has one exit, opening, which may be the better rule.
- "An ally's vent still cools the sealed shell" (his word of 20 October) has no vent to mean;
  the Puffin's rework may be the one piece that brings it back.

## What stands

- The form itself and its numbers, his try: armour 1 open, 4 sealed; the Flinger's lure at the
  price of its action; its weapons 1 harder on Cryo. His play of it is still owed (the to-do),
  and can be had today, since the engine runs the old kinds until the pass lands.
- Cryo as the team's cooling: any machine's weapon hit on the mark cools the hitter, his "healthier
  way to grant ally cooling than venting directly". Under the model this is the one cooling a
  team can aim, so the Polar Bear matters more to a hot team, not less.
- Tier one today, pegged for tier two when Minerva's replacement tank lands.

## The question for his session: what seals it, what opens it

Claude's bench, one line each, for him to move:

- **Its own locked piece.** The Lockdown Shutters come back as equipment on its die (they left
  with the Faraday Hull; "the shutters are the look of the seal"): spent, it seals; a weapon
  opens it. A die of six, or the Flinger and the Shutters on five with three hardpoints.
- **The Flinger seals.** The lure is the seal: whistle and shut the doors, one spend. The open
  lure of one foe goes, and the Flinger is always the sealed, Spread one; simpler, and the tank's
  turn is one decision.
- **The hold seals.** A turn in which it fires no weapon ends sealed; a weapon opens it. No
  spend at all, the model's hold as its switch; the risk is a seal that costs nothing.
- **Equipment seals.** Any equipment it spends on itself seals it (the Shoring Jack, the Blast
  Door), the old rule with the new kinds; a second tank verb for every defensive piece.

## Holes raised

- His play of 9 November found the open shell luring and still acting, the seal never needed;
  his kit answers that by the Flinger's price. Whatever seals it must still cost its turn, or
  that play returns.
- A shield that regenerates behind armour 4: the runner read the old seal as armour that stopped
  almost nothing behind the shield its own spend refilled. Under the model the shield refills
  each round by itself, so the sealed shell is read again before its numbers stand.

## What the session hands back

The seal's trigger and its exit in chat, his marks, then content with the refit pass's content;
its runner row; its model unchanged (it shows the seal already).
