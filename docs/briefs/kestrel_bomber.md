# Brief: the bomber (new chassis, decided)

The session designs the decided Kestrel machine whose weapons can be spent on an empty slot in
the enemy's formation, the shot waiting there for whoever steps in. "The bomber" is Ryan's
working word; the name and the look are his.

**Status**: decided (14 and 15 October). **Maker**: Kestrel. **Proposed tier**: two or three,
by the size of its engine piece. **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; the bible's row on
Kestrel's next chassis (section 7); `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 8 (Kestrel gap two, the
interceptor it replaced); `docs/rules.md` on reinforcements, reach and the
formation.

## How it came about

- Claude, 14 October (the analysis): an interceptor acting on the reinforcements waiting to
  step in.
- Ryan, 14 October: "Soft control we cut but still has merit - preventing reinforcements from
  coming in. Or, trapping the SLOT so that a reinforcement comes in damaged. Similar to your
  interceptor while perhaps being more readable and less overhead, and I'd probably make it a
  bomber of some kind, maybe."
- Claude: the softer form, a reinforcement stepping onto a mined slot takes the mine's blow as
  it arrives, rather than a denied slot, which would be a suppress of the reinforcement by
  another name. The Marsh's Reraise and the Meadow's Gel halves walk into it; kill-then-mine is
  a tempo decision in one turn, since a kill clears its slot at once and reinforcements step in
  at the round's end.
- Ryan, 14 October: "Could have a variety of bombs, maybe they take on the properties of its
  weapons or something: napalm, aoe, shock, even suppress, so many options depending on how
  it's designed."
- Claude, 14 October (the shape recorded in the bible): the weapon is the bomb. Its weapons may
  be spent on an empty slot, and the shot waits there for whoever steps in with that weapon's
  keywords (a Burn piece napalm, a Spread piece a cluster, a Shock piece a shock mine, a
  Suppress piece a stun on arrival); the heat is paid now and the damage lands later; the
  locked piece is only the bay.
- Ryan, 15 October: "Is that strong enough? If it kills a foe with a weapon, would the keywords
  still drop? Or do they drop regardless, replacing the mine with the fresh keywords every
  time? How flexible does it need to be? What damage does the mine do?"
- Claude's answers, 15 October (the refined shape): its
  passive lets any weapon be spent on an empty slot, under the reach rules as if a foe stood
  there; the shot waits with its number (rolled when laid, shown on the slot) and its keywords;
  it lands as the foe steps in, before it acts; one shot a slot, a new one replacing the old.
  Kills drop no mine on their own, except the locked bay's: its own kills leave its shot in the
  slot they empty, so the free mine lives on one piece and every other mine is a spend the
  player chooses. The damage is the weapon's own.
- Ryan asked "locked bay?"; Claude: a locked bomb weapon with a payload (for example Indirect,
  Spread and Burn, a napalm cluster), there for tier-one legibility and as the one home of the
  automatic mine; the alternative is to lock nothing at tier two, where its stock carries
  keyword pieces. Ryan: "Locked bay makes sense, just didn't understand the term."

## Why it is strong enough (Claude, 15 October)

A mine is a shot delayed, so against a present foe it is always the worse spend; what makes it
worth laying is that the arrival pays before it acts. A stun mine removes the arrival's first
action (hard control carried by the blueprint, not the chassis); a
burn mine ticks on its first action; a shock mine doubles the team's first blow on it; a mine
on an empty column catches the massive reinforcement that takes columns first. With
reinforcements in the fight it is an engine; without them it is a plain machine, the right
floor for its tier. Levers if weak: the bay's payload, a laid shot striking twice.
