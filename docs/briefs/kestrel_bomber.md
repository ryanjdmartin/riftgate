# Brief: the bomber (new chassis, decided)

The session designs the decided Kestrel machine whose weapons can be spent on an empty slot in
the enemy's formation, the shot waiting there for whoever steps in. "The bomber" is Ryan's
working word; the name and the look are his.

**Status**: decided (14 and 15 October). **Maker**: Kestrel. **Tier**: three (Ryan, 15
October). **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; the bible's row on
Kestrel's next chassis (section 7); `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 8 (Kestrel gap two, the
interceptor it replaced); `docs/rules.md` on reinforcements, reach and the
formation.

## How it came about

- Claude, 14 October (the analysis): an interceptor acting on the reinforcements waiting to
  step in.
- Ryan, 14 October: "Soft control we cut but still has merit - preventing reinforcements from
  coming in. Or, trapping the SLOT so that a reinforcement comes in damaged. Similar to your
  interceptor while perhaps being more readable and less overhead, and I'd probably make it a
  bomber of some kind, maybe."
- Claude: the softer form, a reinforcement stepping onto a mined slot takes the mine's blow as
  it arrives, rather than a denied slot, which would be a suppress of the reinforcement by
  another name. The Marsh's Reraise and the Meadow's Gel halves walk into it; kill-then-mine is
  a tempo decision in one turn, since a kill clears its slot at once and reinforcements step in
  at the round's end.
- Ryan, 14 October: "Could have a variety of bombs, maybe they take on the properties of its
  weapons or something: napalm, aoe, shock, even suppress, so many options depending on how
  it's designed."
- Claude, 14 October (the shape recorded in the bible): the weapon is the bomb. Its weapons may
  be spent on an empty slot, and the shot waits there for whoever steps in with that weapon's
  keywords (a Burn piece napalm, a Spread piece a cluster, a Shock piece a shock mine, a
  Suppress piece a stun on arrival); the heat is paid now and the damage lands later; the
  locked piece is only the bay.
- Ryan, 15 October: "Is that strong enough? If it kills a foe with a weapon, would the keywords
  still drop? Or do they drop regardless, replacing the mine with the fresh keywords every
  time? How flexible does it need to be? What damage does the mine do?"
- Claude's answers, 15 October (the refined shape): its
  passive lets any weapon be spent on an empty slot, under the reach rules as if a foe stood
  there; the shot waits with its number (rolled when laid, shown on the slot) and its keywords;
  it lands as the foe steps in, before it acts; one shot a slot, a new one replacing the old.
  Kills drop no mine on their own, except the locked bay's: its own kills leave its shot in the
  slot they empty, so the free mine lives on one piece and every other mine is a spend the
  player chooses. The damage is the weapon's own.
- Ryan asked "locked bay?"; Claude: a locked bomb weapon with a payload (for example Indirect,
  Spread and Burn, a napalm cluster), there for tier-one legibility and as the one home of the
  automatic mine; the alternative is to lock nothing at tier two, where its stock carries
  keyword pieces. Ryan: "Locked bay makes sense, just didn't understand the term."

## Why it is strong enough (Claude, 15 October)

A mine is a shot delayed, so against a present foe it is always the worse spend; what makes it
worth laying is that the arrival pays before it acts. A stun mine removes the arrival's first
action (hard control carried by the blueprint, not the chassis); a
burn mine ticks on its first action; a shock mine doubles the team's first blow on it; a mine
on an empty column catches the massive reinforcement that takes columns first. With
reinforcements in the fight it is an engine; without them it is a plain machine, the right
floor for its tier. Levers if weak: a laid shot striking twice.

## The session (15 October)

- Claude proposed: tier three (it bends a rule the player has learned, a weapon spent on a
  foe; the lowest floor and the highest ceiling; it stocks tier-two pieces, so its stock can
  show the bombs; Kestrel's tier three); the bay Indirect and rolled with Spread and Burn,
  every slot its kills empty taking its shot; a slot reached as if a foe stood there (Direct
  the front slots and a back slot whose front is empty, Indirect any); everything about a laid
  shot fixed when laid and shown on the slot, the arrival's armour and Shock read as it lands;
  a massive arrival filling both slots of a column and taking both shots; Lure, Disrupt and
  Chain inert on a laid shot (the round they work in is over); a mined slot marked by its
  outline and a glyph, never by hue alone; the formation showing what waits before it lands.
- Ryan, 15 October: "we can go ahead with this one too now, it doesn't need to wait. This is a
  solid design already, and i'm not sure it needs the formation view to be viable. Still
  something we should add, but it can be a separate to-do." Tier three, which "can stock all
  kinds of cool stuff, so giving it a locked napalm bomb isn't really that interesting". The
  lock in its place: a buff, self or ally, Rapid: "The next weapon fired by this unit will
  leave a mine if it kills the target. Letting it lend allies its passive is probably busted,
  but I want to try it, because it sounds fun." And: "If Minos or any other move effect causes
  an enemy to step on a mine, it blows up. That's a synergy."
- Claude, 15 October, on the buff: read as lending the kill-mine, not the passive's spend on an
  empty slot (lending that would be much bigger); it lasts until the round's end and the next
  weapon fired spends it, kill or not, so the decision is whom to buff (a flat weapon dragged
  over a hurt foe shows a sure kill); the mine is the killing shot as fired (its number after
  the firer's modifiers, its strikes and its keywords) in the target's slot only, so Spread's
  other kills and Chain's later links leave none. Where it is strong: it copies the team's best
  killer onto the arrival (a Kite's doubled Railgun leaves 100 waiting, and the next foe
  into that slot dies before it acts); its limits are one entry of the bomber's five, Rapid's
  once a round, a kill, and a foe waiting for that rank; the levers if it proves busted: self
  only, the mine at half the shot, a longer cooldown. With nothing waiting the buff does
  nothing, tier three's low floor. Any foe that enters a mined slot by any means sets it off:
  reinforcements, summons, regrown parts and Reraise's returns already arrive through the
  reinforcement step, and Minos's turn of the ranks would be the first other way in, its mine
  landing at once, in the player's turn. Indirect's splash is retired (11 October), so a laid
  shot needs no splash rule.

- Ryan, 15 October, the second round: the buff lends only the mine, "not the targeting
  portion"; it lasts "till end of round or till next weapon spend, whatever comes first".
  "Donating it to a unit with a rapid gun or the Nemesis and letting it lay mines on the entire
  rank is a bad idea, probably. But donating it to Spread or Penetrate is allowed, since those
  are still Targets, correct? what is our rules wording there?"
- Claude, 15 October: in the rules a target is the foe the die is spent on; Spread "also hits
  the target's neighbours in its rank", Penetrate "also hits the other rank of the target's
  column", and the rules say "every foe the spend reaches" where they mean all of them (Shock,
  Lure, Disrupt). So "if it kills the target" mines the target's slot alone; mining every slot
  a spend empties would read "every foe the spend kills". The hole: an explosive carries
  Spread and Penetrate (the area row), so one spend could mine four slots, three of the front
  rank's four, the whole rank in one spend. Claude's lean: the target's slot alone, since a
  Spread weapon's mine carries Spread and hits the arrival's neighbours as it lands.

- Ryan, 16 October, two worries: whether the bomber's own Spread weapon, spent on a rank with
  several empty slots, leaves several mines; and that the kill-mine forces the notion of a
  primary target, "which can be awkward".
- Claude, 16 October: one spend lays one mine, in the slot the die is spent on, and the
  weapon's keywords ride inside it and act as it lands: a Spread mine hits the arrival's
  neighbours, a Penetrate mine the other rank of its column. Mines land once the reinforcement
  step is done, left to right, so a Spread mine catches every arrival beside it. The target is
  already the game's word and gesture, the foe the die is dragged onto (Spending's "drag a die
  onto a target", Spread's "the target's neighbours", the number the die shows for the foe it
  is dragged over, the strafer's aimed first strike), so the kill-mine reads: if the foe it is
  spent on dies, the shot waits in its slot.

- Ryan, 16 October, later: the area keywords travel inside the mine; but "one could make the
  argument that everything damaged by an attack is a target. Maybe it's not worth the confusion
  and we rework the locked parts of the bomber."
- Claude, 16 October: the buff can name no target at all: every foe the next weapon spend kills
  leaves the shot in its slot. Still one spend, so no whole rank from a Rapid gun or the
  Nemesis; a Spread spend that kills three lays three, the most one buff can do. If the lock is
  reworked instead, Claude's pick is a trigger: spent on a mined slot, it sets that mine off now
  into the foes around the slot, so mines pay in a fight with nothing waiting.

- Ryan, 17 October, a new idea: a locked component that pulls every mine on the field into a
  targeted space, a foe there or not, "just so it plays by the passive rules"; with a foe there
  they all blow. "It does implicitly allow mine stacking, which we need to decide if that's ok
  to allow in general or only in this case." It may replace or supplement the buff; if the buff
  stays it becomes "rapid, cooldown 1, mark enemy", and the mark is one of three: the next
  weapon to hit the foe drops a mine ("double hit, probably broken"), the next weapon to hit it
  drops a mine if it dies ("the softest"), or the next weapon to kill it drops a mine ("most
  flexible").
- Claude, 17 October, reading it: the pull is the floor the review asked for. Mines laid in a
  quiet round are cashed on a chosen foe at a chosen moment (a Shock up, a boss stepped in), and
  nothing laid against reinforcements that never come is lost. Each pulled mine lands as it was
  fired, in laid order (its strikes, its keywords, armour on each), so a Shock doubles the first
  mine only and a pile of Burn mines lays every stack; a pile landing as one blow would read
  armour once and double whole, stronger and a second rule. Lean: the pull is a weapon with a
  modest blow of its own, never a dead spend (with no mines it is a gun; on an empty slot it
  lays its own mine and pulls the rest into it; on a foe its blow lands and the mines go off),
  and not Rapid, so a pull costs the turn and the laying still has to be chosen (a Rapid pull
  makes every mine liquid and the slot a formality). On stacking: everywhere, one rule, "a laid
  shot joins what waits in the slot; the slot shows the total", retiring 15 October's "a new one
  replacing the old", since the pull piles anyway and a second rule for the passive is one more
  to learn; the pile on the slot that fills first is the bomber's pre-kill of an elite arrival,
  paid in the spends it did not fire at the foes present. Bench: replace on the passive, pile
  only by the pull (keeps "do not waste a slot" as a decision, at the price of a special case).
  On the marks: the first has no trigger (a mine under a standing foe either blows at once, a
  double hit, or waits for its death, which is the third with a weaker shot); the second adds a
  one-hit timer to track; the third, a mark on the foe that any machine's killing weapon
  cashes, is the lend without the "target" word, one mine a mark, visible as a chip, and a death
  by a tick leaves nothing (finish it with a gun). Lean: the third, supplementing the pull: mark
  the escort, kill it, pull its mine onto the boss.

- Ryan, 17 October, the second round: the mines blow in laid order ("The player probably doesn't
  care anyway, might as well build the fair version"). No Rapid on the pull, "for sure"; it is
  an Indirect weapon, its damage yet to be determined, and whether a pull with damage also lays
  a mine was the uncertainty; a dead spend is no complete no-go ("technically a mech at no heat
  could roll a vent 4 times and still be left with a dead spend"). Mines stack: "Stacking is a
  pretty big tempo loss, and you need a spot empty for multiple turns, so I don't think it
  would be abusable anyway without a lot more rank shuffling in the game. Or a hypothetical
  reinforcement preventer, which I'm still unsure about." The mark is tried in its third form,
  the second if it proves too strong; whether it carries Rapid, has value as a support action
  alone, or needs another rider, is the question.
- Claude, 17 October: a weapon spent on an empty slot lays its shot by the passive's own rule,
  so a pull with damage lays its shot there and gathers the rest into it, one rule and no
  exception; a pull of no damage gathers alone, and one with nothing laid is the dead spend
  Ryan allows. The mark's Rapid: as a full action a mark costs the spend a laid mine would, for
  a mine a teammate's kill has to lay (a bigger shot, a Kite's doubled Railgun say, and
  no empty slot needed in advance, but a gamble on the kill), so alone it rarely beats laying
  one; Rapid with a cooldown of 1, as Ryan first wrote it, is what makes it worth carrying, a
  free mark every other round paid for by the die's free reroll, and the pull is the rider
  that makes every mine pay, so it needs no other. A hole: Marked is a status the team puts
  on a foe, so the Paradox meter would count it (Lure counts for nothing, being no status), a
  cheap Paradox stack every other round beside a Baboon. Lean: it counts, one rule, until the
  pairing proves cheesy in play.
- Ryan, 17 October, the third round: the pull's question was never whether a shot of its own
  would lay a mine (the passive's rule), but whether the component needs one. The Paradox is no
  worry: "it can be tuned for the final ecosystem. Paradox's existence shouldn't dictate the
  game." Marked counts.
- Claude, 17 October: it needs none. The pull's work is gathering and setting off; with nothing
  laid it is the dead spend Ryan allows, and a shot of its own would be a floor bolted on, not
  a part of the design, and would blur what the entry is (a tool, not a gun). Lean: no damage.
  The number is Ryan's.

## The proposal for the go (Claude, 18 October)

Claude's proposal, in chat first; every number a placeholder, the name and the look Ryan's.
The design as decided on 15 to 17 October, written out whole with the two leans Ryan has not
yet moved (the pull of no damage, the mark Rapid with a cooldown of one) and the shape.

- **Shape.** Kestrel, tier three, a medium flyer on big boosters, move 40: Kestrel's biggest
  frame, the Buzzard's class, since the makers table builds "nothing big". Hull 90, shield 60,
  field recharge 25, heat maximum 80, cooling 5, armour 0; four hardpoints beside the two
  locks, a d6; two module slots. Tier three is a lower floor and a higher ceiling, never more
  power (the tiers row), so its numbers sit at the Buzzard's.
- **The passive**, as the codex would print it: its weapons may be spent on an empty slot in
  the enemy's formation, under the reach rules as if a foe stood there (Direct a front slot or
  a back slot whose front is empty, Indirect any). The shot waits there, its number as rolled
  and modified when laid and its keywords, and joins what already waits; whoever steps in
  takes it all, shot by shot in laid order, before it acts. A massive arrival takes both

- **The pull**: Indirect, no damage (the lean stands: a tool, not a gun, and a dead spend
  with nothing laid is the one Ryan allows), never Rapid, no cooldown: every mine on the

- **The mark**: a weapon of no damage, Rapid with a cooldown of one (the lean stands: as a

  reroll, is what makes it worth carrying, the pull the rider that makes every mine pay).
  The foe is Marked, a chip: the next weapon to kill it, any machine's, leaves its shot in the
  slot it empties (the third form; the second, a mine only if that very blow kills, if it
  proves too strong). One mine a mark, it lasts the skirmish, a death by a tick leaves

- **Stock**, stand-ins until the stock pass, keyword pieces so the stock shows the bombs (a
  tier three stocks tier two): the Incendiary Shells (napalm), the Suppression Battery (a stun
  mine: hard control carried by the blueprint, gated by its setup under the stun row, a slot
  mined in advance and a foe stepping into it), the Polarity Projector (a shock mine), the
  Laminar Deflector. Every keyword rides inside the mine and acts as it lands: a Spread mine
  hits the arrival's neighbours, a Penetrate mine the other rank of its column, a Shock mine
  leaves the arrival Shocked for the team's first blow, a Suppress mine takes its first action.
- **The display.** An empty slot lights as a target when a weapon of the right reach is
  dragged, as a foe does; a mined slot shows its outline and a glyph with the total waiting
  and a chip per keyword, never hue alone; the pull's gather is drawn as the mines sliding
  into the slot; a Marked foe wears its chip; the header's REINF count stands beside it until
  the formation view lands (the to-do), so a mine is laid against a count.
- **Engine work.** A slot as a target (`targets_for_face` returns foes today, and the
  bot's option list with it); the mine store on the skirmish, a list of laid shots a slot
  (the number, the strikes, the keywords, in laid order), carried by the save; the landing
  after the reinforcement step, left to right, each shot through the arrival's damage taken
  and armour with its keywords acting; any other way into a mined slot setting it off at once
  (Minos's turn of the ranks, when it exists); the pull and the mark as printed properties,
  Marked a foe status in `FOE_STATUSES` with a kill hook; the bot's value of a mine (a shot
  delayed, worth its number times the chance a foe steps in before the fight ends; the to-do's
  valuations line, before its runner row); each tested with synthetic passives.
- **What the runner reads.** Alone and in a team against the four formations, the only
  fights with reinforcements today: mines laid, mines cashed by an arrival, mines cashed by
  the pull, and the share of its spends that went to a slot; a direction only until the foes'
  fresh start gives fights a wait list worth trapping.

- Ryan, 18 October, on the proposal: "Happy with the bomber as a whole. Only question is
  size, I feel like it should be bigger than medium. What are our size classes currently?"
- Claude, 18 October: four classes by mass (light, medium, heavy, assault; the bible's size

## The line review (Claude, 20 October)

Two items under the action economy's direction (the bible's row, section 5).

- **The mark's gate.** "Rapid, cooldown 1" is the switch-off (the rules: every Rapid piece
  today carries a cooldown of one, the entry switching off for the round), so the mark as
  written is one of the ungated Rapid pieces the row re-prices, free every round it shows, and
  this brief's "a free mark every other round" misread the cooldown (every other round is a
  cooldown of two). Proposed: the mark Rapid while a mine waits on the field, a setup gate of
  the Paradox's shape, so marking is free only once the bomber has invested a spend in a slot
  and is a full action before; its tempo grows with its investment, which is the identity.
  Bench: the mark as a combined entry, a weapon of 5 that Marks, no Rapid, never a dead spend.
- **Chain in mines.** The 15 October reading made Lure, Disrupt and Chain inert on a laid shot
  because the round they work in is over; that is true of the two intents and not of Chain,
  which repeats a kill. Proposed: a Chain mine that kills its arrival repeats the attack on the
  next foe in reach, rolling and paying its heat again to the bomber in the reinforcement step
  (the heat sits on the gauge until the next END TURN, so nothing burns out of turn). The Arc
  Fault Cannon is then Kestrel's own bomb, the lightning mine, and the bomber's stock carries a
  Kestrel piece beside the other makers' keyword pieces. Lure and Disrupt stay inert.

## The mark through Exposed, the look and the name (21 October)

- Ryan, 21 October, on the look: "I'm not sure the look, could be a traditional bomber shape
  like a b-2 or b-52, or something weirder like 4 wings, one front, one aft, or any other kind
  of heavy flyer. What kind of birds carry stuff?" On the mark: "Let's try leveraging our new
  status: the mark, (name help, maybe like a resonating or copying drone or something?),
  applies the bomb debuff and Exposed. If it's already exposed, gain Rapid."
- Claude, 21 October, the mark as read: an Indirect debuff, heat 0. The foe is Marked (the
  next weapon to kill it, any machine's, leaves its shot in the slot it empties; one mine a
  mark; it lasts the skirmish) and Exposed (two rounds, refreshed). If the foe already carried
  Exposed, the mark is Rapid: the die rolls free onto another entry and the mark switches off
  for the round. It replaces the line review's gate (Rapid while a mine waits), which goes to
  the bench. The pairing is the point: the interceptor Exposes a foe for free on its own die,
  and the bomber marks it for free on its; the team's Direct guns reach it and its armour
  counts for nothing, and the kill leaves its shot for whoever steps in.
- Its holes, each with a lean:
  1. The condition is read before the mark lands, or every mark is Rapid on its own Exposed.
  2. "Already exposed" is the debuff, never a back-rank foe exposed by its formation; that
     reading would make the mark free on most back-rank foes after any kill (Ryan's "our new
     status").
  3. The self-loop: a foe the bomber marked stays Exposed for two rounds, so marking it again
     is free and refreshes Exposed, and one foe (the boss) stays armourless all fight for no
     tempo whenever the mark shows; the switch-off keeps it to once a round. Lean: Rapid only
     on a foe that carries Exposed and is not yet Marked. The re-mark on its own target is then
     a full action, and the free mark comes from someone else's Expose (the interceptor's),
     which is the combo asked for.
  4. Two debuffs a spend: the Paradox counts each once a spend, so the mark feeds a Baboon two
     stacks (Ryan, 17 October: the Paradox is tuned for the final ecosystem).
  5. The die cannot show a Rapid chip that depends on the foe until it is dragged: over a foe
     that qualifies, the chip lights, as the number does for a foe today.
  6. Expose gains its second carrier, so it is a keyword proper (the vocabulary plans it), and
     the overlap with the interceptor is in the status, not the shape: a free setter there, a
     setup that pays this machine's mines here.
- The mark's name (Ryan's ask): the Echo Drone (the lean: the kill echoes in the slot, and it
  says copy at a glance), the Resonance Drone (his word), the Doppler Repeater (Kestrel's air
