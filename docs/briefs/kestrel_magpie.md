# Brief: the Magpie, Kestrel's support engine (new chassis, direction)

The session designs the ninth of Kestrel's ten, marked by Ryan on 21 October as a support
engine. "Magpie" is a working name (Ryan, 21 October); the name, the look and the numbers are
Ryan's.

**Status**: direction (Ryan, 21 October); the first round proposed the same day. **Maker**:
Kestrel. **Tier**: open (Kestrel's counts: 3-5-2 puts it at two, 4-4-2 at one). **Read first**:
`docs/briefs/README.md` (the taste lines, the action-economy section); `kestrel_line.md` (the
line, the overlap map, the last two slots); the bible's Kestrel row, the action-economy row and
the Empowered row; `docs/rules.md` on foes' moves and blows.

## What Ryan said

- 21 October: "Mark down Magpie and an Owl (probably snowy owl) as our last two chassis,
  probably a support engine and stealth unit, respectively."
- 21 October: "Magpie (working name) could lean into the thieving space (myth, but decent
  gameplay hook), or something to do with its general intelligence."

## What frames it

- Kestrel's supports already: the Puffin (shields grown by an ally's heat), the Giant Petrel
  (tempo: rerolls, an ally's action, Empowered), the Storm Petrel (the lure, its own regen)
  and the Needletail (a free setup a round, Exposed). A third parts from all four by its shape
  (the overlap map's rule).
- A support engine: support that runs every round off something the fight produces, not a
  burst.
- The thief is folklore (magpies are wary of shiny things when tested); the intelligence is
  real: the Eurasian magpie was the first animal outside the mammals to pass the mirror test,
  and corvids cache food and plan.

## The first round (Claude, 21 October)

**A. The thief (Claude's lean): it steals what a foe would hit with, and hands it to an ally.**

- The lock (a working name, the Pickpocket): a debuff, Indirect, heat 5: the foe's blows this
  round deal 5 less, and the Magpie pockets the 5 as loot, banked up to a cap (10, say).
- The passive (a working name, the Hoard): a buff or a shield it spends on an ally also hands
  that ally its loot, a point of Empowered for each point stolen, so damage taken from them
  becomes damage given to us.
- It reads the foes' telegraphed blows (their numbers), produces a weaker foe blow now and a
  bigger ally blow later, and pays two spends for one effect, the loot idle until delivered.
  The decisions: rob the biggest blow, or the one aimed at the soft machine; give to the hitter
  about to fire, the Skua (Empowered counts on each strike) or whoever carries the Cyclic
  Cannon.
- With basics alone it works (the lock robs, a self-or-ally basic shield delivers), and every
  maker's shield and buff becomes a delivery: fed by other makers' pieces, two routes in and
  two payoffs.
- Holes:
  1. Empowered is the Coil's and the Reheat Uplink's too, so Kestrel would carry three of its
     sources. The lever: the loot as team rerolls (Kestrel's lean), though rerolls bought by
     theft feed the Immelmann Booster's price.
  2. A foe whose move deals nothing (a cure, a brace, a rally) has nothing to steal, unless the
     theft takes the special's payload in kind (a cure as shield, a brace as Armoured, a rally
     as Empowered): richer, and more to learn.
  3. Misharu's Penance (planned) halves a foe's blows; a flat theft is a second weaken, parted
     by the hoard.
  4. A blow at every machine loses 5 on each machine it reaches; the theft's worth there is
     the lever to read.
  5. A weaken on a foe is new engine work (Empowered's hook with a negative number).

**B. The mimic (intelligence): it does again what the team just did.**

- The passive (a working name, the Mirror Test): one entry on its die is its mirror, blank
  until an ally spends an unlocked component this round, then a copy of the last one, spent at
  the Magpie's own heat.
- It reads the team's spends and produces a second use of the round's best unlocked entry (a
  second Coil, a second shield, a second shell); it pays its own action, so nothing is lent
  and the action-economy row's second action is met by its own turn. The decisions: the team's
  order (spend the piece worth copying first), and whether the mirror or its own entry is the
  better use of its turn.
- Holes:
  1. A copy of a gun makes it a hitter, so the mirror may want to copy only what is spent on
     machines (shields, vents, buffs), which keeps it a support.
  2. Locked pieces never copy (unique by rule).
  3. The Lammergeier's Syrinx Drone copies a killing shot into a mine; parted by time and
     target, but two copiers in one maker.
  4. A round where it must act first shows a blank mirror.

**C, one line (intelligence as planning): the cache.** Corvids cache food and come back for
it: the Magpie holds an ally's rolled entry into the next round (a lock that survives the
round's end). Dice control on Kestrel's reroll lean, beside the Giant Petrel's bank: a face
kept, where the Petrel keeps rerolls.

Each could take a map clause too (the Prairie Dog's and the Swallowtail's two-clause shape):
the thief's would be loot on the map (a wreck claimed from a hex away, or more salvage from the
fights it is in), the economy engine the Honey Badger was once offered.
