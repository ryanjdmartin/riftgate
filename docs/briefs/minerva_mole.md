# Brief: the Mole, Minerva's burrowing drill (a planned chassis, a direction)

The session finds a verb for Ryan's drill-tank mole of 4 November, a silhouette with no mechanic
yet, and with it its tier: the mole or the Wild Boar comes down to tier one as the Polar Bear goes
up. Its name is the Mole, Ryan's of 17 November.

**Status**: a direction, in content as a planned draft since 17 November: his burrow and Inertia
Drill, its kit set on 16 November (The kit as he set it, below); its name the Mole and its
designation the TB-2FAB Tunnel Borer, his of 17 November; its model built from the fifth look
round and in the hangar to judge running; its numbers and stock placeholders. **Maker**: Minerva. **Proposed tier**:
one ("still slated ideally for tier 1", Ryan, 16 November), as it or the Wild Boar comes down. **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md` (ten a maker, the orbits); the bible's
rows on a chassis's identity and on map rules (section 7).

## Claude's directions (4 November)

Claude's first lean, every weapon breaching and paid in negative cooling, is withdrawn by Ryan's
word above. The other directions stay for the session:

- **Under the wall.** On the map it tunnels under walls, the first ground machine past one. A map
  rule alone is a weak identity (section 7), so it would want a fight half too.
- **Through the front.** Its Direct blows reach the back rank under the front, or every weapon
  drills through the column (Penetrate granted, the Boar's shape). The Boar's Spillover already
  carries damage past a kill into the foe behind, so the mole parts from it by not needing the
  kill.
- **Deeper each bite.** A drill that bores: each hit on a foe it has already hit this fight deals a
  point more. The line frame's combo counts blows on anything; this counts blows on one foe.
- **The burrow** (from the bench, 17 October; Ryan called it "hilarious"). Spent on itself, it goes
  under and every foe aiming at it re-picks. That form was broken on the spider and was a reverse
  Lure without the choice. It was sound only with Phased's rule as it then stood (only before it
  acts; since 16 November Phased is granted as the round ends and the machine acts through it),
  and it is the time-shifter's shift (`aphelion_time_shifter.md`).

## Mock-ups and rough kits (15 November)

Ryan, 15 November, asking for look tests of the chassis that have a direction: "The mole is the
least far along, maybe just mock that one up a couple different ways and a couple rough kits for
it and we'll see if any have potential."

**The looks** (`docs/looks/minerva_mole.png`, built by `tools/lowforge/looks/mole_looks.py`; look
tests, never content until the chassis lands), three ways, his to pick, mix or send back:

- **A, the drill tank**: a barrel hull between two treads tapering into one great spiral cone
  with a hazard collar, a small cab on the back; about 70 wide and 118 long.
- **B, the tunnel-borer**: a full-face cutter head, its spokes carrying picks and its rim raked
  teeth, on a round body in two sections with a collar of rams between them, four short crawler
  pods and a spoil conveyor out of the tail; about 53 wide and 128 long.
- **C, the digger**: a low wedge, its hump aft, with two spade hands of four thick claws on stub
  arms beside a small snout drill, treads under its rear two thirds; about 100 wide across the
  claws and 93 long.

Each carries three placeholder mounts clear of the digging gear, and the drills and the cutter
head turn on joints; none has an act or a gait. A and B run long beside the Honey Badger's 72 or
so, and B's conveyor reads as a ramp from the codex angle.

**Three rough kits** (Claude's, for him to move; every number a placeholder; each read under the
reboot, the game's rule since 15 November: the rules' The reboot and the refit brief's section
22). Any of them can wear any of the looks.

1. **The drill stays in**, a hitter for the long fight.
   - The drill is its locked weapon, Direct and melee, small and many-toothed (2 ×2 at 3 heat).
     The foe it last damaged is its hole. As each round opens the drill strikes its hole again,
     unasked: no die and no heat, a plain blow as the Juggernaut Drive's is (no keyword, no
     Shock read). The drill comes out when the mole fires a weapon at another foe, or as the
     foe falls.
   - The passive: every weapon the mole fires into its hole deals a point more a blow. So its
     hardpoints want strikes, and the rule is no closed loop between a passive and its own
     locked piece (the bible's identity row).
   - The decision: whom to sink the drill into, and when a second foe is worth pulling it out
     for, since getting it back in waits on the die.
   - What is new: a second thing a round, paid in commitment, where the action economy's homes
     pay in a banked resource, a cooldown or an ally's turn.
   - Where it is blank: a pack of small foes, whose holes die under it. The Kite is the short
     fight's machine; this is the long one's (a flanker, a massive foe, an elite, a boss).
   - Under the reboot: nothing in it reads regen, cooling or a shield gain. The free turn needs
     no spend, so a mole that reboots keeps drilling.
   - Holes: free damage every round wants a limit (his taste line on riders), here one foe and
     the drill's own number; whether the free strike should spend a Shock the squad laid
     (Claude's lean: plain, so it never eats a setup); the bot would have to value the hole.
2. **Dug in**, a tank by staying put.
   - The passive: as a round opens its die does not roll. It stands, locked, on the entry it
     spent last, and while it stands there the mole is dug in: 2 more armour. It is unlocked
     into a reroll like any die; rerolled, it is dug out until the next round opens. A fight's
     first roll is a roll like any. The drill is a plain locked weapon: the kit needs no locked
     rule.
   - The decision: stay (the same entry again, certain, behind plate, its heat climbing) or dig
     out (one of the team's rerolls, the plate gone for the round, some other entry).
   - What is new: it reads the die's own last state, which no machine does (the roster holes'
     line). An own reroll (the Trim Tabs) is a free dig-out; a cool entry or equipment held is a
     Shoring Jack or a lure every round, on schedule; a cooldown piece fights it, held dark
     every other round.
   - Under the reboot: its heat never leaves, so a held basic gun stands at the maximum on its
     third round and the gauge paces the hold. A reboot spends nothing, so whether it keeps its
     entry and its plate through one is open (Claude's lean: it does, the die untouched, which
     makes its bare turn less bare; his to rule).
   - Holes: the best line may be to find the best entry and sit on it, and whether heat, a
     cooldown and a front that changes break that often enough is play's to say; it takes the
     roll off one machine (the Cassowary's die never rolls either, by stepping); flat armour at
     or over a tier's blows is immunity (the lure tank's hole and section 12's floor of 1), and
     2 over a base of 1 shrugs off tier one's rank and file.
3. **It comes up under them**, an ambusher, and the long shot.
   - Its locked piece: the Burrow, equipment spent on itself, cooldown 1. It lays Phased's wait,
     so as the enemy's turn ends it goes under, Phased through the next round and acting while
     it is (Phased as the rules have it since 16 November); the first weapon it fires there is
     Spun Up (2 more a stack, two stacks) and reaches any foe.
   - The decision: reading the next round. No foe picks it while it is under, so a dive moves
     next round's blows onto its allies; it dives when the squad can take that, and comes up
     where the fight wants one big blow.
   - Under the reboot: nothing in it grants shield, regen or cooling. A Phased machine may
     reboot, and that reboot is the safe one (the rules' reboot), so the Burrow is also its
     own cover for a reboot, two actions spent for a recovery nothing reaches.
   - Holes: a tank that sends its blows to its allies is a tank backwards (the burrow above, a
     reverse Lure) and off Minerva's line ("Everything is a tank, even the ones with legs");
     Phased is the shadow panther's orbit by his word of 11 November; its rhythm waits on one
     entry coming up.

Kits 1 and 2 stand at tier one on basics alone, a hitter and a body; kit 3 is a tier two's.

**The bench**, one line each with what set it aside, his to overrule:

- Every weapon breaching: his word of 4 November, above.
- The can-opener, its blows stripping a foe's armour for the fight and for the whole squad: the

- Bite and hold, the foe its drill is in aiming at the mole: the redirection row. A standing

- A tunnel under an ally to take its blow: the same row. An interpose comes back only on a

- Through the front, Penetrate granted or Direct reaching the back: the Wild Boar's shape in the

- A tunnel that opens a column to the squad's Direct fire: Exposed's reach half, which three

- A drill that winds up while it is left unspent: a charge is the siege battery's question, and

- Under the wall, the one ground machine past one: a map rule alone is a weak identity (the

## The glue (16 November)

Ryan, 16 November, on the mock-ups and the kits: "I'm not sold on the drill tank but that's not a
fault of your renders. It just needs a hook that isn't just "drill tank." Some of your ideas are
good, but it needs a glue to hold it together that isn't already on the design docket. It's still
slated ideally for tier 1, so that's part of the challenge".

Claude's second round: three glues for him to move, each one line that a tier one carries on
basics alone, with what hangs off it and its hole. None is another design's identity; where a
neighbour stands close it is named.

1. **The dive: its reboot is its move.** The round after it reboots, the mole acts twice: after
   its first spend its die rolls again.
   - What hangs off it: going under (the reboot, the machine digging itself in), coming up under
     them (the two actions), the drill and the claws as how it digs. Setup and cash in one round,
     the second action the action economy's list wants a home for, paid with the turn it gave
     (an action moved in time).
   - The decision: when to give the turn. Any machine reboots when it must; this one also dives
     by choice, to come up with two actions where the fight wants them.
   - Read as a system: a reboot costs a turn and a bare turn. This gives the turn back and keeps
     the bare one, so what threatens a rebooting machine threatens it at every dive (a pick that
     finds the broken shield), and a dive every other round is no shelter: half its enemy turns
     land on bare hull.
   - Its dials, his: the other cost in place of the turn (nothing picks it while it reboots,
     Phased's rule, the picks moving to its allies: a body, and the tank backwards again); or
     plate in place of either (dug in, armour while its shield is Broken, the reboot's turn
     included: a read of Broken that no machine has, and the lure tank's hole, flat armour at a
     tier's blows).
   - Neighbours: the Whip Scorpion is paid for a passed turn, a release on every foe (a reboot
     is no pass); the covers for a reboot in the briefs (the Barrier and Ward support, the
     time-shifter, the Magpie) are an ally's.
2. **Behind their line: it comes up at their backs.** Its Direct weapons reach the back rank,
   and a front-rank foe only where no one stands behind it: Direct's rule, mirrored on one
   machine.
   - What hangs off it: the tunnel is why it is there, the drill bites the back rank, and every
     Direct basic is a back-rank gun on it.
   - The decision: the squad splits the pack, the front the others' and the back its own, and
     its die says which it can reach.
   - Its hole: Indirect reaches the back already, so what it sells is Direct's numbers there and
     what it pays is the front: a reach perk, as "ignores armour" was an armour perk. Exposed
     hands the same reach to the whole squad for one foe.
3. **Undermining: what it hits shelters nothing.** A front-rank foe the mole has damaged this
   round covers no one: the foe behind it stands in the squad's Direct reach until the round's
   end.
   - What hangs off it: the sap. It digs the ground out from under their front and the squad
     fires through the gap, so the squad's order is the play: the mole first, then the guns it
     opened for.
   - Its hole: its product is Exposed's reach half by another road (the blocker struck, where
     Exposed marks the foe behind), and three machines set Exposed. A hitter whose worth is its
     allies' guns leans to support, which the tiers' sort keeps off tier one.

Claude's lean is the dive: the one glue built on the reboot, the rule every tier one now has to
teach, and the one that turns a machine's worst turn into its best. Any of the three looks
carries any of them; the dive suits the digger, whose claws are how it goes under.

Set aside with its rule, his to overrule: undermined ground, a point more for each empty slot
round its target (the Lammergeier's row: "A chassis based around opening an empty spot isn't a
good design").

## The tempo bruiser (16 November)

Ryan, 16 November, on the three glues: "The drill tank reboot is cool, not sure how to connect it
with "drill tank", but it's a great hook for a Minervan machine: tempo bruiser. Maybe can be
tweaked a bit." So the dive is the slot's hook, its role a tempo bruiser, and the drill is
open. Claude's round on it, for him to move:

**Read as a system.** The dive gives back the turn every other machine pays for a reboot and
keeps the bare enemy turn, so:

- Heat stops being its pace and becomes a burst's budget. It opens every double round cold, so
  its two spends together must fit under its maximum; past it, the overheat breaks the shield
  it has just got back and burns hull at that END TURN. So it wants the hottest guns, the big
  numbers other machines cannot sustain, two of them a burst: a build question that is its own.
- Its cadence reads the foes' blows. Diving every other round is bare at every other enemy turn
  with a whole shield at the rest; diving seldom is bare once and a shield that wears down. At
  tier one's small blows the slow cadence loses less hull, against big blows the fast one does,
  and the fast one keeps it cold for the hot guns: no line wins everywhere.
- Minerva's plate is what pays for the bare turns: its base armour stands while its shield is
  down. So a bruiser's hull and base armour, numbers rather than a rule, are its toughness.
- Its threats are the foes that find a broken shield (the exposed aim) and big single blows on
  its bare turns, and the counterplay on its side is choosing when to dive; that is the system
  the defence side's pass sizes the foes against.

**How it ties to the drill.** The reboot is the drill's act, never a pause: while it reboots it
bores into the ground and the hull settles, still in the fight; as the burst round opens it
comes up with the drill screaming. That is a model's act, judged running once there is one.
If that does not land, the hook needs no drill: the slot is Minerva's tier one, and its look can
follow the hook (a heavy brawler that winds up and unloads), a new round of looks once he says.

**Tweaks, his to take or leave.**

1. *As is* (Claude's lean): the round after it reboots it acts twice, its die rolling again
   after its first spend. Two actions bunch the tempo: a brace and a blow, or two kills before
   the foes act again.
2. *The drill's form*: the round after it reboots, its first weapon strikes twice. Simpler for a
   tier one and tied to the drill, but damage alone, one target and heat paid once, so it is
   the stronger per heat and the poorer in choices.
3. *Fight, then dive*: it may reboot after its first spend, so its reboot costs no turn and
   nothing bunches. The steady form, if a double round is too swingy at tier one; less tempo,
   so less of what he named.
4. *Dug in*, a dial on any of them: Armoured 1 while it reboots. It softens the bare turn, and
   at tier one's blows of 2 or 3 a point of armour takes a third to a half of each, so it tips
   the cadence to the fast one against most of tier one; Claude's lean is the numbers above
   instead.

## The burrow and the Inertia Drill (16 November)

Ryan's design, the same day, in the dive's place:

> Mole: Instead of Reboot, it Burrows. While burrowed, it gains gains a unpurgable buff called
> "Burrowed" that grants 1 Armour, enough Shield Regen and Cooling to recover fully in say, 3
> rounds, and all its die faces change to its locked piece, the Inertia Drill, and it remains
> burrowed until it uses it. At the start of every turn while burrowed, it gains a stack of
> Conserved Momentum.
>
> Inertia Drill: Equipment, direct. Consumes all Conserved Momentum and grants a stack of
> Applied Momentum for each one. Applied Momentum decays 1 at the start of the target's turn.
> Here's where we need to pick a shape, or shapes that are upgrade options:
>
> -Applied momentum grants Exposed before it decays. So it's an extended exposed.
> -Applied momentum grants 1 Brittle and Pinned before it decays.
> -Applied momentum deals damage equal to its stacks before it decays.
> -Applied momentum grants 1 Ablate and Seared before it decays.
>
> Applied Momentum is removed upon Burrowing. The Inertia Drill is only available while
> burrowed, it's not on the unburrowed die.
>
> Thematically, it's a rocket drill. It returns to the Mole when it burrows, taking the debuff
> with it.
>
> One thing I'm not certain of, is if applied Momentum should return to conserved momentum when
> it burrows. If the Regen and cooling it gets while burrowed is too encouraging to stay
> Burrowed and avoid fighting (not that it's going to win that way), then we can scale it based
> on percent missing shield and percent heat.

**What follows from it, no question.** Burrowed, it is still in the fight and may be picked;
its armour is for that. Rerolls and locks do nothing for its die while every entry is the
Drill. The Drill is a mark the team laid, so a Ward turns it away and a foe's cleanse takes it,
as with the Mine. A drilled foe that falls takes what is left with it, so the Drill wants a
target that lives through its stacks: an elite, a boss, a massive foe. Seared has had no
carrier ("no piece lays it yet"), and the fourth shape would be its first; the Dingo's harpoon
carries Pinned today.

**Where his words give two answers or none** (Claude's lean on each, his to rule):

1. *What a burrow resets.* A Broken shield gains no shield by any method until a reboot's
   recovery, so Burrowed's regen cannot touch one unless burrowing lifts Broken; and with no
   reboot, the one break and the one overheat between reboots would never reset. Lean:
   burrowing is its reboot for both, lifting Broken at once and resetting the two; the shield
   is not emptied, since the slow recovery is the point; and like a rebooting machine it
   neither overheats nor burns at the END TURN it burrows.
2. *Its gate.* Lean: the reboot's, a turn with nothing spent, given whole.
3. *A cap on Conserved Momentum.* None is stated, and three of the shapes pay more than one for
   one: on a foe that lives through them, k stacks deal k + (k-1) + ... + 1 as damage, twice
   that past armour as Ablating held by Seared, and on the squad's blows the round after each
   decay +1, +2, ... +k with Brittle held by Pinned. So each turn under pays more than the one
   before, and the longest dig always wins. Lean: a cap of 3, the recovery's rounds.
4. *The damage shape's damage.* A blow (armour, a Barrier and a Shock read) or a tick past
   armour like Ablating's. Lean: a tick, since it lands at the foe's turn start and no strike
   carries it, so a foe's armour does not eat a stack of 1.

**The four shapes at a cap of 3** (one foe living through it; the Brittle line on three squad
blows a round): damage 3 + 2 + 1, so 6; Ablate and Seared 12, past armour; Brittle and Pinned
about +21 to the squad's blows; Exposed four rounds, of which only the armour half counts, since
the Drill is Direct and its target is already in Direct reach. Against a reboot, a dig of three
rounds spends four turns of its die (the burrow, three under, the Drill on the last) where a
reboot spends one; the three more buy no bare turn, armour 1 for three enemy turns, and the
Drill. Claude's mapping, on the locked pieces' branch shape (two directions, two steps):

- *Base*: damage equal to its stacks. Its own payoff, the plainest to read, and the smallest,
  right for a tier one's base.
- *The squad's direction*: Brittle 1 and Pinned, then the Drill reaching any foe with Exposed
  joining, so its target opens to the squad's Direct guns as well; Exposed is only worth its
  place once the Drill reaches past the front.
- *Its own direction*: Ablate 1 and Seared in place of the plain damage (double it, past
  armour), then the cap one or two higher.

**His two questions.**

- *Applied returning as Conserved on a burrow.* Claude's lean: no. With the return, momentum is
  never lost and climbs across burrows, a ratchet that pays a long fight more than one for one;
  and the loss is the decision the rocket drill sets up: pull it back to recover now and lose
  what is left, or leave it working and stay out. If he wants some carry, half of what is left.
- *The recovery tempting it to stay under.* What pays for staying is momentum more than the
  regen, so the cap is the first lever: with it, nothing grows past the third round and staying
  buys armour 1 alone. His scaling by what is missing then stops the other way of staying, a
  sponge: the recovery sized as it burrows, a third of what is missing a round for three
  rounds, so blows taken while under are not healed.

**His answers, the same day** (where the read above had him wrong, his word stands):

> -Yes, burrowed clears broken and overheated at the start of each turn while burrowed, just like
> reboot would. Its mechanically exactly like using Reboot every turn, it just doesn't explode
> its own shield when it does, and it doesn't automatically return to combat like reboot does.
> -If a machine uses Reboot, and it already has overheated stacks, it should take that damage.
> Heat is cleared when the shield comes back. Otherwise the heat penalty is probably not hard
> enough. I thought that's how it was built.
> -We can cap it at 3, sure.
> -Drill should be indirect always, that was a mistake on my part. Makes sense cause it tunnels.
>
> Theoretically, momentum is always lost. You drill a target, one of the stacks gets consumed on
> that turn. If you return to burrowed next turn, you'd get one back the start of turn after. It
> doesn't keep climbing. My thought behind this, is that feathering burrow-unburrow is probably
> way more engaging against swarms, and the first round is the most dangerous, so opening with a
> two-round Burrow gets you two to keep applying across the pack.
>
> My main concern with this design, is that it's SLOW. 3 turns is a LONG time. Otherwise I think
> it is very cool.

What they settle: a turn burrowed is a reboot's recovery of the two states, Broken and
Overheated cleared as each turn opens, with the shield never emptied and the way back the Drill;
Conserved Momentum caps at 3; the Drill is Indirect, so it reaches any foe and the Exposed shape
opens a back-rank target to the squad's Direct guns; what is left of Applied returns as Conserved
on a burrow, since a stack goes at every decay and the count holds rather than climbs (the read
above had it climbing; with the cap and the decay it cannot). The reboot's turn burns what a
machine carries past its maximum, every machine's rule: the first build spared it, and it is
built to his word on this branch (16 November), so a burrow's turn burns the same. Still open of
the four: its gate (the lean, the reboot's) and the damage shape's damage (the lean, a tick).

**It is slow, in numbers.** As he has it (a third of the recovery a turn, a stack a turn under,
the Drill its turn's action): a quick dig is two turns for one stack; a dig to the cap is four
turns (the burrow, two turns under, the Drill on the third) for three stacks, and the recovery is
whole on the same turn; feathering spends every turn on a burrow or the Drill and none on its
guns. A dig to the cap with the damage shape pays 6 over the four turns, where four shots of the
vanilla gun are 12.

Three levers, each a turn faster, Claude's for him to move:

1. *The dig is the first push*: a stack of Conserved as it burrows, so one turn under brings 2
   and two turns the cap.
2. *Recovery in two turns*: half of what is missing a turn, so two turns under is whole and cold
   on the turn the cap is reached; with 1, the two clocks line up.
3. *The Drill brings it up firing*: spent, it surfaces and its own die rolls at once and it acts
   again (Rapid's path), so the Drill's turn is no lost turn and a quick dig costs one turn, the
   reboot's price.

Claude's lean: 1 and 2, which keep his shape (the Drill its turn's action) and take a turn off
every dig: a quick dig is two turns for 2 stacks at half recovery, a full dig three turns for 3
stacks whole and cold, and his opening, a burrow in the first round, drills with 2 in the
second. 3 is the bigger step: it makes a dig a reboot's turn with the Drill on top, a better
reboot outright (no bare turn, armour 1, its guns' damage kept), so it is the one to hold until
his play says the first two are still slow.

## The kit as he set it (16 November)

His word on the round above: "I like the burrow change, go with 1 and 2. And yes, only on a
nothing spend, and make the damage a blow since I want it to scale with Brittle. In fact:

> Base: Flat 2 damage blow a turn. Lines up with our vanilla -1.
> Ablate branch: Change damage to Ablate 2 (but it decays), and then second upgrade adds Seared.
> Squad branch: ADDS brittle 1 and pinned to the blow. Second upgrade adds exposed."

So the mole's kit, whole (his names; every number a placeholder):

- **The burrow**, in its reboot's place: only on a turn it has spent nothing, and its whole
  turn. As it burrows it gains a stack of Conserved Momentum, and what is left of its Applied
  Momentum comes back to it as Conserved; the cap is 3. Its burrow's turn burns what it carries
  past its maximum, as a reboot's does.
- **Burrowed**, a buff no Purge takes: armour 1; its die's every entry the Inertia Drill; as each
  of its turns opens, Broken and Overheated cleared as a reboot clears them, half of what was
  missing as it burrowed comes back to its shield and goes from its heat (so two turns under is
  whole and cold, and a blow taken under is not mended), and a stack of Conserved Momentum. Its
  shield is never emptied, and it stays under until it spends the Drill.
- **The Inertia Drill**, equipment, Indirect, its one entry while burrowed and none on its die
  above ground: it consumes all its Conserved Momentum and lays as many stacks of Applied
  Momentum on the foe, and the mole comes up. At the start of each of that foe's turns, Applied
  Momentum strikes it with a blow of 2, then a stack decays. A burrow takes the drill back,
  and what is left of the Applied with it.
- **Its upgrades**, on the locked pieces' branch shape: the Ablate branch, Ablate 2 in the
  blow's place (the Ablating decaying as Ablating does), then Seared with it; the squad branch,
  Brittle 1 and Pinned added to the blow, then Exposed with them.

What follows, built so and no question: the blow is a plain blow, as the Juggernaut Drive's
is, so the foe's Brittle adds to it and its armour and its Barrier take it, and it reads no
Shock, since no weapon's strike carries it; so on the squad branch each turn's blow carries the
Brittle the turns before laid. A Ward turns the Applied away and a foe's cleanse takes it, as
with the Mine; a drilled foe that falls takes what is left with it.

The rhythm: a burrow, then the Drill the next turn with 2 stacks at half recovery, or a turn
more under for 3, whole and cold; burrowed in the first round, it drills with 2 in the second.
What a drill of k stacks delivers on a foe that lives through it (k = 1, 2, 3): the base 2, 4,
6; Ablate 2, decaying, 3, 8, 15 past armour over twice as many turns; with Seared 5, 16, 33;
the squad branch's own blows 2, 5, 9, and a point more a stack on every squad blow on that
foe, the pin holding its Brittle while the drill is in. So each of the Ablate branch's steps
more than doubles the one before at the cap: Seared is the step to watch if it reads too much.
