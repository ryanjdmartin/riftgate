# Brief: the fan-boat (proposal, back to the board)

The session reworks the fan-boat hovercraft, whose weapons all fire its deck gun's shot with
their own keywords, so that its identity shows at its tier.

**Status**: settled in chat (Ryan, 19 October: versatility of reach, the shape by the aim, tier
one), the go pending; open: the deck gun's number and name, the stock. **Maker**:
Wainwright & Sutler. **Proposed tier**: one in its proposal; two in the analysis and the
reshuffle. **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 4.

## Where it stands (19 October)

- **What it is** (Ryan, 19 October): the maker's versatility in a third form, of reach, beside
  the Clydesdale's (loadout) and the gunship's (stats); tier one, since it plugs holes in many
  draft compositions.
- **Passive**: everything it fires goes through its deck gun: each weapon fires the deck gun's
  shot, with its own keywords.
- **The deck gun** (locked; the Fowling Cannon was Claude's working name, the name Ryan's):
  Indirect, flat 15, heat 5, Ballistic. Fired at the back rank its shot punches through
  (Penetrate, the front foe of that column too); fired at the front rank it sprays (Spread, the
  row's neighbours). The shape follows the aim, so a column or a row is the decision on every
  shot. The number is the lever at tier one.
- **The die**: three hardpoints, a d4; stock basics (two basic guns, which become area shots
  at the deck gun's number, and a basic shield, which equipment being no weapon fires as
  itself).
- **Numbers** (placeholders): light, hover, move 50; hull 60, shield 30, armour 0, heat 60,
  cooling 10, field recharge 30.
- **Engine**: the passive rewrites every weapon it spends to the deck gun's number, reach and
  heat, one blow, the weapon's keywords, rule and tags kept; a keyword granted by the target's
  rank (a new condition), the die showing the shot as it will fire over the foe it is dragged
  over. While danger close stands an Indirect shot at the front rank costs the firer shield, so
  until the stock pass retires it the fan-boat pays shield to spray and nothing to punch.
- **Status spread** (Ryan, 19 October): it spreads statuses well, which steps on Aphelion's
  toes, and that is fine: status spread is no monopoly and no complete identity.

## The design (the proposal)

"Everything it fires goes through its deck gun: each weapon fires the deck gun's shot, with its
own keywords." Locked: the deck gun, Direct and flat. Ryan, 13 October: the shells, "and see
where it takes us".

The rest of the proposal (12 and 13 October; Claude's unless marked Ryan's):

- **The fantasy** (Ryan, 13 October): a simple hovercraft with a deck gun, and everything it
  fires goes through the deck gun. The rule it bends: a weapon's number, reach and heat are its
  own.
- **The shot**: the other guns lose their bodies and keep what they do. The shot is the deck
  gun's number, reach and heat, one blow (strikes fold into it); the weapon brings its
  keywords, any unique rule and its tags, so a tag reader still sees a Flame shell as Flame.
- **What it opens**: pieces small by number and rich in keywords become its best guns, from any
  maker: a Suppress shell holds a foe at 15, a Burn shell sets one alight, a Penetrate shell
  goes down the column, a Rapid shell fires and rolls again. Each round asks which shell to
  fire. The proposal set it at tier one, its stock basics all firing the same 15 until shells
  were found, which is why it went back (below).
- **The cost**: big numbers are wasted on it, Indirect reach too (the shot is Direct), and so
  are the modules that move a rolled range: the punt gun is flat.
- **Engine**: a passive that rewrites the entry of every other weapon it spends: the deck gun's
  number, reach and heat in place of the weapon's own, one blow (strikes fold into it), the
  weapon's keywords, unique rule and tags kept; the die shows the shot as it will fire.
- **The magazine, as weighed on 13 October**: the other guns fire as themselves and leave their
  keyword in the deck gun, which holds three, the oldest dropping out, so the firing order
  loads it; its entry shows the three chips. The deepest shape and the most assimilating, kept
  as an idea for a tier two or another machine. Its holes: it pays only when the deck gun comes
  up (one roll in four on a d4), loading takes rounds, the player carries its state (shown on
  the entry), and it is upside with no cost, its interest all in the sequencing.
- **Numbers and kit** (the proposal's placeholders, set under the whole-block shield rule):
  light, hover, move 50; hull 60, shield 30, armour 0, heat 60, cooling 10 (the fan), field
  recharge 30. Locked: the deck gun (the Fowling Cannon, a working name), Direct, flat 15, heat
  5, tagged Ballistic. Three hardpoints (a d4). Its stock, written for tier one: basics beside
  it, two basic guns and a basic shield.
- **Set aside along the way**:
  - Its Ballistic weapons also hitting the foes beside the target for half (pure upside, no
    interest), and an air-cooled shape beside it, every weapon it fires shedding 10 heat (12
    October).
  - The echo, the deck gun firing again whatever it fired last: the best gun twice, rewarding
    one big gun, the opposite of the shells (13 October; Ryan took the shells).
  - The sponge, the deck gun carrying every keyword on the die (pure upside, and a stack of
    chips no phone can read); the sum, the deck gun dealing every other weapon's number while
    the others never fire (one huge entry on a die of blanks); the hopper, weapons loading the
    deck gun, which fires them all (the Badger's already) (13 October).

## Ryan's direction (18 October)

- "The fan boat is obviously very unrefined, it's a nifty little thing but right now isn't
  earning it's tier 2 status."
- "Maybe we take the 'everything is indirect' angle and apply that to the fan-boat? Still needs
  more but at least it's a role direction."

Claude's reading, the same day: the deck gun becomes Indirect (a punt gun on a flat-bottomed
boat is the silhouette), and every weapon fires its Indirect shot at its number with the
weapon's own keywords. It passes the test that benched "every weapon Indirect" (free reach once
danger close goes): here the reach costs the number, since a Howitzer's 25 or a big flat gun is
wasted on it, and the deck gun's heat and danger close (while that rule stands) are the rest of
the price. It also answers why the machine went back: with the deck gun Indirect the basics show
the identity on their own (every basic reaches any foe), so tier one is open again and the
keyword shells are the growth, not the premise. The "more": as a tier one, the price of reach
sized right; as a tier two, the magazine (below) or the area half of the maker's lean, a punt
gun's spread of shot, which the bench marks as pure upside unless its price shows. Claude's
lean: tier one, the maker's light artillery (keyword shells at any reach), the heavy artillery
being the maker's tier-three hole (the README). The role is a direction, not a design: the
session proposes the rest in chat.

## Ryan's area question (19 October)

"Fan boat - is everything penetrate too bold? Might not be. Could also have two locked, one
that fires penetrate, one that fires spread? Or the same one that flips shapes by some
mechanism? With the fixed damage of the gun, it could be balanced, maybe?"

Claude's reading, the same day: not too bold at a fixed 15, since the fixed number is what
makes any shape balanceable (the per-foe hit never grows, so the ceiling is 15 times the foes
in the shape and a lone boss takes 15). The shape by the aim, a fourth form that is the second
and third without a mechanism: fired at the back rank the shot punches through (Penetrate, the
front foe of that column too); fired at the front rank it sprays (Spread, the row's
neighbours). No second locked piece ("goes through the deck gun" stops meaning one thing with
two), no flip state (a counter to track for a choice the aim makes for free), one sentence, and
a decision on every shot with full information. With area it earns tier two on its own (the
maker's light artillery; the tiers then 3-5-2, the README), and its stock carries a keyword
shell from the first fight. The hole to read: every status shell becomes a two-or-three-foe
status (a Burn shell burns a row, a Lure shell lures a column, a Suppress shell would stun two),
so the gate principle holds per piece (the Battery's counter-battery form would still stun only
the back-rank foe); beside the Baboon it is a Paradox engine, for the boss cells. The bench's
earlier forms (Indirect striking twice, a Ballistic splash for half) failed as pure upside; this
one pays in the number and the light hull, and the aim is its interest.

## Ryan's calls (19 October)

- "I think the point of the fan boat is to show W&S versatility in a third form. Clydesdale
  is versatility of loadout, gunship is versatility of stats, and this is versatility of reach.
  For that reason, I think it belongs in tier 1, as it can plug holes in a lot of draft comps.
  Boiler should be tier 2, so not both damage dealers are in 1. Plus, it's more complex. If the
  target based version can be done, then let's do that."
- "Obviously it's able to spread status well, and that can step on Aphelion's toes, but I think
  that's fine. Status spread isn't a monopy, nor is it a complete identity."

## Why it went back (the analysis, 14 October)

A tier-one chassis stocks basics beside its locked piece, and basics carry no keyword by
definition. At the tender the fan-boat would be four entries all firing the same flat shot, its
passive invisible until the first keyword blueprint is found; a tier one must show its identity
with basics alone. Two ways back: tier two, where it stocks tier-one keyword pieces and its
passive shows from the first fight; or a locked piece that carries the first keyword. The
proposal's own question points the same way: whether the magazine (the deck gun firing with the
last three keywords its other weapons fired) makes a better tier two.
