# Brief: the strafer (new chassis, decided)

The session designs the decided Kestrel tier one whose every weapon strikes twice at half. The
mechanic is decided; its numbers, locked piece and shape are open. "The strafer" is a working
label; the name and the look are Ryan's.

**Status**: decided (Ryan, 14 October: "Love the [strafer], lock that in and place it
accordingly"). **Maker**: Kestrel. **Tier**: one. **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; the
bible's row on Kestrel's next chassis (section 7); `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 8
(Kestrel's gaps, where the raider and the interceptor it replaced are);
`content/chassis.json` (sparrowhawk); `docs/rules.md` on strikes and armour.

## The design as decided

Every weapon it fires strikes twice at half: the Sparrowhawk's bargain in count instead of
magnitude. Each strike loses the foe's armour, so it slaughters trash and bounces off a Golem:
a detachment the map can read ("send it at the goblins, not the knights"). Riders per blow love
it (a burn per strike lays more stacks than one blow would). It is Kestrel's second tier one,
so Kestrel's tender deal varies, and the chassis-level answer to multi-strike kit (Ryan, 14
October: "rapid and multihit weapons (which we have none of and badly need, I think)"; the
Maxigun is the one multi-strike piece today).

## How it came about

The analysis (section 8) proposed a raider (shield spent for damage) as Kestrel's detachment.
Ryan found it fights itself as a solo unit; Claude withdrew it from that niche and proposed the
strafer in its place (14 October). The raider returns as its own direction
(`kestrel_shield_spender.md`).

## The session (15 October)

- Claude proposed: the passive halves the weapon's own number and the attacker's modifiers
  count whole on every strike, so everything counted per blow counts twice, the armour it
  loses included (Slay the Spire's Strength on a multi-hit card). Per strike: Burn a stack a
  strike (a gain on strikes of 10 or less), Primed doubled, Suppress two tries through Numbed,
  Shock spent by the first strike at half (the Sparrowhawk's opposite), Paradox once a spend
  as now, Spread and Penetrate on every strike, a multi-strike piece's strikes doubled (the
  Maxigun's three to six). The die shows "8 ×2" with the Maxigun's mark, and dragged over a
  foe the number a strike deals with its armour off. With basics alone the passive gains
  nothing: the basics carry no keyword or strikes, almost no foe carries armour (the four
  big foes, the Zombie Knight and the bosses, at 5), and every strike of a spend lands on the one target
  today, so a strike after a kill is lost on the dead foe. Claude's lock for it: a Rapid
  self-buff of Overcharged.
- Ryan, 15 October: "halve base damage only, yes": what is printed on the entry halves,
  rounded up; buffs, passives and the foe's own conditions count on every strike. Chain
  repeats the whole spend, both strikes, as the engine already does ("I'm not too worried
  since chain is a unique up for adjusting"). As a general rule a multi-hit weapon strikes the
  same target, "because it's intuitive". A locked component could offer retarget-on-kill; a
  global rule that a kill by a Direct multi-hit carries to the back rank, or the passive
  carrying it, he raised unsettled ("I'm not sure"). Multi-hit with the Wild Boar's Spillover
  is flagged for review from the same point. Tier one stands, since its identity is hitting
  twice and what to do with that is plain; no Rapid Overcharge, which "just gives the unit a
  damage identity when it doesn't necessarily have one to start": it can stack on-hits, proc
  effects and flat damage, all viable.
- Claude, 15 October, on where retarget-on-kill lives: the locked piece. The global rule
  follows from the hole rule (a kill opens its column's back to Direct), but it hands the
  Boar's verb (damage past a front-rank kill carries behind) to every multi-hit piece, more
  of it with every one the stock pass adds, and on the strafer, where every weapon is
  multi-hit, it makes every entry a column-breaker: a fixed identity of the kind the
  Overcharge was turned down for. In the passive it is two ideas on one passive and the Boar's
  verb on a second chassis. On a locked piece the general rule stays intuitive and the
  exception is printed where it lives; a locked piece is never a blueprint, so it never
  reaches the Boar; it is a targeting trait, not damage, so the other entries stay open; it
  pays with basics alone, as a finisher whose second strike is not lost; and the tier row's
  locked piece needs no rewording. Its strike goes where Chain's jump goes (the next foe in
  reach), not behind, so it walks no column of the Boar's, and section 12's open question on
  Chain's jump (reach order as now, or the weakest in reach) settles both. It differs from
  Chain in size: it moves only strikes already paid for, where Chain fires the whole attack
  again. Flat, so a drag shows whether the first strike kills.
- The Boar's flag, as the engine stands: a multi-hit weapon on the Boar spills only the
  killing strike's overkill into the foe behind, and the strikes after it land on the dead
  foe and are lost, though its passive says that damage past the kill carries behind.
  Claude's lean: those strikes spill too, a fix on the Boar alone that reads its passive as
  written and adds no rule. Ryan's call.

- Ryan, 15 October, the second round: the Boar is fixed as described, "All its damage or
  subsequent strikes spill over"; retarget-on-kill "can then be ignored entirely, and just be
  a Boar thing". A locked weapon makes sense, in place of a carry on a kill: "Ranged damage,
  x2 (so 4 with the passive), each shot chooses a new, random target. So it will either spray
  over the frontline, or if well-planned, it can punch through the frontline and hit the back
  3 more times." The passive shows its multiplier on the entries, "that should be
  self-evident".
- Claude, 15 October, reading the lock: rolled ("ranged" in the code's sense) and Direct, since
  punching through needs a reach that opens as the front falls; the first strike lands where
  it is dragged, so the punch is planned (a weapon is spent on a foe), and each strike after it
  draws at random among the foes in reach at that moment, the same foe allowed again (so a lone
  foe left behind a hole takes all three); reach is read again between strikes, a kill clearing
  its slot at once (the engine clears the dead only after a spend today); a printed property
  with the star, the general rule unchanged. The random pick passes section 2's test as the
  boiler's random blast did: the drag lights every foe in reach, and the player shifts the odds
  by what falls first. Engine work: reach per strike, the random pick, and the bot's value of a
  spray (its mean over the foes in reach).

- Ryan, 16 October: the reading stands; "Choose first strike, subsequent random."

## The proposal for the go (Claude, 18 October)

Claude's proposal, in chat first; every number a placeholder, the name and the look Ryan's.

- **Shape.** Kestrel, tier one, a light flyer on big boosters, move 50: the fast jet the makers
  table promises and the detachment the map can read. A hovercraft if Ryan's silhouette is
  one: hover (water fast, rough and ruins slow) would part the two Kestrel tier ones on the map
  as the passives part them on the die, where two light flyers at 50 read the same.
  Hull 70, shield 60, field recharge 30 (fight a small pack, fly off, two map turns refill
  it), heat maximum 60, cooling 5, armour 0; three hardpoints beside the lock, a d4, the
  Sparrowhawk's size once the Pinion Driver locks; two module slots. The Sparrowhawk's
  register with ten more hull, since it fights small packs alone.
- **The passive**, as the codex would print it: every weapon it fires strikes twice at half.
  What is printed on the entry halves, rounded up, and strikes twice; buffs, passives and the
  foe's conditions count on every strike, and so does its armour. The entries show the halved
  number with the strikes mark the Maxigun wears ("5 ×2", "5–8 ×4"), dragged over a foe the
  number one strike deals with its armour off; the hold's line reads the rule.
- **The lock, the spray** (Ryan's description, 15 and 16 October): rolled, Direct, 10 to 15,
  two strikes, heat 5; under the passive 5 to 8 ×4. The first strike lands where the die is
  dragged; each strike after it draws at random among the foes in reach at that moment, the
  same foe again allowed, reach read again between strikes with a kill clearing its slot at
  once. A printed property with the star; the die shows the first strike's number, as Spread
  does (Ryan, 17 October).
- **Stock**, stand-ins until the stock pass: the Laser Carbine, the Twin Autoguns, the
  Laminar Deflector. No vent: its heat is the base weapon's, paid once a spend.
- **The arithmetic**, mean damage a spend (today's numbers; "plain" is the weapon on any
  other machine): at armour 0 the strafer deals the base weapon's damage for the base heat,
  so nothing is gained or lost there; its gains are the riders (a Burn strike lays a stack
  per 10 rounded up, so two half blows lay two stacks where one whole blow laid one or two),
  flat buffs (Overcharged and Primed count per strike, so the Overcharge Coil is worth twice
  on it: the Puffin's coil is its route in, Kestrel's locked pieces working together) and
  multi-strike pieces (the Maxigun's six bites, and Vitriol's stacks if it ever meets the
  funnel-web's windows); its loss is armour, paid twice a weapon.

  | Weapon | Plain, armour 0 / 5 | Strafer: a strike | Strafer, armour 0 / 5 | Strafer, armour 5, two Overcharged stacks |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Laser Carbine 10 flat | 10 / 5 | 5 ×2 | 10 / 0 | 20 |
  | Twin Autoguns 5–15 | 10 / 5 | 3–8 ×2 | 11 / 1 | 21 |
  | Slugthrower 15–30 | 22.5 / 17.5 | 8–15 ×2 | 23 / 13 | 33 |
  | Maxigun 5–10 ×3 | 22.5 / 7.5 | 3–5 ×6 | 24 / 0 | 54 |
  | the spray 10–15 ×2 | 25 / 15 | 5–8 ×4 | 26 / 6 | 46 |

  The read: a halved basic (5) equals today's armour 5 exactly, so with basics alone the
  strafer cannot scratch a Zombie Knight, the four big foes or a boss. That is the design's
  "bounces off a Golem" at its harshest, and a hole for the start (below).
- **The spray's spread.** With N foes in reach the aimed foe takes 1 + 3/N strikes on
  average: 1.75 of the four against a full front, all four against a lone foe. So on a lone
  boss the spray is a plain 5 to 8 ×4 and against a full front it is the volume piece the
  brief named; a hole in the front turns the odds toward the foe behind it, which is the
  punch Ryan described, planned by what falls first.
- **Engine work.** A passive effect that halves the printed number (rounded up) and doubles
  the strikes on the effective face (`Effects.effective_face` carries the reach, the granted
  keywords and a raised ceiling today and would carry these two, so the die, the bot and the
  resolver all read one face); the spray as a printed property with its random pick per strike
  and reach re-read between strikes (today every strike of a spend lands on the one target's
  hit list and the dead are cleared after the spend); the bot's value of a spray, its mean over
  the foes in reach; each tested with synthetic passives.
- **What the runner reads.** Alone against the packs (the trash claim) and the four big foes
  (the bounce); beside the Puffin with the Overcharge Coil (the doubling of a flat buff); the
  spray's pick share against a full front and against a thinned one.

## The line review (Claude, 20 October)

Under the action economy's direction (the bible's row, section 5) the strafer is the home of
the many-blows want: a design that wants many blows is pointed at strikes, and this is the
machine whose every weapon strikes. It stands as proposed for the go. The review re-flags the
start's hole with its root named: with basics alone it gains nothing against bare foes (a
basic halved and struck twice is the basic) and loses against plate (a halved basic is 5,
today's armour is 5), so its identity shows on the die (5 ×2, the spray) and its payoff waits
on riders (Burn and Flak a strike), flat buffs (the Puffin's Coil counted twice) and
multi-strike pieces, which are cross-maker by design; the root is section 12's scales
question, never an exception on the passive. The line review (`kestrel_line.md`) asks the
stock pass for a Kestrel multi-strike gun of its own (flat and hot, parted from the Maxigun),
so one payoff is Kestrel's. Nothing else moves.

## Holes already raised

- In a long fight it pays two armours a weapon; against heavy plate it is the wrong machine, by
  design. At today's numbers the design's edge is a cliff (Claude, 18 October): a halved
  basic is 5, today's armour is 5, so with basics alone it deals nothing to a Zombie Knight (a
  Marsh pack foe) or to any of the four big foes, and a tier one is meant to stand in a start
  with basics alone. Section 12's scales question (our armour of 2 or 3 first, then the foes')
  decides whether a halved basic bites or bounces on plate, and the stock pass's basic numbers
  too; until one moves, its flat buffs are the way through plate (the table above).
- Two light flyers at 50 (the Sparrowhawk and this) read the same on the map though not on the
  die; a hovercraft silhouette would part them there too (Claude, 18 October; Ryan's call).
- Riders double: Burn lays a stack per 10 of a blow, rounded up, so two half blows can lay more
  than one whole blow; Spread and Penetrate fire per strike. The arithmetic per strike is in
  the session above.
- Beside the Sparrowhawk, both Kestrel tier ones change every weapon; they part on magnitude
  against count.
- The review (Claude, 16 October) found the engine applying Suppress per strike, so a Suppress
  piece on it tried twice a spend, and a landed first strike's second try mostly added Numbed
  (four from one spend). Ryan, 17 October: "That suppressed interaction is silly. A suppressed
  foe should not gain numbed from being re-supressed, since suppressed doesn't stack." Built
  that day: a suppress on a foe already suppressed for that action does nothing, so the second
  strike's try counts only when the first was shrugged off, a small gain of the strafer's own.
- The review raised a random strike of the lock spending a Shock at half, and the die showing
  the first strike's number only. Ryan, 17 October: with one of four shocked, firing the spray
  is an understood risk, "no different than using a cleave", not random that takes away "since
  you still hit that guy for normal damage", and sometimes the result hoped for; showing the
  first strike's number is correct, "exactly the same as spread". Both stand as designed.
