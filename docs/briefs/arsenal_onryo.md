# Brief: the Onryō and its Grudge (proposal reworked into a direction)

The session settles what the Onryō's curse does, whether a damage over time or the team's flat
on-hit, and what lays it.

**Status**: direction (14 October). **Maker**: the Arsenal. **Proposed tier**: two. **Read
first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; "The design as proposed" below, with Ryan's calls and the
Pall in full; `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 4 (its rating); the bible's rows on retaliation
and thorns, and on damage redirection (section 5).

## The design (the proposal)

Passive: each blow a foe lands on a machine's hull gives that foe a stack of Grudge, 5 damage a
stack at its action, never fading, even after the Onryō falls. Locked: the Pall, a buff on an
ally moving the hull damage foes deal that ally onto the Onryō until the round's end (the
interpose the bible allows on a locked piece). Ryan, 12 October: Grudge is the build-around;
never popped for burst; for reliability it may stay after the machine dies.

## The analysis (14 October)

Back to the board: Grudge reads the roster's most crowded state (a blow on hull, also read by
the Armadillo's Hull, the Nemesis's rage and two modules), in the Arsenal's own house, at the
Nemesis's tier, so the two would compete for the same wounds; and its product is damage over
time in the crowded class. The Pall is new. The analysis suggested Grudge laid by the Pall (a
wound the Onryō takes for an ally curses the striker), so the machine that suffers is the one
that chose to.

## What the go said

- Ryan, 14 October: "i liked the onryo's curse being a unique status, maybe there's one that
  isn't a DoT, or maybe the DoT is ok and its the DoT capstone? maybe instead of hull read, its
  on debuff applied by an enemy?"
- Claude: the curse that fills an empty class is amplify for everyone: a foe that wrongs the
  team is marked and every blow on it lands harder, never fading. On the enemy-debuff trigger:
  better in principle, nothing reads it, but today only the Peak and the Waste lay debuffs on
  machines, so it would be dead in two domains of four; keep the wound until the foes' fresh
  start gives every domain a debuff on machines. The Pall stays.
- Ryan, 14 October: "I want to hear the argument about not having multiple DoTs in the game,
  especially if conditional. Burn is the only one, isn't it? The onryo's niche is either adding
  a reliable DoT for a team that doesn't have one, or a soft synergy with Burn as a way of
  doubling down on a DoT team. That said, If we went with a damage amp, I would probably make
  it a flat number per stack, because we already have a multiplier with Shock. A flat number
  reads as an on-hit and it's not something we have. It also reduces the concern of snowballing
  a boss, to a point."
- Claude: Burning is the only one today, and what the engine lets a second damage over time
  differ in is decay, spread and source; a DoT that ticks a stack at the foe's action is
  Burning with another decay and another chip. The one way a second DoT is a new kind is
  reading something else, which is the spider's Venom (a share of current hull), so the
  roster's second DoT should be that one and the Onryō's should not. On the amp: a flat number
  per stack, Shock staying the multiplier that spends, Grudge the on-hit that stays; a cap on
  stacks, never fading inside the cap.

## The design as proposed (12 and 13 October)

Claude's proposal for Ryan to move, except where marked as his; names are working names and
numbers the proposal's placeholders. It reads Grudge as a damage over time, the shape the go
moved away from (open item 1), and it was written under the whole-block shield rule.

**Ryan, 12 October**, as the proposal recorded his notes. First: as for Misharu but leaning to
retribution: perhaps still the hull-damage trigger, but it must open build space; perhaps it
absorbs damage dealt to other allies, for something. Second: the Onryō needs refinement. It
cannot always take its allies' hull damage; a guard may come back conditionally, on a locked
component, never as a core mechanic. Grudge is the thematic build-around, whatever it turns out
to be: perhaps a stacking damage over time on the foes that injure the Onryō or the team, so
not straight thorns. How does that open build space, or is another stacking debuff enough?
Third: no popping the Grudge for burst: a different playstyle, one the game has plenty of
already, and it cheapens the effect. If it needs reliability, it can stay after the machine
dies.

**The chassis**: Arsenal Instrument IX (sinister: retribution), tier two, medium, walker. Hull
120, shield 30, armour 0, move 30, heat maximum 60, cooling 5, field 20, two modules; a die of
five, one locked entry and four hardpoints. The name: the onryō, the Japanese spirit that
returns to avenge a wrong; Share Tech Mono has no ō (the Rajdhani and Barlow faces do), so
content may want Onryo.

**The passive**, the Martyrology Engine (a martyrology is the Church's register of martyrs and
their sufferings: it keeps the team's, and curses whoever caused them). As drafted: *while it
fights, each blow a foe lands on a machine's hull gives that foe a stack of Grudge: 5 damage a
stack at its action, past armour. Grudge never fades, even after the Onryō falls.*

- What counts: a foe's blow that puts hull damage on any machine while the Onryō fights; a
  blow that leaves the hull untouched earns nothing. The draft's words, "on bare hull or
  through a breach's overflow", are the whole-block rule's (open item 5).
- A stack a wounding blow, never a stack per 10: Grudge never fades, so Burning's rate would
  make one golem's fist four stacks for the rest of the fight.
- As a damage over time it ticks at the start of the foe's action, before it acts, past
  armour, like Burning, and even when the foe is suppressed; it never decays and nothing lifts
  it (a foe's cure might, once foes cure). The Onryō's fall stops new Grudge and lifts none.
- Not a trigger: bites (Corrupted), Volatile, an overheat, splash. None is a foe's blow.
- It is a debuff the team's machine put on, so it counts toward the Paradox and, once listed
  with the others, for the Susceptibility Tensor.
- Why it is not thorns (Claude, 12 October): nothing lands at the blow; the striker pays at its
  own actions, slowly, and only for wounds. A blow that never reaches the hull earns nothing,
  so a team built to block earns little and a megatank's team almost none.
- Claude's case for a second stacking debuff (12 October): its source differs from Burning's.
  Burning comes from the team's weapons, laid by spending dice, and burns out; Grudge comes
  from the foes' own blows, laid by being wounded and surviving, and lingers, so the build is
  around taking hits, which nothing else rewards with damage. Claude's argument of 14 October
  (above) weighs it against a second damage over time.

**The Pall** (a pall is the cloth over a coffin), the locked piece: a buff, an ally only.
*Until the round's end, the hull damage foes deal this ally is dealt to the Onryō instead.*

- The ally's shield and armour still come first; the hull loss after them moves.
- The moved wound lands straight on the Onryō's hull, past its shield and armour (it is the
  ally's wound, not a blow on the Onryō), and injures neither machine, as a bite does, so the
  Pall also shelters a fragile ally's components.
- Its striker still earns a Grudge.
- If the Onryō falls mid-round, the rest of the round's wounds land on the ally as usual.
- Aims, intents and arrows stay as they were shown; only the hull loss moves.
- The Warrior's planned Cover is the same piece on the foes' side.

**Stock**, four hardpoints: Meteor Artillery, the Plasma Arquebus, the Laminar Deflector, the
Proximity Klaxon.

**Build space, in four places**, for the wound trigger:

1. What it reads: every wound the team takes, so the team's make-up is the build: a team built
   to bleed (light armour, glass cannons, the Nemesis, which banks rage on hull damage anyway)
   curses more than a team built to block.
2. Where the wounds land: the Pall moves a fragile ally's wounds onto the Onryō; a lure (the
   Proximity Klaxon) draws a pack onto whichever machine can afford them.
3. What reads it: Grudge is a debuff the team put on, so the Susceptibility Tensor and the
   Baboon's Paradox read it.
4. Keeping it standing: it earns only while it fights, so plating and the Spall Liner (each
   wound stands up 10 shield) buy more curses, and its four hardpoints choose between guns and
   its own guard.

**Its cost**: no armour, a target the foes' exposed aim prefers, and the Pall puts its allies'
wounds on its own hull, which never heals in a battle: it is the one that suffers. **What it
answered**: the Waste. The Needlers breach every shield, and every breach earns its Needler a
Grudge.

**Engine and tests**, as proposed for the damage over time: medium. Grudge as a status that
ticks 5 a stack with no decay (`Effects.tick` already skips decay for `DECAY_NONE`), laid on
the striker where `_damage_mech` sees hull damage and knows the source (`_hull_damaged` takes
the machine only today), then `_foe_gained_status`; outliving the Onryō costs nothing, since
the stacks sit on the foe. The Pall: a machine buff, and the redirect in `_damage_mech`,
skipping the injury. The chip and its glyph. Tests: a stack a wounding blow; none on a blow
that leaves the hull untouched; the tick past armour, while suppressed, never fading, after
the Onryō's fall; no Onryō, no Grudge; the Pall moves the hull loss, injures neither, ends if
the Onryō falls, and still curses the striker. The draft's "a breach's overflow counts"
predates the shield rule.

**What the runner should read**: the Waste's cells, team rows beside fragile machines, and the
boss cells (long fights).

**The look**: Claude's (12 October), a crawler of long wrong-jointed limbs under a fall of
black cable, the Pall a mantle of black lamellar plates spread toward the ally it covers, a
homage to the crawling ghosts of Japanese horror (The Grudge); its detail is in git. Looks are
Ryan's.

## The bench, with what set each aside

- **The first Onryō**: an ally's wound Overcharged it. As for Misharu (Ryan, 12 October), the
  Arsenal needs archetypes other than Overcharge scaling.
- **All its allies' hull damage taken as a core mechanic**: too much (Ryan, 12 October: it
  cannot always take its allies' hull damage); the Pall keeps a conditional part.
- **The Requiem Pipes**: a locked weapon calling every Grudge in at once. Ryan's no (12
  October): a burst, a playstyle the game has plenty of, and it cheapened the curse. Grudge
  lifting at the Onryō's fall went with them; whether it outlasts the Onryō is open item 3.
- **Grudge spent by its next blow** (Claude, 12 October): the striker carried a Grudge the size
  of the wound it dealt, and the Onryō's own next blow on it dealt that much more, spending it;
  set aside as a third copy of Shocked. It is close to the go's on-hit lean, which differs: a
  flat number per stack on every team blow on the cursed foe, kept and capped, with Shock
  staying the multiplier that spends. The session decides whether the bench's reason still
  holds against the lean.

## Open for the session

1. The curse's kind: the flat on-hit (Claude's lean, Ryan's preferred amp shape), or a DoT
   after all (Ryan's two niches for one).
2. The trigger: a foe's blow that wounds any machine while the Onryō fights, the wound the
   Onryō takes through the Pall, or a debuff a foe lays (once every domain lays one).
3. The cap, and whether Grudge outlasts the Onryō.
4. The Pall's details (above).
5. The wound trigger under the shield rule: the proposal counted wounds when a standing shield
   blocked a whole blow and only a breach's overflow passed it; now every blow's excess past
   the shield reaches the hull, and a breach's whole blow does, so more blows wound.
6. If the curse stays a damage over time, whether the Bellows spreads it: the proposal kept
   Mastema's Bellows off Grudge unless Ryan wanted the Arsenal's two curses to spread together,
   and his 14 October niches for a DoT include "a soft synergy with Burn".

## Holes already raised

- Two Arsenal tier twos (the Nemesis and the Onryō) would both read wounds; they should part by
  payoff or by trigger.
- The greedy bot prevents wounds and values neither a curse laid nor the Pall's move, so the
  runner reads the Onryō as a worse tank until it does.
- A team built to block earns little Grudge, which is the tension in the design (the
  well-played team feeds it least).
- Grudge's chip needs a glyph and structure of its own beside Burning's, never hue alone.
- As a damage over time that never fades, Grudge piles up on a boss that wounds often: six
  wounds is 30 a round for the rest of the fight (the cap is open item 3).
- As a damage over time, Grudge and a Corrupted moved onto a foe (the Scapegoat Brand's shape
  D, `arsenal_mastema.md`) would be one shape twice; Ryan named Grudge, so that call is his.
- The lure route (build space, place 2: a lure draws a pack onto whichever machine can afford
  the wounds, each wound a Grudge) may sit against Ryan's taste line in the briefs' README, no
  reward for being struck that scales with the enemy's blows (said of tanks, 14 and 15
  October): does a curse per wound, invited by a lure, scale the same way?
