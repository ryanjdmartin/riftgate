# Brief: the interceptor (new chassis, proposal)

The session designs the Kestrel tier one that acts twice a round, Ryan's pick of 18 October
from the shield-spender's alternative kits, and finds the build hook its first kit lacked.
"The interceptor" is a working label; the name and the look are Ryan's.

**Status**: proposal (the passive Ryan's, 18 October; the kit Claude's, 20 October, its third
form, below the second; the fourth round, late that day, names the mark Exposed and widens the
gate to buffs). **Maker**: Kestrel. **Proposed tier**: one. **Shape**: a tilt-jet (Ryan, 20 October:
"agile and omnidirectional"). **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; `kestrel_line.md` (the
line review that gave it this brief); the second and third rounds of
`kestrel_shield_spender.md` (where it was kit 2); the bible's rows on the action economy
(section 5), on Rapid's limiter and on rerolls; `docs/rules.md` on reach and the round.

## What Ryan said

- 18 October: "first fire rapid is a great tier 1 design, but I don't want to give up on the
  shield-centric design." (The shield-centric design became the off-tank; this is the other
  tier one.)
- 19 October, on shapes: Kestrel was never given specific shapes, only "things other than
  dogfighters"; Claude's list for matching put the tilt-jet with swinging nacelles and the
  exposed-rotor helicopter against this machine.
- 20 October, on the first kit: "The unnamed interceptor as a tilt-jet makes sense, agile and
  omnidirectional. I think the exposed rotor can go to Aphelion after all, they have rotor
  precedence there. This kit is thin. We should take another look. What's the build hook? How
  does it open build space?"

## How it came about

Kit 2 of the shield-spender's second round (Claude, 18 October): its first weapon each round
is Rapid. The decision is the order of two spends, the second entry the die's; the price two
spends' heat a round and a small die. The holes raised then: tempo is the line frame's claim;
"twice" is the strafer's word too, strikes there and spends here; two spends at tier one wants
glass numbers; the Paradox and riders double a round.

The first kit (Claude, 20 October, morning): the passive as Ryan wrote it, and the dive as its
lock, a Direct gun of 15 that may be spent on a back-rank foe behind a standing front, the
front foe striking it as it passes. Its reasoning holds and moves into the second kit: the
designator first proposed was the Polarity Projector with a Kestrel name, a copy; Kestrel's
reach is through the front at a price, where the artillery makers' is Indirect and free; the
price scales with the front, so "dive past the goblin, not the knight" is read on the die; and
it takes the priority-target fantasy the Sparrowhawk's codex describes and its rework leaves
behind. What was thin: "the first weapon is Rapid" chooses no piece, so the three hardpoints
had nothing to be about, and the dive was one more gun.

## The direction's read

The action economy's row (the bible, section 5) allows Rapid for the cantrip, gated; the gate
the second kit takes is the row's "on a cold spend" shape turned to damage: a weapon that
deals none. Its price is the front foe's blow and two spends' heat, Kestrel's resource.

## The second kit, written out (Claude, 20 October)

Every number a placeholder; the name and the look Ryan's. The stock is by kind (Ryan, 20
October: the stock pieces are placeholders until the stock pass).

**The build hook, first.** Every weapon of no damage in the game is a cantrip on it: the
Shock setters, the probes, the lures, the mark, the web, whoever makes them. It cashes them
itself with the second spend, and its lock lights a back-rank foe for the whole team's Direct
guns. So its hardpoints are about something: one control piece from any maker, one Direct gun
to cash the paint, one vent for the heat of two spends; and the team's build changes around
it, since a Direct-heavy team gets a back-rank answer without artillery. It feeds the Baboon's
Paradox (a free status a round), the Sparrowhawk (a Shocked foe cashed at four times) and the
roster's thinnest class, soft control, which has no Kestrel piece and no enabler today.

**The frame.** A tilt-jet at move 50, Kestrel's pace. Hull 60, shield 50, field recharge 30,
heat maximum 60, cooling 5, armour 0. Three hardpoints beside the lock, a d4; two module
slots. Glass on purpose: two spends a round at tier one wants glass numbers, and the paint's
price lands on this frame.

**The passive**, as the codex would print it: a weapon it fires that deals no damage is Rapid,
once a round: the die rolls free onto another entry and the machine acts again. A gun first
earns nothing; a setup first earns the second spend.

**The lock, the designator dive** (working name): a weapon of no damage, Direct, heat 5, with
two printed properties (the star). It may be spent on a back-rank foe behind a standing front,
and as it passes, the front foe of that column strikes it: that foe's blow (its base attack
with its swarm, no factor, no rider), through the shield like any blow, an injury if enough
reaches the hull. The foe it is spent on is Painted (working name) for the round: it stands in
reach of Direct weapons, every machine's, as if its column's front were empty. Spent on a
front foe it paints nothing new and pays nothing, and is still a cantrip. A suppressed front
foe strikes nothing (the team's setup pays the way in); a Barrier takes the price; a massive
foe has nothing behind it; a Lured front foe still strikes, since the price is not its aim.
Painted is a status the team put on the foe (the Paradox counts it).

**The die.**

| Entry | Piece | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| I | the designator dive: Direct, no damage, heat 5 | a back-rank foe lit for the team, at the front foe's price; a cantrip |
| II | a Direct flat gun | the cash: the painted caster, or the front |
| III | a control piece of another maker (a Shock setter, a probe, a lure), or a cold rolled gun with basics alone | a second cantrip, or volume |
| IV | a vent | two spends a round heat it |

With basics alone the lock is its one cantrip, so its identity shows on a d4 one round in four
and after a reroll, the tier-one floor; the stock pass's control pieces are its ceiling.

**The round as played.** The designator showing: paint the caster through the front, pay the
front foe's blow, the die rolls again, and shoot the caster with the Direct gun, or leave it
lit for the team's guns and shoot the front. A control piece showing: lay it, roll again,
cash it. A gun showing: fire it, one spend, or reroll for a setup. The second spend is the
die's, so the first is the choice, and the choice is always a setup.

**The arithmetic.** A paint and a flat 10 is 10 on the caster for 15 heat and the price: a
lone goblin's 5, six goblins' 30 with their swarm, a Warrior's 30; its shield of 50 pays the
first dives and its hull the later ones, an injury once 15 reaches the hull. A paint and the
team's three Direct guns is a caster of 90 hull dead in a round, which is what an interceptor
is for. Two cantrips a round (the designator and a stocked setter) are two statuses and one
cash, the control build.

**With the team.** It wants a front softened or suppressed (the funnel-web's web, the
Armadillo's seal, a Barrier or an overshield before a dive), control pieces of every maker and
a Direct-heavy team to cash its paint. It gives the caster dead before its special, soft
control at no tempo, and a second spend's riders a round.

**Why it is Kestrel.** The price is risk read on the die (the maverick through the flak), the
reach is the maker's (through the front, never free), the tempo is Kestrel's lean gated as the
row asks, and its heat is two spends'. Rerolls and strikes are its siblings' currencies.

**Engine.** Rapid granted by the passive to a spend of a weapon with no number, once a round
(the engine grants keywords by passive today; the condition, a weapon of no damage not yet
spent this round, is new and small); the dive as a printed property: a reach exception on one
face (`targets_for_face`), a foe's blow in the player's turn (the path a death blow takes);
Painted as a foe status in `FOE_STATUSES` that `targets_for_face` reads for Direct; the bot's
valuation (the paint's worth to the team's Direct spends, less the price); synthetic passives
in the tests.

**What the runner reads.** Spends a round against the other tier ones; the paint's share of
its spends, the hull it costs and the Direct spends it opened; alone against the packs (the
glass) and the formations (the casters); beside a body; beside the funnel-web.

## The third kit, refined (Claude, 20 October, evening)

- Ryan, 20 October, on the second kit: "Interesting, possibly overdesigned? I feel like some
  of this can be refined, but there is a good nugget in here, I think."
- Claude, 20 October: the nugget as read, two things: setups are free on it, and a foe it
  paints is reachable by the whole team. The overdesign: the dive's price (a foe's blow in the
  player's turn, with four exceptions to learn) stacked under the gate. So the price goes and
  the designator is a plain setter with free reach, as the probe and the Klaxon already are.
  The machine in two sentences:
  - **The passive**: a weapon it fires that deals no damage is Rapid, once a round.
  - **The lock, the designator**: a weapon of no damage, Indirect, heat 5. The foe it is spent
    on is Painted for the round: it stands in reach of Direct weapons, every machine's, as if
    its column's front were empty.
  The stock by kind as before: a Direct flat gun to cash the paint, a control piece of another
  maker or a cold rolled gun, a vent. The round: paint the caster, the die rolls again, shoot
  it or leave it lit for the team and shoot the front; a stocked setter: lay it, roll again,
  cash it. The frame as before, a tilt-jet at 50, hull 60, shield 50, a d4. Its risk is now
  the glass and the heat of two spends; the dive moves to the bench as a priced reach for a
  smaller Kestrel if one is ever wanted. The levers if the paint is too strong: Painted
  readable by this machine's Direct weapons only, or its heat.

## The fourth round (20 October, late): Exposed, and the gate widened to buffs

- Ryan, 20 October: "Interceptor seems good. I think the mark should be called Exposed, a new
  debuff that lets it be targeted from anywhere and also makes the target take on-hit damage,
  and goes away at the end of the round. Possibly stackable, but also possibly rare, so not
  sure. Evaluate that and let me know what you think, as we have a few on-hit items hanging
  around, such as the new W&S flak debuff. Is there a way to generalize the interceptor
  passive to non-damaging weapons AND buff-only faces, while making it clear that doesn't
  mean vent or shield faces that also buff? What does our iconography say?"

**Exposed, evaluated (Claude, 20 October).**

- **The name is the game's own word, so it wins.** The vocabulary already holds Exposed, a
  formation word: a back-rank foe is exposed when the front slot in its column is empty, and
  exposed foes are in Direct reach. The engine reads it in one place, the reach check
  (`is_exposed` in `sim/skirmish.gd`, read by `targets_for_face`). The designator makes a foe
  Exposed: the same fact from the targeting side, so one word keeps one meaning and Painted
  dies. The records' cost is a sentence on the entry (a foe is also exposed while it carries
  Exposed, a debuff, until the round's end), a clause on the check (the column's front empty,
  or the foe carries it) and the status in the list the Paradox counts. The keyword that lays
  it takes the control keywords' shape, Expose: every foe the entry reaches and leaves
  standing is Exposed until the round's end, a chip on the entry beside Shock, Lure and
  Disrupt; the designator is then the probe's shape with a different chip (a weapon of no
  damage, Indirect, heat 5, Expose).
- **The on-hit half, declined.** The flat on-hit is Flak's kind: Wainwright & Sutler's
  signature status (Ryan, 19 October), every blow a point more a stack, the stacks halving at
  the round's end; the Prairie Dog row says "the flat on-hit is one kind, so Flak takes it and
  the Onryō's Grudge finds another shape". An Exposed that stacks a point a blow is Flak with
  a reach rider and a Kestrel name, the copy the Grudge was turned away for; and "possibly
  rare" is the hedge stacking forces, which a binary status never needs. Under the action
  economy's row the cantrip's payoff is the second action; a cantrip that also stacks damage
  on its target is the pile. The damage is in the reach already: a foe Exposed takes the
  blows of every Direct gun that could not reach it, a caster's hull in a round.
- **If it must bite as well as open: the armour kind, never Flak's.** "Armour counts for
  nothing against a foe that is Exposed." Armour is a flat number off every blow, and the
  foes that carry it are the big ones (the treant, the sandworm, the bone dragon and the
  magma golem, massive at 5; the zombie knight at 5) and any foe Braced; a massive foe's
  column is never exposed, so the reach half does nothing on it and the armour half does its
  work there, one half for the caster and one for the boss. It pairs with the strafer (two
  strikes a weapon pay the armour twice, nothing on an Exposed foe) and with every
  multi-strike gun. On the bench for now: reach alone first, since the designator on a boss is
  still a cantrip (a reroll and a second spend for 5 heat), and the clause reopens if the
  runner shows the lock a dead spend in the boss fights.
- **Stacking and rarity.** Binary: Exposed is on or off, a second Expose on the same foe is
  wasted, so it needs no rarity rule; the carriers are the question (the designator at first;
  the keyword is free for a Kestrel spotter or a module if the stock pass wants one), and the
  test for ubiquity is every status's (a status every team wants is the Armadillo's lure
  problem). Until the round's end, the locks' and Overshield's clock; it matters only in the
  player's turn, since reach is the player's.
- **The collision to flag: the foes' aim.** "The exposed" is also the default aim's word on
  the player's side (`AIMS` in `sim/foe.gd`, the codex's "aims at the exposed", the
  vocabulary's Aim entry: the machine its blow would hurt most). The two senses share the
  word today; a debuff named Exposed makes the foe-side sense the loud one, and "aims at the
  exposed" would read as "aims at a machine carrying Exposed", which no machine ever does.
  Lean: the aim's shown word changes (the id stays; the shown text is the codex's and
  `AIM_TEXT`'s): "aims at the vulnerable", or "the weak"; Ryan's word.

**The gate widened to buffs (Claude, 20 October).**

- **What the iconography says.** The die-entry row (section 5): the primary glyph top centre
  says what the entry does when spent, one of six (a crosshair for Direct, an arc for
  Indirect, the shield, the vent, the buff, the blank); the number beneath; a chip per keyword
  down the right edge, a star for a printed rule; and "a shield that also buffs is the shield
  glyph plus a buff chip". So a shield or vent that also buffs is a shield or a vent on the
  die, wearing a chip, and the widened gate reads the glyph and the number, never the rail:
  an entry whose glyph is the buff, or whose glyph is a weapon's with no damage beneath it.
  The shield and the vent are excluded by their own glyph, which is the clarity Ryan asked
  for, and it costs no wording.
- **The passive as the codex would print it.** A buff, or a weapon that deals no damage, is
  Rapid on this machine, once a round. In the die's terms: an entry with no damage to print.
- **The print.** A weapon of no damage prints "0" beneath its crosshair today
  (`Face.number_text`), and the number the HUD shows is the blow after buffs
  (`shown_number_text`), so an Overcharged probe prints 5 and deals 5 (the arithmetic adds a
  flat bonus to any attack; nothing guards the zero). Two proposals: a weapon of no damage
  prints no number (the glyph alone, as a blank prints nothing), so the gate's entries are
  exactly the ones with no damage printed and the icon is the rule; and the gate reads the
  printed entry (the component's number), so a buffed probe is still a setup. Whether a flat
  buff should lift a weapon of nothing at all is the stock pass's (Claude's lean: no, the
  buff is for guns; a hole below).
- **What it opens.** The buffs become cantrips beside the setters: the Overcharge Coil
  (Kestrel's, Overcharged two stacks, self or ally), the Aurora Lattice (Primed), the Shoring
  Jack (Braced) and the Retribution Thurible (Retaliate), buff then cash, or buff an ally then
  shoot. Today's cantrip pool is four weapons of no damage, none Kestrel's (the Klaxon, the
  probe, the projector, the Web Bomb planned), and four buffs, one Kestrel's; the wider gate
  doubles the pool and puts the Coil in it, so the Kestrel build is Coil then gun (a flat +10
  on one blow with the Coil's spend refunded, a buff's standard worth) and the control build
  probe then gun. The Spin-Up is Rapid already, and a stepping die's.

**The machine as it stands (the fourth round).** The passive above. The lock, the designator
(working name): a weapon of no damage, Indirect, heat 5, Expose. The die: I the designator;
II a Direct flat gun, the cash; III a buff (the Coil's kind) or a control piece of another
maker or a cold rolled gun, the second cantrip or volume; IV a vent. The frame as the second
kit: a tilt-jet at 50, hull 60, shield 50, field recharge 30, heat maximum 60, cooling 5, a
d4, two module slots. The round: a setup first (the designator, a buff, a setter), the die
rolls again, the cash; a gun first is one spend. Every number a placeholder; the names Ryan's.

## Holes already raised

- The Paradox counts once a spend, so two spends a round feed a Baboon twice, and Painted is a
  status it counts: a free stack a round beside a Baboon. Ryan, 17 October, of the Paradox:
  tuned for the final ecosystem, never dictating the game.
- Rapid pieces on it extend the round to three spends: its own gate is once a round, theirs
  are theirs, a third chase for the weak Rapid pieces, never a pile.
- Tempo is the line frame's claim; shapes differ: a combo climbing with every spend there, a
  setup cashed here.
- A painted foe opens the back rank to a whole team's Direct fire for one spend and one blow
  taken; the levers are the paint's reach (the lit foe in reach of this machine alone) and
  its price (the front foe's blow with a factor).
- With basics alone the identity rides on the lock showing; the control pieces are other
  makers' until Kestrel has one (the line review's stock holes).
- The glass: alone it loses what the Sparrowhawk used to win; its team rows are the read.
- The aim's word (the fourth round): "the exposed" names the foes' default aim on the player's
  side and the formation state on the foes'; Exposed as a debuff makes the foe-side sense the
  loud one. The aim's shown word is Ryan's to change.
- A flat buff on a weapon of no damage: the engine lifts a probe's blow by Overcharged today
  (no zero guard), and the die would print it; the gate reads the printed entry either way,
  and whether the lift stays is the stock pass's (Claude's lean: a weapon of nothing stays
  nothing).

## Bench, one line each

- The designator (0 damage, Direct, Shock), the first proposal: struck, a Polarity Projector
  copy; Shock in place of Painted (set aside: Shock is a state the roster has, a lit back-rank
  foe is not).
- The dive (the second kit's price: the designator spent on a back-rank foe through a standing
  front, the front foe striking it as it passes): Ryan, 20 October, overdesigned; a priced
  reach for a smaller Kestrel if one is ever wanted, since the artillery makers' reach is free
  and Kestrel's could be paid.
- The first kit's passive (the first weapon each round is Rapid) with the dive as a gun of 15:
  Ryan, 20 October: thin, no build hook.
- The cold gate (a weapon it fires while it carries no heat is Rapid, once a round): vents and
  cold guns as its build, the hit-and-run rhythm; set aside for the control gate, which opens
  a class the roster lacks where cold guns are the Sparrowhawk's pull.
- The second-shot gun (a Direct gun that hits 10 harder as the round's second spend): a lock
  feeding only its own passive, the burrow's flaw.
- The exposed-rotor helicopter as its shape: to Aphelion (Ryan, 20 October).
- Painted (the second and third kits' name for the mark): struck for Exposed, the game's own
  word (the fourth round).
- The flat on-hit half of Exposed (a point a blow, stacking; Ryan's first shape, 20 October):
  Flak's kind by the Prairie Dog row, so never a second carrier under a Kestrel name.
- The armour clause (armour counts for nothing against an Exposed foe): the bite if the lock
  proves a dead spend in the boss fights, the runner's read; a kind of its own, never Flak's.
