# Brief: Mastema, fire through the team (proposal reworked into a direction)

The session reworks Mastema's tools so its fire never sits blank and never doubles a boss for
free, around Ryan's idea of setting its own allies alight.

**Status**: direction (14 October); in session since 15 October (Claude's first round and its
review, below; Ryan has answered none of it). **Maker**: the Arsenal. **Proposed tier**: three.
**Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; "The design as proposed" and "The Scapegoat Brand"
below; `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 4; the bible's rows on Burning and Scorched and on
buffs and debuffs (section 5); `docs/rules.md`, "Heat" and "Buffs, debuffs and resistance".

## The design (the proposal)

Passive, the Brimstone Bellows: a Burning foe that dies passes its Burning to the foes beside
it. Locked tools, not a gun (Ryan, 12 October): the Chrism Ewer (doubles a target's Burning)
and the Votive Ward (now to be named the Votive Screen: shield 10 for each Burning foe). The
burners live in its hardpoints.

## What the go said

- Ryan, 14 October: "Change mastema's double-burn since that sounds scary against a boss. Maybe
  it's actually an ally cleanse that also moves burn and/or scorch to an enemy, chosen or
  random, focused or spread?"
- Claude: the mover is the Scapegoat Brand's shape on Mastema's locked piece: one drag onto a
  chosen foe takes Burning and Scorched off every machine and lands them as Burning on that
  foe. Chosen, not random, since a cleanse is timed by the debuff and a random target would be
  noise. The hole: it is blank in every domain that lays no heat debuff on machines (two of
  four today), beside the Screen's blank when nothing burns. The way through: the foes' fresh
  start gives every domain a debuff on machines, or the Ewer keeps its doubling with a cap.
- Ryan, 14 October: "That was exactly why I thought we could staple a cleanse to it, to give it
  conditional utility when no burn is present. Further thought - what if the Mastema was able
  to light its ALLIES on fire somehow? Now there's a niche that we don't have - damage the
  whole team. But for what payoff?"
- Claude: the payoff is already on its kit. Burning on a machine is a heat tick, so lighting
  the team feeds every hot build (the Goshawk, the Stingray, the boiler, the Nemesis); the
  Screen counts burning bodies on either side; the mover carries the fire off the allies onto a
  chosen foe as hull ticks. Its three pieces become one economy: light the team, shield it by
  the count, scapegoat the fire onto the boss. No entry is blank in any domain, since allies
  always exist, and the double-burn on a boss comes back paid for, in the team's heat, a round
  at a time, in the open.

## The design as proposed (12 and 13 October)

Claude's proposal for Ryan to move, except where marked as his; names are working names and
numbers the proposal's placeholders. The go reworks the tools (above); this is the design the
session starts from, with the doubling now the fallback.

**Ryan, 12 October**, as the proposal recorded his notes. First: Mastema is the
everything-burns archetype, and liked. Ditch the cauldron: a bat-winged flyer with arms is
plenty of visual, no feet, a creepy hovering flying thing. Fire spreading from the dead is more
interesting than "every blow burns". Third: an innate area burn on Mastema would be the default
best entry and want no upgrades. Better more tools, unique utility: a locked component that
doubles a target's burn; one that grants shield for each burning foe or stack on the board; one
that moves burn from an ally to a foe.

**The chassis**: Arsenal Instrument XI (demonic: everything burns), tier three, heavy, flyer,
the slowest flyer. Hull 120, shield 40, armour 0, move 30, heat maximum 100, cooling 5, field
20, two modules; a die of six, two locked entries and four hardpoints. The name: Mastema, chief
of the spirits in the Book of Jubilees, God's own executioner with demons at his command. It
answered the Meadow: numbers that swarm.

**The passive**, the Brimstone Bellows: *while it fights, a Burning foe that dies passes its
Burning to the foes beside it.*

- Any death of a Burning foe counts, however it comes: a team blow, its own Burning tick,
  Spillover, a Chain link, a Bomb's burst, a foe that will get back up.
- The stacks it carried as it died pass whole to each foe beside it, not split between them.
  Beside is Spread's shape: the columns either side, in its rank; a massive foe's neighbours
  are the front-rank foes in the columns either side, as Spread finds them.
- A foe killed by the same blow receives nothing.
- In the enemy's turn the fire travels: a foe that dies of its tick at its action passes its
  stacks on at once, and a neighbour later in the order ticks with them, so a rank can catch
  and burn down within one enemy turn.
- Each neighbour that gains Burning counts toward the Paradox.
- A Gel's halves join the reinforcements as new foes and carry no Burning.

**Locked pieces: tools, not a gun.** Neither deals damage, and each is blank until the team has
burn down, so the burners live in the hardpoints, where the upgrades go.

| Piece | Kind | Reach | Number | Heat | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chrism Ewer | attack | Indirect | none: *doubles the target's Burning* | 10 | a foe |
| Votive Screen | shield | | *10 for each Burning foe* | | self or an ally |

- The Ewer (chrism, the oil of anointing): the target gains as many Burning stacks as it
  carries. Everything else in the spend lands first, so a Primed stack is doubled too. It
  cashes nothing in: the damage still lands a stack at a time, at the foe's own actions. It
  deals no damage, so it spends no shock and splashes no shield. The die shows the stacks it
  will leave, over the foe it is dragged across.
- The Screen (the Votive Ward before Ryan's rename of 15 October; a votive is a candle lit as
  an offering, and here the foes are the candles): its number is read when spent, 10 for each
  foe on the field carrying Burning, either rank, a massive foe once, up to the target's
  maximum (no overshield). The die shows the number now.
- Per foe, not per stack: per stack, the Ewer would fill the Screen every time and the two
  pieces would say one thing. Per foe they pull apart: the Ewer wants one foe burning deep, the
  Screen many foes burning a little, and the Bellows turns deep into wide at a death.
- The arithmetic: Burn lays a stack per 10 of the blow, rounded up (Meteor Artillery's 15 lays
  two, the Blazethrower's 10 one). A stack deals 5 at the foe's action and then one fades, so
  stacks still to burn are worth 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, 105, 140 and 180 for one to eight. The
  Ewer on four (50 to come) leaves eight (180 to come), and the foe's death passes eight to
  each neighbour.
- Under the whole-block rule the proposal read the Screen against a swarm as buying blows
  turned rather than a bigger number: eight burning foes are 80, but a shield stops at its
  maximum (40 to 60 on most machines). Under the shield rule a shield absorbs its number (open
  item 2).

**Stock**, four hardpoints: Meteor Artillery (Indirect, flat 15, heat 15, Burn), the
Blazethrower (Direct, flat 10, heat 10, Burn), Incendiary Shells (Indirect, 5 to 15, Burn), the
Entropy Condenser (a vent).

**Build space: deep or wide.** The Ewer wants one foe carrying a lot (the Stingray's deepened
stacks, the Aurora Lattice's Primed, the Photosphere Lance); the Screen wants many foes
carrying a little (a burner spent on each, an area burner found as a blueprint, the Bellows at
every death), since the area burn kept off its locked pieces belongs in a hardpoint. Kill order
is the bridge: the burning foe in the middle of a rank is worth more dead than the one at its
end, and more again once the Ewer has doubled it. Every burn on the team counts, not only its
own.

**The look**, on Ryan's call (Claude's detail): a hunched torso and a horned head with a long
jaw, broad bat wings of black chrome panels ribbed in red glass that tilt and vector and never
flap, two long arms ending in claws, and nothing below the ribs but a tapering keel; boosters
under the wing roots. The Ewer is a long spout under its right wrist; the Screen a rack of
votive lights in its open chest, one lit for each Burning foe, a count read without hue. The
homage: the Stryge, Viollet-le-Duc's chimera on Notre-Dame, off its perch. Mounts: hp_1 and
hp_2 on the wing roots, hp_3 and hp_4 on the shoulders. Acts: a thing on a wire (spreads its
wings and holds, flexes its claws as embers spit from its forearms, wraps its wings round
itself like a cloak, tilts its head down along its arms). Its model is a new lowforge kind, a
legless winged flyer.

**Engine and tests**, as proposed: small, three pieces. The Bellows: a hook at a foe's death
(the tick kill in `_enemy_turn`, the kills in `_resolve_attack`, Spillover), reading the foe's
Burning before the dead are cleared and stacking it on the neighbours as Spread finds them. The
Ewer: a component rule that doubles a status, run last in the spend. The Screen: a shield whose
number is a question through `sim/effects.gd`, so the die and the forecast show it. Tests: a
pass from a team kill, a tick kill and a Spillover kill; both sides and the edges; a massive
foe; no Mastema, no pass; the Ewer on none, on some, with Primed; the Screen on none, one and
many, and at the maximum. The bot: `BURN_WEIGHT` (`sim/policy.gd`) is a flat guess on a
burning blow; the Ewer needs the payout of the stacks it adds over the foe's expected life, or
the runner reads it as a wasted spend.

**What the runner should read**: the Meadow's swarms and formation, team rows with burners, and
the Ewer's and the Screen's spend shares against the burners'. Mastema alone is a diagnostic.

## The Scapegoat Brand

Proposed as a rare Arsenal component that moves the team's debuffs onto a foe, in four shapes
(below; shape D, one meaning on any body, the principled one, waiting on the buffs-and-debuffs
pass). The analysis: a rare after the vocabulary pass, not on Mastema. The go made half of it
Mastema's mover; the session decides whether the Brand survives as a rare beside it.

Ryan, 12 October, as the proposal recorded his notes: a tool that moves burn from an ally to a
foe (his third notes); an Arsenal cleanse that moves the applicable debuffs onto a foe is
interesting, but depends on which terms are even shared between ally and enemy (his fourth).
The rest is Claude's, 12 October unless dated. The working name: the Scapegoat Brand
(Leviticus 16, the goat that carries the people's sins into the wilderness; sanctified on the
face, Azazel on reflection).

**What the two sides share.** Burning, since 13 October: a heat tick on a machine, a hull tick
on a foe. Which foe moves lay Burning and Scorched on machines is in `docs/rules.md`, "Heat";
the proposal's table of 12 October predates it. The proposal's reading of the rest on a foe:
Scorched means nothing, since a foe has no heat (Claude's mover of 14 October lands it as
Burning); Scrambled nothing, since a foe never rolls, and no foe lays it; Disabled (the Waste:
the Cockatrice's Stone) nearest Suppressed, a translation and a strong one; Corrupted (the
Waste: the Thief's Mug) nearest a bite of 10 a stack at each of its actions for the skirmish,
with no foe word today. The other way, Burning is the only foe debuff a machine carries
(Suppressed, Shocked and Paradox never touch one; Numbed is a resistance nothing moves). So
today the Meadow and the Marsh lay no debuff on a machine, the Peak lays Burning and Scorched
and the Waste Disabled and Corrupted: a mover is blank in two domains of four whatever it
moves, until the foes' fresh start.

**Four shapes.**

- **A. Shared words only.** It moves what means the same on a foe and leaves the rest.
  Simplest to read, and thin: Burning alone, so the Peak alone.
- **B. Shared words and translations.** Disabled becomes Suppressed, Corrupted a bite at each
  action. Live in the Peak and the Waste; each translation is a rule to learn, and a stun from
  a cleanse is strong (Numbed still resists it).
- **C. One conversion.** Every debuff stack it takes becomes a Burning stack on the foe: one
  rule in the Arsenal's fire, live wherever the team carries debuffs, at the cost of "moves"
  meaning "turns into".
- **D. One meaning on any body.** The buffs-and-debuffs pass (the bible's section 5, open:
  polarity belongs to the effect, not the carrier) writes each debuff to mean one thing on
  whatever carries it: Burning is heat on a machine and hull on a foe; Corrupted bites
  whatever its carrier does next. Then the mover moves every debuff with no exceptions and no
  table. The principled shape, waiting on that pass.

The proposal's lean: D as the target, A as the first build once the heat debuffs were named
(they were, on 13 October), B or C if the vocabulary pass keeps the two sides apart. If D
gives Corrupted a meaning on a foe, a moved Corrupted and the Onryō's Grudge as a damage over
time would be one shape twice (`arsenal_onryo.md`); Ryan named Grudge, so that call is his.

**How it is spent.** On a foe, as a weapon (a debuff on a foe stays a weapon), taking from
every machine at once: one target and one drag, and the scapegoat carries the whole people's
sins. The alternative, spent on one ally with the foe chosen by a rule (the one that laid it,
say), needs a second target the gesture does not have, and the engine records a debuff's
source by foe type, not by the foe.

**Where it lives.** As proposed, a rare Arsenal component with its unique rule (the chase),
fitted anywhere and best against the Peak and the Waste: returning the enemy's curses is
retribution, the maker's, more than Mastema's alone. The go's direction puts the mover on
Mastema's Ewer; the Brand itself, if it survives, is a rare after the vocabulary pass.

**Engine**, as proposed: small once the words are settled. Read each machine's debuff stacks,
remove them, and stack their foe meaning on the target; `CLEANSABLE` (`sim/effects.gd`)
already tags what a cleanse may take, and a Rapid switch-off (not a debuff) never moves.

**Claude's first round (15 October)**, by the open items below:

- **The lighter and the count (items 1 and 2).** The lighter goes on the Votive Screen: its
  target catches fire (two Burning, a placeholder), then gains 10 shield for each burning body
  on the field, foes and machines, its target included. A votive is a candle lit as an
  offering; the Screen is never blank, and it feeds its own count.
- **The mover and its words (items 3 and 6).** The Chrism Ewer, on one chosen foe: every
  machine's Burning and Scorched come off and land on that foe as Burning. One target, since
  the Bellows does the spreading: the scapegoat dies and passes its fire to its neighbours.
- **The doubling (item 4)** retires, no longer needed as the fallback, so burn is deepened in
  two places (the Stingray's Exothermic Suite, the Aurora Lattice's Primed), not three; the
  Bellows as drafted.
- **The Brand (item 5)** folds into the Ewer and goes to the bench, since a rare doing the same
  on any machine would repeat the Ewer; it reopens if the buffs-and-debuffs pass gives every
  debuff one meaning on any body.

The economy it gives: a hot ally keeps the fire as fuel (the Goshawk's shield, the Stingray's
deeper stacks, the Nemesis's rage through the overheat) and a cold ally hands it to the Ewer;
deep against wide survives, the Screen wanting many burning bodies and the Ewer gathering them
onto one. With the lighter on its own kit the mover is never blank in any domain, so Mastema
would no longer wait on the foes' fresh start.

Its holes: the Screen then the Ewer in one turn, and the ally never pays the heat (Burning
ticks at the turn's end), at the price of both locked dice that turn, kept; the Bellows passing
every stack whole to each neighbour after the Ewer has piled the team's fire on one foe
compounds; in the Peak the Golem's Eruption scorches every machine twice, so the Ewer could land
eight Burning on the Golem. The runner decides whether either wants a cap.

**Claude's review (16 October)**, correcting the round:

- The Screen lights one target, where Ryan said "what if the Mastema was able to light its
  ALLIES on fire somehow? Now there's a niche that we don't have - damage the whole team": the
  round shrank it for decision density without saying so. His version, every ally lit at once
  with the shield read from the whole count, is the stronger spend and the real team-wide
  price, and stands beside the round's.
- The Screen then the Ewer in one turn makes the heat optional, so the 14 October line that the
  double-burn comes back "paid for, in the team's heat" holds only when the player keeps the
  fire: a decision, but the argument overclaimed.
- Three decisions live on one machine (whom to light, whom to scapegoat, the kill order for the
  Bellows): a tier three earns it, but it is past the one-to-three budget of a round with four
  machines in it.
- Moving Mastema off the foes' fresh start is a records change (the README's order, the to-do)
  the round slid in as an aside: Ryan's call, with the lighter.
- Unexamined: the Bellows passing fire onto a Guarded core (a boss in parts), whose ticks deal
  nothing while its parts stand, as the rules read, a stack fading at each action.

  burst, a playstyle Ryan has turned down before.

- A cold team hates it, which is a composition statement worth keeping.
- The doubling has no cap, and a doubled stack passed whole to two neighbours compounds; the
  proposal left the cap to the runner (ten stacks more at most, say).

  Aurora Lattice's Primed, the Ewer.
