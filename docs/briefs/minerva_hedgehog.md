# Brief: the echidna (the hedgehog), a tank that fights (new chassis)

The session designs a lighter Minerva tier two that wants to be struck, first as the Faraday
Hull's new home, since 15 October around Ryan's Sonic Needle (below, "The Minerva session").
"The echidna" and "the hedgehog" are working labels from Ryan's own list; the name is his.

**Status**: direction, left open for now (Ryan, 20 October); two rounds in session (15 October), Ryan's Sonic Needle passive; his new shape of 4 November (strikers debuffed to hurt themselves after they act), Claude's first round on it below; the kit before the look (Ryan, 20 November: "We need to set
kit first"), Claude's round of 20 November on the kit below. **Maker**:
Minerva. **Proposed tier**: two. **Read first**:
`docs/briefs/README.md`; `minerva_armadillo.md`; the bible's rows on the Faraday Hull (section
7) and on retaliation and thorns (section 5); `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 8 (Minerva's
gaps); `content/components.json` (proximity_klaxon, retribution_thurible; the Lockdown Shutters left
content with the Polar Bear's landing, 20 October).

## What Ryan said

- 14 October: "we move faraday to a new unit that still wants to get hit, but actually has to
  worry slightly about its own hull (Echidna/Hedgehog/etc), whereas the runner shows the
  armadillo is pretty much indestructible."
- On thorns, 15 October: a thorns tank is balanceable only if the thorns carry a drawback of
  their own; rewarding a tank for doing its job (being struck under a draw) is dangerous (said
  of the hypertank).
- On its look, 15 November: "Echidna I had two general ideas, either a half track, or a heavily
  armored car with protected wheels." (His "wheeled crawler" of 15 October is below, in the
  Minerva session.) Then, of the first sheet: "I like the armoured car", and the spines need not
  be "so on the nose" (The look, below).

## What Claude proposed

- 14 October: a lighter Minerva tier two struck by its own choice: a locked lure to bring the
  blows in, and the Faraday Hull (a foe that strikes its bare hull is Shocked) for the team to
  cash. Low armour makes the exposed aim choose it, so the Hull fires often, and its hull is
  what it pays: the drawback the thorns need. A lure keeps the multiplication a spend rather
  than a standing state. Two Minerva tanks that read apart: the Polar Bear the wall, this one
  the bait that punishes.
- 15 October, why the Hull lives here and not on the Polar Bear: Shock on a striker lands no
  damage at the blow and must be cashed by a team spend, so it is capped by the team's dice
  rather than the enemy's blows; but under any draw on a heavy machine it is too much, and on
  armour 15 without a draw too little. On a light machine with a lure it fires often and costs
  hull each time.
- The analysis (section 8) had first pencilled Minerva's tier two as "the line", every weapon
  luring; the hedgehog took the lure line when the Hull moved here.

## Related: the Retribution Thurible

The analysis (section 2) found the Thurible (a buff: for two rounds a foe that strikes the
machine's bare hull is Shocked) gives any machine the Faraday Hull's effect, stocked on the
Nemesis. With the Hull on the hedgehog, the Thurible duplicates the hedgehog's passive.
Claude's lean: it should differ on purpose or become a rare tier-three piece, and the Nemesis
stock something of its own. The session decides this with the chassis. Ryan, 15 October (below):
both, a rare tier three that differs, and in his second round a tier-three module.

## The Minerva session (15 October)

**Claude's first round**, in chat: the Faraday Hull as it stands, every wounding blow (Shocked
never stacks, so once a foe; once a round the lever); a locked gun that lures, a Direct weapon
whose blow turns the foe it hits (no damaging lure exists, and the engine already runs Lure on
one, so content only); medium, hull 110, shield 20, armour 5, four hardpoints; not the
discharge, since a Shock lands no damage and the team's dice cap its cashing, and each Shock
costs a wound; amplify for others as a chassis identity, which nobody has; Sealed Live retiring;
the Thurible a tier-three rare, or differing by taking Sealed Live's half. Bench: a lure that
drops its own shield; every weapon luring (the analysis's "line").

**Ryan**, in his words: "Hedgehog/Echidna - We can make this work, and I think the right way
isn't necessarily to make it rely on hull damage. We just need to make the tank not only use
tank abilities. What about Passive: Foes the echidna damages get marked with a Sonic Needle
(sonic the hedgehog reference). When an enemy with a sonic needle strikes the Echidna, consume
the Sonic needle to give it Shocked. The damaging lure you mentioned does both in one shot, and
the only concern i have with that is that it eats all the die's uses."

On the Thurible: "Thurible - I think a rare tier three AND it differs. it can be a retaliate
chip damage for now, like deal 10 back to every attacker. We can make it more interesting later
if need be."

What it settles: the passive is the Sonic Needle, a debuff its blows leave that a strike on it
turns into Shocked, so the Shock no longer rests on hull damage and comes only from foes it has
already hit; the tank also fights; the damaging lure does both in one shot, at the cost of
being the entry the die collapses onto; the Thurible leaves the Nemesis's stock as a tier-three
rare whose Retaliate deals chip damage (10 to each attacker) in place of Shocked.

**Claude's second round**, in chat: count only a blow that deals it damage (shield or hull), so
Braced or a Barrier on it cannot make free Shocks; fold the draw into the passive, Lure granted
to every weapon it fires (the Boar's shape), so every gun is the damaging lure and none eats the
die, or a locked damaging lure with Rapid (the Polar Bear's shape again); no locked piece needed;
medium, hull 110, shield 30, armour 5; the Faraday Hull and Sealed Live retiring, Shocked on a
strike leaving the game except through the needle; on the Thurible, chip damage is thorns, so
keep Retaliate's condition (a blow on bare hull) and let the wound be its drawback.

**Ryan, second round**, in his words: "Echidna - I thought mitigation would be ok, since 1) it
has to hit something to mark it, and 2) it should have a more aggressive stats package compared
to the armadillo. I'm actually thinking of a wheeled crawler for this one, so it could be
relatively fast for a Minerva, more offensive, play up the "knuckles the echidna" soft
reference. Isn't giving it both the needle and the lure a little self-serving? I don't disagree,
but it seems like we can either play up the spikes, or the luring tank, not both. If we keep the
damaging lure, I would absolutely not give it rapid, for the same reason, but not sure."

On the Thurible: "Thurible, sounds good. If it damages hull, it retaliates, thats fine as a tier
3 (rare) module, maybe with a -shield -shield regen +hull stat profile to both nerf shield and
help it achieve its goals."

What it settles: a mitigated strike still counts for the needle (it must hit a foe first, and
its stats are aggressive); a wheeled crawler, fast for a Minerva and offensive, a soft nod to
Knuckles the Echidna; the needle and a built-in lure together read as self-serving, so it is
the spikes or the luring tank, not both; a damaging lure, if kept, is never Rapid; the Thurible
becomes a tier-three module whose rule pays back a blow that damages its hull, with shield and
field recharge down and hull up.

Ryan, 20 October: "Echidna I will leave open for now." The Polar Bear landed the same day (the
bible's Polar Bear row), so the Faraday Hull and Sealed Live are out of content: the Hull's idea,
a tank struck by its own choice that Shocks for the team, is this design's if he takes it.

## Ryan, 4 November: a new shape

In his words: "Minerva Echidna: new shape idea, debuff foes that strike it so that they damage
themselves after they act. So it's not immediate thorns damage, it's delayed. Locked component
can spread or synergize the debuff to make it interesting."

The same day he listed a tier-one Minerva tank whose every hit lures. It has its own brief
(`minerva_lure_tank.md`; the briefs README's "Ten a maker"). So the two halves he
split on 15 October ("we can either play up the spikes, or the luring tank, not both") go to two
machines. Claude reads open item 1 as answered (the spikes here, the lure there), unless he says
otherwise.

**Claude's first round** (4 November), for him to move:

- **In today's engine.** The debuff is laid on a foe whose blow lands on the echidna: Retaliate's
  trigger, read on any blow or only on one that reaches the hull. It then damages its carrier on
  the foe's own clock: the Web's (at the end of its action, skipped or not) or Vitriol's (at its
  action). Laying a status on the striker is built. A status that damages its carrier at the end
  of an action is new, and small.
- **The drawback the thorns line asks for.** The bible's retaliation row: "a thorn is balanced
  only by a drawback it carries and never rides a megatank". What reopens a reward for being
  struck is a machine that spends its own turn to be struck, deals nothing that turn, and is paid
  later and at random. The delay covers "later" but not the rest, so the drawback is still the
  question. Three candidates:
  - its own hull, this brief's first answer (Ryan, 14 October: a unit that "actually has to worry
    slightly about its own hull");
  - the damage is lost if the foe dies first, or if a foe's Cleanse takes the debuff, so the team
    must choose to let a striker live long enough to pay;
  - the debuff sticks only on a blow that reaches its hull, so it must bleed to retaliate.

  Claude's lean: the first and the third together, the spikes paid for in wounds. That is the
  Hull's idea, which the bible keeps for this machine.
- **Which action.** "After they act" could mean the end of the action that struck it, which is
  thorns a beat late: the foe's blow lands either way and nothing is decided. It could mean the
  end of the foe's next action, which gives a round's warning on its chip, so the team chooses to
  kill it first, let it pay, or Suppress it. Claude's lean: its next action, skipped or not, the
  way the Web bursts.
- **How much.** A fixed number a stack, a stack for each strike that lands, so a swarm piles them
  up. Never the striker's own blow. That would be the deterministic reflect the bible pins as a
  boss effect, and it scales with the size of the enemy's blows, the old discharge's shape. Paid
  once, or a tick a stack that fades a stack at each action (Burning's clock): Ryan's call.
- **The locked piece**, "spread or synergize". To spread, the piece lays the debuff on a foe it
  hits, so the echidna seeds its own spikes on its turn and the foes' strikes add to them: the
  Sonic Needle's mark half (15 October), recast. To synergize, the piece makes every marked foe
  pay now. Claude's lean: the spread. It keeps the spikes the whole identity, and the Sonic
  Needle's mark becomes the setup the strikes pile onto. A locked lure would be the "self-serving"
  pair Ryan turned down on 15 October.
- **The Sonic Needle** (15 October: foes it damages are marked, and a marked foe that strikes it is
  Shocked). Is it replaced by the new shape, or kept as the locked piece's setup?
- **Overlaps.** The Aphelion status detonator of the same list, now the jellyfish's EMP
  (`aphelion_jellyfish.md`), also bites "after their action".
  They part by what they count (here, the strikes it took; there, every stack on the foe) and by
  who sets them (here, the striker's own blow; there, a debuff spent on the foe). If the locked
  piece's spread carries the debuff to a marked foe's neighbours as it pays, that is the Static
  machine's Splash on another clock (`aphelion_static.md`).

## Claude's round of 20 November: the kit

### The proposal

**The chassis.** Minerva, tier two, medium (the armoured car). Hull 32, shield 8, armour 1, heat
maximum 12, move 4 (fast for a Minerva), four hardpoints and the locked piece (a die of five),
two module slots. It is lighter than the Polar Bear (assault, 40 hull) and more offensive: four
guns' worth of hardpoints. His words: "a more aggressive stats package", "relatively fast for a
Minerva", "has to worry slightly about its own hull".

**The passive, the Sonic Needle** (his name for it). Draft text: *"Every foe its weapons damage
carries a needle. A foe with a needle whose blow lands on it loses the needle, and gains
Ablating 2 and Shocked."*

- The needle is the gate. It is his own reason, from 15 October: "it has to hit something to mark
  it". A needle never stacks and lasts until it is spent.
- The payment is his 4 November shape. Ablating on a foe ticks as its own turn ends, after its
  action, so a striker "damages itself after it acts": 2 that turn and 1 the next.
- The Shock is the 15 October payoff: the team's next weapon strike on the striker is doubled. It
  fills the roster's open "amplify for others" slot, which the briefs README gives to this
  machine.
- A blow a Barrier or armour takes whole still counts, on his 15 October word ("I thought
  mitigation would be ok").

**The locked piece, recommended: synergize.** The Bristle (working label), equipment on itself,
heat 2. Draft text: *"Gain Armoured 1. Until its next turn, every foe whose blow lands on it pays
as if it carried a needle."* Foes' picks show before the player acts. When arrows point at it, the
Bristle opens the gate for the round, and the round's price is the shot it did not fire. The
bible's condition for reopening a reward for being struck (his, 20 October) is that "the machine
spends its own turn to be struck, deals nothing that turn, and the reward comes later and at
random, so it is paid for in tempo". The Bristle spends the turn, deals nothing and is paid later
(the burn as the striker's turn ends, the Shock on a later strike), but not at random: every
striker that round pays. That last part is his to waive or keep. No Rapid and no cooldown.

**The alternative: spread.** The Quill (working label), a weapon: 2, Direct, Spread 2, heat 3. The
passive needles the target and the foes beside it in one spend. The case against it: the stock
can carry the spread already (the Howitzer below), so the Quill adds little that only it does.

**The stock** (up to tier two): Rivet Gun, Howitzer (Wainwright & Sutler, tier one: Spread 1 and
Penetrate 1, so up to four needles in one spend), Slugthrower (4, for an armoured foe), Shoring
Jack. There is no lure in it. His 15 October line was "we can either play up the spikes, or the
luring tank, not both", and the lure tank took the lure. A player who finds the Proximity Klaxon
may slot one, and that is the player's choice.

**The upgrade** (a tier two arrives at level two: the lift, and one direction's first step).

- The lift: hull +6, and the spike's Ablating 3.
- **Spread** (his word): *"A foe it spikes passes a needle to the foe beside it in its rank"* (the
  web's neighbour, the right-hand one first). A cluster of strikers hands the trap along.
- **Curl**: *"While it reboots, every foe whose blow lands on it pays as if it carried a needle."*
  Its bare turn bristles. The price is the reboot's turn and a hull with no shield in front of it.
  An echidna curls when it is threatened, and his 15 November words allow a nickname for how a
  machine acts: "nicknamed after animals they resemble or act like".

**The brief's open items:**

1. Answered: the spikes are here and the lure is the tier-one lure tank's, unless he says
   otherwise.
2. The shape is the needle as gate, the spike (Ablating) and the Shock as payment, and a piece
   that opens the gate for a round.
3. Numbers as above; the Bristle as the locked piece.
4. The look waits on this. If the Bristle lands, look B's raked plates read as it (the bristle as
   armour); A's rods were a guess at needle emitters.
5. The Faraday Hull and Sealed Live retire. Both left content on 20 October, and the needle now

6. The Retribution Thurible, a tier-three module (the Arsenal's): *"A foe whose blow takes hull

### Where it departs from the round of 4 November

That round's leans, unanswered, were: the drawback in its own hull, a spike only on a blow that
reaches the hull; the spike on the striker's next action, for a round's warning; and the locked
piece as the spread, seeding the debuff on the foes it hits. This round moves each:

- **The hull.** The needle is the gate in its place, on his 15 October "the right way isn't
  necessarily to make it rely on hull damage". The hull form goes to the Thurible (item 6).
- **The clock.** Ablating's own: 2 as the striker's turn ends, 1 as its next ends. The first point
  is the thorns a beat late; the second, the warning; the Shock is the team's decision.
- **The spread.** The passive's needle is the spread now (its weapons lay it), so the locked
  piece's verb is free for synergize. The spread survives as the Quill and the Spread direction.

### A round, played

Picks show: two goblins and a wolf are aimed at the echidna, and the echidna already needled one
goblin last round. Its die shows the Howitzer and the Bristle.

- The Howitzer hits the wolf's cluster: three foes needled, one killed. The needled goblin and the
  wolf strike it in the enemy's turn. Each loses its needle, burns 2 as its turn ends (and 1 next
  turn), and is Shocked for the team.
- The Bristle instead: Armoured 1, and all three strikers pay as if needled, needle or not. That
  is three spikes and three Shocks, for a turn with no shot.

### The system, before the numbers

- **Whom it protects, and how.** Itself, a little (armour 1, the Bristle's 1). The team, by
  punishment: the foes that strike it burn, and the team's next strikes on them double. It draws
  nothing and shields nobody, so it is no wall. It is the tank that fights.
- **What threatens it.** Its blows are real: the shield does not mend, the hull never heals in a
  battle, and armour 1 takes little off a big blow. A boss's single blow is the worst case for the
  spikes, and the Shock is there for that case. A foe's Ward every foe turns needles and spikes
  (the Warding Orb, the Astromancer). The Spectre's and the Wraith's Hallow lifts them. A foe's
  Barrier eats a Shock. The team's own stuns keep a needled foe from striking. And picks that miss
  it leave its needles idle.
- **Counterplay, the player's.** Needle the foes aimed at it, since intents are shown. Bristle
  under arrows. Reboot when it is bare (Curl makes that turn pay). Cash the Shocks with big blows,
  the Ablating with the Backdraft Charge, and both with the Baboon's Paradox (a needle is one
  stack,
  Ablating two, Shocked one). A foe's Expose on it turns blows onto it, and that feeds it.
- **Counterplay, the foes'.** Ward, cleanse, purge (the Bristle's Armoured), blows at every
  machine
  wearing it down (the Treants and Sandworms, which also spike every needled one), and the
  "most buffed" pick (the Djinn's Unmake) finding a bristled echidna.
- **Under the reboot.** It needs no shield to spike. A needled foe pays even while the echidna
  reboots bare. Its reboot is the turn its hull takes the most, which is what Curl prices.
- **What caps it.** A payment is one needle spent, and needles come from its own spends, so the
  spikes are capped by its dice, never by the enemy's blows. The one exception is the Bristle's
  round, and that is paid for in tempo.

### A small model

On tier-one placeholders: a pack of five, blow 4, HP 6, armour 0; four machines, picks level.

| Per round | Value |
|---|---|
| Foes aimed at it | 5 × ¼ = 1.25 |
| Damage it takes | 1.25 × (4 − 1) ≈ 3.75: its shield 8 is gone in round two, its hull 32 lasts about 8½ rounds more |
| Needles laid | 1 a gun, up to 4 a Howitzer (target 4, beside and behind 1, each damaged) |
| Spikes, with 4 needles standing | 4 × ¼ = 1 a round |
| One spike pays | Ablating 2 = 3 over two of its turns, plus a Shock (a 4-gun's strike becomes 8, +4) |
| The passive at steady state | about 7 a round, beside its own gun's 4 |
| A Bristle round, two arrows on it | 2 × (3 + a Shock); it takes 2 × 2 (armour 1 and the Bristle's 1) |
| Against a boss (blow 10, aims at it a quarter of the time) | needle once; when it strikes, 3 and a doubled big blow (a railgun's 8 becomes 16) |

So it is strong against a horde only after it has spent its turns needling, and against a boss
through the Shock. It takes the team's ordinary share of the blows, not more: it draws none.

### What the engine runs, and what it needs

- **Runs:** Ablating on a foe, ticking at the end of its own action, past armour. Shocked on a foe
  (the Shock keyword's path). A passive laying a status on each foe its blows hit (it needs a
  "damaged" condition: a blow of nothing lays no needle). The blow-taken path with its striker
  (`Skirmish._damage_mech`'s source). A foe's Ward turning a mark. The Paradox counting debuffs.
  The web's neighbour, for Spread.
- **Needs:** a foe mark, the needle (status, chip, term, lasting until spent). An on-struck
  trigger
  that reads the striker: the striker path went with Retaliate on 15 November, and this would be
  the needle's own. The Bristle's round-long buff, read in that path. Curl reading Rebooting. The
  round preview showing a needled foe's spike when its arrow is on the echidna. And the bot's
  valuations (needling the foes aimed at it, bristling under arrows, a Shock laid for an ally),
  which the brief already flags as missing.

### Rules-sweep items it touches (each awaiting his marks, none relied on)

- **S10** ("No reward for being struck that scales with the enemy's blows"): the needle caps the
  payments by its own spends. The Bristle's round scales with that round's strikers, paid in
  tempo.
- **S3** ("debuffs grow by kinds, never by copies"): the spike lays Ablating, an existing kind,
  and
  the needle is a new kind, a trap mark. Making the spike a debuff of its own would copy Ablating.
- **D22** ("a retaliation elsewhere needs a fantasy of its own"): the spines are its own, so it
  passes either way. The Thurible stays the Arsenal's.
- **R1** (Barrier: a cooldown on every granter): no piece or step grants a Barrier, so it does not
  touch the kit; the bench's Barrier idea names it.
- **V10** (a round-long grant for one round): the Bristle lasts one round.
- **S5** (the Cryo defence kept "clear of the echidna's hull"): moot, since the echidna no longer
  reads its hull.
- The bible's retaliation row's own lead, "Retaliation punishes the striker with a debuff",
  matches.

### The bench (each idea with the rule or lean that killed it, his to overrule)

- A built-in damaging lure: his "the spikes, or the luring tank, not both" (15 October).
- Rapid on a lure: his "I would absolutely not give it rapid".
- Every striker spiked, ungated (his 4 November words alone): the bible's thorns row (a drawback
  of its own, never on a megatank). The needle is the drawback here; this stays his to choose.
- Shock on every striker (the Faraday Hull): measured too strong against rank and file on the
  Polar Bear (the Faraday row).
- Reflecting the striker's own blow: the deterministic reflect the bible keeps for a boss, which
  scales with blow size.
- The spike as its own debuff, a unique chip (the briefs README: "A new chip is fine"): it
  copies Ablating (S3, which awaits your marks).
- A Barrier in the Bristle: R1's cooldown (awaiting your marks), and the thorns row's "never
  rides a megatank".
- A draw only onto needled foes: the spikes and the lure together again.
- A detonator that bursts every needle on its own turn: Claude's lean, since it undercuts wanting
  to be struck and overlaps the jellyfish's EMP.
- Spikes reading hull damage only (the 14 October Hull): his "the right way isn't necessarily to
  make it rely on hull damage". That form moves to the Thurible.

### Holes

- With no draw, its spike rate is the foes' picks, about a quarter of the single-target blows on a
  four-machine team. The lure tank on the same team pulls blows away from it.
