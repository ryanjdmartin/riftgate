## What stands

From the Aphelion chat (11 to 13 October), written up on 13 October for the batch evaluation.
The bible's Aphelion row records Ryan's call of 13 October: that session went off the rails,
and its proposal is "a list of ideas, not a design to defend". Everything here is Claude's
unless marked Ryan's, and the numbers are the proposal's placeholders.

- Ryan, 11 October, his brief for Aphelion: "a flying fortress (many options from helicarrier
  to capital-class spacecraft)".
- Ryan, 12 October: "What if the capital ship was the Protoss Carrier foil to the Minervan
  Terran Battlecruiser?" Ryan, 20 October: "The battlecruiser session should never join this
  one, it's not related at all. Joined by homage, perhaps, but even that is not necessarily
  locked in." So the Carrier is designed on its own, the homage pairing at most a perhaps.
- The frame: tier three, assault, a flyer, move 20 (round one).
- The proposal's round one, the flying fortress: a deep-space hull (the makers table's
  "deep-space programme"), the helicarrier and the capital ship at once, its lift fans gone
  with Ryan's propulsion call ("Aphelion doesn't use rotors as a general rule", 12 October). Three
  verbs, all new code: the deck (allies that end their turn
  beside it regain shield as if they had not fought); the long guns, joining fights from
  farther off (Ryan liked them and parked them while the engagement range may change); a second
  die for its air wing, the purest carrier and the dearest (two dice on one machine, on a phone
  screen).
- The proposal's round two: a locked Launch Bay giving every machine in the fight 10 shield (a
  screen), or interceptors as extra strikes on its weapons (many small blows, the target's
  armour coming off each one); interceptors that build up through a fight were ruled out when
  proposed and reopened by the bible's revision of the locked-pieces row (growth through a
  fight is fine for now).
- Assault is the largest size class, so a capital ship draws no bigger than any assault machine
  unless it is allowed a bigger draw.

## Since then

- The screen was proposed under the whole-block rule, which read it as one blow stopped on
  each machine (a standing shield blocked a whole blow however small; a swarm's second blow
  landed, and a breach went through). The honest shield rule (14 October) makes a screen of
  shield to every machine a fair number rather than four blows negated: 10 hit points on each
  machine, which a breach ignores. A Barrier is never granted to every ally at once, so a
  screen must stay a shield number.
- Ryan's taste, as the briefs README records it from his words in the Polar Bear's go: no
  team-wide armour, Braced or shield handed out in bulk ("giving the team tank stats is just
  as toxic as taking all the blows"). Does a screen of shield to every machine pass that line?
  It stays open with the verb.
- Interceptors as extra strikes, the target's armour coming off each, are now the strafer's
  verb: Kestrel's tier one, decided 14 October, every weapon it fires striking twice at half,
  each strike losing the foe's armour. Niches may overlap, but mechanics should not repeat (the
  briefs README); the siege battery's brief set its own strikes aside for the same overlap.

## The Aphelion session, first round (15 to 20 October)

Opened by Ryan on 15 October with the Macaque, the Baboon and the jellyfish (its record is
in `git show e285ff51:docs/briefs/aphelion_line_frame.md`): Claude's proposal in chat that day, Claude's review of it on 16
October at Ryan's ask, written down on 20 October. Ryan's call on the battlecruiser came the
same day (above, under What stands); everything else here is Claude's, for him to move, and
every number is a placeholder. "Interceptors" is a working
label only: Kestrel's interceptor is a tier-one chassis (the briefs README's tiers table), and
names are Ryan's.

**The proposal** (15 October)

- Both earlier verbs to the bench: the screen, a shield number on every machine, is the team
  tank stat Ryan called toxic; interceptors as extra strikes are the strafer's pattern.
- **Interceptors that stay**: every weapon it spends also leaves an interceptor on the foe it
  hit. At the end of each player turn every interceptor strikes its foe for 5, a plain blow:
  the Carrier's own buffs apply (Empowered, Primed), never the weapon's keywords, so a
  Suppress weapon cannot launch a stunlock. When its foe falls an interceptor comes home; it
  flies four at most.
- The rule it bends at tier three: a spent weapon hits once, and these keep hitting. It ramps
  against a boss or an elite and does little against trash that dies at once.
- The foil as proposed that day: the battlecruiser as a Yamato cannon, one locked gun, one huge
  blow, then dark.

**What the review corrected** (16 October)

- The Yamato foil was stale, and designed from inside Aphelion's session on Minerva's machine:
  the battlecruiser had a brief of its own by then (`minerva_battlecruiser.md`), and Ryan's
  note on the H-tank rules out one big shot as "boom-boom-skirmish won". The review then leaned
  on that brief's "sustained where the broadside is burst"; Ryan's call of 20 October retires
  the pairing itself, so that reason goes too. Interceptors that stay stand on their own: the
  rule they bend at tier three, the launching the helicarrier leaves to this Carrier, and the
  homage.
- By the overlap rule (a mechanic is its pattern in its context, Ryan, 16 October) the bench
  reason for extra strikes is the strafer's pattern, not the shared trait of many blows; and
  interceptors that stay part from Burning by pattern (lasting, capped, armoured, re-aimed by
  a death, fed by every weapon).
- Holes to write out before the go: Primed on the Carrier lays four Burning stacks a turn;
  whether an interceptor's strike counts as a spend for the Paradox; a strike at the turn's end
  spends a Shocked foe's doubling unless the player spends it first; 5 against a boss's armour
  of 5 is nothing, and letting interceptors pass armour makes them Burning by another name.

**Since then, on main** (to 20 October)

- Wainwright & Sutler's multi-rotor is becoming that maker's helicarrier at tier three (Ryan,
  20 October, `ws_multirotor.md`): the carry on the map as its passive, and a mark, a recovery
  and a dock (granting Phased: cannot act, take damage or be targeted until the round's end,
  and only to a unit that has not acted) locked on cooldowns; its hand-over slots were cut the
  same day. Its brief leaves launching to this Carrier ("It supplies and never launches"), so
  the two carriers part by pattern, supply against launch.
- "The capital ship that it seems like every maker is going to end up getting" (Ryan, 15
  October): Minerva's battlecruiser, Wainwright & Sutler's helicarrier, and on the Kestrel
  branch (20 October, not on main yet) the gun platform as Kestrel's.
- Aphelion's other tier-three candidate: the aura-shifter on the briefs README's bench (Ryan, 19
  October: "an all-in aura support, maybe, with shifting auras for the team"), for an Aphelion
  session beside the Carrier; Ryan's counts (3-5-2 or 4-4-2) allow two tier threes.
