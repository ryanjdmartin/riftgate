# Brief: the artillery, the ammo build (new chassis)

The session designs Wainwright & Sutler's second tier three, the old-world artillery of the
maker's lean, as the machine that runs on shells where every other machine runs on heat.

**Status**: direction (Ryan, 20 October: the all-shells candidate from the Minerva session's
siege battery brief read as a Wainwright & Sutler tier three; "I agree on the artillery as the
ammo build. It makes sense. Brief it."). **Maker**: Wainwright & Sutler. **Tier**: three (the
roster row: the maker's tenth, the raining-death niche the carrier leaves whole). **Read
first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; `minerva_siege_battery.md` (the third round of 17 October,
where the shape was first written, and the charge Minerva kept); the bible's heat row (section
5), its rows on the Wainwright & Sutler roster and the Whip Scorpion (section 7), and the
action-economy row (section 5, multi-strike as the blow currency).

## Where it came from

The siege battery's candidates (17 October) were a guard that drops, a magazine, and a telegraph
hunter. Ryan, 17 October: "the ammo is an interesting idea, but somewhere in here needs to live
the tanks identity, the build openers. Since this is a tier 3, we can go wild with rule breaks,
and if its a Minervan capstone even better. Something about needing ammo, and a way of
recharging it faster... Do all the tanks guns use ammo? Is that the gimmick?" Claude's third
round answered yes, and wrote the shape below for Minerva. Ryan, 20 October, in the carrier's
session: the ammo-based chassis sounded like a Wainwright & Sutler tier-three build; Claude
agreed, for three reasons: the heat row asks that a cold build pay somewhere and never for free,
and shells are that price on the one maker whose kit is cold; big Indirect blows that cost
shells are old-world artillery by definition, the maker's lean; and the carrier's recovery is
its loader, so the maker's two capstones pair, one directing fire and refuelling, the other
raining death. Minerva's siege battery keeps its charge, which builds where a magazine depletes:
mirror patterns in two makers.

## The design as it stands (Claude's, from the 17 October shape; Ryan's to move)

- **The passive**: its weapons add no heat and each costs a shell from its magazine, the big
  guns several; a vent spent on it reloads shells instead of cooling (so much vented a shell).
  Its heat gauge is a magazine shown in shells; the foes' Burning and Scorched still land on a
  small heat gauge of its own, so the Peak keeps its answer to it.
- **The big guns**: the raining death. Locked, the maker's old-world piece: Indirect, a big flat
  number with area (the stock pass's explosives: Spread and Penetrate both, an explosive's
  shape), several shells a shot. Whether one locked gun or two (the siege battery's two turrets
  were Minerva's picture; a battery of field guns on a carriage is this maker's) is the
  session's.
- **The magazine's choice**: "fire now or hold" is "is this target worth three shells of eight,
  against the vents I will see"; two big shots back to back are possible at the price of
  everything else going quiet and the magazine dry, the capstone doing its job once a fight.
- **What it opens**: every vent in the game is its loader (the Coolant Jacket, the Blowout Stack,
  the Radiator Sail, the Puffin's shield that vents); every hot gun in the game is cold on it
  (the Thermal Ray's 30 heat becomes a shell), so the hottest pieces have a home that pays in
  shells; the carrier's recovery, heat to zero, is a full reload (the pair); Flak shells through
  it lay the maker's on-hit by the rank. A tier-three module may widen the magazine.
- **Numbers and kit** (placeholders): heavy or assault, wheeled or a walker (mobile where
  Minerva's siege battery is treaded), move 30; hull 140, shield 30, armour 10; a magazine of 8;
  the locked gun 3 shells a shot; four hardpoints, a d5 or d6; stock the maker's Indirect pieces
  and a vent.
- **The identity test**: a new price (shells), a change to what two kinds of piece do (weapons
  and vents), routes in (every vent, every hot gun, the carrier) and payoffs (the big guns).

## Holes already raised

- A dry gun is an entry that cannot be spent that turn, so a die of guns and no vent wastes
  its turn once the magazine is empty: the heat build's overheat by another name, and
  mismatch costs time, never ability, since shields and buffs still spend.
- Short fights are free and long ones are where it bites, the inverse of heat, which is right
  for artillery and wrong if the boss cells are the long ones it was built for; the runner reads
  it.
- A Rapid vent reloads and acts again: the Wake Diffuser's question (the Kestrel session's)
  gains a second machine.
- Two Wainwright & Sutler machines read vents: the Whip Scorpion's blast and this one's reload
  are two patterns, fuel against ammunition, and a team that fields both wants every vent in
  the game.
- The siege battery's charge and this magazine are mirrors; the Minerva session's brief keeps
  the charge, and this brief keeps the magazine, so neither maker loses a candidate.
