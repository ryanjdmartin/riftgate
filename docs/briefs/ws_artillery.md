# Brief: the artillery, the ammo build (new chassis)

The session designs Wainwright & Sutler's second tier three, the old-world artillery of the
maker's lean, as the machine that runs on ammo where every other machine runs on heat.

**Status**: in session (Ryan's shape, 21 October). **Maker**: Wainwright & Sutler. **Tier**:
three (the roster row: the maker's tenth, the raining-death niche the Swallowtail leaves whole).
**Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; `minerva_siege_battery.md` (the third round of 17
October, where the shape was first written, and the charge Minerva kept); the bible's heat row
and action-economy row (section 5), its Rapid row and its rows on the Wainwright & Sutler
roster, the Whip Scorpion and the Swallowtail (section 7); the heat scale the stock pass leans
to (a fifth of today's, gauges up to 20: the Kestrel branch, 20 October).

## Where it came from

The siege battery's candidates (17 October) were a guard that drops, a magazine, and a telegraph
hunter. Ryan, 17 October: "the ammo is an interesting idea, but somewhere in here needs to live
the tanks identity, the build openers. Since this is a tier 3, we can go wild with rule breaks,
and if its a Minervan capstone even better. Something about needing ammo, and a way of
recharging it faster... Do all the tanks guns use ammo? Is that the gimmick?" Claude's third
round answered yes, and wrote a shape for Minerva (a magazine of 8, the big guns several shells
a shot, a vent reloading by its number; `git show d9e8c99:docs/briefs/ws_artillery.md`, superseded
by Ryan's shape below). Ryan, 20 October, in the carrier's session: the ammo-based chassis
sounded like a Wainwright & Sutler tier-three build; Claude agreed: the heat row asks that a
cold build pay somewhere and never for free, and ammo is that price on the one maker whose
kit is cold; big Indirect blows that cost ammo are old-world artillery by definition, the
maker's lean. (A third reason, the carrier's recovery as its loader, retired on 21 October,
below.) Minerva's siege battery keeps its charge, which builds where a magazine depletes:
mirror patterns in two makers.

## Ryan's shape (21 October)

In his words: "I don't think the hoist should fully reload the artillery. That seems like a
locked pairing then, and removes the artillery's balancing levers.

The artillery - name uncertain. I envision a mobile missile/artillery vehicle, like a cross
between a Big Bertha, a M270 MLRS, and/or a truck-mounted MEADS launcher. Big. Big wheels.
Massive launchers and massive artillery cannons, not sure if lined up in series or parallel -
the long truck AA-style truck is obvious and effective, the wide version is weirder and might
be more interesting. Because it converts heat costs to ammo costs, it will inevitably be
outfitted with high-heat energy components, which is the intended dichotomy of a W&S chassis
that usually favours cold ballistics. But that means that the design can't look too much like
a pure ballistics platform, this is the "high-tech" W&S design where they tried to resolve
their inherent bias against energy weapons.

Passive - Weapons are fired using Ammo instead of Heat, another bar under the heat bar, with a
cap of 10, making the conversion 2:1 of heat to ammo, rounded up. So a 1 heat still costs 1
ammo as the floor. Self-shields and self-vents add 2 ammo. Maybe gains 1 ammo each turn. A
locked Rapid component adds 2 ammo. A locked weapon maybe costs 1 ammo, and returns an ammo
for every foe hit or killed - not sure, but some way of ramping. The trick then, is other than
not overheating, how does this new resource system actually pay off, and not just be inverse
heat?"

So (decided, 21 October): no ally's piece reloads it, the Swallowtail's Hoist included (a locked
pairing takes the artillery's balancing levers); only its own pieces spent on itself reload it,
which his "self" already says. The pairing left the Swallowtail's bible row the same day.

## Claude's reading (21 October)

**As written, it is inverse heat.** At the stock pass's heat scale (a fifth of today's), every
rule in the passive has a heat twin: a magazine of 10 at 2:1 is a gauge of 20; an ammo a turn
is cooling 2; a reload of 2 is a vent of 4; the floor of an ammo a shot makes every cold gun a
2-heat gun; a dry gun is the overheat as a hard stop instead of hull. Two rows differ in kind,
and both are wanted: the floor makes cold guns cost, which is what pulls the energy guns on (on
any other machine a cold gun is free and the hottest costs 6 heat; here they cost 1 and 3), and the dry stop moves its risk from hull to tempo. The rest is heat renamed, so the
payoff has to come from rules heat cannot have. Three:

1. **It opens low and ramps.** Heat opens every fight with a full budget, so every heat machine
   bursts first and fades. A magazine that opens at 3 of 10 gives the opposite curve: the early
   turns earn, the later ones spend. The ramp Ryan asked for, from one number, and a tier
   three's lower floor and higher ceiling (the tier row).
2. **Its old gun pays for its new guns.** Heat comes back by waiting; ammo can come back by
   hitting. The locked cannon costs 1 and returns 1 for every foe it hits (an explosive:
   Spread and Penetrate, so four at most), so a crowd is ammunition and a lone boss is a wash;
   the energy guns in the hardpoints spend what the cannon earns. Ryan's dichotomy as a loop:
   the maker's ballistic gun feeds its high-tech ones. Read at the aim, so the die shows the net
   ammo as the cannon is dragged over a target: full information.
3. **A full magazine buys what heat never could: the salvo.** A locked launcher fires all the
   ammo left, a strike an ammo, on one target. The Buzzard's cannon (Vents all) cashes a
   liability; this cashes savings. The raining death in strikes, the maker's blow currency
   (the action-economy row), so every strike takes Flak and Exposed lets every strike past
   armour; and it competes with the energy guns for the same ammo (an armoured target wants
   the energy gun, a Flak'd or Exposed one the salvo).

Ryan's locked Rapid reloader closes the loop if it is Rapid only from an empty magazine (the
speed-loader): the salvo empties it, the loader refills and acts again, the cannon tops it up.
Three locked pieces that feed each other (the bible's row on locked pieces working together),
three hardpoints, a d6; or the loader cut for a fourth hardpoint.

**The kit as proposed** (Claude's; every number a placeholder, at the stock pass's heat scale):

- **The passive** (working name the Caisson, the ammunition wagon behind the guns): its
  weapons fire from its magazine instead of heating it, an ammo for every 2 heat, rounded up,
  at least an ammo a shot; it gains an ammo each round; a vent it spends on itself reloads half
  its number. The unit is Ryan's word, ammo, or shells; never rounds, which is the combat
  round's word. The magazine holds 10 and opens each fight at 3. The heat gauge stays for the
  foes' Burning and for equipment heat, with a little cooling of its own; Scorched makes its
  guns cost more ammo (the conversion reads the heat after modifiers).
- **The cannon** (locked, the old-world gun, the Big Bertha): Indirect, a big flat number,
  Spread and Penetrate, 1 ammo a shot, 1 back for every foe it hits.
- **The launcher** (locked, the MLRS): the salvo, all the ammo in the magazine as a strike an
  ammo on one target, a small number a strike.
- **The loader** (locked, Ryan's Rapid reloader): reloads 2; Rapid only from an empty magazine,
  a plain reload that takes the turn otherwise.
- **Three hardpoints** for the high-tech guns; assault, wheeled, move 20 or 30.

**Holes in the numbers** (Claude, 21 October):

- **Shields crowd out vents.** Its weapons make no heat, so a vent's cooling idles on it, and a
  shield that reloads the same 2 wins every hardpoint. Lean: vents alone reload, by their own
  number at the passive's rate run backwards (a vent of 4 reloads 2), so one 2:1 rule reads
  every heat number on its die both ways and the vent has a job only it has here. If the reload
  turn should be protected, shields reload 1.
- **The Rapid reloader is a Rapid vent by another name.** Ryan's reason for keeping Rapid vents
  rare (the Rapid row: a free way out of the resource) holds for ammo, and the action-economy
  row gates every new Rapid; gated on empty, running dry on purpose becomes a play.
- **Hit, not kill.** A hit is read at the aim, before the spend; a kill is swingier, and the
  Kite's and the Ammit's. The refund per foe hit is the Honey Badger's hopper pattern (a crowd as
  ammunition for the boss); it passes Ryan's rule that a mechanic is its pattern in its context
  (16 October), since here it prices every gun and is no bonus on one pair, but the kinship is
  his to weigh.
- **The floor covers cold guns.** Claude reads "at least one" as every weapon, cold ones
  included; a free cold gun would field free ballistics and the dichotomy would vanish. His to
  confirm.
- **The siege battery's charge is the kin to watch.** Its input is the same (using components
  adds charge); the line is the output: ammo is the artillery's price, the charge Minerva's
  power. So the artillery's guns keep fixed prices, and spending more for keywords stays the
  siege battery's; the salvo spends for count, never keywords.
- **Today's heat numbers break it.** The Thermal Ray's 30 heat is 15 ammo, past the cap; the
  numbers wait on the stock pass's heat scale, where the hottest gun is 3 ammo. A gun whose
  price passes the magazine never fires, so the conversion caps at the magazine or the scale
  guarantees it never arises.
- **It leaves the heat economy.** The Puffin's growth, Cryo's cooling and the Whip Scorpion's
  blasts all skip it: the price of firing hot guns without heat, accepted.

**The look** (Claude's lean, for the asset session): the wide build. Machines show in a rear
three-quarter view in the fight and from above on the map; a long truck foreshortens into one
mass where a wide battery shows every mount, and battery is the artillery's own word. It shows
the identity at a glance: the old cannon on the centreline, the high-tech mounts on flank
sponsons. The high-tech read: the energy guns fire from cartridges, a rack of glowing cells
feeding the flanks and an act that ejects a spent one, the maker's register (it never trusted a
reactor, so it put the lasers on cartridges). Ryan's long truck (the MEADS and MLRS line) is
the obvious and effective form; the wide one is his weirder one.

## Holes already raised

- A dry gun is an entry that cannot be spent that turn, so a die of guns and no reload wastes
  its turn once the magazine is empty; mismatch costs time, never ability, since the cannon
  costs 1 and the ammo a turn always arms it.
- With the ramp, short fights are where it is weakest and long ones where it is strongest, the
  inverse of every heat machine: right for the formation fights and the boss with its formation
  (the pacing row's exam), and the runner reads the small packs.
- Two Wainwright & Sutler machines read vents: the Whip Scorpion's blast and this one's reload
  are two patterns, fuel against ammunition, and a team that fields both wants every vent in
  the game.
- Three bars on one maker (Spun Up's pips, this magazine, the Swallowtail's Muster), each shown
  on its own machine: a phone read to judge.
