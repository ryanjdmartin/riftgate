# Brief: the Honey Badger's identity (rework)

The session settles who the Honey Badger is: its passive, which today says nothing in a fight,
and how its hardpoints get a route into its locked pair.

**Status**: rework, in session (15 October): the hopper lean taken up, its numbers in
discussion. **Maker**: Minerva.
**Proposed tier**: one. **Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; the bible's open row "The
Honey Badger's identity" (section 7); `docs/roster_analysis.md` section 2 (the Badger's rating)
and section 11 (the bench); `content/chassis.json` (badger) and `content/components.json`
(chainsaw_claw, bullion_fragmenter).

## Where it stands in content

A heavy tracked brawler: the locked Chainsaw Claw (Spread; each foe its blows reach puts a load
in the hopper) and the locked Bullion Fragmenter (flat; fires up to three loads a shot at +5
each); three hardpoints; the passive Reinforced Skeleton (components crack but are never
destroyed). It is the roster's one named detachment identity: the runner has it taking a small
pack alone for a tenth of its hull (a diagnostic).

## What Ryan said

- 12 October (the bible's open row): scaling threats are fine for now, but scaling is not the
  Badger's identity. Is it damage, support, a tank, or something off its mining roots, an
  economy engine? His earlier thought: every melee hit filling the hopper.

## What Claude proposed

- 12 October (the bible's row): none of the three; a bruiser that will not stay down. Its
  passive could say so in the fight (once a battle, a blow that would wreck it leaves it at 1
  hull), in the campaign (it never sits out after a wreck and never loses a component), or off
  its roots (more salvage from what it kills). The hopper stays its kit, the mining in it.
- 14 October (the analysis, section 2), the lean: the hopper as the passive's business. Every
  weapon it fires puts a load in the hopper for each foe its blows reach, the claw's spread
  fastest; the hopper holds so many loads; loads held are plate (armour per load) and loads
  fired are damage. Any maker's Spread, Penetrate or multi-strike piece then becomes a filler,
  and the round's decision deepens: hold the ore and stand, or fire it and hit. "Never
  destroyed" leaves the passive for a Minerva module, where its campaign meaning survives.

## Holes already raised

- A filler on every weapon overflows the hopper unless the cap bites.
- A hopper always full makes the fragmenter a flat number again; the cap and the armour per
  load are the levers.
- The claw must stay the best filler, or it becomes a plain gun.
- The analysis rated its passive thin and its build diversity thin: only the claw fills and
  only the fragmenter fires, so no other maker's piece has a route in today.

## The bench (section 11), with the rule that set each aside

- The last stand (once a battle, a blow that would wreck it leaves it at 1 hull): binary, and
  rare in a fight.
- Campaign insurance (never sits out after a wreck, never loses a component): no job in a
  fight, so it is a module's text.
- Salvage from its kills: an economy engine as a chassis passive, and the economy is unbuilt.
- Every blow filling with no cap or plate: the hopper overflows and the fragmenter becomes a
  flat number.

## Since then

- The honest shield rule and Ryan's line against an unkillable team: plate from held loads is
  the Badger's own, never handed to allies.
- Ryan's taste: one passive, one idea; the hopper lean is one idea (ore held is plate, ore
  fired is damage) if it stays simple.

## The Minerva session (15 October)

**Claude's first round**, in chat: the hopper as the passive's business, a bruiser that turns a
fight into plate and shot. Every weapon it fires puts a load in the hopper for each foe its
blow damages; the hopper holds five; each load held is 2 armour; base armour 10 down to 5 (5
empty, 15 full); the claw digs two loads a foe so it stays the best filler once the explosives
carry Spread and Penetrate; the fragmenter still fires up to three at +5. Firing strips plate,
and the exposed aim weighs hull reached, so an emptied Badger draws fire and a full one sheds
it. Capped and drained, the hopper oscillates rather than ramps. "Never destroyed" to a Minerva
module keeping its name, Reinforced Skeleton, and Ryan's text. Bench: melee hits only (five of
the seven melee pieces are locked, so it would feed only fixed parts); Braced in place of
armour (a derived Braced muddies "lasts the round"); leftover ore as salvage at a fight's end
(for when the economy exists); riders never sticking to it (a permanent Ward, thin in two
domains of four).

**Ryan**, in his words: "Badger - worth considering, the effect can be dangerous if tuned badly,
as you've stated. Various ranges exist from the fragmenter spending small amounts of ore to the
whole load. it does push it into the detachment niche, which is fine, as it needs a proper role
other than "bruiser." I think if we stick with "every foe damaged," that's going to push it into
AoE territory anyway, so we can probably go to 1 armour a stack and assume that a competent
player is going to hit 2-3 enemies a hit anyway, and then make 10 the max stacks to keep your
final number. I would make the claw hit twice for sure, and maybe at 2 per hit, making it the
most efficient fill but perhaps not the most effective weapon. Fragmenter can start by losing 5
stacks, so half of max."

What it settles: the hopper as the identity, every foe damaged loading it (the area build, and
the detachment as the Badger's role beyond "bruiser"); 1 armour a load, ten at most, so 5 empty
and 15 full; the claw strikes twice, the most efficient filler and not the most effective
weapon; the fragmenter fires five loads, half the hopper.

## Open for the session

2. Whether a load counts for each blow (so a multi-strike piece fills by its strikes) or once a

3. The fragmenter's bonus a load, now that a shot fires five.
4. Where "never destroyed" goes (a module, or gone).
