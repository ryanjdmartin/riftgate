# The refit pass: the brief

The battle's resource model, remade from Ryan's play of Phase 2 (9 November) and ruled in its
final form on 13 November: shields and vents are off the die, a component is a weapon or
equipment, and the shield and the heat come back by a reboot alone. Section 22, the reboot's
rules with his words, is the core, and `CLAUDE.md` sends every session to it. Sections 7, 10 to
14, 16, 21 and 23 keep what is open or still leaned on. Every other section is a stub.

Cut down on 17 November from 3,478 lines. The long form, with every read, bench and model
table, is `git show 07f01fb:docs/briefs/refit_pass.md`, a stub's text under the same section
number. The numbers that follow from this pass are `docs/briefs/numbers_pass.md`, the rules as
they run are `docs/rules.md`, and every run named here is in `docs/measurements.md` by its date.

## 10. The squad a battle is tuned for, by the Convergence (11 November)

**His direction**: "the first battle should be tuned around 3 units ... 3rd convergence battles
onward we should tune it around 4 units. I'm harping on this for a couple points. One, it
significantly affects boss scaling, since the whole team is brought in. Second, the main
battlefield power a fourth unit gets is being able to reliably cover a flank, and stay out
there without presumably needing to come back to reinforce for an elite, most of the time anyway."

**Settled** (the bible's squad row; built 19 November): the first battle is the Meadow's, fought by the
three machines of the draft, and it brings the fourth: won, a choice of one of three machines
from the maker whose city was defended; lost, the draft's missing pick. So tier one has two
bands, the Meadow's first battle at three machines and every battle after at four, and a fight
is read at its rating: a lone patrol one, a pair or a hunting pack two, a formation three, an
elite and a boss four. His words: 11 November, "if the choice is in which city to defend in
order to get a guaranteed choice from that maker, then that works"; 18 November, "the waste
will only ever be fought with 4 machines. It's just the meadow at level 1 that gets 3"; and 19
November, of what he had held settled since the 11th, when this brief asked "Meadow first, yes
or no?" and wrote down no answer: "First battle, meadow, 3 machines from the draft, reward is a
choice of 1 of 3 machines from the maker, or the missing 4th draft pick on loss. I'm ok with
first battle being from any domain, really, but it's a whole other thing to balance, right?"
The opening domain is content's mark (a domain's `opening`), and the rules' The first battle
has the rule as it runs.

**His scope: tier one only.** "Let's focus only on balancing tier 1 fights for now. If you've
read the other work done then you'll know that we're considering tier 3 machines to have higher
stats, and lower tiers be upgradeable. That lets us actually scale player durability slightly
for higher tiers. And we don't have enough higher tier content right now to satisfactorily
outfit a higher tier." So tier one's numbers are level one's (`docs/locked_pieces_proposal.md`).

**His lean for the first battle.** "It seems to me like battle one should be relatively easy,
even considering the party of 3. It's more of an opportunity to gain resources, win, get your
party in order, then the real game starts from battle 2." By his word it sends one elite and is
no shorter. The reads of 11 and 12 November, in kind: a flat cut makes the Meadow's first battle
easy and not the Waste's or the Peak's; hull and the wall lose it separately, since the opening
asks three machines for a formation and two flank patrols (`Ramp.formations_at`,
`Ramp.patrols_at`); and the boss wants a block of its own, where `ContentDB.BOSS_TIERS` is by tier.

**What a trio pays today** (19 November, `tools/anchor_read.gd` with `machines=3`, tier one as
sized for four, the dice as dealt; `docs/measurements.md`). A formation is rated three, so a
trio pays the anchor's rows, 2.4% to 9.3% of a four's hull. A leader is a true four: an elite
fight costs a trio 24% to 35% by domain, and a boss 42% in the Meadow, won four fights in
five, 38% and 40% in the Marsh and the Peak, and 56% in the Waste, won one in two. So the
Meadow's first battle is still to be sized, its three bosses and two elite fights (Floraison
a coin's toss for three), and the first battle in any domain would be thirteen bosses and
eleven elite fights that do not take one cut: his "it's a whole other thing to balance".

**Open, his:**

2. How it is made easy. Lean: a block at Convergence 0, the first rift's foes at a share of

## 11. Pacing and length, from scratch (11 November)

Decided and built, the bible's pacing row and Rupture row with his words: a battle is counted
in beats of the tide, the Rupture takes what the opening places and the tide sends through its
second peak, a cleared field does nothing, and menace is a pack's threat rating, one to four.
Equal worth is not equal difficulty: tuning each composition to its rating within a band is the
numbers pass's, and what a boss pays and salvage's growth by tier are the economy pass's.

**On trial, four dials** (a debug build's TRIALS, each read as the next battle starts):

| Dial | Its choices | What it asks |
|---|---|---|

| STRIKE | 10, 7, 5 or 3 a pip, ten the game's | His "each needs to do less to the wall" |

## 16. The shield as a system (13 November, a frame and a model; nothing decided)

His words set the method, a rule in `CLAUDE.md` since: "Who are we trying to protect, how do we
protect them, how are we threatened, what counterplay exists on both sides." The three shapes
modelled here went with the reboot. The frame stands:

- *Who is protected.* The lights from the spike, a big blow or two on one machine: his Prairie
  Dog of 9 November, lost on a boss's first turn. And the battle's hull from the drip.
- *How, by what each layer answers.* The gauge, a fight's, answers both in part. Armour and
  Armoured answer the drip. The Barrier answers the spike. The lure moves either and a kill
  ends either. Nothing that is not a die answers the spike but the gauge's own number.
- *How we are threatened.* A pack's damage has a shape: its size and count (one blow, strikes,
  a swarm), where it lands (one pick, each its own, every machine, the same machine again),
  what it carries (a rider) and when (now, a follow-up, a countdown).
- *A test for a threat*, Claude's, from his line that breach foes were "either garbage, or
  broken ... never in between": a threat is fair when some layer answers it in part with
  nothing spent and some tool answers it whole with a die. Breach failed both ways and is cut;
  the Barrier basic fails its mirror, erasing the one shape most elites and bosses were.
- *The model.* the shield model of that week (`git show cc446826:tools/shield_model.py`, retired for `tools/budget_model.py`) sets a rule against the foes' shapes with no dice and no
  content: what a rule does with a shape, never what a fight costs.

## 21. The reboot by choice (13 November; nothing decided)

The shape that section 22 rules, in his words: "Let's look again but letting the player ride at
max heat and the reboot being a choice to vent heat and recover shield. If the shield is not
empty and the unit reboots, I'd feel like we should empty the shield. It's thematic for a
'shield reboot' and that way there's no gaming an overheat to recover pseudo partial shield,
when convenient." The frame it was reasoned in, in today's words:

- *Who it protects.* The machine no foe is aiming at, which may have both gauges back for a
  turn. The machine under the picks may not without standing bare. A regen was the reverse.
- *How.* Two budgets and one reset, the player's to call. It costs the turn, and the shield for
  the foes' turn.
- *What threatens it.* A pick on the machine that needs the reset. A blow at every machine,
  into which every reboot is bare. One foe on one machine, round after round. A blow over a
  shield's size, since nothing mends.
- *The player's counterplay.* Read the picks, which are set as the round opens and do not move,
  and reboot where none points. Ride at the maximum under a pick, holding or on a cool piece. A
  lure, which moves a pick. A Barrier over a machine that reboots. The kill of the foe that pins
  the hot machine.
- *The foes' counterplay.* An attack that goes for the hottest machine, which finds the one
  that wants the reset. Exposed, which turns the field on one machine. A blow at every machine.
  Numbers, each one more pick. A countdown, whose pick holds while it gathers.
- *What the model said* (`--choice` of the same file): the choice protects as a
  forced shutdown did; firing past the maximum is a trap; and his emptying rule closes the stall
  of 9 November, since a squad that could top up a standing shield would pass half its turns.

## 22. The reboot's rules (13 November; ruled by him, the build on his go)

Read with `docs/rules.md` (The reboot, Heat, and Shields, armour, hull), which states the rule
as it runs, and the bible's row "Nothing cools or mends by itself". His words, on section 21's
read:

> Yes let's go for it. Dump the rules spec here first before we start, though.
>
> The one main undecided point, that I currently see, is what happens at max heat. Either we
> let them burn hull, or we prevent them from using anything that creates more heat. The
> former is the better agency choice, but it has to be costed properly.
>
> One change I'd make to the current rules, is that heat over the maximum does NOT go away. We
> display it in an uncleanseable debuff on the unit, a stack for every point of heat, and it's
> treated like any other heat - so if a hypothetical cooling ability comes through, it would
> reduce the stacks first before hitting the bar. Obviously it can be tracked however in the
> engine, but that's how I would display it on screen.
>
> There's a couple other edge cases that would need addressing:
>
> -If the shield is broken, by either foes or overheat, that's another uncleanseable debuff. It
> states that the unit cannot gain shield by any method. So granting a shield or shield Regen
> through whatever abilities we might allow, only applies to a "live" shield.
>
> -any triggers on Overheat, if they should exist, only fire the first time it overheats,
> reset on the "reboot." So if the machine overheats, then somehow gets back under the cap (it
> gains cooling, is cooled, or its cap is temporarily raised), it cannot trigger an overheat
> event even if it goes back over. Not until it reboots.
>
> Any other edge cases you need me to rule on?
>
> I'd also like a list of the chassis that break with this change, so that I can start

### The spec, as ruled and as it runs

1. A shield takes a blow up to its number and the rest reaches the hull. It does not come back
   by itself.
2. A spend adds its heat. Heat does not leave by itself.
3. Regen and cooling exist only where something grants them: a passive, a module, a status, a
   component. No chassis carries either, and the validator refuses both keys.
4. On the player's turn a machine that has spent nothing this turn may reboot, its die dragged
   onto REBOOT. It is its whole turn: a machine that could act more than once, by Rapid, Instant
   or a grant, gives all of it up.
5. At once its shield goes to nothing and its heat with it, Overheated's stacks too ("Maybe
   the heat gets shunted when the shield gets taken down?", 17 November), and it is free to
   overheat again. It is Rebooting through the enemy's turn, bare. UNDO takes it back.
6. As the next round opens, before the roll and before the foes pick, its shield is whole and
   Broken is gone. Heat laid on it since the vent stays ("That's the risk, same as taking hull
   damage with no shield").
7. Nothing forces a reboot, and it clears nothing else: the machine's statuses stand. It is no
   pass, no entry spent and no shield gain, so nothing that reads one fires on it.
8. A rebooting machine has vented, so it carries nothing to burn at END TURN, and no rule spares
   it: what lands on it after burns like any (the bible's row has the two forms before this).
9. A spend is never refused for its heat. Heat may pass the maximum, and it stays there. What
   stands past it shows as Overheated, an uncleanseable debuff, a stack a point. It is heat like
   any other: every rule that reads heat counts it, anything that cools takes the stacks first,
   and a maximum that rises takes stacks back onto the bar.
10. At END TURN, after its Ablating has ticked, a machine past its maximum loses a point of
    hull for every stack it carries, at every END TURN it stands there. The stacks build with
    every spend, so the burn grows until the machine reboots or is wrecked. A stack costs one
    hull ("Overheat damage did felt fair at 1 per heat ... I'd go with that to start"; settled
    after his play of two and three, 23 November: "overheat at x1").
11. The first END TURN a machine stands past its maximum since its last reboot is its overheat:
    its shield breaks, and anything that triggers on an overheat fires. One overheat between
    reboots: a machine that falls back under its maximum and passes it again only burns.
12. A shield at nothing is Broken, an uncleanseable debuff, whatever took it there: a foe's
    blow, the overheat, its own reboot, or a shield spend or cost of its own ("you can break
    yourself"). A Broken shield gains no shield by any method, a grant, a regen, a refill on a
    kill or a module's, until the reboot's recovery, so a shield breaks once between reboots.
13. A Barrier is a buff in front of shield and hull. It touches neither bar, and it lands on
    any machine, Broken or rebooting.

What follows from those, built so:

- The recovery lands before the foes pick, so an attack that goes for the weakest machine reads
  a whole shield, and one that goes for the hottest reads only what was laid on since the vent.
- The burn is hull damage for every passive that reads hull damage, each END TURN it burns.
- A Swallowed machine cannot reboot: it has no turn to give. A Phased one may, and its reboot
  is the safe one. A machine out of reach neither burns nor overheats: what it carries past its
  maximum stays and burns the turn it is back, the first such turn its overheat.
- A sealed machine opens as it reboots ("Polar bear unseals when rebooting. It's a good change
  because you're never accidentally stuck sealed"). The Whip Scorpion's vent lands on every foe
  in reach, 1 for every 2 heat it sheds ("the reboot vent is the one that explodes").
- Overheated, Broken and Rebooting are terms, each a draft for his marks. A fight opens with none.
- REBOOT is a drop's target between END TURN and UNDO and no button ("dragging is the gesture we
  already have, we should stick with that"). Its look is his to judge running (the to-do).
- The fight bot reboots before it burns (`Policy._reboot_first`, `_reboot_idle`), so no read
  says whether one hull a stack is too soft: line 10's dial is that lever, and his play the read.

## 23. The stock basics by the fodder's health (14 November; his logic read, nothing decided)

**His logic** (the bible's vanilla row holds the decision it led to; the long form has his
words whole). "A 'standard' vanilla shot should be 1 less than our 'standard' fodder health,
whatever that is. So if a goblin, the most basic swarm unit, is 4, then we set a normal, no
rider, direct gun at 3. That's the baseline ... I have no idea what each rider is worth. But I
would cost them by damage first, not heat. So a basic with one decent rider gets -1 damage, and
one with a more powerful effect gets -2, and so on until it turns into Equipment by dealing no
damage. Then I go up. In the example of the 4 health goblin, a 4 damage shot is premium since
it prevents one instance of damage. That needs to cost something, and for basics, the only cost
we should have is Heat. So the 4 damage basic, or more explicitly the +1 damage gun over the
vanilla, needs to bite appropriately. My thought process is that that extra point of damage
should be something to work for as a first build step for the player. Reliably one-shotting the
core fodder units."

**The read**, with `tools/basics_model.py` (three machines, one gun a round each, no dice):

- *What holds.* The fodder's hit points are the unit a gun is measured in. A kill is worth more
  than its damage, since a foe dead this round does not strike this round. His rule fits his
  kill-time test of 21 October, and riders paid in damage keep heat the one budget a gun draws on.
- *One under the fodder is a knife's edge, by design.* Every point on either side crosses the
  line: a stack of any amp doubles the squad's kills on standard fodder, and a point of plate on
  a foe undoes it. So amps and plate are the dearest things in tier one.
- *The roster sits on one number.* Most of tier one's formation slots are a plain 4, so the
  step from 3 to 4 is a cliff. What turns it into steps is the foes' spread of thin, standard
  and thick, never the gun. Plate and thick fodder are also what make a rider's price real.
- *Heat cannot price a point that crosses the line.* A shot's heat is a share of a reboot, a
  straight price, and the point's worth is a step. So the gun of 4's heat sets how often it
  speaks, in kills a reboot, and the roster sets how good it is.
- *"Accessible" is how often the die shows it.* One entry in six is a sixth to two fifths of a
  machine's turns; the Skua's first stack is every turn, so the Skua is the baseline's canary.

Set aside, each with the rule that killed it: the vanilla at half the fodder (a point more is
dead, and the thick take three and four shots); the vanilla equal to the fodder (every gun
kills in one and the round's question goes); riders paid in heat (the rider and the gun would
draw on one budget); the base gun moved down for the Skua (one passive setting every gun).

**His scope**: "Remember that I'm just trying to price the basics, and from there estimate the
cost of the riders. Tier two foes are facing a more coherent build from the player, so these
+/- 1 questions aren't relevant there." And of heat: "we have heat as a second cost lever. One
step down, but heat can rise on a 1 damage weapon."

**What landed, and what is open.** The plain basic guns deal 3 and the Laser Carbine 4 since 15
November, their heat unmoved at 4 and 5. All three are settled as built since 23 November, after
his play of the dials that moved them (every weapon cooler, the Carbine's point hotter, the
standard foe at 5: "We can lock in the rank and file setting as 4, heat as built, top basic as
built ... That all felt fine enough as a starting point, to me"), where this pass leaned a point
cooler on every weapon and two hotter on the Carbine. Nothing here is open. What
each point is worth, the riders' prices and the bench are the numbers brief's sections 4 and 6.
