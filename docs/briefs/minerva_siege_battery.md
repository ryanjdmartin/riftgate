# Brief: the siege battery, Ryan's H-tank (new chassis)

The session designs Minerva's treaded tier-three capstone from Ryan's old pitch of a gargantuan
H-tank with two massive turrets, and finds the charge that makes the player decide when to fire
them. The working label "the siege battery" stays; the name is Ryan's. Minerva's other tier
three, the battlecruiser, has a brief of its own (`minerva_battlecruiser.md`), which also holds
the broadside first proposed here.

**Status**: direction, parked on Ryan's charge brief (20 October) to pick up later; his pitch 15
October, the shells proposed 17 October and given to Wainwright & Sutler, 20 October. **Maker**: Minerva. **Proposed tier**: three.
**Read first**: `docs/briefs/README.md`; `minerva_battlecruiser.md`; the bible's rows on
cooldowns (section 8), the makers table and the size classes (section 7), and the ways of
moving (section 4).

## Ryan's pitch (15 October)

In his words: "Siege battery: I had an old pitch that resembled this. A gargantuan, true H-tank
with 4 separate tracks. Two massive double-barreled turrets, one in front, one behind, the
chassis between the rear two treads higher so the rear turret rises above the front turret,
armoured panels above the treads, gimbaled hardpoint mounts on each of them. The turrets are "so
powerful that Minerva couldn't help but build a chassis big enough to mount two," so need a huge
hit and maybe a massive cooldown or charge time or some other balancing mechanic. Slowest unit
in the game, passive might be something like "Massive Chassis: terrain penalties don't apply"
so that it is a steady slow crawler.
-Updates to this based on newer learning: I think a massive gun (or two) with a cooldown isn't
enough to balance it, since it will result in boom-boom-skirmish won. The cannons need a
charge-up mechanic that will make the player decide WHEN to fire one, not how often. The obvious
one is "every shield used on this" but I feel like that's been done to death."

And on the battlecruiser: "I think the battlecruiser should be a different design from the H
tank, and the "fire everything" you mentioned kind of makes sense for a flying brick. The tank
and the cruiser could be the two tier 3s, one the capstone treaded unit, one the capital ship
that it seems like every maker is going to end up getting."

What it settles: two locked turrets, front and rear, the rear superfiring over the front; four
hardpoints, the gimbaled mounts on the four armoured panels; the slowest machine in the game;
the turrets' balance is a charge that asks when, never a cooldown that asks how often; no charge
from shields spent on it.

## How it got here

- The sketch (Claude, 14 October, the analysis's gap two): every weapon it fires strikes twice
  and goes dark for two rounds after, the cooldown made an identity. Set aside: the strafer was
  decided (14 October) with "every weapon it fires strikes twice at half", and mechanics should
  not repeat.
- The analysis's bench: Minerva's hold-fire line (an entry left unspent at the end of its turn
  braces it for the round, a read nothing uses); a tier three as one big hit with a cooldown on
  its locked gun (smaller and safer), which Ryan's update rules out as boom-boom.
- Claude's first round in the session (15 October): the broadside (spend a weapon and every
  live weapon on its die fires at that foe until it falls; each that fires goes dark for two
  rounds) with a locked loader, as Minerva's battlecruiser. Ryan made the battlecruiser a design
  of its own and kept the broadside for it (`minerva_battlecruiser.md`).

## The charge (15 and 16 October)

Claude's second round (15 October): "steady aim", one charge the two turrets share, growing by
one each round to three, a wound on the tank knocking one off, a turret's shot spending it at
+10 a charge; the foes' shown intents say when a wound is coming, so "fire now or lose a
charge" is read off the screen; the alternative "ripe target", +10 a debuff on the target.
Claude's review (16 October): steady aim is a cooldown in disguise, since the charge grows on
its own and, with the Armadillo pulling blows, the wounds that knock it off are rare, so it
becomes "fire every third round". Ryan, 16 October: "correct. Need help here. Looking for a soft
way to push the player to timing their shots instead of just firing them on cooldown. It's
possible that doesn't exist and the design is a non starter but that's why we're here."

Claude's candidates (16 October), each against Ryan's test (when, never how often):

- **Firing drops its guard** (Claude's lean): a turret may fire every round, but the tank that
  fires is open until its next turn (armour 0, or Braced lost, or its shield down: the form is
  the stock pass's). The "when" is the foes' shown intents: fire when nothing heavy is aimed at
  it this round, hold or vent when something is, or have the Armadillo lure the blow off it
  first, so Minerva's tanks feed its capstone. Not a cooldown; nothing to count; the foes have
  already picked their targets when the player decides, so no aim reads the dropped guard. The
  hole: against a pack that never aims at it (it is the hardest hull to reach) it fires every
  round, which is a big gun with no limit; its heat is the second price.
- **A magazine**: each turret holds so many shells a fight (two, say), restocked between
  fights, nothing reloads them in one. The "when" is which moments of the fight: the Antlion
  now or the Sandworm later. The one pure "which moments" shape; its risks are hoarding (shells
  left at the fight's end are wasted, which answers it; section 8's consumables row) and a long
  boss fight in which the tank becomes its hardpoints after the shells, which "mismatch costs
  time, never ability" allows. Engine: an entry spent so many times a fight, then blank.
- **The telegraph hunter**: a turret's shot does its full number only against a foe that is
  winding up (a countdown, a special, a blow above some size on its intent), half against the
  rest. The "when" is the enemy's wind-up, the design test's "interrupt the big hit" made a
  gun. Its hole: in a fight with no telegraph it is a half gun fired on cooldown, so it earns
  its keep only in the fights that matter.

The bench, from the review: a charge the player builds by holding fire (an action paid, a
cooldown by another name); a charge that decays or explodes if held (it forces the shot on
time, the opposite of a choice); damage from the target's state kept debuffed (Ryan's
objection to ripe target stands); the Badger's hopper shape (one turret loading the other).

Ryan, 17 October, on the candidates: "the ammo is an interesting idea, but somewhere in here
needs to live the tanks identity, the build openers. Since this is a tier 3, we can go wild with
rule breaks, and if its a Minervan capstone even better. Something about needing ammo, and a
way of recharging it faster... Do all the tanks guns use ammo? Is that the gimmick?"

**Claude's third round** (17 October): yes, all of them, and the gimmick is that it runs on
shells where every other machine runs on heat. The passive breaks the heat rule for one
machine: its weapons add no heat and each costs a shell from its magazine, the turrets several;
a vent spent on it reloads shells instead of cooling (so much vented a shell). What that gives:

- The turrets' limit without a cooldown: a turret's shot is three shells of eight (placeholders),
  so "fire now or hold" is "is this target worth three of what I have, against the vents I will
  see". Two turret shots back to back are possible, at the price of everything else going quiet
  and the magazine dry, which is the capstone doing its job once a fight and the choice the
  test asks for.
- The build openers, both from other makers: every vent in the game is its loader (the Blowout
  Stack, the Coolant Jacket, the Puffin's shield that vents, a Rapid vent reloading and acting
  again, the piece to watch), and every hot gun in the game is cold on it (the Overclocked
  Railgun's 40 heat becomes one shell), so the hottest pieces have a home that pays in shells.
  A tier-three module may widen the magazine.
- The read: its heat gauge is a magazine, shown in shells; the foes' Burning and Scorched still
  land on a small heat gauge of its own, so the Peak keeps its answer to it.
- The identity test: a new price (shells), a change to what two kinds of piece do (weapons and
  vents), two routes in, two payoffs.

Holes: a dry gun is an entry that cannot be spent that turn, so a die of four guns and no vent
wastes its turn once the magazine is empty (the heat build's overheat by another name:
mismatch costs time, never ability, since vents, shields and buffs still spend); short fights
are free and long ones are where it bites, the inverse of heat, which is right for a siege
machine; a Rapid vent on it is a free reload with the turn given back (the Rapid limiter row);
the magazine's size and the turrets' price are the whole balance; and whether shells carry
between fights (a battle-long magazine, reloaded at the gate or from loot) is a dial, kept
per fight in the first cut.

Bench: shells on the turrets only, heat on the hardpoints (two gauges on one phone machine,
and the hot guns lose their home); a locked loader entry in place of vents reloading (the
reload becomes die luck, and other makers' vents lose their route in); steady aim and ripe
target (above).

**Ryan, fourth round** (18 October), in his words: "If we do the ammo version, I would just add a
new ammo bar under or replacing the heat bar, to make it clear it's a resource and avoid
interacting with other game effects. The only issue I have with it is that it really feels like a
W&S design..."

Claude's reading: the ammo bar of its own is better than the third round's "heat gauge shown in
shells", since the Peak's Burning and Scorched then land on a heat gauge the machine still has
and the shells touch nothing else; the heat gauge stays, small, for the foes' fire. On the feel:
what makes a magazine Minervan rather than Wainwright & Sutler's is the register, not the
mechanic. Wainwright & Sutler count rounds because they are poor (a revolver's six, a boiler's
pressure); Minerva would count shells because its guns are too big to feed any other way, so the
text says autoloader, breech and magazine, the reload is the coolant cycle racking a shell (a
vent spent on it), and the numbers are heavy (a turret shot three shells of eight). If that does
not carry it for Ryan, the alternatives are to give shells to Wainwright & Sutler's gunship or
revolver later and find the battery another "when" (the telegraph hunter, or firing drops its
guard, above), or to run the shells and judge the feel in play, since the look of the bar and the
words on it are what read as a maker.

**Ryan, fifth round** (20 October, in the Wainwright & Sutler session; the briefs README's damage
dealers note): the all-shells machine reads to him as a Wainwright & Sutler tier three, and Claude
agreed there that it is that maker's artillery tenth (the cold price the heat row asked for, on
the maker whose kit is cold, the carrier's resupply its loader), a brief of its own when he says
so. So the shells leave this brief, and the battery's charge is open again among the earlier
candidates: firing drops its guard, the magazine as a per-fight count, the telegraph hunter, or
none.

**Ryan, sixth round** (20 October), in his words: "The tier 3 tank, brief something like 'using
components adds one charge, up to a max. Each of the cannons spends charge to gain power, such as
armour piercing at one charge, penetrate with two charge, and so on, and perhaps the cooldown
increases with each charge spent as well.' That will be good enough for now so that we can pick
it up again later."

What it settles: the charge is his, built by spending, any component a charge up to a cap, and
spent by the turrets for power in steps (armour piercing at one, Penetrate at two, more above),
perhaps a longer cooldown the more spent; the design is parked here until he picks it up. Notes
for then, Claude's: a charge a spend accrues at the die's pace, three or four a round with Rapid
in the kit, so the cap and the steps set how often the top shot comes, and the "when" is which
step to fire at (a cheap shot now or the Penetrate later); the cooldown rising with the spend is
the price per die the cooldown row wants; the Armadillo's seal and the Nemesis's rage are the
stores this resembles, so its chip is a count like theirs; the engine has a count on a machine
(a store), a cooldown property not yet built, and nothing that reads a store to change a
weapon's keywords and number by steps, which is the new piece.

## Open for the session
