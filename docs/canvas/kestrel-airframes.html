Kestrel Airframe Proposals

Five new Kestrel chassis that leave the fighter-jet outline: two big ones, a racer, a twin-boom and a flying car. Each has a name, a job, a passive and placeholder numbers. Nothing is in content yet. Pick, tweak and rename in chat, and the picks land as drafts.

On the hex

Drawn the way the map draws them: from above, nose toward the rift, each machine's widest extent fitted to its class (light 0.72 of a hex, medium 0.88, heavy 1.05, assault 1.2). Solid hexes are in service, traced from their lowforge functions; dashed hexes are sketches.

The five

Every number is a placeholder in tens. Armour is 0 on all of them: Kestrel builds light whatever the size.

K-19 Mk I Strategic Bomber

Lammergeier

  • Tier 3
  • Assault
  • Flyer
  • Move 30

Land first

The biggest thing Kestrel has put boosters on: a strategic bomber that opens its bay over whatever is below it. Named for the vulture that drops bones onto rocks from a great height.

Looks fast
A long chrome needle with a drooped nose, a cobalt cranked delta, canards behind the canopy, and six boosters in one bank across the tail, so the camera sees a wall of exhausts. At move 30 it only has to look fast.
Passive
Universal Release Bay (working name). Every weapon it carries is Indirect: it reaches any foe, and a blow on the front rank splashes it as indirect fire does. Runs today The engine already changes reach by passive.
Opens
Direct kit with riders lands behind the front: Chain, Spread, the Headhunter's missing-hull bonus. The Artillery Rangefinder (no splash) is its natural module, and shields pay for danger close.
Fixed
None. Six hardpoints.
Acts
The wingtips fold down for a run and back up; the boosters light along the bank one by one; a slow banked circle on the map.
Name
No clash found in the four properties. A pilots' alternative: Bustard, the heaviest bird that flies.

K-17 Mk I Wing-in-Ground Strike Ship

Cormorant

  • Tier 2
  • Assault
  • Hover
  • Move 30

A ground-effect missile ship, classed as a boat. Eight boosters on the nose canards blow it up onto its own cushion of air, and three pairs of launch tubes ride its spine. Pilots named it for the black bird that runs along the water before it flies.

Looks fast
Its boosters are at the front, two banks of four ahead of the wing, with a long low hull and a tall T-tail behind them. Kestrel's second hovercraft, and its first big one.
Passive
Ripple-Fire Sequencer (working name). It starts every fight with three stacks of Overcharged (+15 on every blow), and one fades at each round's end. Runs today As three lines of one effect; a stack count on stack_status would tidy it.
Opens
Many strikes and Rapid entries, since every blow takes the bonus while it lasts. Short fights. On the map: strike, withdraw, come back with a fresh salvo. The withdrawal still gives the enemy its turn and Rift Pressure its point.
Fixed
Optional: the spine launchers, a two-strike rack that fires Direct. Sea-skimmers fly at the front rank, where Wainwright & Sutler's shells are lobbed.
Acts
The launch tubes lift to firing angle and settle; the canards tilt to blow under the wing and it heaves up on its cushion; a slow balancing heave at rest.
Name
No clash found. Alternative: Shearwater, the seabird that skims the wave tops.

K-13 Mk I Pylon Racer

Hummingbird

  • Tier 2
  • Light
  • Flyer
  • Move 50

Land first

A pylon racer pressed into service. The whole body is one booster with a seat at the back: the Gee Bee's shape on Kestrel's engine. It does everything at once until the heat stops it.

Looks fast
A fat chrome teardrop with a cowled intake for a nose, stub wings with racing numbers, and a tiny canopy faired into the fin at the very back. Nothing in the roster has this outline.
Passive
Throttle Wired Open (working name). Every component it spends is Rapid: it switches off for the round, the die rerolls free, and it acts again. Runs today A granted keyword, like the Wild Boar's Spillover.
Opens
Cheap, cool entries become strong, and vents and shields stop costing its turn. Each spend leaves a dead entry for the next reroll to land on, so a d4 averages about three spends a round. Heat is the real brake.
Fixed
None. Four hardpoints.
Acts
It hovers nose-up and darts sideways and backwards; a throttle blip that shakes the whole body.
Name
BattleTech has an obscure VTOL called the Humming Bird, a vehicle and not a 'Mech: the Sparrowhawk's case, a coincidence kept. Raptor alternative: Falconet, the smallest falcon and an old light cannon.

K-15 Mk I Interdictor

Kite

  • Tier 2
  • Heavy

  • Move 40

Twin booms with a gun gondola between them. The booms are a capacitor's plates, and the gap behind the wing crackles. Everything it touches is set up for someone else's big hit. Named for the raptor with the forked tail.

Looks fast
Two long chrome booms with radome noses and boosters at their tails, forward-swept outer wings, a tailplane bridging the booms, and an arc of light between them that reads from above.
Passive
Fork-Tail Capacitor (working name). Every foe its weapons reach and leave standing is Shocked: the next team blow on it lands double. Runs today A granted keyword.
Opens
Many small hits to set shocks, for allies with one big hit: the Sparrowhawk's Railgun lands double. Its own second blow on a foe always doubles. Every shock feeds the Baboon's Paradox meter.
Fixed
None. Five hardpoints.
Acts
The arc builds between the booms and snaps; the booms flex; on the map, a split-S.
Name
BattleTech has an obscure Clan hover vehicle called the Kite, not a 'Mech: a coincidence, kept. Alternative: Frigatebird, a forked tail and a red throat pouch for the arc.

K-5 Mk VI Executive Runabout

Magpie

  • Tier 1
  • Light
  • Flyer
  • Move 50

Land first

Kestrel's pre-war sky-coupe, drafted: tailfins, chrome, a bubble top and four booster pods where the wheels used to be. The sixth model year of an old civilian line, hence the low number and the high mark. Named for the bird that loves anything shiny.

Looks fast
From above it reads as a car: a bullet nose, a hood stripe, two tailfins with boosters in their tips, and pods at the four corners.
Passive
Bow-Wave Field (working name). It starts a fight with 10 shield for every hex between where it began its turn and the fight, past its maximum; the excess fades at the end of the first round. Needs engine The battle hands that distance to the fight, and a passive's shield may pass the maximum.
Opens
A die with no shield entry, so all guns. On the map, end each turn far from the next fight. A hunter that catches it meets it with no bow wave.
Fixed
None. Four hardpoints. A second Kestrel tier one, so the tender's Kestrel slot stops being the Sparrowhawk every run.
Acts
The pods flip from drive to flight and back; a showroom turn on the spot; the tailfin boosters flare.
Name
No clash in the four properties (a fan-made BattleTech variant uses it; not canon). Raptor alternative: Secretarybird, the raptor that hunts on foot, for an executive's car.

Calls to make first

Where the proposal meets something the bible or the engine already says.

  • “Nothing big” retires. The bible's makers row has Kestrel building small and mid flyers, nothing big. The Lammergeier and the Cormorant overturn that. Proposed row: flyers and hovercraft on big boosters, interceptors to strategic bombers, every one built light (armour 0). Minerva's one flying brick stays apart: slab sides and armour, where Kestrel's big ones are needles and deltas with none.
  • Big is a class, not a model size. The map fits every model's widest extent to its class, so a big airframe reads big only as an assault machine (1.2 of a hex). Both big ones are assault; the Kite is heavy.
  • Indirect fire is Wainwright & Sutler's line. The Release Bay gives a Kestrel indirect fire. My case: W&S builds the indirect guns, while the Lammergeier makes Kestrel's direct guns indirect and pays the splash, so it is a chassis rule and not a maker's weapon. Your call.
  • Variable geometry is Aphelion's word. So no swing wings: the Lammergeier is a fixed delta. Its wingtips fold down in an act; say if that is too close.
  • Six flyers in eight. Four of the five fly, so a Kestrel team ignores crags and water more often, and the bible's open question about a flyer weakness gets more pressing. The Cormorant hovers on purpose.
  • Roster weight. Kestrel would go from three chassis to eight, against two each for Aphelion and the Arsenal. Land two or three now.
  • The Magpie counts distance, not move spent. Move spent would reward wiggling in place before every fight. Distance from where it began its turn makes the position at the end of the last turn the decision.
  • Shock on everything is strong. The Faraday Hull already reads strong against rank and file, and the Kite's own second blow on a foe always doubles. The first one to measure.

What I'd land first

The Lammergeier, the Hummingbird and the Magpie. The bomber is the brief, the racer is the most different thing to play, and the car is a second Kestrel tier one. The first two run on today's engine; the Magpie needs one small piece. The Cormorant and the Kite can follow once those three have been played.
