Working title: Riftgate  ·  art direction, round two

Riftgate
Hybrid Mock

The direction is a JRPG split: chibi 16 px sprites on the battlefield, higher-resolution combat sprites in the skirmish, the way old Final Fantasy separates overworld from battle and Advance Wars separates map from clash. The insight that makes it work is that cuteness comes from proportion, not pixel count. A combat sprite at 48 px reads as a war machine because its head shrinks, its limbs lengthen and its hull gains mass, not because it is painted. Both layers stay outlined pixel art; only the proportions change. Heavies, elites and bosses also overflow their hex on the map, so size becomes information at a glance.

Layer 1

Battlefield

The full 7 × 13 pointy-top grid at 1× logical, gate at the south centre, rift at the north centre. Squads and skirmisher mechs are 16 px chibi sprites at 3× (48 px) sitting inside a 52 px hex. Bulwark and the eldritch elite are authored larger (24 × 27 and 24 × 32 at 3×) and overflow their hex upward, anchored at the hex base.

Every sprite, hex and path on this page is a code-drawn style sketch from a hand-typed character grid, not final art. What is being tested is scale, proportion, the outline rule and the overflow rule, all of which survive a real pixel artist.

Layer 2

Skirmish

The skirmish screen as it exists in the engine at 720 × 1280 logical, shown at half size, with combat sprites added. Enemy cards carry a 48 px combat sprite beside the name and HP bar; each mech card carries its combat sprite above the 120 px die face. Combat sprites are 40 to 48 px, realistic proportions, one pixel of ink outline, at most 16 colours per unit, and no painterly shading.

What the combat sprite adds

At 48 px on a 360 px wide screen each card gets a unit you can actually recognise: the ghoul's ribs, the necromancer's staff, Bulwark's claws, Sentinel's gun. The die face stays the biggest object on the card, so the sprite reads as a portrait of the die's owner, not as a competing UI element.

Sprite palettes

Code-drawn sketches. A real artist would give the ghoul a jaw, the tank real bogies and the necromancer a face; the proportions and the outline rule are the point.

Same unit

Same unit, two scales

The map sprite and the combat sprite of the same two units side by side. The map version is a toy on purpose: big head, stubby limbs, one idle bob. The combat version keeps the outline and the palette, but the head is a fifth of the height, the arms are long and the hull has mass.

Rules

Rules of the direction

  • Map sprites. 16 × 16 chibi, one pixel ink outline, four to six colours per faction so enemies are palette swaps, one idle bob (two frames). Heavies and elites are 24 × 27 to 24 × 32, same rules.
  • Combat sprites. 40 to 64 px, realistic proportions (head a fifth to a sixth of height, long limbs, mass in the hull), one pixel outline on the silhouette, at most 16 colours per unit, no painterly ramps. Animation set: idle, hit, one attack.
  • Overflow. Only heavy player mechs, single huge elites and bosses. Ordinary squads and skirmishers sit inside 48 px. The sprite is anchored at the base of its hex and spills upward; horizontal overflow is capped at a quarter hex (13 px) each side.
  • Draw order. Terrain, then paths and highlights, then units row by row from north to south so a southern unit covers the hex behind it. The tap target is always the hex, never the sprite.
  • UI layer. Text, bars, buttons and the die face live on a native-resolution layer over the sprite layer. Sprites are drawn at integer scale (3× map, 2× skirmish) with smoothing off.
  • Tone. The map is allowed to be a board game with tin toys. The skirmish is where the machine looks like a machine and the rift thing looks like a rift thing. The joke is the distance between the two.
20–40 minper map sprite (16 px, palette swap for variants)
2–4 hper combat sprite, idle + hit + attack
~1.25×cost of a single-set direction, not 2×: the map sprite is a rounding error next to the combat sprite
2 setsper unit, but the map set is the cheap one and the combat set was needed anyway
Decide

Open questions

  1. Squad count on the map. Stacked mini-sprites (two or three chibis offset in the hex) or a number badge in the hex corner? Stacks are more charming and cost nothing extra; badges are more legible at 6+.
  2. Elite portraits in the skirmish. Does an elite or boss get a larger 64 px sprite, a painted portrait, or the same 48 px slot as a squad? The card grid currently assumes six equal cards.
  3. Idle animation on the map. One bob for everything, per-class idles (tracks rock, hovercraft float, aircraft hover), or static with a shadow pulse? Thirteen rows of bobbing sprites might be noisy.
  4. Die face grid. Pixel-grid die (digits and glyphs at 3× on the sprite grid) or vector die at native resolution? The combat sprites are 2×, so a 2× pixel die would match them; the current engine die is vector.
  5. Overflow for enemy formations. If a big elite sits in the north third and overflows upward it covers nothing important; if it advances to the gate it will cover player units behind it. Is that acceptable, or should overflow be suppressed within two rows of the gate?
  6. Combat sprite facing. Enemies face south-west and mechs face north-east, or everyone faces the camera? Facing makes the formation read as a fight; front-facing sprites are cheaper to reuse in the codex.
Hex maths: pointy-top, hex width = √3 × R, row step = 1.5 R, odd rows shifted right by half a hex; with R = 30 the width is 52 px and 7.5 widths is 390 px. Every canvas is rendered at device pixel ratio with image smoothing off.
